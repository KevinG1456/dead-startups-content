# Dead SaaS & Software Graveyard ("Sunset") — 30  Content Titles new Video Ideas

**Niche #21 expansion — Batch 2.** None of these repeat the original 20. Each idea lists the failure pattern it teaches (the evergreen lesson) and the primary public source material, since document-driven research is this niche's moat.

---

## Cluster 1: Money Mechanics (how the cap table kills)

**1. The startup that IPO'd and died within 24 months**
Pattern: public-market exposure revealing what private funding hid. Sources: S-1 vs. final 10-K, delisting notices, shareholder suits.

**2. The bridge round death spiral: 4 companies, 4 "one last raise" stories**
Pattern: bridge-to-nowhere financing. Sources: Crunchbase round sequences, down-round terms leaked in Delaware litigation.

**3. Liquidation preferences: the $100M exit where founders got $0**
Pattern: preference stacks eating the waterfall. Sources: acquisition filings, founder post-mortem essays, cap-table reconstructions.

**4. The company that died of a term sheet (the deal that scared off every other investor)**
Pattern: toxic terms as a signaling death sentence. Sources: founder accounts, standard NVCA docs annotated against the reported terms.

**5. SAFE note pileup: the seed startup that raised 9 times and could never price a round**
Pattern: uncapped/stacked SAFEs destroying Series A math. Sources: founder post-mortems, YC deal docs, dilution modeling.

**6. The revenue-based financing trap: when 8% of monthly revenue became 80% of margin**
Pattern: RBF covenants vs. seasonal SaaS revenue. Sources: lender marketing archives, founder essays, court disputes.

**7. The startup that got margin-called: venture debt's ugliest quarter**
Pattern: MAC clauses and covenant breaches. Sources: UCC filings, bank litigation, the SVB post-collapse debt landscape.

## Cluster 2: Product & Market Autopsies

**8. The best product in the category lost. Twice. (When distribution beats quality)**
Pattern: distribution > product. Sources: G2/Capterra review archives, traffic history, competitor S-1s.

**9. Killed by a feature: the startup that became a checkbox in Salesforce**
Pattern: feature-vs-product risk. Sources: release-note archives, Wayback pricing pages, churn commentary in founder interviews.

**10. The horizontal trap: the tool for everyone that no one paid for**
Pattern: no ICP, no wedge. Sources: archived homepages showing positioning drift quarter by quarter.

**11. Too early twice: the company that built the right product 8 years before the market**
Pattern: timing risk (the idea later worked for someone else). Sources: patent trail, press archives, the successor company's S-1.

**12. The enterprise pivot that killed the SMB cash cow**
Pattern: abandoning the base for logos. Sources: pricing-page history, sales-team LinkedIn growth, churned-customer forum threads.

**13. Per-seat pricing in an AI world: the first SaaS casualties of usage-based disruption**
Pattern: pricing-model obsolescence. Sources: earnings transcripts, archived pricing tiers, analyst notes.

**14. The integration that was the whole company (and the day the API key stopped working)**
Pattern: single-platform dependency, told through a new case (distinct from the "40 startups in one day" ecosystem view in Batch 1 — this is one company, hour by hour).

## Cluster 3: Operating Failures (inside the building)

**15. 14 months of runway, 19 months of roadmap: reconstructing a burn-rate suicide from job postings**
Pattern: hiring ahead of revenue. Sources: LinkedIn headcount curves, archived careers pages, layoffs.fyi data.

**16. The remote-first company that quietly stopped shipping**
Pattern: execution decay. Sources: GitHub commit-frequency graphs, changelog archives, Glassdoor review timeline.

**17. The sales-led startup where CAC never once beat LTV — and everyone knew**
Pattern: unit-economics denial with a growth board. Sources: leaked board decks, founder confessional essays, benchmark comparisons.

**18. Support ticket necrology: reading a startup's death in its own status page**
Pattern: incident cadence as a leading indicator. Sources: archived status pages, uptime-tracker history, community forum panic threads.

**19. The acquisition integration that killed both companies**
Pattern: merger execution risk. Sources: press releases vs. two years of product changelogs, employee attrition data.

**20. The founder who wouldn't sell: the $200M offer, declined, and the $0 outcome**
Pattern: valuation anchoring. Sources: reported term sheets, funding history, eventual wind-down filings.

**21. Security debt: the breach that a seed-stage startup could not afford to survive**
Pattern: trust products die of one incident. Sources: breach disclosures, customer exodus threads, state AG filings.

## Cluster 4: The Endgame (how software actually dies)

**22. The 90-day sunset notice: what a well-run shutdown looks like (a rare success autopsy)**
Pattern: graceful wind-down as reputation preservation. Sources: shutdown blog posts, data-export docs, founders' next-company outcomes.

**23. Zombie SaaS: the products that still charge cards but shipped nothing in 3 years**
Pattern: the undead middle between alive and shut down. Sources: changelog archives, App Store update history, subscription-refund threads.

**24. The fire sale: what a startup's assets fetch at auction, line by line**
Pattern: liquidation value vs. last valuation. Sources: assignment-for-benefit-of-creditors notices, domain/patent auction records.

**25. Your data after the funeral: tracing where a dead app's user data actually went**
Pattern: data as a distressed asset. Sources: bankruptcy sale orders, privacy-policy diffs, FTC interventions (RadioShack-style precedents applied to SaaS).

**26. The open-source afterlife: the dead company whose code outlived it and won**
Pattern: code escrow and community forks as resurrection. Sources: GitHub fork history, foundation announcements, maintainer interviews in public talks.

**27. Acqui-hired into oblivion: the "we're thrilled to announce" posts, revisited 5 years later**
Pattern: what talent acquisitions really pay and really kill. Sources: announcement archives, LinkedIn trajectories, product shutdown dates.

## Cluster 5: Systemic & Meta (patterns across the graveyard)

**28. One vertical, five funerals: why every startup in [one niche] died within 3 years**
Pattern: category-level failure (pick a vertical like social audio, rapid grocery delivery for SMB SaaS parallels, or NFT tooling). Sources: comparative funding/shutdown timelines.

**29. The cohort study: tracking every startup from one accelerator demo day, 10 years on**
Pattern: base rates made visceral (distinct from Batch 1's YC-batch audit — this one follows the *survivors' pivots* as much as the deaths, with a survivorship-bias framing). Sources: demo-day archives, current domain status of all ~120 companies.

**30. The pre-mortem that came true: the internal memo that predicted the company's death 2 years early**
Pattern: ignored internal warnings. Sources: leaked memos entered into litigation records, wrongful-termination suits, founder retrospectives.

## Production notes for this batch

- **Series architecture:** Clusters 1 and 4 are natural playlists ("How Startups Die: The Money" / "The Endgame") — playlists drive the session watch time this niche needs, since individual company names have limited search volume.
- **Search strategy:** Batch 2 leans harder on *pattern* keywords ("liquidation preference explained," "venture debt risks," "what happens to my data when an app shuts down") than company names — these have durable search volume where dead-company names decay.
- **Recurring formats hiding in here:** #23 (Zombie SaaS) and #24 (fire sales) can become quarterly recurring episodes; #29 can become an annual update — recurring formats are subscription-newsletter feeders.
- **Sourcing caution:** Ideas #17 and #30 depend on leaked/litigation documents — only use material already in public court records or published reporting, and attribute it. That discipline is also the channel's credibility moat.
