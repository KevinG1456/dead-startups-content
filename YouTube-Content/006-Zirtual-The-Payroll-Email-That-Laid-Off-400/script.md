## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "How do 400 people lose their jobs from a single email, with zero warning?"
- Visual: "400" numeral counts up, envelope icon appears with a notification badge, hard cut with a single sharp notification SFX (used once, deliberately)
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This actually happened. In one morning."

### Hook (0:15–0:45)
- VO: "This is Zirtual — a virtual assistant marketplace that, in August 2015, laid off roughly 400 workers via a single mass email. The reported cause wasn't bad growth. It was the cost of trying to do the compliant thing."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand exactly how a decision meant to protect the company from legal risk became the reason it nearly collapsed."

### Introduction (0:45–1:30)
- Method statement: reconstructed from press coverage and founder public statements — sourced on screen
- One-sentence framing: this is the companion story to the Homejoy autopsy — same underlying dilemma, opposite choice

### Story — Rise (1:30–4:00)
- Founding story, the virtual-assistant marketplace model, rapid growth built on a contractor workforce
- Visual: press clippings, growth-chart animation, product screenshots (recreated/generic)
- Curiosity gap: tease the reclassification decision without fully explaining its cost yet

### Main Content — The Autopsy (4:00–9:30)
- Segment 1: why the reclassification decision was made — the misclassification legal risk it was meant to avoid (direct callback to Homejoy)
- Segment 2: the actual cost math — contractor cost vs. W-2 employee cost per worker, scaled to the full workforce, shown as a concrete visual comparison
- Segment 3: how the resulting cash crunch led directly to the sudden, all-at-once layoff decision
- Mid-video CTA (~6:30): "If you're thinking about converting contractors to employees, the math in this video is exactly what you need to run first. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- Graphics: cost-breakdown comparison, timeline from reclassification decision to layoff email

### Story — The Fall (9:30–11:30)
- The layoff email itself, the immediate aftermath for the 400 affected workers, the company's path to acquisition/survival afterward
- Human element: what it was like for workers to receive that specific email `[VERIFY: sourced accounts only]`
- Slow the pacing deliberately here — this is the most human-impact-heavy segment in the batch

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — compliance decisions have real cash-flow consequences that must be modeled in advance, not discovered under pressure
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Have you seen the Homejoy video yet? This is the other half of that story — comment which choice you think was worse."
- End screen: Homejoy video + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the cost-breakdown graphic (contractor vs. employee cost, scaled to 400 workers), then rewinds to explain the decision that led to it. Measured, graphics-forward.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the human moment — a worker's account of receiving the email — before any numbers appear. Slower pacing, cinematic, heaviest emotional weighting in the batch.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "the 4 decisions that led to one email," counting down using the same facts. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Segment 2 (cost math) if it becomes a dry numbers lecture
- **Fix:** ground the cost comparison in a single concrete worker example before scaling up to the full 400
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the Rise section if press coverage of the early growth phase is thin
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** move the Homejoy-connection tease earlier if early-retention data shows drop-off before 2:00
- **Recommended visual change:** show the cost-breakdown graphic partially in the cold open, fully revealed only at the end
