# Blog Article: Zirtual — The Payroll Email That Laid Off 400 People Overnight

## Introduction
In August 2015, roughly 400 virtual assistants working with Zirtual, a startup connecting remote workers with clients needing administrative support, received a single email informing them their engagement with the company was ending, effective immediately. The reported cause wasn't a typical failure to grow — it was a cash crunch tied significantly to the cost of converting its workforce from independent contractors to W-2 employees, a move made partly to avoid the same kind of worker-misclassification legal risk that had recently ended Homejoy.

## The Rise
Zirtual, founded by Maren Kate Donovan, built a marketplace connecting clients with virtual assistants for administrative and personal-support tasks. The company grew significantly on the strength of its contractor-based workforce model, which kept costs flexible and allowed rapid scaling `[VERIFY specific growth figures before citing]`.

## The Reclassification Decision
As legal scrutiny of contractor classification in the gig economy intensified — highlighted by cases like Homejoy's — Zirtual reportedly moved to reclassify its virtual assistants from independent contractors to W-2 employees, a step intended to reduce legal exposure. This decision came with a substantial increase in costs: employer-side payroll taxes, benefits obligations, and compliance overhead that a pure contractor model does not carry `[VERIFY specific cost figures and percentage increase before citing]`.

## The Cash Crunch
According to reporting at the time, the increased cost of the reclassified workforce grew faster than the company's revenue and available cash could sustain, creating an acute cash crisis within a relatively short window after the change `[VERIFY specific timeline before citing]`.

## The Layoff
Facing this crunch, Zirtual's leadership made the decision to lay off roughly 400 of its virtual assistants — reportedly nearly its entire workforce of that type — via a single email, effective immediately, with limited severance `[VERIFY specific severance terms before citing]`. The abruptness and impersonal delivery method of the layoff drew significant public criticism and made the story widely cited in discussions of layoff communication practices.

## The Aftermath
Following the layoffs, Zirtual was reportedly acquired to continue operating in some form `[VERIFY: acquirer, terms, and outcome before citing]`.

## The Lesson
Zirtual's story is a useful, if uncomfortable, companion to Homejoy's: two companies facing the same underlying gig-economy tension — the cost and legal risk tradeoff between contractor and employee classification — and arriving at opposite decisions, both of which led to serious crises. For founders operating contractor-based marketplace models, the practical lesson is to model the full cost of potential reclassification well before it becomes urgent, rather than discovering the true cost under financial pressure.
