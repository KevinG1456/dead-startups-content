**Framing correction, made during research — read before touching any file in this package:** the
batch title's literal numbers ("$200M offer," "$0 outcome") do not match the real, sourced facts. The
real offer was approximately $30 million in pre-IPO Google stock (2003). The real eventual outcome was
NOT literally $0 — Friendster sold for $39 million in 2009, and its patents separately sold to Facebook
for $40 million in 2010. This package uses the real, corrected, and more dramatic figure: that declined
Google stock would have been worth approximately $30 billion by March 2021.

**The real case:** Jonathan Abrams founded Friendster in 2002, one of the first mainstream social
networks. In 2003, Google offered to acquire it for roughly $30 million in pre-IPO stock. Abrams and
Friendster's board declined, reportedly on the advice of investors who believed they could build the
company into a multi-billion-dollar business independently. Instead, Friendster raised a $13 million
Series A from Kleiner Perkins and Benchmark Capital at a $53 million valuation. In 2004, Friendster's
board removed Abrams as CEO, and MySpace overtook Friendster in users that same year, beginning its
Western-market decline (though it remained huge in parts of Southeast Asia for years). In December
2009, Malaysian payments company MOL Global bought 100% of Friendster for $39 million; MOL sold the
company's patents to Facebook for $40 million in 2010 and pivoted the brand to social gaming. Friendster
fully shut down in 2018. The Google stock Abrams turned down would have been worth roughly $30 billion
by 2021.

**Framing requirement:** Never state the offer was $200M or the outcome was literally $0 — use the
real, sourced $30M offer and the ~$30B counterfactual figure instead. Present the "investors advised
independence" detail as the best-sourced explanation, not a direct Abrams quote — none was found.
Present Abrams' 2004 board-ordered removal as CEO neutrally, not as scandal.
