## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week:
Friendster — one of Silicon Valley's most famous real examples of a founder declining a real offer,
and exactly what happened after.

Jonathan Abrams founded Friendster in 2002, one of the first mainstream social networks, letting users
connect with friends and friends-of-friends online. In 2003, after about a year of operation, Google
offered to acquire Friendster for approximately $30 million in pre-IPO stock — roughly 200 million
shares, more than a year before Google's own 2004 IPO. Abrams and Friendster's board declined.
Reportedly, several high-powered early investors advised Abrams that they could help build Friendster
into a multi-billion-dollar company independently. Instead, in October 2003, Friendster raised a $13
million Series A from Kleiner Perkins Caufield & Byers and Benchmark Capital, at a $53 million
valuation.

Here's the real, calculable cost of that decision: Google went public in August 2004. The declined
stock, held through Google's subsequent appreciation, would have been worth approximately $30 billion
by March 2021.

In 2004 — about a year after the Google decision — Friendster's board removed Jonathan Abrams as CEO,
installing former Yahoo CEO Tim Koogle as interim CEO. That same year, MySpace overtook Friendster in
total users, beginning its decline in Western markets, compounded by persistent technical scaling
problems. Friendster remained dominant in parts of Southeast Asia for years even as it faded elsewhere,
peaking at more than 111 million registered users worldwide.

In December 2009, Malaysian payments company MOL Global acquired 100% of Friendster for approximately
$39 million. In June 2010, MOL separately sold Friendster's patent portfolio to Facebook for
approximately $40 million — nearly recouping MOL's entire purchase price from the patents alone. MOL
pivoted Friendster to social gaming; the pivot didn't work. In 2015, Friendster "took a break." In
2018, it officially and fully shut down.
