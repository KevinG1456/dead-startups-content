## LinkedIn
"A real, precisely sourced case study in valuation anchoring — and just how large the gap between a
declined offer and its real counterfactual value can get:

Jonathan Abrams founded Friendster in 2002, one of the first mainstream social networks. In 2003,
Google offered to acquire it for approximately $30 million in pre-IPO stock. Abrams and Friendster's
board declined, reportedly on investor advice that they could build something bigger independently.
Instead, Friendster raised a $13 million Series A from Kleiner Perkins and Benchmark Capital.

Google went public in 2004. That declined stock would have been worth approximately $30 billion by
2021. In 2004, Friendster's board removed Abrams as CEO. MySpace overtook Friendster in users the same
year. In December 2009, Friendster sold to MOL Global for $39 million; MOL sold the company's patents
to Facebook separately for $40 million in 2010. Friendster fully shut down in 2018.
