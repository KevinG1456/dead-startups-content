# 010 — The Startup Acquired for $1 (Asset Sales, Acqui-Hires, and What "Acquired" Hides)

**Package status:** Structural framework ready. This is an explainer/mechanism video, not necessarily a single-company autopsy — its job is to demystify what "acquired" actually means across a spectrum of outcomes, anchored by real, well-documented examples. A literal, precisely-sourced "$1" acquisition is hard to document with certainty; the strongest approach is to anchor on real deals with confirmed, dramatic value collapses and use "acquired for $1" as the video's illustrative framing device, made explicit on screen rather than presented as one single literal transaction.

**Strongest documented anchor examples:**
1. **Bebo** — AOL acquired Bebo for a reported ~$850 million in 2008. AOL then sold it on to Criterion Capital Partners for a reported ~$10 million in 2010, and Criterion in turn sold it to founder Michael Birch for a reported ~$1 million in 2013 `[VERIFY exact figures]`. The full three-step chain (AOL → Criterion → Birch), not just "AOL to Birch," is what makes this such a precise fit for the "what acquired hides" theme — the same company "acquired" three times, collapsing in value at each step.
2. **Digg** — once valued at a reported ~$160 million, Digg was broken up into three separate deals in 2012 rather than one sale: the brand, site, and technology went to Betaworks for a reported ~$500,000; the patents were sold separately to LinkedIn for a reported ~$4 million; and roughly 15 staff were transferred to The Washington Post for undisclosed terms `[VERIFY exact figures and buyers]`. Even the "small" number here hides something — the IP alone reportedly went for roughly 8x what the brand and product sold for, an instructive example of an asset-sale-style "acquisition" that was functionally a shutdown.
3. **General acqui-hire pattern** — many "acquisitions" of failing startups are structured primarily to bring the founding/engineering team onto the acquirer's payroll, with the acquired company's product discontinued shortly after and minimal or no payout to investors; use anonymized/generic composite examples if no single well-sourced case is confirmed, and label them clearly as illustrative rather than naming unconfirmed specific deals.

**Framing requirement:** never assert a precise "$1" sale price for a specific real company unless it is directly sourced to a primary document or a direct, on-record founder/executive statement — use "for essentially nothing," "for a fraction of prior value," or the confirmed real figure instead where the exact "$1" number isn't verifiable.
