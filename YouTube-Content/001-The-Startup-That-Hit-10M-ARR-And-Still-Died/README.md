# 001 — Sprig Hit a $22M Run Rate. It Still Lost $850K a Month. Here's the Math.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Sprig, a real, on-demand meal-delivery startup with an unusually candid, on-record founder retrospective (Mixergy, The Full Ratchet — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). The package's own two original candidates were checked and one is disqualified: Fast's real revenue (~$600K) never came close to $10M, contradicting this title's entire premise. Stay Alfred tells a real, teachable unit-economics story but at roughly $100M in revenue — 10x this title's framing — so it's reserved for a separate, correctly-scaled future package rather than forced in here.

**The real case:** Sprig — founded 2013, co-founded and led by CEO Gagan Biyani, raised roughly $56-58M across a $10M Series A (Greylock, March 2014) and a $45M round (Social+Capital, Greylock, Battery Ventures, Accel, April 2015) — hit a run rate Biyani himself described as "$22 million, so or 21, or something like that" around February 2016. It still shut down May 26, 2017. The mechanism was genuinely broken unit economics, not just a cash crunch: Sprig owned and operated its own kitchens rather than partnering with restaurants, meaning it bore the full cost of food production and delivery directly. Per Bloomberg, it was losing roughly $850,000 a month. Uber Eats' relaunch around February 2016 was the precisely-dated trigger — Biyani: "instead of going up by 2%, we were going down by 2%." Roughly $8M was returned to investors out of the ~$56-58M raised.

**Important honesty note:** Sprig's real run rate is "roughly $21-22M," not exactly $10M — the original title framing used $10M as a placeholder round number, and this package updates it to the real, founder-confirmed figure rather than force the placeholder to match. Wikipedia's "peak headcount over 1,300" and Fortune's "~200 staff affected by the shutdown" are two different measures (peak operational scale across kitchens/delivery vs. final shutdown headcount) — present them as such, not as a contradiction. Biyani's own hindsight quote — "I would have shut it down within three months of launching" — refers to the period once the negative unit-economics trend was clear, not literally three months after Sprig's 2013 launch; keep that context intact on screen.

**Framing requirement:** lead with Biyani's own numbers and his own retrospective candor — this is a rare case where the founder gave two separate, detailed, on-record interviews explaining exactly what went wrong, which gives the "unit economics autopsy" format real primary-source teeth instead of requiring reconstruction from secondhand reporting alone.
