Sources Log — Package 001: Sprig
Filled in: 2026-08-13

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
This title was a structural framework with two unverified candidates flagged in the
package's own README: Fast and Stay Alfred. Research checked both against the actual
premise of the title (a company with genuinely meaningful revenue, roughly $10M+ ARR,
that still died from a real unit-economics failure, not just "ran out of money").
Fast is disqualified outright — its real revenue never came close to $10M. Stay Alfred
tells a real, teachable story but at 10x the revenue scale the title implies. Sprig is
the anchor: a founder-confirmed ~$21-22M run rate, genuinely broken unit economics
(owned kitchens, CAC > LTV), and an unusually candid, on-record founder retrospective
across two separate interviews.

FAST — REJECTED, does not meet the title's premise

- 2021 revenue: approximately $600,000, per The Information (syndicated via Morning
  Brew/NPR). TechCrunch independently describes it as "six-figure revenue" — two
  independent characterizations agree it was nowhere near $10M.
- Burn: up to $10M/month.
- Funding: $124.5M total, including a $102M Series B led by Stripe. PitchBook-reported
  valuation approximately $580M post-money.
- Shutdown: April 5, 2022, approximately 450 employees affected.
Source: TechCrunch (techcrunch.com/2022/04/05/fast-shuts-doors-after-slow-growth-high-
burn-precluded-fundraising-options/).
CONCLUSION: Fast's real revenue directly contradicts this title's "$10M ARR" premise.
It's a "$600K revenue at a $580M valuation" story, not a "$10M ARR, still died" story —
a different, still-tellable title, but not this one. Not used.

STAY ALFRED — real story, wrong scale for this title, reserved for a future package

- Revenue: $100M in FY2019, independently confirmed by CB Insights and Bisnow
  ("on track to generate $100 million in revenue this year," pre-COVID). Founder
  Jordan Allen, to the Spokane Journal: "amazing to think that a $100 million
  company... could be wiped out by a virus from Wuhan, China."
- Funding: reported as $47M (Series B, Oct 2018, Nine Four Ventures) to $62M total
  (CB Insights) — sources vary; treat as "over $47M."
- Model: master-lease arbitrage — Stay Alfred paid landlords fixed rent regardless of
  occupancy, then re-let units as short-term rentals. Allen himself called this
  structure a "foundational crack."
- Collapse: temporarily closed March 2020; permanently closed May 20, 2020, after a
  rescue financing round (~$30M) fell through.
Source: CB Insights (cbinsights.com/company/stay-alfred/financials), Bisnow
(bisnow.com/national/news/hotel/short-term-rentals-not-all-recovery-107308).
CONCLUSION: a real, well-documented, teachable unit-economics story (fixed-lease
obligations vs. variable revenue, COVID as the acute trigger revealing a structural
flaw) — but its $100M revenue is roughly 10x this title's "$10M ARR" framing. Using it
here would look like undershooting the real number to a fact-check-heavy audience.
Flagged as a strong candidate for a SEPARATE, differently-titled future package (e.g.
built around "$100M in Revenue, One Bad Lease Structure, Gone in 90 Days"). Not used
here.

SPRIG — the anchor for this package

COMPANY / FOUNDER
- Sprig — on-demand meal delivery startup, founded 2013, owned and operated its own
  kitchens (vertically integrated, not a marketplace of restaurants).
- Co-founder and CEO: Gagan Biyani.
Source: Mixergy interview, TechCrunch.

FUNDING
- $10M Series A, March 2014 (Greylock Partners).
- $45M round, April 2015, led by Social+Capital and Greylock, with Battery Ventures
  and Accel participating — cumulative funding to date at that point reported around
  $55M by TechCrunch.
- Total lifetime funding: reported as $56.7M (Crunchbase, via Failory, 4 rounds, 26
  investors) to $57M (Wikipedia) to "more than $56 million" (Fortune) — treat as
  "roughly $56-58M" rather than one single precise figure, since sources cluster
  tightly but don't agree to the dollar.
Source: TechCrunch (Series A and Series B coverage), Failory/Crunchbase, Wikipedia,
Fortune.

REVENUE — the title's core number, founder-confirmed
- Gagan Biyani, on the record, Mixergy interview (mixergy.com/interviews/sprig-with-
  gagan-biyani/), describing the company's status around February 2016: "we were on a
  run rate of $22 million, so or 21, or something like that."
  [Present as "roughly $21-22M run rate," per Biyani's own words — a primary-source,
  on-the-record figure, not a false-precision single number.]
- Secondary retrospectives (aggregators, not primary journalism) cite "$20M revenue,"
  consistent with Biyani's own figure.
Source: Mixergy (primary interview).

THE MECHANISM — genuinely broken unit economics, not just "ran out of money"
- Sprig built and owned its own kitchens rather than partnering with existing
  restaurants — meaning it bore the full cost of food production and delivery
  directly, with no restaurant partner absorbing any of that cost or risk.
- Biyani, describing the margin dynamics as growth reversed: "when your business is
  declining... your gross margin gets harder and harder... their margins are already
  so thin, that overhead is cutting a greater percentage."
- Bloomberg (cited via Fortune): Sprig was losing approximately $850,000 per month.
- Customer acquisition cost consistently outpaced customer lifetime value — the
  company could not produce and deliver a meal for less than it cost to make,
  especially once growth slowed and fixed kitchen/overhead costs had to be spread
  across fewer orders.
Source: Mixergy interview, Bloomberg (via Fortune).

THE TRIGGER — a precisely dated inflection point
- Uber Eats' third major product iteration launched around February 2016, directly
  competing for the same on-demand meal-delivery customers.
- Biyani: "instead of going up by 2%, we were going down by 2%" — describing the
  immediate shift in Sprig's own weekly growth rate around this launch.
Source: Mixergy interview.

SHUTDOWN
- May 26, 2017: Sprig shuts down.
- Approximately 200 staff affected at the time of shutdown, per Fortune.
- Peak headcount reported as "over 1,300" per Wikipedia — this appears to refer to
  Sprig's full historical operational headcount (including kitchen and delivery
  staff at its peak scale), not the final shutdown headcount. [Present these as two
  different measures — peak operational scale vs. final shutdown headcount — do not
  conflate them as contradictory figures.]
- Approximately $8M returned to investors out of roughly $56-58M raised; commonly
  summarized as "roughly $50M lost."
Source: Fortune, Wikipedia.

FOUNDER'S OWN HINDSIGHT — strong, quotable, on-record
- Biyani, reflecting afterward: "I would have shut it down within three months of
  launching" [referring to the period once the negative unit-economics trend was
  clear, not literally three months after Sprig's original 2013 launch — present this
  quote with that context, not as a literal timeline claim].
Source: Mixergy interview.

SECOND SOURCE — a distinct, compounding causal thread
- A separate interview (The Full Ratchet, fullratchet.net/what-killed-sprig/) covers
  product-quality decline as a compounding factor alongside the unit-economics
  problem: food arriving cold, declining presentation/taste, and resulting churn.
  This gives the video a second, distinct thread beyond pure unit economics if useful
  for pacing/depth.
Source: The Full Ratchet (fullratchet.net/what-killed-sprig/).

MUNCHERY — checked, not used as a second anchor
- Real financial distress (losing up to $5M/month per Bloomberg, valuation cut from
  $300M to an $80M-capped convertible note by 2017, 16% food waste reported) but no
  clean, multiply-sourced ARR figure comparable to Sprig's founder-confirmed run rate.
  Usable only as a one-line "Sprig wasn't alone" comparison, not as a second full
  segment or anchor.
