# Master Research — Sprig

Single source of truth for facts, figures, and quotes. Every localized script (EN/DE/FR/NL/NO/SV)
must preserve every number, date, name, and quote meaning below exactly — localize the language
around these facts, never the facts themselves. Full citation map: `../sources-log.txt`.

## Core Facts (do not alter across languages)

- **Company:** Sprig, on-demand meal delivery, founded 2013, San Francisco.
- **Founder/CEO:** Gagan Biyani.
- **Business model:** vertically integrated — Sprig owned and operated its own kitchens rather
  than partnering with existing restaurants (unlike a marketplace model).
- **Funding:** $10M Series A (Greylock Partners, March 2014); $45M round (Social+Capital and
  Greylock, with Battery Ventures and Accel participating, April 2015). Total raised: roughly
  $56-58M — sources cluster tightly but don't agree to the exact dollar; never state one false-
  precision figure.
- **Revenue:** run rate described by Biyani himself (Mixergy interview, ~February 2016) as
  "$22 million, so or 21, or something like that." Always present as "roughly $21-22M," never as
  a single exact number — this is a direct quote's own hedge, not a sourcing gap to smooth over.
- **Burn:** losing roughly $850,000/month, per Bloomberg.
- **The trigger:** Uber Eats' third major product iteration launched around February 2016. Biyani:
  "instead of going up by 2%, we were going down by 2%" — describing Sprig's own weekly growth
  rate flipping from positive to negative.
- **Shutdown date:** May 26, 2017.
- **Headcount:** two DIFFERENT measures, never conflate — peak operational headcount reported at
  over 1,300 (across kitchens/delivery at largest scale) vs. roughly 200 staff affected at the
  time of the final 2017 shutdown.
- **Investor return:** roughly $8M returned to investors out of the ~$56-58M raised.

## Key Quotes (translate meaning + register, not word-for-word; preserve attribution)

1. Gagan Biyani, on Sprig's run rate (Mixergy interview, ~Feb 2016):
   *"we were on a run rate of $22 million, so or 21, or something like that."*
   — Localize as a natural-sounding hedge/approximation in the target language. Do not resolve it
   into a single confident number; the hedging IS the point (a founder speaking candidly, not a
   press release).

2. Gagan Biyani, on margin dynamics as growth reversed:
   *"when your business is declining... your gross margin gets harder and harder... their margins
   are already so thin, that overhead is cutting a greater percentage."*
   — Technical/explanatory register. Localize for clarity over literalness; the underlying
   mechanism (fixed costs against shrinking revenue) must remain intact.

3. Gagan Biyani, on the Uber Eats trigger:
   *"instead of going up by 2%, we were going down by 2%."*
   — Keep the parallel-structure rhetorical device (up 2% / down 2%) in localization; it's
   effective in any language as a stated contrast, not idiom-dependent.

4. Gagan Biyani, retrospective hindsight (most quotable line):
   *"I would have shut it down within three months."*
   — CONTEXT REQUIRED EVERY TIME THIS APPEARS: this refers to three months after the negative
   unit-economics trend became clear, NOT three months after Sprig's 2013 launch. Every localized
   script/description that uses this line must carry that clarification, not just the quote.

## The One-Sentence Lesson (must survive localization intact in meaning)
Real revenue at meaningful scale does not automatically mean a viable business if the cost of
producing what you sell is structurally too high — especially once growth slows and fixed costs
have fewer orders to spread across.
