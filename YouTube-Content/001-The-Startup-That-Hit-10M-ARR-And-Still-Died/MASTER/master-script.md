# Master Script — Sprig

This is the narrative logic and beat structure, upstream of any language. Each localized `EN/DE/
FR/NL/NO/SV/script.md` writes its own native dialogue from these beats — do not translate this
document's English phrasing sentence-by-sentence into other languages; translate the IDEA of each
beat, then write it as a native speaker would say it. Every fact/quote referenced here comes from
`master-research.md` — do not introduce new facts at the localization stage.

**Target length:** 12–15 minutes across all languages (natural pacing may vary a beat or two
minutes either way per language — do not force identical timestamps, see `master-editing-
timeline.md`).

---

## Story Logic (the throughline every language must preserve)

A company reaches real, meaningful revenue (~$21-22M/year) and still dies — not from running out
of money in the generic sense, but from a specific, teachable mechanism: owning production
(kitchens) instead of using a marketplace model meant fixed costs that didn't shrink when growth
did. A single competitive event (Uber Eats' relaunch) triggered the reversal, and the founder
later explained all of this himself, on the record, including a rare admission that he'd have
acted sooner. This is a "the founder did the forensic work himself" video — that's the distinct
angle vs. other startup-autopsy content.

## Beat-by-Beat Structure

### 1. Cold Open (~0:00–0:15)
Beat: state the core contradiction immediately — real, large revenue number, paired with a real,
large loss number, same company, same time period.
Facts to hit: ~$22M/year revenue, ~$850K/month loss.
Visual: revenue figure animates in, loss counter ticks below it.
Tone: blunt, declarative, no preamble.

### 2. Hook (~0:15–0:50)
Beat: name the company, establish the "founder did the forensic work himself" differentiator.
Facts to hit: Sprig, on-demand meal delivery, real revenue, still died, founder went on record
twice.
Open loop to plant: by the end, viewer will understand the cost structure, the competitive
trigger, and what the founder says he'd do differently.

### 3. Introduction (~0:50–1:40)
Beat: state the method (built from the founder's own interviews + press), establish credibility.
One-sentence framing: rare case where the founder did the forensic work himself, publicly, after
the fact.

### 4. Segment — The Raise (~1:40–3:00)
Facts to hit: 2013 founding, Gagan Biyani CEO, owned-kitchen model (not marketplace), $10M Series
A (Greylock, March 2014), $45M round (Social+Capital/Greylock/Battery/Accel, April 2015), ~$56-58M
total raised.
Visual: funding timeline climbing.

### 5. Segment — The Run Rate (~3:00–4:00)
Facts to hit: ~Feb 2016, Biyani's own "$22 million, so or 21, or something like that" quote.
Visual: run-rate graphic + quote card.
Tone note: this is a real, credible revenue number — the video should treat it with respect, not
mockery, before the contradiction lands.

### 6. Segment — The Cost Structure (~4:00–6:30)
Facts to hit: owned kitchens = full cost of production/delivery borne directly, no restaurant
partner absorbing risk. Biyani's margin-dynamics quote (see master-research.md quote #2). ~$850K/
month loss (Bloomberg).
Visual: "who bears the cost" diagram, Sprig's model vs. a typical marketplace model side by side.
Retention note: keep this segment visual and concrete — risk of becoming a dry margin lecture if
over-explained in any language.

### 7. Segment — The Trigger (~6:30–8:00)
Facts to hit: ~Feb 2016, Uber Eats' third major product iteration launches, directly competing.
Biyani's "up 2% / down 2%" quote (see master-research.md quote #3).
Visual: growth-rate graph flipping from positive to negative at the exact dated moment.

### 8. Segment — The Shutdown (~8:00–9:30)
Facts to hit: May 26, 2017 shutdown. ~200 staff affected at shutdown vs. 1,300+ peak operational
headcount (two distinct measures — see master-research.md). ~$8M returned to investors of ~$56-
58M raised.
Visual: shutdown headline, timeline closing.

### 9. Mid-Video CTA (~8:30, inside Segment 8)
Beat: direct address, brief tonal lift, subscribe prompt tied to relevance ("if you're building
anything with a physical cost structure").

### 10. Segment — The Founder's Own Hindsight (~9:30–11:30)
Facts to hit: food-quality decline as a compounding factor (per The Full Ratchet interview).
Biyani's "I would have shut it down within three months" quote — WITH its required context (see
master-research.md quote #4) — must appear in every language with that context intact, not as a
bare soundbite.
Tone: slow the pacing deliberately here in every language — this is the reflective beat, not a
data segment. Let it breathe.

### 11. Final CTA + Outro (~11:30–13:30)
Beat: recap the one-sentence lesson (see master-research.md), subscribe CTA, engagement question
("did you ever order from Sprig? what do you think of a founder being this candid?"), end screen
reference to related content.

## Alternative Script Concepts (available for any language's alt-cut, not required per language)
- **Data-First / Analyst Style:** open on the completed run-rate-to-shutdown timeline graphic,
  rewind to explain in order.
- **Narrative / Documentary Style:** open on the "I would have shut it down within three months"
  quote in full before any numbers appear.
- **Fast-Cut / Listicle Style:** "3 signs your unit economics are broken, even with real revenue,"
  each beat illustrated by a Sprig moment. Strong Shorts source material.

## Retention Notes (apply per language, pacing may vary naturally)
- Biggest drop-off risk: The Cost Structure segment becoming a technical lecture — keep it visual.
- Tease the "shut it down sooner" quote in the Hook; pay it off fully in the Hindsight segment.
- Keep the growth-rate graph visible in a corner through Segments 7-8 so the trigger's consequence
  stays concrete.
