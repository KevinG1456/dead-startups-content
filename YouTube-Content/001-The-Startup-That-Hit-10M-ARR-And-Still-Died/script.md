## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 12–15 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "Sprig was doing $22 million a year. It still lost $850,000 a month."
- Visual: "$22M/YEAR" graphic animates in, a red "-$850K/MONTH" counter ticks below it, hard SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This isn't a story about running out of money. It's a story about math that never worked."

### Hook (0:15–0:50)
- VO: "This is Sprig — a real, funded, on-demand meal delivery startup that hit real revenue and still died. And unlike most of these stories, the founder himself went on the record, twice, to explain exactly what went wrong."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand the specific cost structure that made Sprig's growth work against it, the exact competitive moment that triggered the collapse, and what the founder himself says he'd do differently."

### Introduction (0:50–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed primarily from Gagan Biyani's own on-record interviews (Mixergy, The Full Ratchet) plus contemporaneous press (TechCrunch, Fortune, Bloomberg) — sourced on screen throughout
- One-sentence framing: this is a rare case where the founder did much of the forensic work himself, in public, after the fact

### Main Content — The Sprig Case (1:40–9:30)
- Segment 1 — The Raise (1:40–3:00): 2013, Sprig founded by CEO Gagan Biyani, building on-demand meal delivery — but unlike marketplace competitors, Sprig built and operated its own kitchens. $10M Series A from Greylock in March 2014, then $45M in April 2015 led by Social+Capital and Greylock with Battery Ventures and Accel participating — roughly $56-58M raised total. Visual: funding-round timeline graphic climbing.
- Segment 2 — The Run Rate (3:00–4:00): by around February 2016, Biyani himself put Sprig's run rate at "$22 million, so or 21, or something like that" — a real, meaningful revenue number for a startup at this stage. Visual: "$22M RUN RATE" graphic, Biyani quote card.
- Segment 3 — The Cost Structure (4:00–6:30): explain the mechanism plainly — because Sprig owned its own kitchens rather than partnering with restaurants, it bore the full cost of food production and delivery directly, with no partner absorbing any of that risk. Biyani's own words: "when your business is declining... your gross margin gets harder and harder... their margins are already so thin, that overhead is cutting a greater percentage." Per Bloomberg, Sprig was losing roughly $850,000 a month. Visual: the "who bears the cost" diagram — Sprig's owned-kitchen model vs. a typical restaurant-marketplace model side by side.
- Segment 4 — The Trigger (6:30–8:00): around February 2016, Uber Eats launched its third major product iteration, competing directly for the same customers. Biyani: "instead of going up by 2%, we were going down by 2%" — describing the immediate flip in Sprig's own weekly growth rate. Visual: growth-rate graph flipping from positive to negative at the exact dated moment.
- Segment 5 — The Shutdown (8:00–9:30): May 26, 2017, Sprig shuts down. Roughly 200 staff affected at the time, against a peak operational headcount reported over 1,300 across its kitchens and delivery operations at its largest scale — two different measures, not a contradiction. Roughly $8M returned to investors out of the ~$56-58M raised. Visual: shutdown headline, timeline closing.
- Mid-video CTA (~8:30): "If you're building anything with a physical cost structure — kitchens, warehouses, fleets — the next part is exactly what you need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Founder's Own Hindsight (9:30–11:30)
- Biyani's own retrospective, presented as direct on-screen quotes, not paraphrased: the food-quality decline covered separately in his Full Ratchet interview (cold food, declining presentation, resulting churn) as a compounding factor alongside the pure cost-structure problem
- His most quotable line: "I would have shut it down within three months" — of the point where the negative trend became clear, not literally three months after the 2013 launch. On-screen lower third clarifies this context.
- Slow the pacing deliberately here — this is the reflective beat, not another data segment

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — real revenue at meaningful scale doesn't automatically mean a viable business if the cost of producing what you sell is structurally too high, especially once growth slows and fixed costs have fewer orders to spread across
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you ever order from Sprig? What do you think of a founder being this candid about his own company's failure? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: two related autopsies (any other food-delivery/unit-economics package once produced) + subscribe

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the cost-structure explainer (Segment 3) if it becomes an overly technical margin lecture before the Uber Eats trigger arrives
- **Fix:** keep the "who bears the cost" diagram simple and visual, get to the dated Uber Eats trigger as quickly as possible
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general on-demand-delivery-industry background if audience-retention data shows strong baseline familiarity
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease Biyani's "I would have shut it down sooner" quote in the cold open or hook (recommended addition) and pay it off fully in the Founder's Own Hindsight section
- **Recommended visual change:** keep the growth-rate graph (flipping from +2% to -2%) visible in a corner throughout Segment 4-5, so the trigger's consequence stays concrete
