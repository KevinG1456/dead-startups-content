## Introduction (0:50–1:40)
Method statement: reconstructed primarily from Gagan Biyani's own on-record interviews (Mixergy,
The Full Ratchet) plus contemporaneous press (TechCrunch, Fortune, Bloomberg) — sourced on screen
throughout.
One-sentence framing: this is a rare case where the founder did much of the forensic work
himself, in public, after the fact.

## The Raise (1:40–3:00)
2013, Sprig founded by CEO Gagan Biyani, building on-demand meal delivery — but unlike
marketplace competitors, Sprig built and operated its own kitchens. $10M Series A from Greylock
in March 2014, then $45M in April 2015 led by Social+Capital and Greylock with Battery Ventures
and Accel participating — roughly $56-58M raised total.
Visual: funding-round timeline graphic climbing.

## The Run Rate (3:00–4:00)
By around February 2016, Biyani himself put Sprig's run rate at "$22 million, so or 21, or
something like that" — a real, meaningful revenue number for a startup at this stage.
Visual: "$22M RUN RATE" graphic, Biyani quote card.

## The Cost Structure (4:00–6:30)
Because Sprig owned its own kitchens rather than partnering with restaurants, it bore the full
cost of food production and delivery directly, with no partner absorbing any of that risk.
Biyani's own words: "when your business is declining... your gross margin gets harder and
harder... their margins are already so thin, that overhead is cutting a greater percentage." Per
Bloomberg, Sprig was losing roughly $850,000 a month.
Visual: the "who bears the cost" diagram — Sprig's owned-kitchen model vs. a typical restaurant-
marketplace model side by side.

## The Trigger (6:30–8:00)
Around February 2016, Uber Eats launched its third major product iteration, competing directly
for the same customers. Biyani: "instead of going up by 2%, we were going down by 2%" —
describing the immediate flip in Sprig's own weekly growth rate.
Visual: growth-rate graph flipping from positive to negative at the exact dated moment.

## The Shutdown (8:00–9:30)
May 26, 2017, Sprig shuts down. Roughly 200 staff affected at the time, against a peak
operational headcount reported over 1,300 across its kitchens and delivery operations at its
largest scale — two different measures, not a contradiction. Roughly $8M returned to investors
out of the ~$56-58M raised.
Visual: shutdown headline, timeline closing.

## The Founder's Own Hindsight (9:30–11:30)
Biyani's own retrospective, presented as direct on-screen quotes, not paraphrased: the
food-quality decline covered separately in his Full Ratchet interview (cold food, declining
presentation, resulting churn) as a compounding factor alongside the pure cost-structure problem.
His most quotable line: "I would have shut it down within three months" — of the point where the
negative trend became clear, not literally three months after the 2013 launch. On-screen lower
third clarifies this context.
Pacing: slow down deliberately here — this is the reflective beat, not another data segment.
