## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Sprig, an on-demand meal-delivery startup founded in 2013 by CEO Gagan Biyani, which raised roughly $56-58 million across a $10 million Series A (Greylock, March 2014) and a $45 million round (Social+Capital, Greylock, Battery Ventures, Accel, April 2015).

By early 2016, Biyani himself described Sprig's run rate as "$22 million, so or 21, or something like that" — real, meaningful revenue. It still wasn't enough. Because Sprig owned and operated its own kitchens rather than partnering with restaurants, it bore the full cost of food production and delivery directly, and per Bloomberg it was losing roughly $850,000 a month. Around February 2016, Uber Eats' relaunch triggered a precise, dated inflection point — Biyani's own words: "instead of going up by 2%, we were going down by 2%." Sprig shut down May 26, 2017, returning roughly $8 million to investors out of the ~$56-58 million raised.

What makes this case unusually well-documented is that Biyani gave two separate, detailed, on-record interviews explaining exactly what went wrong — including the now-famous line, "I would have shut it down within three months," once the negative trend was clear.
