## LinkedIn
"One of the clearest unit-economics case studies I've come across: Sprig, an on-demand meal-delivery startup, hit a run rate its own founder described as '$22 million, so or 21, or something like that.'

It was still losing roughly $850,000 a month. Why? Sprig owned and operated its own kitchens instead of partnering with restaurants — meaning it bore the full cost of food production and delivery directly, with no partner absorbing any of that risk.

When Uber Eats relaunched its product around February 2016, Sprig's weekly growth rate flipped from +2% to -2% almost overnight. Sprig shut down in May 2017, returning roughly $8M of the ~$56-58M it had raised.

What makes this case unusually well-documented: founder Gagan Biyani gave two separate, detailed interviews afterward explaining exactly what went wrong — including saying, on the record, that he would have shut it down sooner.
