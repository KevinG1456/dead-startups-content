## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "What happens when you subsidize both sides of a marketplace to build trust?"
- Visual: car icon animates in, dollar signs leak outward on both sides, hard cut with a heavy SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This actually happened. On purpose. For years."

### Hook (0:15–0:45)
- VO: "This is Beepi — a used-car marketplace that raised a reported $150 million. It subsidized both buyers and sellers on nearly every transaction to solve the trust problem in used cars. That subsidy is also what ended it."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll see the exact per-transaction math that made this model unsustainable — and what it means if you're building a marketplace of your own."

### Introduction (0:45–1:30)
- Method statement: reconstructed from funding records, press coverage, and shutdown reporting — sourced on screen
- One-sentence framing: this is a marketplace economics lesson, not just a company failure story

### Story — Rise (1:30–4:00)
- Founding story, the trust problem in used cars, the white-glove model (inspections, delivery, money-back guarantees), early growth and funding
- Visual: press clippings, generic marketplace-interface mockups, growth-chart animation
- Curiosity gap: tease the specific per-transaction cost breakdown without fully revealing it yet

### Main Content — The Autopsy (4:00–9:30)
- Segment 1: what the white-glove model actually cost — inspection, delivery, guarantee costs per car (reveal the teased breakdown)
- Segment 2: why this subsidy was necessary for trust but structurally incompatible with profitability at the chosen price point
- Segment 3: the layoffs and pivots as the company tried to find a sustainable version of the model, and why it couldn't in time
- Mid-video CTA (~6:30): "If you're building a two-sided marketplace right now, the next part is exactly what you need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- Graphics: per-transaction subsidy ledger, timeline of pivots/layoffs

### Story — The Fall (9:30–11:30)
- The shutdown, the fallen-through Fair.com merger and the creditor-liquidation sale that followed, what happened to employees
- Slow the pacing deliberately here

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — solving a real customer trust problem is necessary but not sufficient; the subsidy required to solve it has to have a credible path to shrinking as the marketplace scales
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "If you're building a marketplace, how are you thinking about subsidy economics? Comment below."
- End screen: two related autopsies + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the completed per-transaction subsidy ledger, then rewinds to explain the trust problem and the model built to solve it. Measured, graphics-forward.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the customer experience — what it actually felt like to buy or sell a car through Beepi — before any numbers appear. Slower pacing, cinematic.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "3 marketplace subsidy mistakes this case reveals," counting down using the same facts. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

---

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Main Content section (4:00–9:30) if the per-transaction cost breakdown becomes overly technical
- **Fix:** ground the cost breakdown in a single concrete example car sale before generalizing; cut every 20–30 seconds between talking head, B-roll, and animated graphic
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the Rise section if press coverage of the early growth phase is thin
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** move the per-transaction-cost tease earlier if early-retention data shows drop-off before 2:00
- **Recommended visual change:** show the subsidy ledger partially in the cold open, fully revealed only at the end
