## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct a funded startup's failure using primary sources — no speculation, just documents and decisions.

This week: Beepi, a used-car marketplace founded by Alejandro Resnik and Owen Savir, which raised a reported $150 million to build a high-touch, white-glove buying and selling experience — in-person inspections, delivery, money-back guarantees — designed to solve the trust problem that makes used cars a uniquely difficult category to sell online.

The catch: subsidizing both the buy side and the sell side of every transaction to build that trust meant the company was losing money on a huge share of its sales, with no clear path to positive unit economics at scale. We break down exactly what that subsidy math looked like, and what it teaches anyone building a two-sided marketplace today.
