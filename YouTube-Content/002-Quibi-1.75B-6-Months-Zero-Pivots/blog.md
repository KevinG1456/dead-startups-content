# Blog Article: Quibi — $1.75B, 6 Months, Zero Pivots

## Introduction
Quibi raised roughly $1.75 billion before it ever launched — one of the largest pre-launch war chests in media startup history. Backed by Jeffrey Katzenberg and led by CEO Meg Whitman, it promised premium, short-form ("Quick Bites") video built specifically for mobile, on-the-go viewing. It launched April 6, 2020. By October 21, 2020, the company announced it was shutting down. This is a breakdown of what happened in between — and specifically, what didn't happen: a single meaningful strategic pivot.

## The Rise
Quibi's pitch was distinctive: Hollywood-grade production values delivered in short, mobile-first episodes, positioned as premium content for commutes and in-between moments. The fundraise reflected serious confidence in that thesis, pulling in investment from major studios and media companies alongside financial investors `[VERIFY: specific investor list before citing by name]`.

## What the Launch Didn't Account For
Quibi launched into COVID-19 lockdowns — a moment when the "on-the-go" viewing occasion the entire product was built around had largely disappeared, since most of its target audience was suddenly home. This alone wasn't necessarily fatal; what compounded it was the app's initial lack of basic features competitors and early users immediately flagged, most notably no support for casting content to a television — a significant gap for a video-subscription product competing against services with mature TV experiences.

## The Pivot Timeline
`[VERIFY exact dates before publishing]` — TV casting support and other requested features were reportedly added only in the months following launch, after early reviews and usage data had already signaled the mismatch between the product and how people actually wanted to watch. A free, ad-supported tier — a common lever for struggling subscription products — was also not part of the original launch strategy.

## The Fall
By October 2020, roughly six months after launch, Quibi announced it would shut down, citing that the standalone business model was not going to succeed `[VERIFY: exact language from the official shutdown statement]`. The company's content library (~75 shows) was later acquired by Roku for a reported price under $100 million, announced January 2021.

## The Lesson
Capital can buy time, but it cannot substitute for a willingness to change course. Quibi had the resources to test alternative approaches — pricing, features, distribution — far earlier than it did. The company's failure is less a story about insufficient funding and more a story about how difficult it can be, even with extraordinary resources, to admit a launch strategy isn't working and act on that early enough to matter.
