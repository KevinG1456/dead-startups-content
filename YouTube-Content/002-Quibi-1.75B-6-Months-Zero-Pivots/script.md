## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "How do you burn $1.75 billion in six months?"
- Visual: full-zero dollar figure counts up rapidly on black, hard cut to red "SHUT DOWN" stamp with heavy SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This actually happened. In six months."

### Hook (0:15–0:45)
- VO: "This is Quibi. Backed by Jeffrey Katzenberg, led by Meg Whitman, launched with roughly $1.75 billion in funding — more than almost any media startup in history. Six months after launch, it was gone."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll know exactly which decision — made and then never revisited — sealed it."

### Introduction (0:45–1:30)
- Method statement: reconstructed from press coverage, executive interviews, and public timeline records — sourced on screen
- One-sentence framing: this isn't a COVID story, it's a decision-making story

### Story — Rise (1:30–4:00)
- Founding story, the pitch (premium short-form mobile video, "Quick Bites"), the fundraise, pre-launch hype
- Visual: press clippings, launch trailer stills, funding announcement headlines
- Curiosity gap: tease the specific missing feature without naming it yet

### Main Content — The Autopsy (4:00–9:30)
- Segment 1: the launch — timing against COVID lockdowns, mismatch between "on-the-go" premise and stay-at-home reality
- Segment 2: the missing features — no TV casting at launch, no free tier, no easy social sharing — reveal the teased feature
- Segment 3: the decision timeline — show, month by month, when each fix was requested internally/externally vs. when (if ever) it shipped
- Mid-video CTA (~6:30): "If you're building something right now and nobody around you is willing to say 'this isn't working' — the next part is for you. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- SFX: ticking clock under the timeline segment

### Story — The Fall (9:30–11:30)
- The shutdown announcement, employee impact, asset sale aftermath
- Human element: what it was like inside the company in the final weeks `[VERIFY: sourced quotes only]`
- Slow the pacing deliberately here

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — money buys time, not correct strategy; the failure was refusing to use the time to change course
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Which pivot do you think would've saved it? Comment below."
- End screen: two related autopsies + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the decision-timeline graphic fully populated, then rewinds to explain each marker. Measured, graphics-forward, minimal music.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the human moment (employees learning of the shutdown) before any numbers appear. Slower pacing, cinematic, heavier ambient scoring.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "5 warning signs Quibi ignored," counting down using the same facts reorganized around a countdown hook. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

---

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Main Content section (4:00–9:30) — highest risk if the timeline segment becomes a flat list-read
- **Fix:** cut every 20–30s between talking head, archival B-roll, and the animated timeline graphic
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the Rise section if pre-launch hype footage is thin
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** move the "missing feature" tease earlier if early-retention data shows drop-off before 2:00
- **Recommended visual change:** show the decision-timeline graphic partially in the cold open, fully revealed only at the end
