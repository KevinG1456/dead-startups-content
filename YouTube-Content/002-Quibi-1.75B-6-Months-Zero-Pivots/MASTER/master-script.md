# Master Script — Quibi

Narrative logic and beat structure, upstream of any language. Each localized `EN/DE/FR/NL/NO/SV/
script.md` writes its own native dialogue from these beats — translate the IDEA of each beat, not
this document's English sentences. All facts referenced here come from `master-research.md`.

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes across all languages (natural pacing may vary per language).

---

## Story Logic (the throughline every language must preserve)
A media startup raised more pre-launch funding than almost any competitor in history, launched
into the worst possible timing (a mobile-first, on-the-go product launching during lockdowns),
and then had six full months of runway and repeated, specific opportunities to change course —
and took none of them. The video's differentiator vs. existing Quibi coverage: don't treat COVID
as the explanation, isolate and walk through the specific rejected pivots.

## Beat-by-Beat Structure

### 1. Cold Open (~0:00–0:15)
Beat: pose the contradiction as a direct question or statement — huge number, tiny timeframe.
Facts: ~$1.75B raised, 6 months to shutdown.
Visual: dollar figure counts up rapidly, hard cut to "SHUT DOWN" stamp.

### 2. Hook (~0:15–0:45)
Beat: name the company, the two headline executives, the funding scale, the timeframe.
Facts: Quibi, Jeffrey Katzenberg (Chairman), Meg Whitman (CEO), ~$1.75B raised, 6 months to
shutdown.
Open loop: by the end, viewer will know the specific decision that sealed it.

### 3. Introduction (~0:45–1:30)
Beat: method statement (press coverage, executive interviews, public timeline), framing: this is
a decision-making story, not a COVID story.

### 4. Segment — The Rise (~1:30–4:00)
Facts: founding story, the pitch (premium short-form mobile video, "Quick Bites"), the fundraise,
pre-launch hype.
Technique: tease the specific missing feature without naming it yet — a curiosity gap to pay off
in the next segment.

### 5. Segment — The Autopsy (~4:00–9:30), three sub-beats:
a. **The launch mismatch:** timing against COVID lockdowns — an "on-the-go" premise launching
   into a stay-at-home reality.
b. **The missing features:** no TV casting at launch, no free tier, no easy social sharing —
   this is where the teased feature from Segment 4 is revealed.
c. **The decision timeline:** month by month, show when each fix was requested (internally or
   externally) vs. when — if ever — it shipped. This is the video's structural spine and its
   actual differentiator vs. other Quibi coverage — do not compress or skip this in any language.

### 6. Mid-Video CTA (~6:30, inside Segment 5)
Beat: direct address — "if you're building something right now and nobody around you is willing
to say 'this isn't working'" — subscribe prompt.

### 7. Segment — The Fall (~9:30–11:30)
Facts: the shutdown announcement, employee impact, asset sale aftermath (Roku acquiring the
content library, ~75 shows, reported price under $100M, announced Jan 2021).
Tone: slow the pacing deliberately in every language — human element, not another data segment.
Caution: only use sourced quotes for "what it was like inside the company" — do not invent a
first-person account in localization.

### 8. Final CTA + Outro (~11:30–13:00)
Beat: recap the one-sentence lesson (money buys time, not strategy — the failure was refusing to
use the time), subscribe CTA, engagement question ("which pivot do you think would've saved it?"),
end screen.

## Alternative Script Concepts (available for any language's alt-cut)
- **Data-First / Analyst Style:** open directly on the fully-populated decision-timeline graphic,
  rewind to explain each marker.
- **Narrative / Documentary Style:** open on the human moment (employees learning of the
  shutdown) before any numbers appear.
- **Fast-Cut / Listicle Style:** "5 warning signs Quibi ignored," countdown format. Strong Shorts
  source material.

## Retention Notes (apply per language)
- Highest drop-off risk: The Autopsy segment (4:00–9:30) if the decision timeline becomes a flat
  list-read — cut every 20-30 seconds between talking-head, archival B-roll, and the animated
  timeline graphic in every language's edit.
- Trim The Rise section if pre-launch hype footage is thin for a given language's cut.
- Consider moving the "missing feature" tease earlier if a language's retention data shows
  drop-off before 2:00.
