# Editing Notes

## Pacing
- Cold open through Segment 6 (the broken checkout) should build steadily — this is the "how bad was
  it, technically" half of the story, and it should feel increasingly absurd as it goes.
- Segments 7-8 (the marketing-vs-awareness gap) are the sharpest, most differentiating beat in the
  package — don't rush past the Pets.com contrast, let the two-bar comparison land visually.
- Segments 9-11 (burn rate, receivership, the two-buyer fire sale) are the serious/analytical core —
  slow down here, especially on the "$135M raised, under $2M recovered" framing card.
- Segment 12 (the book) should breathe slightly — it's the closing "lasting legacy" beat, not a rushed
  footnote.

## Graphics Priorities (build these first)
1. Dial-up loading-bar graphic frozen partway — the single most important visual, needs to land
   clearly on the "56K modems" line
2. Two-bar marketing-spend-vs-awareness comparison graphic (Boo.com vs. an unnamed/generic
   "Company B" if direct Pets.com branding needs to stay verbal-only)
3. Two-buyers fire-sale graphic (two separate shopping-bag icons)
4. Full-arc timeline (closing lesson)
5. Ascending funding graphic (early segment)

## Text-Card Discipline
- Never render the ~$390M valuation figure — it's unconfirmed and deliberately excluded.
- Never render a single confident founding year — use "1998/1999" or narrate the discrepancy verbally
  instead of committing to one date on screen.
- Never render a dollar figure for the Fashionmall.com brand purchase — state it as "undisclosed."
- When showing the "$135M raised" and "£125M in six months" burn figures together, caption both with
  a small "(different time windows)" note rather than implying one contradicts or replaces the other.
- Do not depict any real, current brand logos for Boo.com, Fashionmall.com, or Bright Station — none
  of these entities operate today in the same form; use generic/abstracted graphics only.

## Audio/Music Direction
- Curious, slightly ominous bed through the founding/pitch/funding segments (1:40-4:00) — building
  anticipation rather than celebration, since the ambition itself is the setup for the fall.
- Shift to a wry, slightly absurdist bed for the technology/checkout segments (4:40-7:30) — the "56K
  modems" detail should land as genuinely funny, not tragic.
- Drop to a more serious, analytical bed for the marketing-gap and burn-rate segments (7:30-10:00).
- Neutral, matter-of-fact bed for the fire-sale segment (11:00-12:15) — avoid over-dramatizing a
  fairly mundane liquidation process.
- Warm, reflective bed for the book/closing-lesson segments (12:15-14:00).

## Caption/Subtitle Note
- Spell out "receivership" in plain language on first use if it appears in captions — assume a
  portion of the audience is unfamiliar with UK insolvency terminology (distinct from US "Chapter 11"
  language used in most of this channel's other packages).
- Caption the "56K modems" line exactly as sourced — don't paraphrase it into something punchier than
  the real quote.
