# Video Description

## Main Description (YouTube, long-form)

This company's own homepage warned you that you needed a faster modem just to load it. It had raised
roughly $135 million.

Boo.com planned to launch in May 1999. It actually launched in November — six months late, burning
cash with zero revenue the entire time. When it finally went live, its 3D "spin and try on" product
viewer took minutes to load on a dial-up connection, didn't work properly on Macintosh computers at
all, and let only about 1 in 4 attempted purchases actually go through.

The company had already spent $25 million on advertising before it even opened. One retrospective
account puts the actual brand awareness it bought at only around 13.2% of internet users — a sharp
contrast to this channel's other big 1999-2000 marketing story, Pets.com, whose sock puppet became
almost universally recognized on a smaller budget.

May 18, 2000: placed into receivership. Two separate companies split what was left — one bought the
technology, one bought the brand — for a combined total reportedly under $2 million.

This is the original dot-com-bust case study, the one business schools were teaching before almost
any other company like it. This video is about what it actually got right (more than you'd think),
what actually killed it, and the book its own founder wrote about the whole thing afterward.

Honesty note up front: sources genuinely disagree on some specifics here, including this company's own
founding year — where they disagree, this video states both versions rather than picking whichever
sounds cleaner. Full source list in the pinned comment and in this channel's production notes.
