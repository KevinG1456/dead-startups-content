# Title Options — Ranked

Anchored on Boo.com: conceived 1998, founded March 1999 by Ernst Malmsten, Kajsa Leander, Patrik
Hedelin; ~$135M raised (Goldman Sachs, J.P. Morgan, LVMH's Europ@web, Benetton family's investment
arm); planned launch May 1999, actual launch Nov 3 1999 (6 months late); site "designed for 56K
modems and above," didn't work on Mac, only ~25% of purchase attempts completed; $25M ad spend
pre-launch, reportedly only ~13.2% brand awareness achieved; receivership May 18 2000; Bright Station
bought the tech for £250K (~$375K), Fashionmall.com separately bought the brand for an undisclosed
sum — combined under $2M against $135M raised. Book: "Boo Hoo" (2001).

## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **This Website Warned You It Needed a Faster Modem to Even Load** — concrete, funny, immediately
   legible even to viewers with zero startup knowledge; the single most quotable real detail in the
   whole package
2. They Spent $25 Million on Ads. 13% of People Noticed. — sharp, specific, sets up the package's
   real differentiator (the Pets.com contrast) without needing to name Pets.com in the title itself
3. Only 1 in 4 People Who Tried to Buy Something From This Site Could — a brutal, specific,
   easy-to-visualize failure statistic
4. This Startup Sold Its Entire Technology for Less Than a New Car — strong "the scale of the fall"
   hook, doesn't require prior Boo.com knowledge
5. $135 Million Raised. Sold for Under $2 Million. Here's What Happened In Between. — clean,
   high-clarity, strong for the founder/VC-focused audience segment
6. The Startup That Raised More Than Pets.com and Is Remembered Less — strong differentiator, best
   used once 054/Pets.com already has traction as a channel reference point
7. They Built the Future of Online Shopping. It Needed a Modem That Didn't Exist Yet. — strong
   "too early" framing, echoes 038/Webvan's angle without duplicating it
8. Two Companies Bought the Wreckage of This Startup For Almost Nothing — good but slightly less
   specific than the modem/awareness hooks
9. $6 Million On 3D Software. It Didn't Work on Half the Computers in the World. — precise but
   slightly denser/less immediately visual than the top hooks
10. The Founders Wrote a Book About Their Own $135 Million Failure — good closing-beat framing,
    weaker as a cold-open hook since it requires context first

## Recommended Title
**"This Website Warned You It Needed a Faster Modem to Even Load"**

Reasoning: leads with a real, sourced, immediately funny/absurd detail that needs zero prior knowledge
of Boo.com to land, sets up the whole video's throughline (a company built years ahead of the
infrastructure its actual users had), and avoids the generic "another dot-com bust" framing that most
existing Boo.com coverage falls into. Matches the folder name's headline stat ($135M/18 months) as the
secondary beat once the hook has landed.
