# Email Campaign

## Email 1 — Launch Announcement
**Subject:** This site literally told you your modem wasn't fast enough
**Preview text:** $135 million raised. Launched six months late. Here's what actually happened.

Body:
This week's autopsy is the original dot-com disaster — the one business schools were teaching before
almost any other company on this channel.

Boo.com planned to launch in May 1999. It actually launched in November, six months late, burning
cash the entire time. When it finally went live, the homepage displayed its own warning: "designed
for 56K modems and above." Only about 1 in 4 attempted purchases actually completed.

They'd already spent $25 million on advertising before launch. Reported brand awareness afterward:
around 13.2% of internet users — a fraction of what this channel's other 1999-2000 marketing story,
Pets.com, achieved on a smaller budget.

May 2000: receivership, having burned through roughly $135 million. Two separate companies split the
wreckage for a combined total reportedly under $2 million.

There's also a genuinely fascinating detail in here about the book the founder wrote afterward.
