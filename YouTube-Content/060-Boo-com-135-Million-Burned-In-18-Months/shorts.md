# Shorts Scripts

Rule for this package: stay strictly to sourced, dated facts. Never state the ~$390M valuation figure.
Never conflate the two fire-sale buyers or state a dollar figure for the Fashionmall.com deal. Each
script targets 30-45 seconds.

## Short 1 — "Designed for 56K Modems"
- Hook (0-3s): "This website's own homepage warned you that you needed a faster modem just to load
  it."
- Body: Boo.com's 3D product viewer took minutes to load on dial-up and didn't work on Mac computers
  at all. Only about 1 in 4 attempted purchases actually went through.
- Payoff: "They built the future of the internet. Their customers were still on dial-up."
- CTA: "Full story on the channel."

## Short 2 — "$25 Million, 13% Awareness"
- Hook (0-3s): "This company spent twenty-five million dollars on ads before it even launched."
- Body: One retrospective account puts total brand awareness at only around 13.2% of internet users —
  a fraction of what Pets.com's sock puppet achieved on a smaller budget the same era.
- Payoff: "Same era, bigger budget, almost nobody remembers the name."
- CTA: "Full story on the channel."

## Short 3 — "Two Buyers, Almost Nothing"
- Hook (0-3s): "This startup raised $135 million. Here's what it actually sold for."
- Body: Two separate companies split what was left — one bought the technology for about $375,000,
  one bought the brand name for an undisclosed amount. Combined: reportedly under $2 million.
- Payoff: "$135 million in. Under $2 million out."
- CTA: "Watch the full breakdown — link in bio."

## Short 4 — "Six Months Late"
- Hook (0-3s): "This company was supposed to launch in May. It launched in November."
- Body: Six months of burning cash with zero revenue before the site ever went live — and it still
  wasn't ready when it did.
- Payoff: "Being late didn't fix the problem. It just spent more money finding it."
- CTA: "Full story on the channel."

## Short 5 — "The Founders Wrote the Book On It"
- Hook (0-3s): "The founders of this failed startup wrote an entire book about exactly how they lost
  $135 million."
- Body: "Boo Hoo: A Dot.Com Story from Concept to Catastrophe" (2001) became one of the earliest,
  most detailed insider accounts of a dot-com collapse — still taught in business schools today.
- Payoff: "Most founders hide the failure. These two wrote the whole thing down."
- CTA: "Full story — link in bio."
