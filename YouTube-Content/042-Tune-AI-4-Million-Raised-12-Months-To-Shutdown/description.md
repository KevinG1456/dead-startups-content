## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Tune AI —
a real, current, fully-documented example of a company caught by the exact trend it repositioned to
ride.

Founded in San Francisco in 2018 as NimbleBox by co-founders Anshuman Pandey, Naman Maheshwari, and
Rohan Pooniwala, the company spent years building AI-infrastructure and MLOps tooling. On February 6,
2024, as the generative-AI boom accelerated, NimbleBox rebranded as Tune AI, launching two products:
Tune Chat, a multi-model AI chat interface, and Tune Studio, a platform to deploy and fine-tune large
language models. The company raised approximately $4.4 million total from Accel, Flipkart Ventures,
Together Fund, Speciale Invest, Techstars, and CSVP Fund — including a $250,000 round as recently as
March 27, 2024, just seven weeks after the rebrand.

Here's the mechanism that explains what happened next. Between 2023 and 2025, the cost of the AI
underneath products like Tune AI's collapsed: GPT-4-quality output fell from roughly $30 per million
input tokens in 2023 to under $0.50 per million tokens by 2025 — a decline of about 95%. A mid-size
company's AI chatbot, which cost an estimated $8,000 to $15,000 a month to run in 2023, cost under $200 a
month by 2025. At the same time, at least six providers were offering frontier-class AI models by late
2024, and cloud providers and the open-source ecosystem increasingly bundled comparable tooling for free
— eroding exactly the layer Tune AI was charging for.

Per retrospective coverage, a large share of Tune AI's users were free developers rather than paying
enterprises, meaning infrastructure costs stayed high while the revenue to cover them didn't scale. On
February 28, 2025 — almost exactly twelve months after the rebrand — Tune AI shut down entirely. Both
Tune Chat and Tune Studio were taken offline, and the company processed refunds for affected customers.

Tune AI wasn't alone: an estimated 3,800 of the roughly 14,000-plus AI startups that launched in 2024
had shut down by the end of 2025 — a failure rate near 27% within about eighteen months.
