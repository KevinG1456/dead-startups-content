# Script Package

Anchored on Tune AI — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. KEY RULE FOR THIS SCRIPT: no direct founder
quote explaining the shutdown was found — present the "free users, thin margins, commoditized tooling"
cause as the best-sourced available explanation from retrospective coverage, not as a company statement.
Never invent specific revenue or customer-count figures. Tone: analytical, structural, not mocking — a
business-model story, not a scandal.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes (the founding, the pivot, the macro AI-cost collapse, the shutdown,
and the lesson all covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "This company rebranded specifically to catch the AI boom. Twelve months later, it was gone."
- Visual: "NIMBLEBOX → TUNE AI" logo-swap animation, then hard cut to a "SHUT DOWN" stamp
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This isn't a story about a bad
  decision. It's a story about what happens when the thing you sell gets 95% cheaper in two years."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Tune AI — formerly NimbleBox, an AI-infrastructure company founded in San Francisco back
  in 2018, years before anyone outside the industry had heard of ChatGPT. In February 2024, they
  rebranded to ride the generative-AI wave."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll see the exact 12-month arc from that rebrand to the
  shutdown — and the macro trend that explains why this keeps happening to companies like it."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from Crunchbase, TechStartups.com, LinkedIn, and public
  inference-pricing data — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "One honesty note up front: no direct statement from
  Tune AI's founders about why they shut down has been found publicly. What you're about to hear is the
  best-sourced explanation available from retrospective coverage — clearly flagged as that, not
  presented as a direct quote."

### Main Content — The Company (1:40–4:30)
- **Segment 1 — The Founding (1:40–2:20):** 2018, San Francisco. Co-founders Anshuman Pandey, Naman
  Maheshwari, and Rohan Pooniwala build NimbleBox — MLOps and AI-infrastructure tooling, aimed at
  simplifying how companies build and deploy machine learning models. Visual: generic AI-infrastructure
  dashboard graphic, "FOUNDED 2018 — NIMBLEBOX" title card.
- **Segment 2 — The Rebrand (2:20–3:20):** February 6, 2024. As the generative-AI boom accelerates,
  NimbleBox rebrands as Tune AI, launching two products: Tune Chat, a multi-model AI chat interface, and
  Tune Studio, a platform to deploy and fine-tune large language models. Visual: "FEBRUARY 6, 2024 —
  REBRANDED AS TUNE AI" title card.
- **Segment 3 — The Money (3:20–4:30):** Tune AI raises approximately $4.4 million total from Accel,
  Flipkart Ventures, Together Fund, Speciale Invest, Techstars, and CSVP Fund — including a $250,000
  round as recently as March 27, 2024, just seven weeks after the rebrand. On-screen framing card:
  "Real, credible investors. This wasn't an under-resourced company." Visual: "$4.4 MILLION RAISED"
  title card with investor logos/names listed.

### Main Content — The Mechanism (4:30–8:30)
- **Segment 4 — What Was Actually Being Sold (4:30–5:15):** Tune Chat and Tune Studio were, at their
  core, a paid layer on top of large language models — tooling to access, compare, deploy, and fine-tune
  AI models more easily than doing it raw. Visual: simple "YOU PAY → TUNE AI → THE UNDERLYING AI MODELS"
  layer diagram.
- **Segment 5 — The Floor Fell Out (5:15–6:45):** between 2023 and 2025, the cost of the AI underneath
  that layer collapsed. GPT-4-quality output fell from roughly $30 per million input tokens in 2023 to
  under $0.50 per million tokens by 2025 — a decline of about 95%. A mid-size company's AI chatbot,
  which cost an estimated $8,000 to $15,000 a month to run on 2023-era GPT-4, cost under $200 a month by
  2025 on newer, cheaper models. On-screen framing card: "This is a real, sourced, dramatic collapse —
  not a rounded-up estimate." Visual: "$30 → $0.50 PER MILLION TOKENS" numeral-collapse graphic.
- **Segment 6 — Everyone Started Giving It Away (6:45–7:45):** by late 2024, at least six providers
  offered frontier-class AI models — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek, Mistral, and Meta via API
  partners. DeepSeek's entry forced across-the-board repricing. Google introduced free tiers for its
  Gemini Flash models. Anthropic cut its own premium-model pricing by roughly 67%. The tooling layer
  Tune AI charged for increasingly came bundled, free, directly from the model providers and cloud
  platforms themselves. Visual: logo wall of AI providers with "FREE TIER" stamps appearing one by one.
- **Segment 7 — Who Was Actually Paying (7:45–8:30):** per retrospective coverage, a large share of
  Tune AI's users were free developers testing the product rather than paying enterprise customers —
  meaning infrastructure costs stayed high while the revenue to cover them didn't scale. On-screen
  framing card: "This explanation comes from retrospective analysis, not a direct company statement —
  presented as the best-sourced account available." Visual: "MOSTLY FREE USERS" title card.

### Main Content — The Ending (8:30–10:00)
- **Segment 8 — The Shutdown (8:30–9:15):** February 28, 2025 — almost exactly twelve months after the
  Tune AI rebrand — the company ceased operations. Both Tune Chat and Tune Studio were taken offline.
  The company processed refunds for affected customers as part of the wind-down. Visual: "SHUT DOWN —
  FEBRUARY 28, 2025" title card with a "12 MONTHS AFTER THE REBRAND" subtitle.
- **Segment 9 — Not an Isolated Case (9:15–10:00):** an estimated 3,800 of the roughly 14,000-plus AI
  startups that launched in 2024 had shut down by the end of 2025 — a failure rate near 27% within about
  eighteen months, with thousands more closing in early 2026. Visual: "3,800 OF 14,000+ AI STARTUPS —
  GONE WITHIN 18 MONTHS" title card.
- Mid-video CTA (~9:45): "This channel usually covers companies killed by their own decisions. This one
  was caught by the market itself moving faster than any decision could keep up with. Subscribe for a
  new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Lesson (10:00–11:30)
- Plain-language comparison: Tune AI didn't fail to see the AI wave coming — it rebranded specifically
  to ride it, with real funding and real products. The problem wasn't foresight. It was that the exact
  trend that created the opportunity also erased the price anyone was willing to pay for accessing it.
- The core lesson, stated directly: when your product's entire value is "easier access to someone else's
  underlying technology," you are betting that access stays scarce and expensive. If the underlying
  technology's cost collapses and its owners start giving away your layer for free, there's no amount of
  good execution that outruns that math.
- Visual: full-arc timeline graphic — founded (2018) → rebranded as Tune AI (Feb 2024) → $250K round
  (Mar 2024) → inference costs collapsing across 2024 → shutdown (Feb 2025).

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–12:45)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — building a paid layer on top of someone else's rapidly
  commoditizing technology means your margin is only as durable as that technology's price stays high
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and this is the first one on the channel centered
  entirely on a company caught by its own core input collapsing in price"
- Engagement prompt: "If you're building something on top of AI right now — what's your answer to what
  happens when the model providers give away what you're charging for? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the inference-cost collapse chart ($30 to $0.50 per million tokens) and the AI-startup
failure-rate statistic, then introduces Tune AI as the concrete case study proving the trend. Measured,
graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine market-structure case study rather than a company
retrospective.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the February 6, 2024 rebrand announcement itself — the optimism, the new products, the fresh
funding — before cutting hard to the February 28, 2025 shutdown, then rewinding to fill in the macro
cost-collapse mechanism in between. Slower pacing, holds the "95% cheaper" reveal until the midpoint,
resolving with the broader AI-startup failure-rate statistic as the closing beat.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "one year, one collapsing market" leading into rapid-fire beats: founding, rebrand,
funding, inference-cost collapse, free-tier commoditization, mostly-free users, shutdown, broader
failure-rate context — landing on the "paid layer on collapsing-cost technology" lesson as the final,
clarifying beat. Strong Shorts-extraction potential, especially the $30-to-$0.50 numeral collapse and
the "12 months after the rebrand" detail.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the funding segment (3:20–4:30) if it reads as a dry Crunchbase recitation
- **Fix:** frame the $4.4M and the March 2024 round explicitly as "real money, real investors, right
  after the rebrand — this wasn't an under-resourced company," connecting it forward to the irony that
  funding didn't matter against the macro trend
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the general AI-infrastructure/MLOps context in Segment 1 if
  audience familiarity with AI-tooling categories is already high
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "95% cheaper" figure even more explicitly in the cold
  open if early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 5 (5:15)
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small running "cost per million tokens" corner counter visible
  from Segment 5 onward, ticking downward as the timeline advances toward 2025, landing on "$0.50"
