## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Color
Labs — one of the clearest, most expensive real examples of a startup with no defined customer at all.

Color Labs was founded by Bill Nguyen, a serial entrepreneur who had previously sold his company Lala
to Apple, and co-founder Peter Pham. Before their product, Color, ever launched, the company raised
$41 million: $25 million from Sequoia Capital, $9 million from Bain Capital, and $7 million in venture
debt from Silicon Valley Bank.

Color's concept was "elastic," proximity-based photo sharing — automatically seeing photos from any
other Color user physically nearby, no friend requests or follows needed. It was built for everyone in
physical proximity, not for any specific customer or use case. When it launched in March 2011, it drove
over a million downloads within weeks — and immediate, widespread confusion about what the app was
actually for. By September 2011, monthly active users had fallen below 100,000. Co-founder Peter Pham
had already left the company in June, less than three months after launch.

On September 18, 2011, at TechCrunch Disrupt, Sequoia partner and Color board member Doug Leone
publicly said Sequoia was "thrilled" to be an investor, teasing a pivot — while the first product was
already collapsing. That pivot became "Color for Facebook," a live 30-second video broadcasting app.
It made the same underlying mistake in a new format: still no specific, defined answer to who it was
for or why they'd choose it.

In November 2012, Apple confirmed it was acquiring Color Labs — an "acqui-hire" reported at
approximately $7 million, about 17% of what the company had raised. Apple's stated interest was the
engineering team and a patent portfolio, reportedly to strengthen FaceTime — not the Color product
itself, which shut down December 31, 2012.

This channel mentioned Color Labs briefly before, in package 012, the Pivot Graveyard. This is the
fuller, fully sourced version of what actually happened.
