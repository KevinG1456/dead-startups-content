FOUNDING AND FUNDING — precisely sourced
- Color Labs was founded in 2010/2011 (sources vary on the exact year; the product itself launched
  publicly in March 2011) by Bill Nguyen — a serial entrepreneur whose previous company, Lala (a music
  streaming service), was acquired by Apple in 2009 — and co-founder Peter Pham.
- The company raised $41 million entirely BEFORE its product publicly launched: $25 million from
  Sequoia Capital, $9 million from Bain Capital, and $7 million in venture debt from Silicon Valley
  Bank.
Source: Dealroom; Wikipedia ("Color Labs"); Forbes (Nov 20, 2012, "A Mess Of Family Dynamics Alleged
in Lawsuit..." — funding figures only cited from this piece, not the personal allegations).

THE PRODUCT AND THE POSITIONING PROBLEM — precisely sourced
- Color's core concept: "elastic," proximity-based social photo-sharing. Users automatically saw
  photos taken by any other Color user physically nearby — no friend requests, no follows, no
  specified relationship required. The product was explicitly built for "everyone nearby," not for any
  defined customer segment or specific use case.
- March 2011: public launch. Initial buzz drove over 1 million downloads.
- The launch was met with widespread user confusion about the app's actual purpose and value — users
  did not understand what problem it solved for them specifically.
- By September 2011, monthly active users had fallen to under 100,000 — a collapse from the initial
  download spike within about six months.
- June 2011: co-founder Peter Pham departed the company, less than three months after the product's
  public launch.
Source: The Stupid Founder ("The $41 Million Startup That Never Found Its Place"); Medium/FAIL Company
(Kevin Chao, "What Happened to Color Labs?"); Wikipedia.

THE PIVOT — precisely sourced, still no defined audience
- September 18, 2011: at TechCrunch Disrupt, Sequoia partner and Color board member Doug Leone
  publicly said Sequoia was "thrilled" to be an investor in Color and teased "stay tuned" for a pivot,
  referring to an internal project codenamed "Blue."
- Color subsequently abandoned its location-based photo concept and pivoted to "Color for Facebook" —
  a mobile app for broadcasting live 30-second video clips directly to a user's Facebook Wall. This
  second product also failed to define a specific target user or use case, and also failed to gain a
  significant, retained audience.
Source: TechCrunch (Sept 18, 2011, "Sequoia's Doug Leone: We Are 'Thrilled' To Be Investors In Color,
'Stay Tuned' For The Pivot"); Wikipedia.

THE ACQUI-HIRE AND SHUTDOWN — precisely dated
- October 2012: reports surfaced that Apple was acquiring Color Labs.
- November 2012: the acquisition was confirmed — an "acqui-hire" deal reported at approximately $7
  million, roughly 17% of the $41 million Color had raised. Apple's stated interest was primarily the
  company's engineering team and its patent portfolio, which included technology for sharing content
  between devices and an HD video file format — reportedly intended to bolster Apple's own FaceTime
  product, not to continue operating Color's app.
- December 31, 2012: the Color app was officially shut down.
Source: Forbes (Oct 18, 2012, "Apple To Buy Bill Nguyen's Failed Color Labs For Patents To Bolster
FaceTime"); MacDailyNews (Oct 2012); Wikipedia; Medium/FAIL Company.
