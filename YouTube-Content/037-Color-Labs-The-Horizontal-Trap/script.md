# Script Package

Anchored on Color Labs — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RULE FOR THIS
SCRIPT: never reference the November 2012 lawsuit's personal-misconduct allegations against Bill
Nguyen anywhere in this script — those allegations are deliberately excluded from this package (see
sources-log.txt for full reasoning). Present Nguyen neutrally throughout as Color's co-founder and a
repeat entrepreneur (previously sold Lala to Apple). Focus entirely on the product-positioning/ICP
strategy lesson.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes (the pre-launch funding, the launch and collapse, the pivot, and the
acqui-hire/shutdown all covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "This company raised $41 million before a single user ever opened the app. It sold, eighteen
  months later, for $7 million — and not even for the product itself."
- Visual: large glowing "$41M" numeral shrinking to a small "$7M" numeral
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "The money was never the problem. The
  problem was nobody could answer one question."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Color Labs — founded by Bill Nguyen, who'd previously sold his company Lala to Apple,
  and co-founder Peter Pham. Their app, Color, let you automatically see photos from any other Color
  user physically near you — no friend requests, no follows. It was built for everyone nearby. Not for
  any specific person, or any specific reason to use it."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll see exactly what happens when a well-funded team never
  answers the one question every product actually needs answered — and you'll watch them try to fix it
  with a pivot that makes the exact same mistake in a different form."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from TechCrunch, Forbes, Wikipedia, and Medium/FAIL Company —
  sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "This is a story about product positioning, not about
  money or hype. Color had plenty of both. What it never had was an answer to: who, specifically, is
  this for?"

### Main Content — The Launch (1:40–5:00)
- **Segment 1 — The Pre-Launch Funding (1:40–2:30):** Color raised $41 million entirely before its
  product ever launched — $25 million from Sequoia Capital, $9 million from Bain Capital, $7 million in
  venture debt from Silicon Valley Bank. Visual: "$41 MILLION — BEFORE LAUNCH" title card.
- **Segment 2 — The Product (2:30–3:15):** Color's concept — "elastic," proximity-based photo sharing.
  See photos from anyone nearby, automatically, no relationship required. On-screen framing card: "Who
  is this for? The honest answer, even inside the company, was never fully clear." Visual: generic
  proximity-radar graphic, no real app UI rendered.
- **Segment 3 — The Launch and the Confusion (3:15–4:15):** March 2011, public launch, over 1 million
  downloads in the first weeks — and immediate, widespread confusion about what the app was actually
  for. Visual: download-spike graph.
- **Segment 4 — The Collapse (4:15–5:00):** by September 2011 — six months after launch — monthly
  active users had fallen below 100,000. Co-founder Peter Pham had already left the company in June,
  less than three months after launch. Visual: "UNDER 100,000 MAU — SEPTEMBER 2011" title card.

### Main Content — The Pivot (5:00–7:30)
- **Segment 5 — "Thrilled" (5:00–6:00):** September 18, 2011, at TechCrunch Disrupt, Sequoia partner
  and Color board member Doug Leone says Sequoia is "thrilled" to be an investor and teases a pivot.
  On-screen framing card: "This is a real, on-the-record quote, said in public, while the first product
  was already collapsing." Visual: attributed quote card.
- **Segment 6 — Color for Facebook (6:00–7:30):** the team pivots to live 30-second video broadcasting
  to a user's Facebook Wall. On-screen framing card, held for a beat: "Same underlying problem, new
  format: still no specific answer to who this was for, or why they'd use it over anything else." This
  pivot also fails to gain a significant, retained audience. Visual: "COLOR FOR FACEBOOK — THE SAME
  QUESTION, UNANSWERED AGAIN" title card.

### Main Content — The End (7:30–9:30)
- **Segment 7 — The Acqui-Hire (7:30–8:45):** October-November 2012, reports and then confirmation:
  Apple is acquiring Color Labs, in a deal reported around $7 million — about 17% of what Color had
  raised. Apple's stated interest: the engineering team and a patent portfolio covering device-to-
  device content sharing and an HD video format, reportedly to strengthen FaceTime. Not the Color
  product itself. Visual: "$7 MILLION — ENGINEERS AND PATENTS, NOT THE PRODUCT" title card.
- **Segment 8 — The Shutdown (8:45–9:30):** December 31, 2012, the Color app is officially shut down.
  Visual: "SHUT DOWN — DECEMBER 31, 2012" title card.
- Mid-video CTA (~9:15): "This channel briefly mentioned Color Labs before, in the Pivot Graveyard
  video — package 012. This is the fuller, fully sourced version of what actually happened. Subscribe
  for a new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Lesson (9:30–11:00)
- Plain-language comparison: Color had real money, real engineering talent, and real initial user
  curiosity. What it never had, in either version of the product, was a specific, defined customer with
  a specific, defined reason to keep using it.
- The core lesson, stated directly: "everyone" is not a customer segment, and a pivot doesn't fix a
  positioning problem if it just relocates the same unanswered question into a new format.
- Visual: full-arc timeline graphic — pre-launch funding (2010-2011) → launch (Mar 2011) → co-founder
  departure (Jun 2011) → collapse to <100K MAU (Sept 2011) → "thrilled" quote/pivot tease (Sept 2011) →
  Color for Facebook pivot (2011-2012) → acqui-hire (Nov 2012) → shutdown (Dec 2012).

### Final CTA + Outro (11:00–12:15)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — a horizontal product built for "everyone" is, in practice, built
  for no one in particular, and no amount of funding fixes that on its own
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and if you want the shorter original mention of
  this company, check out package 012, the Pivot Graveyard"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you ever download Color back in 2011? What did you think it was actually
  for? And do you think any pivot could have saved it? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: package 012 (Pivot Graveyard) + most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the $41M-to-$7M numeral graphic and the download-to-MAU collapse chart, then works
through the launch, pivot, and acqui-hire as supporting detail. Measured, graphics-forward, treats the
video as a genuine case study in positioning strategy.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the March 2011 launch itself — the excitement, the download numbers — before cutting hard to
the September 2011 collapse figures, then rewinding to explain the funding and positioning that made
the outcome close to inevitable. Slower pacing, holds the pivot's repeat-mistake reveal until Segment
6. Strongest tonal-irony arc of the three versions.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "$41 million, in order" leading into rapid-fire beats: pre-launch funding, launch,
confusion, collapse, pivot tease, second pivot, acqui-hire, shutdown — landing on the "who is this
for?" lesson as the final, clarifying beat. Strong Shorts-extraction potential, especially the
$41M-vs-$7M contrast and the download-to-MAU collapse numbers.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the pivot segment (5:00–7:30) if "Color for Facebook" feels like a minor footnote
  rather than a meaningful repeat of the same core mistake
- **Fix:** explicitly visually echo Segment 2's "who is this for?" framing card during the pivot
  segment, so the repetition reads as the video's actual thesis landing a second time, not a new
  problem
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general 2011-era startup-funding-climate context if audience
  familiarity with the period is already high
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the $41M-to-$7M contrast even more explicitly in the cold
  open if early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 7 (7:30)
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small running "WHO IS THIS FOR?" unanswered-question tag
  visible in a corner graphic throughout both product segments, disappearing only at the shutdown card
