# Video Editing Notes

## Zoom Suggestions
- Slow push-in on avatar during explainer beats; hard snap-zoom on the cold-open $41M-to-$7M
  numeral-shrink reveal, and on the "UNDER 100,000 MAU — SEPTEMBER 2011" card
- Slow, deliberate push-in (not a snap) on the "who is this for? The honest answer, even inside the
  company, was never fully clear" framing card — the single most important line in the video

## Motion Graphics
- Cold-open large glowing "$41M" numeral shrinking to a small "$7M" numeral
- "The money was never the problem. The problem was nobody could answer one question." pattern-
  interrupt card, white text on black
- "$41 MILLION — BEFORE LAUNCH" title card
- Generic proximity-radar graphic for the product explainer, no real app UI rendered
- Download-spike graph for the March 2011 launch
- "UNDER 100,000 MAU — SEPTEMBER 2011" title card
- Attributed Doug Leone "thrilled" quote card
- "COLOR FOR FACEBOOK — THE SAME QUESTION, UNANSWERED AGAIN" title card
- "$7 MILLION — ENGINEERS AND PATENTS, NOT THE PRODUCT" title card
- "SHUT DOWN — DECEMBER 31, 2012" title card
- Full-arc timeline graphic for The Actual Lesson: pre-launch funding (2010-2011) → launch (Mar 2011)
  → co-founder departure (Jun 2011) → collapse to <100K MAU (Sept 2011) → "thrilled" quote/pivot tease
  (Sept 2011) → Color for Facebook pivot (2011-2012) → acqui-hire (Nov 2012) → shutdown (Dec 2012)
- Running "WHO IS THIS FOR?" unanswered-question corner tag, visible throughout both product segments,
  disappearing only at the shutdown card

## Lower Thirds
- Source citation lower-third whenever a number, date, or term is referenced (TechCrunch, Forbes,
  Wikipedia, Medium/FAIL Company)
- "On-the-record quote, said in public" lower third on the Doug Leone quote card
- No lower third should ever reference the excluded lawsuit content — this is a hard rule, not a
  pacing choice

## Animations
- Text-reveal (not fade) for all key numbers, dates, and quotes
- Ken Burns pan/zoom on any static press photos or generic office/phone imagery — no real readable
  logos/trademarks (Color Labs, Apple, Sequoia, Bain Capital) without rights clearance
- The "WHO IS THIS FOR?" corner tag should visually persist, unanswered, through both the original
  product segment and the pivot segment, then visibly "disappear unresolved" at the shutdown card —
  reinforcing that the question was never answered either time
- The download-spike-to-MAU-collapse graph should animate the spike quickly and the collapse slowly,
  so the six-month decline reads as a genuine, gradual realization rather than an instant crash

## Camera Movement (HeyGen/live-action hybrid)
- Static avatar frame during explanatory beats; reserve any movement for the download-spike graph, the
  quote card, and the full-arc timeline in The Actual Lesson
- Hold the avatar completely still and centered during the "who is this for?" framing cards — no
  camera movement competing with that text for attention

## Background Music
- Cold open: tense, minimal pulse, cutting to near-silence on the pattern-interrupt card
- The Pre-Launch Funding / The Product: bright, optimistic, capturing pre-launch excitement
- The Launch and the Confusion: energetic at first, curdling into something more uncertain as the
  "confusion" framing lands
- The Collapse: uneasy, declining energy
- "Thrilled": a small ironic lift on the quote itself, undercut immediately by the visual context
- Color for Facebook: a brief hopeful rebuild, fading again as the same problem repeats
- The Acqui-Hire: measured, matter-of-fact, not triumphant
- The Shutdown: quiet, restrained
- The Actual Lesson: neutral, clear, reflective
- Outro: return to a softened version of the cold-open motif

## Sound Effects
- Soft "date stamp" SFX on each key date reveal throughout (2011 launch, Jun 2011 departure, Sept 2011
  collapse/quote, 2011-2012 pivot, Nov 2012 acquisition, Dec 2012 shutdown)
- Sharp "cash register" SFX on the "$41M" reveal, a distinctly duller "thud" on the "$7M" reveal
- Soft "download chime" SFX rapid-fire during the download-spike graph animation
- No comedic stingers anywhere in this video — tone stays analytical and precise throughout
- Light, satisfying "chime" SFX on the full-arc timeline's final beat in The Actual Lesson
