## LinkedIn
"A real case study in what a single veto right in a term sheet can do — even when every other investor
agrees to fund a rescue round:

Quixey, an app-search company founded in 2009, raised roughly $173.8 million and peaked at a $600
million valuation in 2015, backed by Goldman Sachs, GGV Capital, Alibaba, and SoftBank. By 2016,
missed revenue targets triggered a leadership crisis — three CEOs in about a year.

In August 2016, in the middle of that churn, Quixey took an Alibaba-led financing package: a $10
million payment plus $30 million in debt, carrying an 18-month repayment deadline and a veto right
over any future equity round. By February 2017, other investors — including Atlantic Bridge Capital —
had agreed to provide $10 million in rescue financing. Alibaba exercised its veto and blocked it. Days
later, Quixey laid off about 50 employees and shut down.
