# 033 — Quixey Signed a Debt Deal With One Veto Clause in It. That Clause Killed the Company.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Quixey's fully documented public history
(TechCrunch, CNBC, Bloomberg, Recode, Global Venturing, The Information, Mountain View Voice,
Wikipedia — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). Third title on the channel sourced from the
"dead-saas-30-Content Titles new-video-ideas.md" batch (Cluster 1, idea #4: "The company that died of
a term sheet"). Also resolves an open `[VERIFY]` flag from package 012 (The Pivot Graveyard), which
mentioned Quixey only briefly and without sourcing.

**The real case:** Quixey, a "functional" app-search engine founded in 2009 by Tomer Kagan and Liron
Shapira, raised roughly $173.8 million over its life, peaking at a ~$600 million valuation in a 2015
round backed by Goldman Sachs, GGV Capital, Alibaba, and SoftBank. By 2016, missed revenue targets
triggered a leadership crisis — three different CEOs in about a year. In August 2016, Quixey took an
Alibaba-led financing package combining a $10 million payment and $30 million in debt — with two
critical terms: an 18-month repayment deadline, and a veto right giving Alibaba the power to block any
future equity round Quixey tried to raise. In November 2016, Quixey began raising a rescue round; by
February 2017, existing investors including Atlantic Bridge Capital had agreed to provide $10 million.
Alibaba exercised its veto and blocked the deal. Days later, Quixey laid off roughly 50 employees,
closed its Mountain View office, and shut down for good.

**Framing requirement:** This is a governance/deal-structure story, not a fraud story — no individual
was charged with anything. Present Tomer Kagan's role precisely: he was replaced as CEO in April 2016
but stayed on Quixey's board, so unlike this channel's other ousted-founder packages (030/MoviePass),
he was never fully separated from the company — don't force a cleaner narrative than the facts
support.
