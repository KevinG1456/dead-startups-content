Sources Log — Package 033: Quixey
Filled in: 2026-08-15

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
Quixey is the anchor for this channel's "Money Mechanics" batch title #4: "The company that died of a
term sheet (the deal that scared off every other investor)." Unlike title #3's premise (which needed
correcting — see package 032), this pattern holds up exactly as described: an August 2016 debt deal
with Alibaba included a contractual veto right over any future equity financing, and Alibaba used that
exact veto in February 2017 to kill a rescue round already agreed to by other investors (including
Atlantic Bridge Capital), directly causing the shutdown days later. This is also a candidate flagged
for deeper research: Quixey previously appeared only as a brief, unsourced one-of-five segment in
package 012 (The Pivot Graveyard), marked "[VERIFY exact figures and pivot sequence]" — this package
resolves that flag with a fully sourced, deeper treatment under a completely different angle (the
term-sheet veto mechanism, not the consumer-to-enterprise pivot 012 focused on).

FOUNDING — precisely sourced
- Quixey was founded November 1, 2009, in Mountain View, California, by Tomer Kagan and Liron
  Shapira. The company built a "functional" search engine for mobile apps — letting users search by
  what an app does rather than requiring them to already know its name.
Source: Wikipedia ("Quixey"); VentureBeat.

FUNDING HISTORY — [VERIFY] total figure varies by source
- Seed: $400,000, led by Eric Schmidt's Innovation Endeavors, with Archimedes Ventures participating.
- Series A (August 2011): $3.8 million, led by U.S. Venture Partners (USVP) and WI Harper Group, with
  Webb Investment Network and Innovation Endeavors also participating.
- Series B (June 2012): $20 million.
- Series C (2013): approximately $50 million, from USVP, GGV Capital, Innovation Endeavors, WI Harper
  Group, Atlantic Bridge, Translink Capital, and Alibaba — Alibaba's first investment in the company.
- 2015 round (announced March 6, 2015): $60 million, from Goldman Sachs, GGV Capital, Alibaba, and
  SoftBank, at a reported valuation of approximately $600 million — the company's peak valuation.
- *** [VERIFY]: total lifetime equity funding is reported as "$173.8 million" by one TechCrunch
  citation and as "$135 million" by aggregator data (Tracxn) — treat $173.8M as the primary figure
  (press-sourced) but flag the discrepancy if a firmer reconciled total is needed on screen. ***
Source: TechCrunch (Aug 4, 2016, "Quixey debt funding"); Wikipedia; jcount.com (2015 round coverage);
Tracxn company profile (aggregator, for the lower total-funding figure).

THE 2016 WARNING SIGNS — precisely sourced
- February 2016: several Quixey executives, reportedly including the COO and CTO, left the company as
  it missed revenue targets.
- April 8, 2016: co-founder and CEO Tomer Kagan was replaced as CEO by Mark Lazar, a serial/turnaround
  CEO brought in from outside. Kagan remained on Quixey's board of directors. *** Framing note: Kagan
  was no longer CEO by the time of the fatal veto incident described below (Feb 2017) — but he
  remained a board member throughout, a less clean separation than this channel's other
  founder-distinction packages (e.g., 030/MoviePass's Spikes, who left the company entirely). State
  this precisely: Kagan was off the CEO role, not off the company, at the time of the veto. ***
- November 2016: Mark Lazar was fired as CEO after roughly seven months; John Foster, formerly CEO of
  Zed USA, was named permanent CEO — Quixey's third CEO within about a year.
Source: Bloomberg (Apr 8, 2016, "Quixey Appoints Lazar as CEO, Co-Founder Kagan to Stay Involved");
Recode (Apr 8, 2016, "Mobile Startup Quixey Names New CEO Amid Sales Struggle, Leadership Churn");
PR Newswire (Nov 2016, "Quixey Names John Foster... as Permanent CEO"); TechCrunch (Apr 11, 2016,
"Mobile search company Quixey swaps CEOs").

THE TOXIC TERM SHEET — the core mechanism, precisely sourced
- August 4, 2016: Quixey announced $30 million in new debt financing. Reporting citing the company's
  own SEC filing (via TechCrunch/CNBC) confirmed the funding but did not disclose detailed terms
  publicly at the time.
- Per later reporting (Global Venturing, and secondary accounts of Axios's Dec 2017 "Behind the fall
  of Quixey" investigation): the August 2016 financing package from Alibaba actually combined a $10
  million payment with $30 million of debt — roughly $40 million total from Alibaba in this round.
  The debt carried two critical terms: an 18-month repayment maturity, AND a veto right giving
  Alibaba the ability to block any future equity financing round Quixey tried to raise. *** This
  veto right is the exact mechanism this title's pattern describes — not a vague "toxic terms scared
  investors away" signal, but a literal, contractual veto over other investors' money. ***
Source: Global Venturing (Mar 30, 2017, "Quixey closure reportedly due to Alibaba debt deal");
TechCrunch (Aug 4, 2016); CNBC (Aug 4, 2016, "Start-up slowdown leads Quixey to $30 million debt
round"); Axios (Dec 15, 2017, "Behind the fall of Quixey" — cited via secondary summaries in this
research pass; the original Axios article could not be directly accessed during this pass and should
be re-verified/re-read in full before final recording if possible).

THE VETO THAT KILLED THE COMPANY — precisely sourced
- November 2016: Quixey began raising a new round of capital to keep the company alive.
- By February 2017: existing investors, including Atlantic Bridge Capital, had agreed to provide $10
  million in rescue financing.
- Alibaba exercised its contractual veto right from the August 2016 debt deal and overruled/blocked
  the agreed rescue round. *** This is the direct causal event: a specific contract term, used by one
  investor, to block money that other investors had already agreed to provide. ***
- This forced Quixey to lay off staff and begin searching for a buyer instead of completing the
  rescue round.
Source: Global Venturing (Mar 30, 2017); The Information (Feb 2017, "Alibaba-Backed Quixey Shutting
Down"); Hacker News discussion thread citing the same reporting (Feb 2017).

THE SHUTDOWN — precisely sourced
- February 2017: Quixey shut down. The company laid off approximately 50 employees and permanently
  closed its Mountain View, California office (at 303 Bryant Street / Castro Street reporting varies
  slightly on the exact address — use "Mountain View" as the primary location detail).
- Alibaba's official public statement on the closure: "Unfortunately, the development of the company
  did not meet expectations and the board made a decision to end the business."
- *** [VERIFY]: one source describes Alibaba's total committed investment across its relationship
  with Quixey as "about $110 million" — this figure has not been fully reconciled against the $60M
  2015 round (which included but was not exclusively Alibaba) plus the ~$40M August 2016 package; use
  with the caveat that it's an aggregate, not confirmed as Alibaba's sole/exclusive contribution. ***
Source: Mountain View Voice (Mar 14, 2017, "Start-up Quixey closes its Mountain View office"); The
Information (Feb 2017); Wikipedia.
