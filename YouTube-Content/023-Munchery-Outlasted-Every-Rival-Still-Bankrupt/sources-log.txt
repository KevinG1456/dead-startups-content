FOUNDING

- Founded 2010 (idea stage), launched publicly May 2011, San Francisco.
- Founders: Tri Tran (CEO) and Conrad Chu (a former colleague from GetActive/Convio).
- Tri Tran: MIT-educated (BS/MEng, EECS), previously Director of Engineering at
  Convio/GetActive. His personal background as a Vietnamese refugee is covered in
  depth in a Bloomberg feature ("How a Vietnamese Refugee Is Rethinking Food
  Delivery in America," Jan 2016) and an Inc. profile — usable human-interest
  material for a founder-portrait beat, not core to the financial narrative.
- Pitch: gourmet, chef-prepared meals with a rotating daily menu, delivered same-day
  — explicitly positioned against restaurant-marketplace delivery (the model
  DoorDash/Grubhub use) and against meal-kit companies (Blue Apron).
Source: Wikipedia, VatorNews, Inc.com, Bloomberg.

FUNDING — mostly reconciled, one disputed figure flagged

- Seed: $210K (2012, private investors).
- Series A: $4M (September 2013, Menlo Ventures, Sherpa Ventures).
- Series B: $28M (April 2014, led by Sherpa Ventures; e.ventures and Menlo
  participating).
- Major round: $85M, announced May 2015, led by Menlo Ventures and Sherpa Ventures,
  with NorthGate Capital, 137 Ventures, Mousse Partners, e.ventures, and Greycroft
  participating. Valued Munchery at approximately $300M. Brought cumulative funding
  to $117.2M at that point. [This round is labeled inconsistently across sources —
  "Series C" per TechCrunch, "Series D" per other aggregators. Present as "a major
  2015 round" rather than asserting one specific series letter as fact.]
- 2017 down-round/recap attempt: reported effort to raise $15M via a convertible
  note capped at an $80M valuation — roughly a quarter of the 2015 peak.
  *** DISPUTED: Munchery publicly pushed back on this characterization at the time,
  stating existing investors could all participate and that there was no
  valuation/note cap as described. Present BOTH the reported terms AND Munchery's
  own denial — do not state the $80M cap as uncontested fact. ***
- Total lifetime funding: reported inconsistently across sources — "$100M+,"
  "$117.2M," "$120M+," "$124M across 5 rounds/16 investors," and "$125M" all
  appear. Use "over $120M, commonly cited around $125M" rather than one precise
  figure.
Source: TechCrunch (funding-round coverage), Crunchbase-style aggregation, the 2017
TechCrunch piece specifically covering the disputed down-round ("It sounds like
Munchery's early investors are screwed," March 2017).

BUSINESS MODEL

- Vertically integrated: large industrial/commissary kitchens, in-house "Resident
  Chefs" plus outside "Chef Partners," and Munchery's own delivery drivers —
  structurally similar to Sprig's owned-kitchen model (already covered in this
  channel's package 001), a useful direct comparison point for the script.
- Peak markets: San Francisco, New York, Los Angeles, and Seattle.
- Reportedly served 5,000+ meals a day in a single city at peak.
- Peak headcount: not confirmed in this research pass — do not state a specific
  number on screen without further sourcing.
Source: Bloomberg, TechCrunch, general contemporaneous coverage.

THE FINANCIAL DISTRESS

- Bloomberg reported Munchery losing up to $5M a month, wasting approximately 16%
  of the food it prepared, and routinely pressuring cooks to cut ingredient costs —
  this reporting clustered around November 2016, alongside the CEO change below.
- November 2016: co-founder/CEO Tri Tran steps down, moving to Chief Strategy
  Officer (he later departed the company entirely). James Beriker is installed as
  President/CEO.
- January 2017: approximately 30% of headquarters staff laid off, described in
  coverage as following "founder departures" — this happened one week after a
  competitor (Zesty)'s own layoffs. This is a real, dateable downsizing event
  distinct from the final 2019 shutdown — do not conflate the two.
- May 2018: Munchery shuts down its Los Angeles, New York, and Seattle operations
  entirely, retreating to San Francisco only — a full year of retrenchment before
  the final collapse.
Source: Bloomberg, Mattermark, TechCrunch.

---

THE SHUTDOWN — precisely dated

- January 21, 2019: Munchery abruptly ceases operations, notifying customers by
  email the same day. Employees were reportedly left in the dark about the timing;
  vendors were reportedly NOT notified at all.
- 257 employees laid off.
- Damning, script-worthy detail: Munchery continued actively marketing gift cards
  to customers in December 2018 — weeks before the shutdown.
- February 28, 2019: Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing. In the filing, CEO James Beriker
  cited "increased competition, over-funding, aggressive expansion efforts, and
  Blue Apron's failed IPO" as reasons for the collapse. THIS IS A RARE, ON-RECORD,
  COURT-FILED CAUSAL STATEMENT — stronger sourcing than most founder retrospective
  quotes, since it's part of an official bankruptcy filing, not a press interview.
- Debts: $28.5M in secured debt to lenders; $6M in unsecured debt, split roughly
  evenly between 230 vendors/suppliers and thousands of customers holding
  unfulfilled gift cards.
Source: TechCrunch, contemporaneous press coverage of the shutdown and bankruptcy
filing.

WARN ACT LAWSUIT
- A WARN Act class action was filed January 25/28, 2019, by former facilities
  worker Joshua Philips on behalf of approximately 250 employees, seeking 60 days'
  wages under the federal WARN Act and California Labor Code — Munchery gave no
  advance notice of the shutdown.
- The case reportedly settled in 2020, with final court approval around 2021. One
  source (warnlawyers.com) cites a $475,000 settlement figure — SINGLE-SOURCED,
  flag as such rather than stating it as confirmed fact.
Source: warnlawyers.com (single source for the settlement figure — corroborate
further before stating the exact dollar amount on screen with full confidence).

CATEGORY COMPARISON — Munchery outlasted both of this channel's other prepared-meal
casualties

Munchery is the last and most heavily-funded survivor of the vertically-integrated
"on-demand meal" category this channel has now covered three times:
- SpoonRocket ($13.5M raised) collapsed March 2016.
- Sprig ($56.7M raised, covered in this channel's package 001) collapsed May 2017.
- Munchery ($120M+ raised) held on until January 2019 — nearly three years after
  the category's first casualty (SpoonRocket) and about 20 months after Sprig.
This "outlasted everyone, still lost" framing is a strong, sourced angle distinct
from Sprig's story — more capital and more time did not solve the same underlying
structural problem (vertically-integrated meal production at thin margins).
Source: cross-referenced against this channel's own package 001 sources-log for the
Sprig/SpoonRocket dates.
