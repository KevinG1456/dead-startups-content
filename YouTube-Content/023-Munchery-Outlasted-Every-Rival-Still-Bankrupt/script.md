## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 13–16 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "Munchery outlasted every other funded startup in its category. It still went bankrupt."
- Visual: three company-silhouette icons in a row, two dim to grey with an X, the third — largest — fades last
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This is the third time this exact category has killed a funded startup. It's also the best-funded one."

### Hook (0:15–0:55)
- VO: "This is Munchery — and if you've watched this channel's video on Sprig, you already know this category. Two funded startups in the exact same business — chef-prepared, vertically-integrated meal delivery — already failed before Munchery did. Munchery raised more money than both of them combined, and lasted years longer. It still ended the same way."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand why more capital and more time didn't fix the underlying problem, what actually happened in Munchery's final weeks, and what its own bankruptcy filing said — on the record — about why it failed."

### Introduction (0:55–1:45)
- Method statement: reconstructed from Bloomberg, TechCrunch, and Munchery's own Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing — sourced on screen throughout
- One-sentence framing: this is the closing chapter of a category story this channel has now told three times — SpoonRocket, Sprig, and now Munchery

### Main Content — The Munchery Case (1:45–9:30)
- Segment 1 — The Category (1:45–3:00): quick recap of the pattern — SpoonRocket ($13.5M raised) collapsed March 2016. Sprig ($56.7M raised, this channel's package 001) collapsed May 2017. Munchery, founded 2011 by CEO Tri Tran and Conrad Chu, raised over $120M — more than both rivals combined — and didn't collapse until January 2019. Visual: three-company timeline graphic building.
- Segment 2 — The Model and the Peak (3:00–4:30): Munchery's model — owned commissary kitchens, in-house "Resident Chefs," its own delivery drivers — nearly identical in structure to Sprig's. A major 2015 raise ($85M) valued the company at roughly $300M; it operated in San Francisco, New York, Los Angeles, and Seattle at peak. Visual: funding/valuation graphic climbing, four-city map.
- Segment 3 — The Cracks (4:30–6:00): by late 2016, Bloomberg reported Munchery losing up to $5 million a month and wasting roughly 16% of the food it prepared. CEO Tri Tran steps down to a strategy role; James Beriker takes over as CEO. About 30% of headquarters staff are laid off in January 2017. On-screen lower third: "A reported 2017 valuation cap of $80M on new investment was publicly disputed by Munchery — presented here as reported, not settled." Visual: burn/waste graphic, leadership-change timeline marker.
- Segment 4 — The Retreat (6:00–7:00): May 2018, Munchery shuts down its Los Angeles, New York, and Seattle operations entirely, retreating to San Francisco only — a full year of contraction before the final collapse. Visual: four-city map narrowing to one.
- Segment 5 — The Shutdown (7:00–8:30): January 21, 2019, Munchery abruptly ceases all operations, notifying customers by email the same day. Vendors reportedly received no notice at all. The company had been actively marketing gift cards to customers as recently as the previous month. 257 employees are laid off. Visual: December gift-card marketing graphic, hard-cutting to the January shutdown headline.
- Segment 6 — The Filing (8:30–9:30): February 28, 2019, Munchery files for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. In the filing itself, CEO James Beriker names the causes on the record: "increased competition, over-funding, aggressive expansion efforts, and Blue Apron's failed IPO." $28.5M in secured debt, $6M in unsecured debt split between roughly 230 vendors and thousands of customers holding unfulfilled gift cards. Visual: the filing's stated-causes quote displayed as text, debt breakdown graphic.
- Mid-video CTA (~9:00): "If you've ever wondered whether more funding actually fixes a broken business model, the next part is the real answer. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Lesson — Capital Doesn't Fix Structure (9:30–11:30)
- Plain-language comparison: SpoonRocket, Sprig, and Munchery all shared the same core structural choice — owning production (kitchens, chefs, delivery) rather than operating as a marketplace — and all three eventually hit the same wall: thin or negative margins that got worse, not better, as growth slowed or competition intensified.
- The practical distinction: Munchery had more capital and more time than either rival, and used both — new leadership, market contraction, a fundraising attempt — to try to fix the underlying model. None of it worked, because the problem was structural, not a matter of execution or runway.
- Visual: three-company comparison graphic — funding raised, years survived, cause of death — landing on the shared structural root cause

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — when a business model has a structural flaw, more capital and more time don't fix it, they just extend the timeline until the same ending arrives
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and if you haven't seen the Sprig video, that's the direct companion piece to this one"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you ever order from Munchery, Sprig, or SpoonRocket? Do you think any of the three could have survived with a different model? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: package 001 (Sprig) + most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Segment 1 (the category recap) if viewers who've already seen the Sprig video feel like it's repeating ground
- **Fix:** keep the category recap brief and treat it explicitly as setup for what's NEW here (Munchery specifically), not a re-telling of Sprig's story
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the four-city map detail in Segment 2 if retention data shows it's not adding curiosity
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the bankruptcy filing's rare on-record causal statement early (in the Hook) and pay it off fully in Segment 6
- **Recommended visual change:** keep the three-company comparison graphic (funding/years survived/cause) visible in a corner throughout the second half, building toward the final full reveal
