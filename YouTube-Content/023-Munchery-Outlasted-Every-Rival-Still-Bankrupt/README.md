# 023 — Munchery Outlasted Every Rival In Its Category. It Still Went Bankrupt.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Munchery, a real, extensively-reported case (Bloomberg, TechCrunch, contemporaneous bankruptcy-filing coverage — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). Munchery came up twice in this channel's prior research (checked and set aside during the 001/Sprig pass, briefly included as a shutdown letter in the 015 corpus) but was never deep-verified into its own package until now.

**The real case:** Munchery — a chef-prepared, same-day meal delivery startup founded in San Francisco by CEO Tri Tran and Conrad Chu, launched publicly May 2011 — raised over $120M (commonly cited around $125M) across multiple rounds, including an $85M raise in 2015 that valued it at roughly $300M. It operated on a vertically-integrated model similar to this channel's Sprig case (001): owned commissary kitchens, in-house chefs, its own delivery drivers. Munchery is the last and most heavily-funded survivor of a category this channel has now covered three times — SpoonRocket ($13.5M raised) collapsed March 2016, Sprig ($56.7M raised) collapsed May 2017, and Munchery held on until January 2019, nearly three years after the category's first casualty. More capital and more time did not solve the same underlying structural problem. By late 2016, Bloomberg reported Munchery losing up to $5M a month and wasting roughly 16% of the food it prepared. A leadership change, a 30%-of-headquarters layoff, and a full retreat from three of its four markets followed over the next 18 months. Munchery abruptly shut down on January 21, 2019, leaving vendors unnotified — despite having actively marketed gift cards to customers as recently as the previous month. It filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on February 28, 2019, and in that filing, CEO James Beriker named the causes on the record: "increased competition, over-funding, aggressive expansion efforts, and Blue Apron's failed IPO."

**Important honesty note:** the reported $80M valuation cap on a 2017 convertible note is disputed — Munchery publicly denied that characterization at the time, saying existing investors could all participate and no such cap existed as described. Present both the reported terms and the company's own denial, not the cap as settled fact. Total lifetime funding is reported inconsistently across sources ($100M+ to $125M) — use "over $120M, commonly cited around $125M." The WARN Act lawsuit's reported $475,000 settlement figure is single-sourced — flag accordingly. Do not state a specific peak headcount; it's not confirmed in available sourcing.

**Framing requirement:** lead with the "outlasted everyone, still lost" angle — Munchery is direct proof that more capital and more runway don't fix a structurally broken unit-economics model, they just extend the timeline. The bankruptcy filing's own stated causes are unusually strong, on-record sourcing (a court filing, not a press quote) and should anchor the "why" segment of the script.
