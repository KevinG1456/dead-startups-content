## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Munchery, the closing chapter of a category story we've now told three times.

SpoonRocket ($13.5M raised) collapsed in March 2016. Sprig ($56.7M raised, covered in this channel's own package 001) collapsed in May 2017. Munchery — founded in San Francisco in 2011 by CEO Tri Tran and Conrad Chu — raised over $120 million, more than both rivals combined, and hit a roughly $300 million valuation on an $85 million round in 2015. It didn't collapse until January 2019, nearly three years after the category's first casualty.

By late 2016, Bloomberg reported Munchery losing up to $5 million a month and wasting roughly 16% of the food it prepared. A leadership change, a 30%-of-headquarters layoff, and a full retreat from three of its four markets followed over the next eighteen months. Munchery abruptly shut down on January 21, 2019 — despite having actively marketed gift cards to customers as recently as the previous month. Vendors reportedly received no notice at all. On February 28, 2019, the company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, and in that filing, CEO James Beriker named the causes on the record: "increased competition, over-funding, aggressive expansion efforts, and Blue Apron's failed IPO."

Munchery's real lesson: more capital and more time don't fix a structurally broken business model — they just extend the timeline until the same ending arrives.
