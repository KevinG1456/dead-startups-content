## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week is a mechanism explainer: what actually happens to a startup's three core assets — its code/IP, its customer data, and its customer relationships — once it shuts down or gets sold off.

Most people assume "the company" is one indivisible thing that simply disappears. It isn't. Code and IP can end up with an entirely different company than the one that built it — as happened with ride-share startup Sidecar, whose technology and assets were acquired by General Motors after its 2015 shutdown, with a license to its patent portfolio (not an outright sale) as part of the deal, closing two weeks after GM's $500M investment in Lyft. Customer data is legally treated as a company asset that can be sold in a bankruptcy proceeding — tested directly in RadioShack's landmark 2015 bankruptcy, where state attorneys general fought to block a customer-database sale that conflicted with the company's own privacy policy. Cambridge Analytica's 2018 bankruptcy shows a related but distinct mechanism: a separate FTC enforcement action, not a bankruptcy-court asset-sale review, ordered deletion of the improperly obtained data. And customer relationships themselves follow a spectrum from graceful (Parse's 2017 shutdown, with a year's notice and an open-sourced self-hosting migration path) to abrupt.

We map all three tracks using real, sourced examples, and close with a practical segment on what to check before trusting a startup with your data.
