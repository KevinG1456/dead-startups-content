## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "How much revenue from one customer is actually safe?"
- Visual: pie chart animates in, one massive slice dominating, a crack forms through it with a heavy SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This actually happened. And it happened fast."

### Hook (0:15–0:45)
- VO: "This company signed a major deal with one of the biggest companies in the world. That single relationship came to represent the overwhelming majority of its relevant business. Within about a year of the relationship breaking down, the company was bankrupt."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand exactly how a deal that looked like the biggest win in the company's history became the reason it collapsed."

### Introduction (0:45–1:30)
- Method statement: reconstructed from financial disclosures, press coverage, and court/bankruptcy filings — sourced on screen
- One-sentence framing: this isn't a story about a bad deal — it's a story about what a great deal can hide

### Story — The Deal (1:30–4:00)
- The company's situation before the deal, how the major customer relationship came together, the initial excitement and market reaction
- Visual: press headlines, stock/valuation reaction graphics, generic facility/manufacturing B-roll
- Curiosity gap: tease the specific term or condition in the relationship that made it so fragile without fully explaining it yet

### Main Content — The Autopsy (4:00–9:30)
- Segment 1: how dependent the company actually became — the concentration percentage, shown as a growing pie-chart slice over time
- Segment 2: the specific condition/clause/dynamic that made the relationship fragile (reveal the teased detail)
- Segment 3: what happened when the relationship broke down — the speed of the collapse from breakdown to bankruptcy
- Mid-video CTA (~6:30): "If one customer makes up a big chunk of your revenue right now, the framework in this next part is exactly what you need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- Graphics: pie-chart timeline, bankruptcy-timeline graphic

### Story — The Fall (9:30–11:30)
- The bankruptcy filing, what happened to employees and the facility, the broader fallout
- Slow the pacing deliberately here

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — evaluate customer concentration explicitly as a risk factor, even (especially) when the deal looks like a huge win
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "What's a safe concentration percentage, in your opinion? Comment below."
- End screen: two related autopsies + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the completed pie-chart-and-bankruptcy-timeline graphic, then rewinds to explain the deal and the specific fragile condition. Measured, graphics-forward.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the human moment — an employee's account of the day the relationship broke down — before any numbers appear. Slower pacing, cinematic.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "3 warning signs this deal had from the start," counting down using the same facts. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

---

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Main Content section (4:00–9:30) if the concentration-percentage explanation becomes overly technical/financial
- **Fix:** ground the percentage in a concrete, relatable comparison (e.g., "imagine if X% of your paycheck came from one client") before adding financial detail
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the Deal section if press coverage of the initial announcement is thin
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** move the "fragile condition" tease earlier if early-retention data shows drop-off before 2:00
- **Recommended visual change:** show the pie-chart graphic partially in the cold open, fully revealed only at the end
