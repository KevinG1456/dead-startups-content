Sources Log — Package 003: Garmin/TomTom (Google Maps Navigation) / Picturelife (free cloud storage)
Filled in: 2026-08-13

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
This title was originally a "platform risk, told 5 ways" structural framework with 5
unverified candidate segments (cloud photo backup, web analytics, cloud storage, maps/
navigation, email tools), each marked [VERIFY]. Research checked all 5 categories
against real, dated, causally-direct sourcing. Only 1 of 5 (maps/navigation) turned up
ironclad, multi-sourced direct causation — and it's a story about public hardware
companies (Garmin, TomTom), not funded startups. A second category (cloud photo
backup) turned up a real funded-startup casualty (Picturelife), but the causal story
is multi-platform (Google + Apple + Amazon collectively), not Google-specific. The
other three categories (web analytics, cloud storage, email tools) had NO defensible
named casualty in reliable press and are dropped entirely. This package now ships as
a 2-segment video instead of the originally planned 5, each segment's fit compromise
disclosed explicitly on screen rather than glossed over.

---

SEGMENT 1: GARMIN / TOMTOM — the single clean, ironclad case

THE EVENT
- October 28, 2009: Google announced free, built-in turn-by-turn navigation for Android
  phones (Google Maps Navigation), eliminating the need for a standalone GPS device or
  a paid navigation app for most consumers.
- Same day: TomTom's stock fell approximately 20-21%. Garmin's stock fell approximately
  16%, wiping out roughly $1.2 billion of Garmin's market capitalization in a single
  trading session.
Source: Fast Company, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Gizmodo (all covered the announcement and
same-day stock reaction contemporaneously).

THE FIT PROBLEM — disclose explicitly, do not gloss over
- Garmin and TomTom are publicly-traded hardware companies, not VC-funded startups —
  this does not match the channel's usual "funded software company" subject. This
  package deliberately includes it anyway because it is the single cleanest, most
  quantifiable, most directly-caused real-world example of "a giant platform launches
  a free version and a market gets crushed the same day" — the literal mechanism the
  original title promised — even though the company type is an exception to format.
  Say so on screen, don't present it as if it were a funded-startup story.

CANDIDATE CHECKED AND REJECTED: Dash Navigation
- A VC-funded navigation-device startup ($41M raised from Kleiner Perkins, Sequoia)
  that would have been a much better niche fit than Garmin/TomTom.
- REJECTED — wrong timeline: RIM acquired Dash Navigation in June 2009, four months
  BEFORE Google's October 2009 free-navigation announcement. Its layoffs and decline
  predate the event entirely and cannot be attributed to it.

SEGMENT 2: PICTURELIFE — the real funded startup, multi-platform causation

COMPANY / FOUNDER
- Picturelife — cloud photo backup and organization app.
- Founder: Charles Forman (also founder of OMGPOP).
- Funding: $4.6M raised. Investors: Spark Capital, SV Angel, Chris Dixon, Founder
  Collective, High Line Venture Partners, plus 7+ additional investors.
Source: Crunchbase-style funding reporting, contemporaneous tech press.

TIMELINE
- Acquired by Streamnation in early 2015.
- Shut down August 22, 2016.
Source: Imaging Resource, Techmeme (contemporaneous shutdown coverage).

THE CAUSAL STORY — multi-platform, not Google-specific
- The company's own stated reason for shutting down, per Imaging Resource/Techmeme
  coverage: "a challenging economic environment and a decreasing quality of service" —
  vague corporate language, not a specific causal attribution to any one platform.
- A secondary retrospective source (Sunset.hq — NOT primary journalism, could not be
  directly fetched/verified in full during this research pass) suggests Picturelife
  struggled to convert free users into paying customers while Google, Apple, AND
  Amazon all simultaneously offered free or cheap cloud photo/file storage.
  [FLAG: this secondary source could not be independently verified in this research
  pass — treat its specific framing as a plausible but not fully confirmed account of
  the mechanism. The funding, acquisition, and shutdown facts above ARE independently
  confirmed; the precise causal narrative is the weaker-sourced part.]
- IMPORTANT: do not say "Google killed Picturelife." The accurate claim is that
  Picturelife couldn't survive in a market where cloud storage became free across
  multiple major platforms simultaneously — Google was one of at least three, not the
  sole or even clearly primary cause.

CANDIDATES CHECKED AND REJECTED (across all 5 original categories)

Cloud photo backup:
- Loom.com (photo/video backup app, unrelated to the current screen-recording Loom) —
  REJECTED, wrong timeline: acquired by Dropbox April 2014 (had raised only $1.3M,
  Google Ventures among its investors), migrated/shut down into Carousel by May 2014 —
  over a year BEFORE Google Photos' free-unlimited launch (May 2015). Cannot be caused
  by an event that hadn't happened yet.
- Everpix — REJECTED, wrong timeline: shut down 2013, also predates Google Photos 2015.

Web analytics vs. Google Analytics going free (2005):
- ClickTracks — REJECTED: merged with J.L. Halsey/Lyris in 2006 and continued as a
  rebranded product (Lyris HQ) for years afterward. Not a Google-caused death.
- No other named, funded analytics startup with a direct, sourced causal link to GA's
  free launch was found — only general "the analytics market dried up" commentary,
  never one company's documented story. Category dropped entirely.

Cloud file storage vs. Google Drive's free-tier moves:
- Bitcasa — REJECTED: its confirmed cause of death (TechCrunch, Data Center Knowledge)
  was self-inflicted unit economics — its own "unlimited" plan was being abused (one
  user stored 82TB, costing Bitcasa $3-4k/month for that single account) — explicitly
  NOT attributed to Google Drive or the 2014 cloud-storage price war by any source
  found. Same mistake pattern as package 008's rejected Socialcam candidate.
- The real 2014 "cloud storage price war" (Google cut prices ~80% in March 2014,
  alongside Amazon/Microsoft/Dropbox) is well documented as an industry-wide event, but
  no specific startup casualty tied primarily to Google's move specifically (rather
  than the multi-competitor collapse generally) was found. Category dropped entirely.

Email productivity tools vs. Gmail shipping features natively:
- Mailbox (Dropbox-owned) — checked: shut down Feb 2016, timing roughly coincides with
  Gmail/Inbox shipping a snooze feature, but Dropbox's and the Mailbox team's own
  stated reason was a strategic refocus toward Dropbox Paper. No source ties the
  shutdown to Gmail specifically. Category dropped entirely — flagged from the start
  as the vaguest of the 5 original candidates, and that held up.
