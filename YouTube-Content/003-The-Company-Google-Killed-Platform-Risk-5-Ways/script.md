## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 12–15 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "On October 28th, 2009, Google made one announcement. By the end of that trading day, one company had lost $1.2 billion in market value."
- Visual: date stamp "OCTOBER 28, 2009" animates in, a stock-ticker line graph crashing downward, hard SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This actually happened, in one afternoon. The second story in this video took years."

### Hook (0:15–0:50)
- VO: "This is what platform risk actually costs — not as a vague warning, but as two real numbers. One company lost over a billion dollars in a single day. Another one took years to lose the same fight, against three giants instead of one."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand the fast version of platform risk and the slow version — and why one of today's two stories is a deliberate exception to how this channel usually works."

### Introduction (0:50–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from contemporaneous financial and tech press (Fast Company, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Gizmodo, Imaging Resource, Techmeme) — sourced on screen throughout
- One-sentence framing: this video originally planned five case studies; real sourcing only supported two — that's disclosed here up front, not hidden

### Case One — Garmin/TomTom: The Fast Version (1:40–6:00)
- Segment 1 — The Industry (1:40–2:40): standalone GPS navigation devices were a real, profitable hardware category through the 2000s — Garmin and TomTom were its two biggest names, both publicly traded, both built substantially around dedicated hardware you bought once. Visual: product-era B-roll, industry revenue graphic climbing.
- Segment 2 — The Announcement (2:40–4:00): October 28, 2009 — Google announces Google Maps Navigation, free, built into Android phones, offering turn-by-turn spoken directions that matched or beat what a $200+ dedicated device did. On-screen lower third: "Garmin and TomTom are public hardware companies — a disclosed exception to this channel's usual funded-startup format." Visual: announcement headline, "FREE" stamp.
- Segment 3 — The Same Day (4:00–5:30): the market reacted immediately — TomTom's stock fell roughly 20-21%, Garmin's fell roughly 16%, wiping out about $1.2 billion of Garmin's market cap in that single trading session. Visual: side-by-side stock charts crashing, $1.2B counter ticking down.
- Segment 4 — What It Meant (5:30–6:00): this wasn't a slow decline — an entire hardware category's core value proposition (dedicated navigation) evaporated in the time it took the market to process one announcement. Visual: closing graphic, "ONE AFTERNOON" stamp.

### Case Two — Picturelife: The Slow Version (6:00–11:00)
- Segment 1 — The Startup (6:00–7:30): Picturelife, a cloud photo backup and organization app founded by OMGPOP's Charles Forman, raised $4.6 million from Spark Capital, SV Angel, Chris Dixon, Founder Collective, and others. It built a real product, acquired by Streamnation in early 2015. Visual: funding graphic, product-era screenshots (illustrative, no real UI rendered by AI).
- Segment 2 — Free, Everywhere, At Once (7:30–9:00): starting around 2015, cloud photo and file storage went free or nearly free simultaneously across Google Photos (free unlimited storage from May 2015), Apple's iCloud tiers, and Amazon's storage offerings. On-screen lower third: "This is a multi-platform story — Google, Apple, and Amazon all mattered here, not Google alone." Visual: three-giant "FREE" graphic converging on Picturelife.
- Segment 3 — The Shutdown (9:00–10:00): August 22, 2016, Picturelife shuts down. Its own stated reason, per contemporaneous coverage: "a challenging economic environment and a decreasing quality of service" — corporate language for a company that couldn't convert free users to paying customers in a market where the free alternative had three different giants behind it. Visual: shutdown headline, timeline closing.
- Segment 4 — The Honest Complication (10:00–11:00): unlike Garmin and TomTom's same-day, unambiguous cause, Picturelife's death is genuinely multi-factor — this video does not claim Google alone killed it, and any video (including this one) that oversimplifies that would be doing exactly what this channel says it won't do. Visual: "MULTI-PLATFORM CAUSE" honesty graphic.
- Mid-video CTA (~9:30): "If you're building a product that depends on charging for something a bigger platform could give away, the next part is exactly what you need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Lesson — Fast vs. Slow (11:00–13:00)
- Plain-language comparison: platform risk isn't one speed. Garmin and TomTom show what it looks like when a platform's decision reprices an entire category in hours — the market doesn't wait for you to adapt. Picturelife shows what it looks like when several platforms independently decide the same thing over a longer window — no single moment to point to, just a slow erosion of the reason a paying customer would choose you.
- The practical distinction for builders: the fast version is at least legible after the fact — you can point to the exact day. The slow version is harder to fight precisely because there's no single villain and no single moment — by the time it's obvious, the market has already moved.
- Visual: the two-speed comparison graphic, building beat by beat

### Final CTA + Outro (13:00–14:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — platform risk has a fast price and a slow price, and both are real, even when — especially when — no single platform intended either outcome
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you ever own a dedicated GPS device, or remember Picturelife? Which version of platform risk worries you more as a builder — the fast one or the slow one? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: Title 008 (Meerkat/Viddy, the companion "platform dependency" pairing) + most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** viewers questioning why public companies (Garmin/TomTom) are in a startup-death video, right at the start of Case One
- **Fix:** the on-screen disclosure lower third in Segment 2 of Case One addresses this immediately rather than letting it fester as an unaddressed objection
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the "Industry" B-roll in Segment 1 of Case One if the cold open already established enough context
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "5 planned, only 2 survived sourcing" honesty angle early (already done in the Introduction) to set up the video as unusually rigorous rather than padded
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small "FAST / SLOW" tag visible in a corner graphic throughout both cases so viewers always know which speed of platform risk they're watching
