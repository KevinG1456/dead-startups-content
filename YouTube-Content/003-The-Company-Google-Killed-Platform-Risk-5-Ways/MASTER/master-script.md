# Master Script — Garmin/TomTom + Picturelife

Narrative logic and beat structure, upstream of any language. Translate the IDEA of each beat, not
this document's English sentences. All facts come from `master-research.md`.

**Target length:** 12–15 minutes across all languages.

---

## Story Logic (the throughline every language must preserve)
Platform risk isn't one thing — it has a fast version and a slow version. Garmin and TomTom show
the fast version: a single Google announcement erased $1.2B in market value in one trading
session. Picturelife shows the slow version: a real funded startup that couldn't survive several
platforms (not just Google) independently making the same product free over a couple of years.
The video is explicit about its own limitations — only 2 of an originally-planned 5 segments
survived real sourcing, and that's disclosed, not hidden.

## Beat-by-Beat Structure

### 1. Cold Open (~0:00–0:15)
Beat: state the fast case's number immediately.
Facts: Oct 28 2009, Google's announcement, ~$1.2B Garmin market-cap loss same day.

### 2. Hook (~0:15–0:50)
Beat: frame the video's actual thesis — platform risk has a fast price and a slow price — using
both cases as proof.

### 3. Introduction (~0:50–1:40)
Beat: method statement (Fast Company, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Gizmodo, Imaging Resource, Techmeme).
One-sentence framing: this video originally planned 5 segments; only 2 survived real sourcing —
disclosed up front, not hidden.

### 4. Segment — Garmin/TomTom: The Industry (~1:40–2:40)
Facts: standalone GPS devices, a real profitable hardware category through the 2000s.

### 5. Segment — The Announcement (~2:40–4:00)
Facts: Oct 28 2009, Google Maps Navigation announced free on Android.
REQUIRED: on-screen disclosure that Garmin/TomTom are public hardware companies, a deliberate
exception to the channel's format.

### 6. Segment — The Same Day (~4:00–5:30)
Facts: TomTom -20-21%, Garmin -16%, ~$1.2B wiped off Garmin's market cap, same trading session.

### 7. Segment — What It Meant (~5:30–6:00)
Beat: an entire category's value proposition evaporated in the time it took markets to process
one announcement.

### 8. Segment — Picturelife: The Startup (~6:00–7:30)
Facts: founder Charles Forman (OMGPOP), $4.6M raised, acquired by Streamnation early 2015.

### 9. Segment — Free, Everywhere, At Once (~7:30–9:00)
Facts: 2015 onward, Google Photos/Apple iCloud/Amazon all offering free or cheap storage roughly
simultaneously.
REQUIRED: on-screen disclosure that this is multi-platform, not Google alone.

### 10. Segment — The Shutdown (~9:00–10:00)
Facts: Aug 22 2016 shutdown; company's own vague stated reason.

### 11. Segment — The Honest Complication (~10:00–11:00)
Beat: explicitly state that this video does not claim Google alone killed Picturelife — the
honest story is multi-platform and partly single-sourced.

### 12. Mid-Video CTA (~9:30, inside Segments 9-10)
Beat: direct address tied to "if you're building on a chargeable feature."

### 13. Segment — Fast vs. Slow — The Actual Lesson (~11:00–13:00)
Beat: comparison graphic, both cases side by side, landing on the shared "platform risk has two
speeds" conclusion.

### 14. Final CTA + Outro (~13:00–14:30)
Beat: recap the one-sentence lesson, subscribe CTA, engagement question, end screen (reference
Title 008 — Meerkat/Viddy — as the companion platform-dependency piece).

## Retention Notes (apply per language)
- Biggest drop-off risk: viewers questioning why public companies (Garmin/TomTom) appear in a
  startup-death video — the disclosure in Segment 5 must land clearly in every language, right at
  the start of that segment, not buried.
- Keep a small "FAST / SLOW" tag visible in a corner graphic throughout both cases in every
  language's edit.
