# Master Research — Garmin/TomTom + Picturelife

## This Is a TWO-SEGMENT Package — Keep Both Fit-Compromises Explicit in Every Language

### Segment 1: Garmin/TomTom — the fast, ironclad case
- **Event:** October 28, 2009, Google announced free, built-in turn-by-turn navigation for
  Android (Google Maps Navigation).
- **Same-day market reaction:** TomTom's stock fell roughly 20-21%; Garmin's fell roughly 16%,
  wiping out approximately $1.2 billion of Garmin's market capitalization in a single trading
  session.
- **REQUIRED DISCLOSURE, every language, every time this segment is introduced:** Garmin and
  TomTom are publicly-traded HARDWARE companies, NOT VC-funded startups — a deliberate, disclosed
  exception to this channel's usual funded-startup format. State this plainly on screen; do not
  let it read as if Garmin/TomTom were startups.

### Segment 2: Picturelife — the real funded startup, multi-platform causation
- **Company:** Picturelife, cloud photo backup/organization app.
- **Founder:** Charles Forman (also founder of OMGPOP).
- **Funding:** $4.6M raised (Spark Capital, SV Angel, Chris Dixon, Founder Collective, High Line
  Venture Partners, plus 7+ additional investors).
- **Timeline:** acquired by Streamnation in early 2015; shut down August 22, 2016.
- **Company's own stated reason:** "a challenging economic environment and a decreasing quality
  of service" (vague, not a specific attribution).
- **REQUIRED FRAMING, every language:** do NOT say "Google killed Picturelife." The accurate
  claim is multi-platform — Google, Apple, AND Amazon all offered free/cheap cloud storage around
  the same time. The specific "three giants" causal narrative traces to a secondary, not-fully-
  verified source (Sunset.hq) — present this detail as reported, not as fully independently
  confirmed.

## Candidates Checked and Explicitly Rejected (context only, not part of the video's content —
useful for localizers to understand why only 2 segments exist, not 5)
- Loom.com and Everpix: shut down before Google Photos' 2015 free-unlimited launch — wrong
  timeline, cannot be caused by an event that hadn't happened yet.
- ClickTracks: merged into Lyris in 2006, continued for years — not a Google-caused death.
- Bitcasa: died from its own unlimited-plan abuse, not Google Drive pricing.
- Dash Navigation: the funded-startup candidate for the maps segment — acquired by RIM in June
  2009, four months BEFORE Google's October 2009 announcement — wrong timeline.
- Mailbox: shut down for Dropbox's own stated strategic reasons, not tied to Gmail.

## The One-Sentence Lesson (must survive localization intact in meaning)
Platform risk has a fast price and a slow price — Garmin and TomTom show what it costs in a
single afternoon; Picturelife shows what it costs over years, with several platforms moving in
the same direction at once rather than one clean villain.
