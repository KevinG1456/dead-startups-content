## Introduction (0:50–1:40)
Method statement: reconstructed from contemporaneous financial and tech press (Fast Company,

One-sentence framing: this video originally planned five case studies; real sourcing only
supported two — that's disclosed here up front, not hidden.

## Garmin/TomTom: The Fast Version (1:40–6:00)
- **The Industry (1:40–2:40):** standalone GPS navigation devices were a real, profitable
  hardware category through the 2000s — Garmin and TomTom were its two biggest names, both
  publicly traded.
- **The Announcement (2:40–4:00):** October 28, 2009 — Google announces Google Maps Navigation,
  free, built into Android phones. On-screen lower third: "Garmin and TomTom are public hardware
  companies — a disclosed exception to this channel's usual funded-startup format."
- **The Same Day (4:00–5:30):** TomTom's stock fell roughly 20-21%, Garmin's fell roughly 16%,
  wiping out about $1.2 billion of Garmin's market cap in that single trading session.
- **What It Meant (5:30–6:00):** an entire hardware category's core value proposition evaporated
  in the time it took the market to process one announcement.

## Picturelife: The Slow Version (6:00–11:00)
- **The Startup (6:00–7:30):** Picturelife, a cloud photo backup and organization app founded by
  OMGPOP's Charles Forman, raised $4.6 million from Spark Capital, SV Angel, Chris Dixon,
  Founder Collective, and others. Acquired by Streamnation in early 2015.
- **Free, Everywhere, At Once (7:30–9:00):** starting around 2015, cloud photo and file storage
  went free or nearly free simultaneously across Google Photos, Apple's iCloud, and Amazon. On-
  screen lower third: "This is a multi-platform story — Google, Apple, and Amazon all mattered
  here, not Google alone."
- **The Shutdown (9:00–10:00):** August 22, 2016, Picturelife shuts down. Its own stated reason:
  "a challenging economic environment and a decreasing quality of service."
- **The Honest Complication (10:00–11:00):** unlike Garmin and TomTom's same-day, unambiguous
  cause, Picturelife's death is genuinely multi-factor — this video does not claim Google alone
  killed it.
- Mid-video CTA (~9:30): "If you're building a product that depends on charging for something a
  bigger platform could give away, the next part is exactly what you need. Subscribe for a new
  autopsy every week."

## Fast vs. Slow — The Actual Lesson (11:00–13:00)
Plain-language comparison: Garmin and TomTom show what it looks like when a platform's decision
reprices an entire category in hours. Picturelife shows what it looks like when several platforms
independently decide the same thing over a longer window — no single moment, just a slow erosion.
