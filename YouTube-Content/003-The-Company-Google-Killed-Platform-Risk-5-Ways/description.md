## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup and market outcomes using primary sources. This week: two real cases of platform risk, told at two different speeds.

On October 28, 2009, Google announced Google Maps Navigation — free, built into Android, matching what a $200+ dedicated GPS device did. The market reacted the same day: TomTom's stock fell roughly 20-21%, Garmin's fell roughly 16%, and Garmin lost about $1.2 billion in market value in a single trading session. Garmin and TomTom are public hardware companies, not funded startups — we say so directly on screen, because this channel is usually about the latter, and we're not pretending otherwise.

Picturelife, a real funded startup ($4.6 million raised, founded by OMGPOP's Charles Forman), tells the slower version. Starting around 2015, cloud photo storage went free or nearly free across Google Photos, Apple's iCloud, and Amazon at roughly the same time. Picturelife shut down August 22, 2016. We don't claim Google alone killed it — the honest story is multi-platform, and we say that on screen too.
