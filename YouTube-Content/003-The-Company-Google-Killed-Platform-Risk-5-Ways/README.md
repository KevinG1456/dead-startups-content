# 003 — Google Erased $1.2 Billion In One Afternoon. A Startup Died Slower — To Google and Two Others.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around two real cases (Fast Company, Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Gizmodo, Imaging Resource, Techmeme — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). The original "platform risk, told 5 ways" framework could not survive real sourcing: of the 5 candidate categories, 3 (web analytics, cloud storage, email tools) had no defensible named casualty in reliable press at all, and are dropped. This package now ships as 2 segments instead of 5, each with its own honestly-disclosed fit compromise, mirroring how package 008 shipped 2 fully-verified cases instead of a forced larger number.

**The real cases:** **Garmin/TomTom** — on October 28, 2009, Google announced free, built-in turn-by-turn navigation for Android, and the same trading day TomTom's stock fell roughly 20-21% and Garmin's fell roughly 16%, wiping out about $1.2 billion of Garmin's market cap in an afternoon. This is the single cleanest, most quantifiable, most directly-caused example of "a giant platform launches a free version and a market gets crushed" anywhere in this research — but Garmin and TomTom are public hardware companies, not VC-funded startups, which is a deliberate, disclosed exception to this channel's usual format. **Picturelife** — a real funded startup ($4.6M raised, led by OMGPOP founder Charles Forman), shut down August 22, 2016, in a market where cloud photo storage had gone free simultaneously across Google, Apple, and Amazon. This is a real funded-startup casualty of the free-storage era, but the causal story is multi-platform, not Google-specific — do not claim "Google killed Picturelife."

**Important honesty note:** this package required checking 5 different candidate categories and rejecting most of them. Loom.com and Everpix (photo backup) predate Google Photos' 2015 free-unlimited launch and can't be caused by it. ClickTracks (web analytics) merged into Lyris and continued for years — not a Google-caused death. Bitcasa (cloud storage) died from its own unlimited-plan unit economics, not Google Drive pricing. Dash Navigation (the funded-startup candidate for the maps segment) was acquired by RIM four months before Google's 2009 announcement — wrong timeline. Mailbox (email) was shut down for Dropbox's own strategic reasons, not tied to Gmail. See `sources-log.txt` for the full rejection log. Do not resurrect any of these without materially better sourcing than this pass found.

**Framing requirement:** lead each segment with its real, sourced number — Garmin's ~$1.2B single-day market-cap loss, and Picturelife's $4.6M raised / real shutdown date — and state each segment's fit compromise on screen rather than smoothing it over. The video's honesty about its own limitations (only 2 of 5 planned segments survived scrutiny) is itself part of what makes this channel's "reconstructed from real numbers" promise credible.

- **Viewer objections:** "Garmin and TomTom aren't startups, why are they in a startup-death video" — address directly and immediately, on screen, as a disclosed exception; "isn't Picturelife's death really about Apple and Amazon too, not just Google" — yes, and the video says so explicitly rather than hiding it

- **Expected comments:** people who remember standalone GPS units or early cloud photo apps, debate over whether the Garmin/TomTom segment "counts" for a startup channel

## 3. Competitor Analysis
