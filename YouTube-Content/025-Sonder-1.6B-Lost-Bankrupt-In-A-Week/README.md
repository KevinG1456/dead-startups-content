# 025 — Sonder Raised $550M+, Went Public on Nasdaq, and Was Bankrupt Within a Week of Marriott Walking Away

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Sonder (Wikipedia, Commercial Observer, Marriott's own press release and bankruptcy-court filing, Hotel Dive, CoStar, CNBC, Skift, Bloomberg Law — full citations in `sources-log.txt`), with Lyric and Zeus Living as companion segments in the same category. This is the "good next candidate" lead flagged in `Come Back Later.txt` and referenced directly in package 021's own README ("comparisons to Lyric/Zeus Living/Sonder in the same category"). Same structure this channel used for package 023 (Munchery/Sprig/SpoonRocket): three companies, one category, anchored on whichever raised the most and lasted longest.

**The real case:** Sonder — founded 2014 in Montreal by Francis Davidson, Lucas Pellan, and Martin Picard, later based in San Francisco — leased and managed its own short-term rental units directly (apartments, later hotel rooms), reaching 9,000+ units in 40+ cities. It raised more than $550 million in private venture funding, then did something almost nothing else on this channel has done: went public, merging with a SPAC (Gores Metropoulos II) to list on Nasdaq in January 2022. It never turned a profit in over a decade — combined net losses of $240M in 2023-2024 alone pushed its cumulative deficit to $1.6 billion by the end of 2024, even with strong 2024 occupancy (80.9%) and healthy rates ($196/night average). In August 2024 it signed what looked like a legitimacy win: a licensing deal putting 10,500+ Sonder rooms on Marriott Bonvoy. Fifteen months later, that same deal ended it. Marriott terminated the agreement November 7, 2025, citing default; Sonder abruptly shut down operations November 10; it filed Chapter 7 liquidation (not reorganization) on November 14, in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware. Marriott's own court filing alleges Sonder threatened to lock guests out of their rooms — "without regard to whether those rooms contained medication, passports, personal effects" — unless Marriott funded the wind-down; Sonder's own public explanation was softer, citing "unanticipated delays and costs" from the Marriott integration itself. Both accounts are presented, attributed, in the script.

**The companion arc:** Lyric ($185M raised, Airbnb-led round) died almost instantly when COVID hit — 20% layoffs Feb 2020, ~100 more in March, down to a skeleton crew by July 2020 — but its pricing-software arm spun out and survives today as Wheelhouse, and its brand was bought and relaunched by an unrelated firm (Black Swan Holdings) in 2023. Zeus Living ($150M raised, also Airbnb-backed) survived the COVID shock that killed Lyric, then died slowly — landlord payments fell behind for months, workforce cuts in 2020 and again in 2022, a quiet November 2023 hand-off of its whole book of business to competitor Blueground. All three ran some version of the same guaranteed-rent-to-landlords master-lease model as package 021 (Stay Alfred) — cross-referenced explicitly on screen both directions.

**Framing requirement:** Lead with the irony, not the bankruptcy filing. Sonder did everything this niche's audience is told de-risks a startup — raised the most capital in its category, survived the longest (over a decade), even reached a public listing — and still collapsed faster and harder than any of its less-funded peers once one partner walked away. The core thesis: going public didn't fix the master-lease math that killed Stay Alfred in 13 days back in 2020; it just gave the same structural flaw more time and a bigger stage to fail on.

## 5. Improvement Suggestions
- Attribute every Marriott-court-filing claim explicitly on screen ("according to Marriott's court filing") and always pair with Sonder's own softer public explanation — do not present only the more dramatic side
- Use November 7 (termination) and November 9 (public announcement) as two distinct, correctly-labeled dates — don't collapse them
- Present Sonder's "total lifetime funding" as "$550M+ in private funding before going public," not one single all-inclusive number — aggregators disagree by over $250M
- Keep Lyric's ending honest and un-flattened: it's a twist (software arm survives as Wheelhouse, brand later revived by Black Swan Holdings), not a clean death
- Include analyst Daniel Lesser's counterpoint ("it's not that Marriott blew this up") for balance against a simplistic "Marriott killed Sonder" framing
