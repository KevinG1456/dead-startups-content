## LinkedIn
"A real, underused case study in partnership-dependency risk — and in why a public listing doesn't
fix a broken cost structure:

Sonder raised more than $550 million in private funding before going public on Nasdaq via SPAC
merger in January 2022 — the only company in its category to reach that milestone. By the end of
2024, it carried a $1.6 billion cumulative deficit, despite strong 80.9% occupancy and healthy
$196/night rates. The problem wasn't demand. It was a master-lease structure with an average of
6.8 years remaining on fixed obligations.

In August 2024, Sonder signed a licensing deal putting 10,500+ rooms on Marriott Bonvoy — widely
covered as a legitimacy win. Marriott terminated that agreement on November 7, 2025. Sonder filed
for Chapter 7 liquidation on November 14.

According to Marriott's own court filing, Sonder threatened to lock guests out of their rooms
unless Marriott financed its wind-down. Sonder's own public account was softer, citing
'unanticipated delays and costs' from the integration itself. I present both, attributed, in the
full breakdown — along with the two smaller companies (Lyric, Zeus Living) that ran the same
guaranteed-rent model and this channel's own prior case study on Stay Alfred, which hit the same
wall in thirteen days back in 2020.
