## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "How do you raise $25 million without shipping a product?"
- Visual: large "$25M" figure grows, tiny phone/app icon appears beside it with a near-zero usage counter, hard cut with a heavy SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This actually happened. And it took years to unravel."

### Hook (0:15–0:45)
- VO: "This is Clinkle — a mobile payments app that raised a reported $25 million seed round in 2013, before it had a real, working product. It would take roughly two years of delays before anything actually launched — and almost nobody ever used it."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand exactly how this round happened, and the red flags that were visible the whole time."

### Introduction (0:45–1:30)
- Method statement: reconstructed from contemporaneous press coverage, funding records, and later reporting — sourced on screen
- One-sentence framing: this is as much a diligence-failure story as it is a product-failure story

### Story — Rise (1:30–4:00)
- Founding story, the pitch, the fundraise, the initial press coverage and hype
- Visual: press clippings, funding announcement headlines, generic pitch-deck-style graphics
- Curiosity gap: tease the specific internal/cultural issue without fully explaining it yet

### Main Content — The Autopsy (4:00–9:30)
- Segment 1: the pitch and the round — what was promised, what convinced investors, at what valuation/terms
- Segment 2: the delays — a visual timeline of announced vs. actual launch dates over roughly two years
- Segment 3: what was happening internally — reported culture/turnover issues, and how the gap between hype and substance widened
- Mid-video CTA (~6:30): "If you're evaluating a deal right now — or pitching one — the red flags in this next part are worth paying attention to. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- Graphics: delay timeline, red-flags checklist building progressively

### Story — The Fall (9:30–11:30)
- The eventual limited launch, the layoffs, the company's quiet wind-down
- Human element: what it was like for employees who joined believing in the vision `[VERIFY: sourced accounts only]`
- Slow the pacing deliberately here

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — a great pitch is not a substitute for a shipped product, and diligence should weight demonstrated execution heavily
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Do you remember Clinkle from when this was happening? Tell me what you recall in the comments."
- End screen: two related autopsies + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the completed delay-timeline graphic, then rewinds to explain the round and the diligence gaps. Measured, graphics-forward.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the human moment — an employee's account of the internal atmosphere during the delays — before any numbers appear. Slower pacing, cinematic.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "5 red flags that were there from day one," counting down using the same facts. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

---

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Segment 2 (delay timeline) if it becomes a flat date-by-date recitation
- **Fix:** pair each delay milestone with a specific, concrete detail (a press quote, a specific promised feature) rather than just dates
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the Rise section if press coverage of the initial pitch/hype phase is thin
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** move the internal-culture tease earlier if early-retention data shows drop-off before 2:00
- **Recommended visual change:** show the delay-timeline graphic partially in the cold open, fully revealed only at the end
