# 009 — Clinkle: $25M for an App Almost Nobody Ever Saw

**Package status:** Production-ready. Clinkle is extensively documented in contemporaneous tech press (TechCrunch, The Verge, San Francisco Chronicle, Valleywag/Gawker-era coverage) as one of the most-mocked hype-vs-substance cases in Silicon Valley history. Core facts below are widely reported; precise figures marked `[VERIFY]` should be confirmed against primary sources before recording.

**Known anchor facts to build from:** Clinkle, a mobile payments startup founded by then-Stanford student Lucas Duplan, raised a reported $25 million seed round in mid-2013 — reportedly the largest seed round in Silicon Valley history at the time, structured as convertible debt so Duplan could avoid giving investors a board seat, and backed by high-profile investors. The product launch was delayed repeatedly over roughly two years, drawing intense press scrutiny and mockery for the gap between funding/hype and actual shipped product. Internal reporting described significant workplace culture problems and high turnover. The company cut roughly a quarter of its staff in December 2013, then lost most of its remaining team through a leadership-crisis wave of departures in May 2015, before quietly winding down by 2016, and the product never achieved meaningful public adoption.
