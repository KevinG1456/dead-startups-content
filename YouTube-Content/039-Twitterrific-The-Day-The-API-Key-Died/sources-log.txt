THE ICONFACTORY — FOUNDING
- The Iconfactory was founded in April 1996 in Greensboro, North Carolina, by Corey Marion, Talos
  Tsui, and Gedeon Maheux — originally an icon and graphic design studio. Lead engineer Craig
  Hockenberry joined in 1997; the company formally incorporated in January 2000.
Source: Wikipedia ("The Iconfactory"); Golden.

TWITTERRIFIC — THE FIRST TWITTER CLIENT, AND THE ORIGIN OF "TWEET"
- Twitterrific was created by Craig Hockenberry at The Iconfactory; the prototype was built essentially
  overnight, with the idea for the app coming together around December 20, 2006. It launched publicly
  January 15, 2007.
- Twitterrific was the first Twitter desktop client for the Mac, and among the earliest applications
  available in Apple's App Store; it later won an Apple Design Award.
- Twitter's own developer, Blaine Cook, suggested Hockenberry use the word "tweet" instead of "twit"
  for referring to posts inside the app — Twitterrific adopted the change immediately, and the term
  "tweet" subsequently entered common usage and the dictionary in connection with the platform.
- In January 2007, Iconfactory designer David Lanham created a cheerful bluebird icon — nicknamed
  Ollie — to represent Twitterrific. Twitter itself later adopted a bluebird as its own official
  platform logo.
Source: Iconfactory's own blog ("Twitterrific: End of an Era," Jan 19, 2023); Wikipedia
("Twitterrific"); furbo.org ("The Origin of Tweet," Craig Hockenberry's own account); TechCrunch (Jan
24, 2023, retrospective on third-party apps' role in shaping Twitter).

THE SHUTDOWN — JANUARY 2023, PRECISELY DATED
- January 12-13, 2023: without warning, Twitter (under new owner Elon Musk) revoked API access for
  third-party client apps, including Twitterrific and the separate app Tweetbot (made by a different
  company, Tapbots). App functionality broke instantly and completely.
- Twitter/Musk remained silent on the cause for several days; a brief, vague policy explanation was
  not issued until January 17, 2023 — roughly five days after the cutoff.
- January 19, 2023: The Iconfactory published its own blog post, "Twitterrific: End of an Era,"
  formally announcing the app's discontinuation, stating plainly: "the app's sudden and undignified
  demise is due to an unannounced and undocumented policy change by an increasingly capricious
  Twitter." The post noted the company had no advance warning and no ability to notify its own
  customers before the app broke.
- Twitterrific was removed from both the iOS and macOS App Stores. The app had run continuously for
  fifteen years, from January 2007 to January 2023.
- The Iconfactory's own words on what the app's customers had meant to the company: "Your financial
  support may have paid the bills, but your spiritual support enriched our souls."
Source: Iconfactory's own blog (Jan 19, 2023); 9to5Mac (Jan 13 and Jan 20, 2023); TechCrunch (Jan 19,
2023, "Twitterrific, Tweetbot pulled from App Store after Twitter API changes"); iClarified (Jan 2023).

THE COMPANION CASE — TWEETBOT, A DIFFERENT COMPANY, SAME DAY
- Tweetbot, a separate and unrelated third-party Twitter client, was made by Tapbots LLC and had
  launched April 14, 2011 (iPhone), with iPad support added February 2012 and Mac support October
  2012 — a nearly 12-year run.
- Tapbots was hit by the exact same January 12, 2023 API cutoff, with the same lack of warning or
  communication from Twitter. Tapbots' own public statement: "We've been proud to serve you over the
  last 12+ years, but due to circumstances beyond our control, we have to shut down Tweetbot."
- Unlike Iconfactory, Tapbots pivoted its engineering effort into a new app, Ivory, built for the
  Mastodon social network, stating: "We have great plans to make Ivory better than Tweetbot ever
  could be."
Source: 9to5Mac (Jan 20, 2023, "Tapbots shuts down Tweetbot, teases Ivory for Mastodon"); Shacknews
(Jan 2023); Tapbots' own website (tapbots.com/tweetbot).
