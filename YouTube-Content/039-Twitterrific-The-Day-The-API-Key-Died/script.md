# Script Package

Anchored on Twitterrific/Iconfactory — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT
RULE FOR THIS SCRIPT: be explicit that The Iconfactory is a bootstrapped, independent software studio,
not a venture-funded startup — flag this honestly rather than implying otherwise. Present the "tweet"/
bluebird origin as real, well-documented cultural history, not a formal or legal claim. No fraud or
misconduct framing toward Twitter/X or Elon Musk — this is a platform-power/dependency-risk story,
told through sourced facts. Keep Tweetbot/Tapbots as a brief companion beat, not the primary anchor.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 10–13 minutes (Twitterrific's origin, its cultural significance, the shutdown, and
the lesson all covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "This app invented the word 'tweet.' This app made the bluebird icon Twitter itself later
  adopted. Twitter still killed it — with zero warning, in hours."
- Visual: cheerful bluebird silhouette icon, crack forming across it, red "ACCESS REVOKED" stamp
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "There was no call. No email. Just a
  broken app."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Twitterrific — built by an independent software studio called The Iconfactory, not a
  venture-funded startup, just a small, real team that had been making software since 1996. In
  December 2006, lead engineer Craig Hockenberry had an idea and built it almost overnight. It became
  the first-ever Twitter client — before Twitter had its own official app."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll know the real, documented origin of the word 'tweet'
  and the bluebird icon — and you'll see exactly what happened, hour by hour, when the platform they
  helped build cut them off completely."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from The Iconfactory's own blog, Wikipedia, TechCrunch, and 9to5Mac
  — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "This isn't a story about fraud or misconduct — Twitter,
  and its owner at the time, made a real business decision with real consequences. This video is about
  what that decision actually did to one specific, real company."

### Main Content — The Origin (1:40–5:00)
- **Segment 1 — The Idea (1:40–2:20):** December 20, 2006, the idea for Twitterrific comes together;
  Craig Hockenberry has a working prototype on his Mac essentially the next day. Visual: generic
  late-night coding graphic.
- **Segment 2 — The Launch (2:20–3:00):** January 15, 2007, Twitterrific launches — the first Twitter
  desktop client for the Mac, among the earliest apps in Apple's App Store, later winning an Apple
  Design Award. Visual: "FIRST TWITTER CLIENT EVER — JANUARY 2007" title card.
- **Segment 3 — The Word "Tweet" (3:00–4:00):** Twitter's own developer, Blaine Cook, suggested
  Hockenberry use the word "tweet" instead of "twit" for posts inside the app. Twitterrific adopted it
  immediately — and the term spread from there into common usage and the dictionary. Visual: "TWEET" —
  attributed origin card.
- **Segment 4 — The Bluebird (4:00–5:00):** in January 2007, Iconfactory designer David Lanham created
  a cheerful bluebird mascot, nicknamed Ollie, for Twitterrific. Twitter itself later adopted a
  bluebird as its own official platform logo. On-screen framing card: "This is real, documented
  cultural history — not a formal trademark claim." Visual: generic bluebird silhouette icon reveal.

### Main Content — The Shutdown (5:00–8:00)
- **Segment 5 — Fifteen Years (5:00–5:30):** Twitterrific runs continuously from January 2007 to
  January 2023 — fifteen years. Visual: "15 YEARS" title card.
- **Segment 6 — The Cutoff (5:30–6:30):** January 12-13, 2023, without warning, Twitter — under new
  owner Elon Musk — revokes API access for third-party client apps. Twitterrific breaks instantly and
  completely. Visual: "API ACCESS REVOKED — JANUARY 12-13, 2023" title card.
- **Segment 7 — The Silence (6:30–7:15):** Twitter stays silent for five days. A brief, vague
  explanation doesn't come until January 17, 2023. Visual: "5 DAYS OF SILENCE" countdown-style card.
- **Segment 8 — The Farewell (7:15–8:00):** January 19, 2023, Iconfactory publishes its own blog post
  announcing Twitterrific's discontinuation: "the app's sudden and undignified demise is due to an
  unannounced and undocumented policy change by an increasingly capricious Twitter." Its final words to
  users: "Your financial support may have paid the bills, but your spiritual support enriched our
  souls." Visual: attributed quote cards, held on screen.

### Main Content — The Companion Case (8:00–9:00)
- **Segment 9 — Tweetbot, Same Day (8:00–9:00):** a separate, unrelated company, Tapbots, lost its own
  Twitter client, Tweetbot — running since April 2011, nearly 12 years — to the exact same cutoff, the
  exact same day. Tapbots' own statement: "due to circumstances beyond our control, we have to shut
  down Tweetbot." Unlike Iconfactory, Tapbots redirected its engineering effort into a new app, Ivory,
  for the Mastodon network. Visual: "TWEETBOT — SAME DAY, DIFFERENT COMPANY" title card.
- Mid-video CTA (~8:45): "This channel has covered platform dependency before — package 008, on
  Meerkat. This is a different era, a different mechanism, and arguably the most ironic version of
  this risk yet. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Lesson (9:00–10:15)
- Plain-language comparison: Twitterrific didn't just build a product on Twitter's platform — it
  helped build the platform's own culture, vocabulary, and visual identity. None of that history
  mattered when the platform decided to cut off third-party access. The switch worked exactly the same
  way for everyone.
- The core lesson, stated directly: no amount of history, loyalty, or cultural contribution protects a
  company whose entire product depends on access it doesn't own or control.
- Visual: full-arc timeline graphic — idea (Dec 2006) → launch (Jan 2007) → "tweet" adopted (2007) →
  bluebird created (Jan 2007) → 15 years running → API cutoff (Jan 12-13, 2023) → silence (5 days) →
  farewell post (Jan 19, 2023).

### Final CTA + Outro (10:15–11:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — building your entire company on a platform you don't control
  means the platform always has the final say, no matter what you've contributed to it
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and if you want the other platform-dependency
  story on this channel, check out package 008 on Meerkat"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you ever use Twitterrific or Tweetbot? And did you know the word 'tweet' had
  a documented origin story before this video? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: package 008 (Meerkat/Viddy) + most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the full-arc timeline graphic and the "15 years / 5 days of silence / 1 policy
change" numbers, then works through the origin story and the shutdown as supporting detail. Measured,
graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine case study in platform-dependency risk.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on Craig Hockenberry's December 2006 late night, the idea coming together — before cutting hard
to January 13, 2023, the app breaking with no warning — then filling in the fifteen years in between.
Slower pacing, holds the "tweet"/bluebird origin reveal as a mid-video surprise rather than an opening
hook. Strongest emotional-irony arc of the three versions.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "one company, hour by hour" leading into rapid-fire beats: idea, launch, "tweet" adopted,
bluebird created, 15 years, API cutoff, silence, farewell post, Tweetbot companion beat — landing on
the platform-dependency lesson as the final, clarifying beat. Strong Shorts-extraction potential,
especially the "tweet" origin reveal and the cracked-bluebird visual.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the origin-story segment (1:40–5:00) if it feels like a detour before the "real"
  story (the shutdown) begins
- **Fix:** frame the origin story explicitly as building toward the irony payoff — remind viewers early
  that this history is what makes the ending land differently than a typical API-shutdown story
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general 2007-era Twitter-culture context if audience familiarity
  is already high
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "tweet" word origin even more explicitly in the cold
  open if early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 3 (3:00)
- **Recommended visual change:** keep the bluebird icon visible in a small corner graphic throughout
  the entire video, cracking further at each escalating beat, so it reaches full fracture exactly at
  the API-cutoff reveal
