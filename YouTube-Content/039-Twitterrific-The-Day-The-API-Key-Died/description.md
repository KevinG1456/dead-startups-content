## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup and software-company outcomes using primary
sources. This week is a little different: Twitterrific, built not by a venture-funded startup but by
an independent software studio, The Iconfactory — and one of the most ironic platform-dependency
stories this channel has covered.

In December 2006, Iconfactory lead engineer Craig Hockenberry had an idea and built a working
prototype almost overnight. Twitterrific launched January 15, 2007 — the first-ever Twitter desktop
client, before Twitter had its own official app. It later won an Apple Design Award.

Twitter's own developer, Blaine Cook, suggested Hockenberry use the word "tweet" instead of "twit" for
posts inside the app. Twitterrific adopted the change immediately, and the term spread into common
usage and the dictionary. That same month, Iconfactory designer David Lanham created a cheerful
bluebird mascot for the app, nicknamed Ollie — and Twitter itself later adopted a bluebird as its own
official platform logo.

Twitterrific ran continuously for fifteen years. Then, on January 12-13, 2023, without any warning,
Twitter — under new owner Elon Musk — revoked API access for third-party client apps. Twitterrific
broke instantly and completely. Twitter stayed silent for five days before offering a vague
explanation. On January 19, 2023, Iconfactory published its own farewell post, writing that the app's
"sudden and undignified demise is due to an unannounced and undocumented policy change by an
increasingly capricious Twitter" — closing with: "Your financial support may have paid the bills, but
your spiritual support enriched our souls."

The exact same cutoff hit a completely separate, unrelated company the same day: Tapbots, maker of
Tweetbot, which had run since April 2011. Tapbots' own statement: "due to circumstances beyond our
control, we have to shut down Tweetbot." Unlike Iconfactory, Tapbots redirected its work into a new
app, Ivory, for the Mastodon network.

This channel has covered platform dependency before — package 008, on Meerkat. This is a different
era, a different mechanism, and arguably the most ironic version of this risk yet: a company that
helped build the platform's own culture, cut off exactly like everyone else.
