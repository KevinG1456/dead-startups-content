## LinkedIn
"A real, underused case study in fixed-cost business model risk:

Stay Alfred, a Spokane, WA short-term-rental startup, raised $62M and hit roughly $100M in revenue by 2019. Its model was master-lease arbitrage — guaranteeing landlords fixed rent regardless of occupancy, while re-letting units as short-term rentals. That meant Stay Alfred itself absorbed 100% of the demand risk.

When COVID collapsed travel in March 2020, that structure turned a temporary shock into an immediate one. The company temporarily closed, then on May 7, 2020 publicly announced a plan to reopen. Thirteen days later, on May 20, founder Jordan Allen reversed course entirely: 'amazing to think that a $100 million company in Spokane, Washington, could be wiped out by a virus from Wuhan, China.' He later called the master-lease structure itself a 'foundational crack.'

I broke down the full timeline and the specific mechanism that made this collapse so fast.
