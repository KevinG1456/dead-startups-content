## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Stay Alfred, a short-term-rental management startup founded in Spokane, WA around 2011-2012 by CEO Jordan Allen, a former U.S. Army Ranger Captain, alongside co-founder Conrad Manfred (the name "Alfred" is a portmanteau of both founders' surnames).

Stay Alfred raised $62 million total — a $15 million Series A and a $47 million Series B led by Nine Four Ventures in October 2018 — and by FY2019 had grown to roughly $100 million in revenue, confirmed independently by CB Insights and Bisnow, operating roughly 2,000-2,500 apartments across about 30 cities.

Its business model was master-lease arbitrage: Stay Alfred signed fixed leases with landlords, guaranteeing them rent regardless of occupancy, while re-letting the units as short-term rentals. That meant Stay Alfred itself absorbed 100% of the demand risk. When COVID collapsed travel in March 2020, a rescue funding round of up to $30 million fell through at the last minute, and the company temporarily closed all properties on March 26. On May 7, 2020, it publicly announced a plan to reopen August 1. Just thirteen days later, on May 20, founder Jordan Allen reversed course entirely: "amazing to think that a $100 million company in Spokane, Washington, could be wiped out by a virus from Wuhan, China." He later called the master-lease structure itself a "foundational crack."
