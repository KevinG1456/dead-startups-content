## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 13–16 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "On May 7th, 2020, this company announced it was reopening. Thirteen days later, it was gone for good."
- Visual: "MAY 7, 2020 — REOPENING ANNOUNCED" text, hard cut to "MAY 20, 2020 — PERMANENTLY CLOSED," a countdown ticking between the two dates
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This is a real timeline. Thirteen real days."

### Hook (0:15–0:55)
- VO: "This is Stay Alfred — a real, funded, $100-million-a-year company out of Spokane, Washington. And unlike most 'COVID killed my startup' stories, this one has a specific, structural reason why the shock was instantly fatal instead of gradual."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand exactly how Stay Alfred's business model worked, why that model turned a temporary crisis into a permanent one, and what happened in the thirteen days between its reopening announcement and its final shutdown."

### Introduction (0:55–1:45)
- Method statement: reconstructed from contemporaneous regional and trade press (Spokesman-Review, Spokane Journal of Business, GeekWire, Bisnow, HousingWire) plus Jordan Allen's own on-record statements — sourced on screen throughout
- One-sentence framing: this isn't primarily a "COVID killed a startup" story — it's a story about a specific cost structure that made COVID uniquely fatal for this particular company

### Main Content — The Stay Alfred Case (1:45–9:30)
- Segment 1 — The Founder and the Build (1:45–3:15): Jordan Allen, a former U.S. Army Ranger Captain, co-founded Stay Alfred with Conrad Manfred (the name is a portmanteau of both founders' surnames) around 2011-2012, bootstrapped for years before raising outside capital starting around 2016. By 2018, the company had grown to roughly 2,000 apartments across 28 markets and 230 employees. Visual: founder photo-style graphic, growth timeline climbing.
- Segment 2 — The Raise (3:15–4:15): $15M Series A, followed by a $47M Series B in October 2018 led by Nine Four Ventures — $62M raised total. By FY2019, revenue reached roughly $100M, confirmed independently by multiple sources. Visual: funding-to-revenue timeline graphic.
- Segment 3 — The Model (4:15–6:30): explain the mechanism plainly — Stay Alfred signed master leases across roughly 2,000-2,500 apartments in about 30 cities, guaranteeing landlords fixed rent regardless of occupancy. Allen's own pitch to landlords framed this as risk-reducing for property owners — because Stay Alfred, not them, absorbed 100% of the demand risk. Visual: the "who bears the risk" diagram — landlord gets guaranteed rent at the top, Stay Alfred absorbs all occupancy risk underneath.
- Segment 4 — The Shock (6:30–8:00): March 2020, COVID collapses travel demand nationwide. Stay Alfred was simultaneously trying to close a rescue round of up to $30M — investors pulled out at the last minute. The company temporarily closed all properties March 26, 2020, and furloughed down from over 230 employees to a skeleton crew of roughly 50. Visual: demand graph cratering, funding-round graphic failing to close.
- Segment 5 — The Reversal (8:00–9:30): May 7, 2020, the company publicly announces a plan to reopen August 1 with a smaller footprint. Thirteen days later, on May 20, 2020, Jordan Allen reverses course entirely and announces permanent closure: "amazing to think that a $100 million company in Spokane, Washington, could be wiped out by a virus from Wuhan, China." Visual: the two dates, side by side, with the 13-day gap counted out visually.
- Mid-video CTA (~8:45): "If your business has any fixed obligation that doesn't flex with demand — a lease, a guarantee, a minimum commitment — the next part is exactly what you need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Founder's Own Words (9:30–11:00)
- Allen's own retrospective: he called the master-lease structure itself a "foundational crack," and said in hindsight he'd have leaned more toward an ownership-based model rather than concentrating so heavily on leases.
- A brief, honest complication: the Pennsylvania tenant-eviction dispute, where Stay Alfred tried to remove tenants sheltering in place during the shutdown, framed by the company as compliance with a state order, not predation — a messy edge case that shows the collapse wasn't clean or simple.
- Slow the pacing deliberately here — this is the reflective beat, not another data segment

### Final CTA + Outro (11:00–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — real revenue at real scale doesn't protect you from a shock if your core cost structure is fixed regardless of demand, and the businesses most exposed to this risk are often the ones whose growth story sounds the most impressive right up until the moment it doesn't
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you ever stay at a Stay Alfred property? Does your own business have any fixed obligation that doesn't flex with demand? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: two related autopsies (any other fixed-cost/COVID-collapse package) + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the completed funding-to-shutdown timeline graphic ($62M raised → $100M revenue → 13-day reversal → shutdown), then rewinds to explain the master-lease mechanism and the COVID shock in order. Measured, graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine business-risk resource for founders with fixed-cost or lease-heavy models.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the May 7 reopening announcement in full — the hope, the plan, the specific August 1 date — before revealing what happened thirteen days later. Slower pacing, more narrative-driven, builds outward from the reversal to the mechanism.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "3 signs your business model has this exact risk," each illustrated with a beat from Stay Alfred's story. Strong Shorts-extraction potential, especially the 13-day reversal beat alone.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the master-lease mechanism explainer (Segment 3) if it becomes an overly technical real-estate lecture before the COVID shock and reversal arrive
- **Fix:** keep the "who bears the risk" diagram simple and visual, get to the dated shock and reversal as quickly as possible
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general short-term-rental-industry background if audience-retention data shows strong baseline familiarity
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the 13-day reversal in the cold open (already done) and hold the full "what happened in between" reveal for Segment 5
- **Recommended visual change:** keep the "who bears the risk" diagram visible in a corner throughout Segments 4-5, so the mechanism stays concrete as the shock unfolds
