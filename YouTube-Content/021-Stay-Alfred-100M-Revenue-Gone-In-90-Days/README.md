# 021 — Stay Alfred Hit $100M a Year. It Announced Reopening. 13 Days Later, It Was Gone.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Stay Alfred, a real, extensively-reported case (Spokesman-Review, GeekWire, Bisnow, HousingWire, Spokane Journal of Business — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This title was first identified during the 001 (Sprig) sourcing pass and deliberately set aside as "a strong candidate for a separate, differently-scaled future package" — Stay Alfred's real revenue (~$100M) was roughly 10x too large for 001's "$10M ARR" framing. This is that package, and the first entirely new title added since the original 001-020 set.

**The real case:** Stay Alfred — a Spokane, WA short-term-rental management startup co-founded by CEO Jordan Allen (a former U.S. Army Ranger Captain) — raised $62M total ($15M Series A, $47M Series B led by Nine Four Ventures, Oct 2018) and hit roughly $100M in revenue in FY2019, confirmed independently by CB Insights and Bisnow. Its business model was master-lease arbitrage: it signed fixed-rent leases with landlords across roughly 2,000-2,500 apartments in about 30 cities, guaranteeing owners rent regardless of occupancy — meaning Stay Alfred itself absorbed 100% of the demand risk. When COVID collapsed travel in March 2020, that fixed-cost structure became fatal almost overnight. The company temporarily closed March 26, 2020, furloughed down to a skeleton crew, and on May 7, 2020 publicly announced a plan to reopen August 1. Just 13 days later, on May 20, 2020, Jordan Allen reversed course entirely and announced permanent closure, after a rescue financing round of up to $30M fell through at the last minute. His own words: "amazing to think that a $100 million company in Spokane, Washington, could be wiped out by a virus from Wuhan, China."

**Important honesty note:** Stay Alfred's peak scale (units/cities) is reported inconsistently across sources by a small margin depending on the exact snapshot date — present it as an approximate range ("roughly 2,000-2,500 units, ~30 cities"), not one precise figure. The exact month of the Series A ($15M) needs a final check before recording (Crunchbase's listing date and a contemporaneous PR mention differ by roughly a year) — the amount and year are solid regardless. The earlier "$47M vs. $62M" funding figures that appeared in shallower research are NOT actually in conflict: $15M + $47M = $62M cumulative total, confirmed directly by a contemporaneous headline.

**Framing requirement:** lead with the real reversal — the company publicly committed to reopening on May 7 and killed itself off 13 days later — rather than just stating the final shutdown date. That whiplash, combined with Allen's own "wiped out by a virus" quote, does more work than any invented framing could, and it's the clearest illustration of this channel's recurring thesis: real revenue at real scale does not equal resilience when the underlying cost structure is fixed regardless of demand.

## 2. Viewer Psychology
- **Curiosity triggers:** how a $100M-revenue company could disappear within about eight weeks; what actually happened in the 13 days between the reopening announcement and the permanent closure
- **Emotional triggers:** shock at the speed and scale, a specific whiplash reaction to the reversed reopening plan, respect for Allen's own direct "foundational crack" admission
- **Viewer objections:** "this is just a COVID story, not really a business-model story" — pre-empt directly by grounding the mechanism in the master-lease structure specifically, which is what made the COVID shock uniquely fatal for Stay Alfred compared to companies with more variable cost structures
- **Motivation to watch:** founders/operators with fixed-cost or lease-heavy models want a real diligence case; general audience drawn to the dramatic, fast collapse and the specific reversal beat
- **Expected comments:** people who stayed at a Stay Alfred property sharing their experience, debate over whether the master-lease model or COVID specifically was "really" to blame, comparisons to Lyric/Zeus Living/Sonder in the same category

## 3. Competitor Analysis
- **Similar creators:** Stay Alfred's shutdown got regional business-press coverage (Spokesman-Review, Spokane Journal of Business) at the time but almost no retrospective treatment since, and no video breakdown of the master-lease mechanism specifically
- **Content gap:** most COVID-era startup-death coverage treats the pandemic as a blanket explanation; nobody has isolated the specific structural mechanism (fixed lease obligations vs. variable revenue) that made this company's collapse uniquely fast and total
- **How to outperform:** build a clear "who bears the risk" diagram (landlord gets guaranteed rent, Stay Alfred bears 100% of occupancy risk) as the video's core educational asset, and use the real 13-day reversal timeline as the dramatic spine
