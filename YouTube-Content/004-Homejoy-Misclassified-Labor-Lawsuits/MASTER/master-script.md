# Master Script — Homejoy

This is the narrative logic and beat structure, upstream of any language. Each localized `EN/DE/
FR/NL/NO/SV/script.md` writes its own native dialogue from these beats — do not translate this
document's English phrasing sentence-by-sentence into other languages; translate the IDEA of each
beat, then write it as a native speaker would say it. Every fact referenced here comes from
`master-research.md` — do not introduce new facts at the localization stage, and do not invent
quotes (see `master-research.md`'s "No Verbatim Founder Quotes" note).

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes across all languages (natural pacing may vary a beat or two
minutes either way per language — do not force identical timestamps, see `master-editing-
timeline.md`).

---

## Story Logic (the throughline every language must preserve)

A fast-growing, well-funded marketplace startup shuts down — not from a single obvious cause, but
from two compounding weaknesses: a growth strategy subsidized by unsustainable first-time-customer
discounts with weak repeat-customer retention, and a legal classification risk (cleaners as
contractors vs. employees) that materialized into four lawsuits at exactly the moment the company
needed to close a new funding round. This is both a business-model story and a legal story, and
the video must present both honestly, resisting the temptation to oversell the lawsuits as the sole
cause. The distinct angle: Homejoy's 2015 collapse predates the Uber/Lyft/DoorDash classification
battles that followed, making it an early warning sign the industry didn't fully heed.

## Beat-by-Beat Structure

Chapter labels and timestamps below are the channel's established navigation markers for this
title (also used in every language's `description.md`/`subtitles.md`/YouTube chapters) — do not
rename or re-time them per language; only translate the chapter label text.

### 1. Cold Open — 00:00 (~0:00–0:15)
Beat: state the core contradiction immediately — a specific, small-sounding number (four lawsuits)
paired with a large financial scale (a well-funded, fast-growing company), same story.
Facts to hit: four lawsuits, fast-growing well-funded startup.
Visual: dollar figure and gavel icon animate in, crack line splits them apart, heavy SFX hit.
Tone: blunt, declarative, no preamble.

### 2. The Contradiction (Hook) — 00:15 (~0:15–0:45)
Beat: name the company, state what it was, state that it shut down, name the lawsuits as the
factor leadership cited.
Facts to hit: Homejoy, on-demand home cleaning marketplace, raised roughly $38-40M, grew fast, shut
down in 2015, leadership pointed to four lawsuits over cleaner classification.
Open loop to plant: by the end, the viewer will understand exactly how a legal classification
question became the difference between raising another round and shutting down.

### 3. How This Video Is Built (Sources) — 00:45 (~0:45–1:30)
Beat: state the method (reconstructed from court filings, press coverage, founder statements),
establish credibility, frame the two-sided nature of the story explicitly.
One-sentence framing: this is both a business-model story and a legal story, and both matter —
avoid oversimplifying to a single cause.

### 4. The Rise — 01:30 (~1:30–4:00)
Facts to hit: founding by Adora Cheung and Aaron Cheung, Y Combinator, the marketplace model, the
deep first-time-customer discount strategy that drove rapid growth.
Visual: press clippings, growth-chart animation, generic/recreated app screenshots.
Curiosity gap: tease the classification question without fully explaining it yet.

### 5. The Autopsy Begins — 04:00 (~4:00–9:30)
Three sub-beats within this single chapter block (do not split into separate chapter markers —
matches the established chapter structure):
- **Sub-beat A — The Retention Problem:** how much of the reported growth depended on heavily
  discounted first cleanings, and what the underlying repeat-customer economics looked like (weak
  conversion to full-price repeat customers). Present as a real, independent vulnerability — not a
  footnote to the legal story.
- **Sub-beat B — The Classification Question:** plain-language explanation of independent
  contractor vs. employee status, and why it mattered specifically for Homejoy's model (degree of
  control exercised over cleaners). Ground this in a concrete, relatable example rather than
  abstract legal language — biggest drop-off risk in the whole video if it becomes a dry legal
  lecture.
- **Sub-beat C — The Lawsuits and the Funding Consequence:** at least four lawsuits filed
  2014-2015 alleging misclassification; the lawsuits' reported effect on the ability to close a
  new funding round.
Visual: timeline showing founding → growth → first lawsuit → subsequent lawsuits → funding round
stalls → shutdown; plain-language contractor-vs-employee diagram for Sub-beat B.

### 6. Mid-Video: Why This Matters If You're Building Something — 06:30 (inside chapter 5)
Beat: direct address, brief tonal lift, subscribe prompt tied to relevance ("if you're building a
marketplace or gig-economy product right now, this is the part you need to hear").

### 7. The Fall — 09:30 (~9:30–11:30)
Facts to hit: the July 2015 shutdown, what happened to cleaners and customers, the broader gig-
economy context that followed (Uber/Lyft/DoorDash classification litigation came later).
Tone: slow the pacing deliberately here in every language — this is the reflective/human beat, not
another data segment.

### 8. The One-Sentence Lesson (Final CTA + Outro) — 11:30 (~11:30–12:30)
Beat: recap the one-sentence lesson (see `master-research.md`), subscribe CTA, engagement question
("where do you land on gig-economy worker classification?").

### 9. What's Next — 12:30 (~12:30–13:00)
Beat: end screen referencing two related autopsies + subscribe.

## Alternative Script Concepts (available for any language's alt-cut, not required per language)
- **Data-First / Analyst Style:** open directly on the completed founding→shutdown timeline
  graphic, then rewind to explain each marker, weighting the retention data and legal exposure
  equally.
- **Narrative / Documentary Style:** open on the human moment — a cleaner's perspective on the
  lawsuit, or a founder's account of the final months — before any numbers appear.
- **Fast-Cut / Listicle Style:** "3 warning signs Homejoy's model had from day one," counting down
  using the same facts. Strong Shorts source material.

## Retention Notes (apply per language, pacing may vary naturally)
- Biggest drop-off risk: the Classification Question segment becoming a dry legal lecture — keep
  it grounded in a concrete, relatable example.
- Move the "why couldn't they just raise again" tease earlier if early-retention data shows
  drop-off before 2:00 in a given language cut.
- Show the timeline graphic partially in the cold open, fully revealed only at the end.
