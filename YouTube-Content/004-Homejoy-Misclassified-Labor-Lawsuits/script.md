## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "How do four lawsuits end a fast-growing, well-funded startup?"
- Visual: dollar figure and gavel icon animate in, crack line splits them apart with a heavy SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This actually happened. Four times."

### Hook (0:15–0:45)
- VO: "This is Homejoy — an on-demand home cleaning marketplace that raised roughly $40 million and grew fast. In 2015, it shut down. Leadership pointed to four lawsuits over how it classified its cleaners."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand exactly how a legal question became the difference between raising another round and shutting down."

### Introduction (0:45–1:30)
- Method statement: reconstructed from court filings, press coverage, and founder statements — sourced on screen
- One-sentence framing: this is both a business-model story and a legal story, and both matter

### Story — Rise (1:30–4:00)
- Founding story, the marketplace model, the deep first-time-customer discount strategy that drove rapid growth
- Visual: press clippings, growth-chart animation, app screenshots (recreated/generic)
- Curiosity gap: tease the classification question without fully explaining it yet

### Main Content — The Autopsy (4:00–9:30)
- Segment 1: the retention problem — how much of the reported growth depended on heavily discounted first cleanings, and what the underlying repeat-customer economics looked like
- Segment 2: the classification question — plain-language explanation of independent contractor vs. employee status, and why it mattered specifically for Homejoy's model
- Segment 3: the lawsuits and their financial consequence — how the legal exposure affected the ability to close a new funding round
- Mid-video CTA (~6:30): "If you're building a marketplace or gig-economy product right now, the next part is the part you actually need to hear. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- Graphics: timeline showing founding → growth → first lawsuit → subsequent lawsuits → funding round stalls → shutdown

### Story — The Fall (9:30–11:30)
- The shutdown announcement, what happened to cleaners and customers, the broader gig-economy context that followed
- Slow the pacing deliberately here

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — legal classification risk is a real, structural business risk for marketplace models, not a footnote
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Where do you land on gig-economy worker classification? Comment below."
- End screen: two related autopsies + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the completed timeline graphic (founding → shutdown), then rewinds to explain each marker, weighting the retention data and legal exposure equally. Measured, graphics-forward.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the human moment — a cleaner's perspective on the lawsuit, or a founder's account of the final months — before any numbers appear. Slower pacing, cinematic.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "3 warning signs Homejoy's model had from day one," counting down using the same facts. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

---

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Segment 2 (classification explainer) if it becomes a dry legal lecture
- **Fix:** ground the legal explanation in a concrete, relatable example (a single cleaner's situation) rather than abstract legal language
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the Rise section if press coverage of the early growth phase is thin
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** move the "why couldn't they just raise again" tease earlier if early-retention data shows drop-off before 2:00
- **Recommended visual change:** show the timeline graphic partially in the cold open, fully revealed only at the end
