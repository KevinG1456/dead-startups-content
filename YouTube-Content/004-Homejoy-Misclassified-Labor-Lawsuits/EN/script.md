## The Rise (1:30–4:00)
Homejoy was founded by Adora Cheung and Aaron Cheung, went through Y Combinator, and built a
marketplace connecting customers with independent cleaners. Growth came fast — driven by deep
discounts for first-time customers that made the first cleaning almost free.
Visual: press clippings, growth-chart animation, generic app screenshots.
Curiosity gap: tease the classification question without fully explaining it yet.

## The Autopsy Begins (4:00–9:30)

**The Retention Problem:** behind the growth numbers, a large share of customers who tried Homejoy
at a steep discount never converted into regular, full-price repeat customers. That meant a real
chunk of the company's growth was subsidized by unsustainable discounting, not durable unit
economics — a vulnerability that existed independent of any lawsuit.

**The Classification Question:** Homejoy, like many marketplace and gig-economy companies,
classified its cleaners as independent contractors rather than employees. That classification
determines who's responsible for benefits, minimum wage guarantees, overtime, and expense
reimbursement. Starting in 2014, at least four lawsuits were filed alleging that classification was
wrong, given how much control Homejoy reportedly exercised over how its cleaners actually did their
work.

**The Lawsuits and the Funding Consequence:** Homejoy was reportedly trying to close a new funding
round around the same time. The lawsuits reportedly became a significant factor giving prospective
investors pause — and the round never closed.
Visual: timeline showing founding → growth → first lawsuit → subsequent lawsuits → funding round
stalls → shutdown.

## The Fall (9:30–11:30)
In July 2015, Homejoy shut down. The story predates much of the classification litigation that
later became prominent in cases involving Uber, Lyft, DoorDash, and other gig-economy companies —
Homejoy is often cited as an early, largely overlooked signal of a legal risk that would go on to
shape the entire industry.
Pacing: slow down deliberately here — this is the reflective beat, not another data segment.
