# 004 — Homejoy: How Misclassified Labor Lawsuits Ended a Rocket Ship

**Package status:** Production-ready. Homejoy is well-documented in press coverage (TechCrunch, Forbes, re/code-era reporting). Core facts stated below are widely reported; precise figures marked `[VERIFY]` should be confirmed against primary sources before recording.

**Known anchor facts to build from:** Founded by Adora Cheung and Aaron Cheung; on-demand home cleaning marketplace connecting customers with independent cleaners; went through Y Combinator; raised significant venture funding (reported figures around `[VERIFY: ~$38-40M]` total, investors including Google Ventures and others); grew quickly using deep first-time-customer discounts; faced at least four lawsuits alleging cleaners were misclassified as independent contractors rather than employees; shut down operations in July 2015 after reportedly failing to close a new funding round, with leadership publicly citing the lawsuits as a significant factor.

## 2. Viewer Psychology
- **Curiosity triggers:** how specifically lawsuits (not just bad unit economics) ended a fast-growing company
- **Emotional triggers:** sympathy for founders who built something people used often; potential frustration/validation depending on the viewer's view of gig-economy labor practices
- **Viewer objections:** "wasn't it really about bad retention/discounting, not the lawsuits?" — address directly by presenting both factors honestly rather than overselling a single cause
- **Motivation to watch:** marketplace/gig-economy founders want to understand classification risk; general audience interested in labor-rights debates
- **Expected comments:** debate over whether contractors were misclassified fairly, comparisons to Uber/Lyft/DoorDash classification battles, "this happened to a company I used" recognition comments

## 3. Competitor Analysis
- **Similar creators:** gig-economy explainer channels, labor-focused journalism channels (More Perfect Union-style content covers labor angle but rarely does a full startup autopsy format)
- **Content gap:** most gig-economy labor content focuses on Uber/Lyft/Instacart (still operating); a fully-collapsed case study like Homejoy is underused despite being a cleaner, more contained story
- **How to outperform:** connect Homejoy explicitly to the present-day gig-economy classification landscape — most existing Homejoy coverage is dated and doesn't tie it to current debates

## 5. Improvement Suggestions
- Present both the lawsuits and the underlying retention/discounting weakness honestly — avoid oversimplifying to a single cause, which this audience will fact-check
- Tie the ending explicitly to present-day gig-economy classification debates for relevance and discovery
- Consider a follow-up video specifically on "how gig-economy companies structured around Homejoy's failure" (Uber, DoorDash, Instacart's subsequent legal strategies) as a natural next episode
