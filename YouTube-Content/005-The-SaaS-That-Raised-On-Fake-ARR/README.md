# 005 — HeadSpin: The Unicorn That Faked Its Revenue 4x — And Sold for $28 Million

**Package status:** Production-ready. Anchored to HeadSpin (mobile app testing infrastructure) and founder/CEO Manish Lachwani — a legally resolved case (guilty plea + criminal conviction + SEC civil charges), not a disputed allegation. Every figure below is sourced to the DOJ, SEC, or contemporaneous reporting citing those filings; cite sources on screen per the original production requirement.

**The verified case:**
- **Company:** HeadSpin, Inc. — founded 2015, a legitimate enterprise SaaS product providing mobile app testing infrastructure on real physical devices across real global carrier networks (not simulators). The product itself worked; the fraud was entirely in the financial reporting.
- **Founder/CEO:** Manish Lachwani.
- **Funding:** Raised $117 million total since 2015 (Crunchbase), including more than $100 million between April 2017–April 2020 (DOJ), from Google Ventures (GV), ICONIQ Capital, Dell Technologies Capital, Battery Ventures, Felicis Ventures, and Tiger Global.
- **Peak valuation:** ~$1.1 billion in a 2020 round led by ICONIQ Capital and Dell Technologies Capital — unicorn status.
- **The fraud (SEC complaint, 2018–2020):** Lachwani gave investors inflated ARR and revenue figures and doctored internal sales records. In one specific, documented instance during 2019 fundraising, he told investors HeadSpin's ARR was **$54 million**; HeadSpin's board later determined the actual year-end 2019 ARR was roughly **$10 million**. Reporting describes the overall inflation across the scheme as "nearly fourfold." He also falsely claimed Apple and American Express as customers, created fake invoices, altered real invoices to show higher billed amounts, counted revenue from prospects who'd never agreed to pay, and kept counting revenue from customers who had already churned. `[VERIFY exact figures against the SEC complaint / plea agreement before recording — cite SEC v. Lachwani litigation release and DOJ N.D. Cal. plea agreement on screen]`
- **Governance vacuum:** HeadSpin had no CFO, no HR department, and was never audited during this period. Investor ICONIQ Capital pushed for financial controls; Lachwani resisted, and reporting describes a resulting "rift" in which he considered returning ICONIQ's investment rather than comply.
- **How it unraveled:** An employee whistleblower raised concerns to HeadSpin's board in early 2020. The board launched an internal investigation, Lachwani stepped down as CEO, and the investigation revised the company's internal valuation from $1.1 billion down to $300 million.
- **Legal outcome:** SEC civil charges filed August 26, 2021 ("SEC Charges Former CEO of Technology Company With $80 Million Fraud" — the $80M figure is the amount of investor funding obtained through the fraudulent scheme, distinct from the ARR figures above). Lachwani pleaded guilty April 23, 2023, in the Northern District of California (before Judge Charles Breyer) to two counts of wire fraud and one count of securities fraud. Sentenced in April 2024 `[VERIFY exact sentencing date — reported as either April 19 or April 22, 2024, across sources]` to 18 months in prison, 3 years supervised release, a $1,000,000 fine, and restitution (reported at $8.6 million, finalized at a July 31, 2024 hearing).
- **Notable nuance:** HeadSpin the *company* was not penalized by the SEC — its post-discovery cooperation helped it avoid SEC penalties. This is an individual-founder fraud case, not a company-wide conspiracy; the product and the rest of the team were not implicated.
- **The coda:** The company survived under new leadership with a real, much smaller revenue base — $21M in 2023, ~$5M in Q1 2024 (~$20M annualized). In July 2024, HeadSpin was sold to Canadian PE firm PartnerOne for a reported **$28.2 million** — about 1.4x revenue, and roughly 2.5% of its $1.1B peak valuation. Most former employees received nothing for their stock options, vested or unvested.

**Framing requirement:** state clearly, early, that this is a resolved legal case (guilty plea + SEC charges), not an allegation. Distinguish the individual founder's criminal conduct from the company/product, which was legitimate — this is a more instructive failure mode for a B2B SaaS audience than a "the whole thing was fake" story: the product was real, the growth numbers were not.
