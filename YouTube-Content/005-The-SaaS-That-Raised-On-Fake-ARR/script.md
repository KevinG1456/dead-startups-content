## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 12–15 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "HeadSpin was valued at $1.1 billion. Three years later, it sold for $28 million. Here's exactly what happened in between."
- Visual: large "$1.1B" figure, hard cut down to "$28.2M" with a crack/X SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This isn't speculation. The founder pleaded guilty. This is the court record."

### Hook (0:15–0:50)
- VO: "This is HeadSpin — a real, working SaaS company that tested mobile apps on actual physical devices across real carrier networks. In 2019, its founder told investors the company had $54 million in annual recurring revenue. According to HeadSpin's own board, the real number was closer to $10 million. This isn't an allegation. Manish Lachwani pleaded guilty to wire fraud and securities fraud in federal court."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll know exactly how the fake number was built, how it got past investors like ICONIQ Capital and Tiger Global, and exactly how one employee ended it."

### Introduction (0:50–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from the SEC's litigation release, the DOJ's plea and sentencing press releases, and contemporaneous reporting — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit legal-disclaimer beat: state clearly this is a resolved case — a guilty plea and SEC civil charges — not a disputed allegation
- Important distinction, stated early: "This is not a story about a fake product. HeadSpin's testing infrastructure was real and it worked. This is a story about a founder who faked the growth numbers behind a real business — which is a more common, and more instructive, failure mode than it sounds."

### Story — Rise (1:40–4:15)
- Founding story (2015), what HeadSpin's product actually did (real-device, real-carrier mobile app testing — a genuine, differentiated infrastructure product), the early growth narrative
- The raise: $117M total since founding, $100M+ raised April 2017–April 2020, from Google Ventures, ICONIQ Capital, Dell Technologies Capital, Battery Ventures, Felicis, and Tiger Global — culminating in a ~$1.1B valuation in 2020
- Visual: pitch-deck-style slides (recreated, not real documents), press headlines, growth-chart animation climbing toward "$1.1B UNICORN"
- Curiosity gap: tease that the growth chart driving that valuation wasn't real, without revealing the mechanism yet

### Main Content — The Autopsy (4:15–10:00)
- **Segment 1 — The claimed vs. real numbers:** the reconstruction, shown side by side: $54M claimed 2019 ARR vs. ~$10M actual, per HeadSpin's own board investigation. Note the broader pattern reported across the 2018–2020 scheme: revenue inflated by roughly fourfold.
- **Segment 2 — How the fraud was built:** per the SEC complaint — Lachwani created fake invoices and altered real ones to show higher billed amounts, counted revenue from prospective customers who had never agreed to pay, kept counting revenue from customers who had already churned, and falsely told investors that Apple and American Express were customers.
- **Segment 3 — Why nobody caught it sooner:** HeadSpin had no CFO, no HR department, and was never audited during this period. When investor ICONIQ Capital pushed for financial controls, reporting describes a "rift" — Lachwani reportedly considered returning ICONIQ's investment rather than add the oversight.
- **Segment 4 — How it unraveled:** an employee whistleblower raised concerns to HeadSpin's board in early 2020. The board launched an internal investigation, Lachwani stepped down as CEO, and the investigation revised the company's internal valuation from $1.1B down to $300M.
- Mid-video CTA (~7:00): "If you're an investor or a founder, the diligence lesson in this next part is the part you actually need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- Graphics: the claimed-vs-real ARR comparison, a "how the fraud was constructed" step-by-step animated breakdown, and a simple governance-checklist graphic (CFO: ❌ / HR: ❌ / Audited: ❌)

### Story — The Fall (10:00–12:30)
- The legal process: SEC civil charges filed August 26, 2021 (titled by the SEC as an "$80 million fraud" — clarify on screen that this figure refers to the investor funding obtained through the scheme, distinct from the ARR figures above, to avoid confusing the two numbers); Lachwani pleaded guilty April 23, 2023, to two counts of wire fraud and one count of securities fraud; sentenced in April 2024 `[VERIFY exact date]` to 18 months in prison, three years of supervised release, a $1,000,000 fine, and restitution reported at $8.6 million
- Notable nuance: HeadSpin the company was not penalized by the SEC — its cooperation after discovering the fraud helped it avoid company-level penalties. This was an individual founder's crime, not a company-wide conspiracy.
- Human element: most former HeadSpin employees received nothing for their stock options, vested or unvested, when the company was later sold
- Slow the pacing deliberately here

### The Coda — What HeadSpin Actually Sold For (12:30–13:45)
- The company kept operating under new leadership with real, much smaller revenue: $21M in 2023, roughly $5M in Q1 2024
- In July 2024, HeadSpin was sold to Canadian private equity firm PartnerOne for a reported $28.2 million — about 1.4x revenue, and roughly 2.5% of its $1.1B peak valuation
- Visual: the valuation line graph completing its full arc — founding, climb to $1.1B, the investigation, the collapse to $300M, and the final $28.2M sale point

### Final CTA + Outro (13:45–15:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — verify revenue independently; a company can have a completely real, working product and still be built on completely fake growth numbers
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "How would you verify a startup's ARR before investing? Comment below."
- End screen: two related autopsies + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

Opens directly on the claimed-vs-real ARR comparison fully populated ($54M vs. $10M), then rewinds to explain how each figure was constructed and confirmed via the SEC complaint. Measured, graphics-forward, minimal music.

Opens on the human moment — the whistleblowing employee's decision to raise concerns to the board in early 2020 — before any numbers appear. Slower pacing, cinematic, builds outward from that single decision to the full scale of the fraud.

Structured as "3 red flags that were there the whole time: no CFO, no audit, and customers who don't exist," counting down using the same underlying SEC-sourced facts. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Main Content section (4:15–10:00) if the fraud-mechanism explanation becomes overly technical or reads like a legal recitation
- **Fix:** ground each mechanism (fake invoices, phantom Apple/Amex deals, churned-customer double-counting) in a single concrete example before moving to the next; cut every 20–30 seconds between talking head, document B-roll, and animated graphic
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the Rise section if audience feedback shows strong familiarity with the "unicorn raise" narrative pattern already
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** move the "no CFO, no audit" governance-vacuum reveal earlier if early-retention data shows drop-off before 3:00 — it's a strong, concrete curiosity hook on its own
- **Recommended visual change:** show the $1.1B→$28M valuation arc partially in the cold open, fully revealed only in the Coda
