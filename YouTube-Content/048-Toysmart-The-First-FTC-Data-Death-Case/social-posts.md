## LinkedIn Post
In 1999, Disney took a majority stake in an online toy retailer for roughly $45 million. Within 18
months, that company was bankrupt — and its customer database, covering thousands of families, was
listed for sale as a distressed asset.

This became the first-ever FTC case testing whether a bankrupt company can sell customer data despite
promising never to share it. The FTC's own first proposed settlement wasn't a full block — it would
have allowed a sale to a "qualified buyer." It took 41 state attorneys general formally objecting to
change the outcome.

In the end, Disney's own subsidiary paid $50,000 — not to acquire the data, but specifically to have
it destroyed.

This case, from 2000, is the direct legal ancestor of nearly every "what happens to my data" story
since. Full breakdown in my latest video — link in comments.
