# Shorts Package

Rule: Shorts stay strictly to ironclad, sourced facts — no speculative framing, no legal-nuance
compression that could misstate the case. Every figure here is directly pulled from `sources-log.txt`.

## Short 1 — "$45M In, $50K to Destroy It" (0:00-0:45)
- Hook: "Disney put $45 million into this company. Then it paid $50,000 just to destroy what was left
  of it."
- Beat 1: Toysmart.com, 1999, Disney takes a majority stake for ~$45M
- Beat 2: 2000, bankrupt, tries to sell customer data despite a "never share" privacy promise
- Beat 3: FTC sues, 41 states object, Disney's own subsidiary pays $50,000 to have the data destroyed
  instead
- CTA: "Full story on the channel — the first-ever case like it."

## Short 2 — "The First Time This Ever Happened" (0:00-0:45)
- Hook: "This is the first time in history the FTC went after a company for trying to sell customer
  data in bankruptcy."
- Beat 1: Toysmart's privacy policy said "never share." Bankruptcy put the data up for sale anyway.
- Beat 2: The FTC's first plan wasn't even a full block — just a "qualified buyer" rule
- Beat 3: 41 states said that wasn't enough. The data was destroyed, not sold.
- CTA: "This is the case behind every 'what happens to my data' story since. Full breakdown on the
  channel."

## Short 3 — "Why Did Disney Pay to Destroy Its Own Asset?" (0:00-0:40)
- Hook: "Why would a company pay money to destroy something it already owned?"
- Beat 1: Disney's subsidiary owned 60% of bankrupt Toysmart.com
- Beat 2: The customer database was worth something — but selling it would break the company's own
  privacy promise
- Beat 3: $50,000, paid specifically for the right to destroy it instead
- CTA: "The full story — link/full video on the channel."
