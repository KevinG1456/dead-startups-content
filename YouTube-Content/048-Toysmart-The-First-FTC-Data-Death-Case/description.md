## Full Description (YouTube)

In 1999, Disney bought a majority stake in an online toy company for approximately $45 million. Less
than 18 months later, that company was bankrupt — and its customer database, covering thousands of
families and children, was listed for sale as a company asset, despite a privacy policy that
explicitly promised the data would "never be shared with third parties."

What happened next became the first-ever FTC case of its kind — and the direct legal ancestor of
every "what happens to my data when a company dies" story since, including RadioShack's own 2015
bankruptcy (which we've already covered on this channel).

This is the real, fully-sourced story of Toysmart.com: the Disney investment, the collapse, the
attempted data sale, the FTC's surprisingly incomplete first settlement proposal, the 41 states that
pushed back, and the $50,000 payment that decided how it all ended.
