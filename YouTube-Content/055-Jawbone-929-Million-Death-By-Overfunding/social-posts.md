## LinkedIn
"A real case study in what overfunding without focus actually looks like, in order:

Jawbone — founded 1999 in San Francisco as AliphCom, originally building military-derived noise-cancellation tech — raised $929.9 million over its lifetime and hit a $3.3 billion valuation in February 2014. Along the way it built three separate hardware product lines at once: Bluetooth headsets, Jambox wireless speakers, and UP fitness trackers.

In 2015, BlackRock put in $300 million, publicly described as an investment. Bloomberg later reported, based on California filings, that it was actually structured as debt. Around the same time, Jawbone sued rival Fitbit three times for trade-secret theft — and lost every claim; Fitbit's own patent case against Jawbone also failed. A global settlement closed both in December 2017, months after Jawbone had already quietly wound down via an Assignment for the Benefit of Creditors — not a bankruptcy filing.

What makes this case unusual: there's no single smoking-gun document. The story is assembled from a funding history, a legal war Jawbone started and lost, a disguised loan, and a quiet liquidation filing most people never noticed — which is itself worth sitting with if you're deciding how many markets to compete in at once with a single round of funding.
