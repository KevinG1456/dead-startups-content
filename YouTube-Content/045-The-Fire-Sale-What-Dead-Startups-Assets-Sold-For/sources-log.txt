COMPANY 1: QUIBI — content library sold to Roku
- Raised approximately $1.75B before launch. Founded by Jeffrey Katzenberg (Chairman) and Meg Whitman
  (CEO). Launched April 6, 2020; shutdown announced October 21, 2020 — roughly 6 months post-launch.
- Its content library (~75 shows) was acquired by Roku for a reported price under $100 million,
  announced January 2021 — NOT an acquisition of "assets" broadly, specifically the content library.
- Ratio: under $100M against $1.75B raised is under 5.7% of lifetime funding.
Source: verified in this channel's own `002-Quibi-1.75B-6-Months-Zero-Pivots/README.md` (originally
corrected via this channel's 2026-08-13 sourcing sweep, per `Fact-Check Reminder.txt`'s "002 - Quibi"
FIXED entry) — re-used here, not re-derived.

COMPANY 2: BIRD — sold to its own lenders
- Founded September 2017 by Travis VanderZanden. Peaked at a $2.3-2.5B valuation around its November
  2021 SPAC merger; combined pre-SPAC VC funding (~$700M+) plus the SPAC's own $414M cash proceeds
  gives a rough combined total north of $1B raised across Bird's full history.
- December 20, 2023: Chapter 11 filed. April 5, 2024: Bird Global's assets sold for approximately $145
  million to Third Lane Mobility — a new private holding company formed by a consortium of Bird's OWN
  first- and second-lien lenders, led by MidCap Financial (an Apollo Global Management affiliate) and
  Obelysk. This is Bird's own lenders converting debt to ownership, not an outside acquirer.
- Ratio: $145M against a $2.3-2.5B peak valuation is roughly 6% of peak value.
Source: verified in this channel's own `028-Bird-Scooter-SPAC-To-Bankruptcy/sources-log.txt` — re-used
here, not re-derived.

COMPANY 3: VIRGIN ORBIT — sold to three buyers at a bankruptcy auction
- Founded March 2, 2017, spun off from Virgin Galactic under Richard Branson's Virgin Group. ~$1B
  invested pre-IPO by Virgin Group and Mubadala (a reported aggregate, not an audited total). SPAC
  merger closed Dec 30, 2021 at a $3.7B valuation.
- Chapter 11 filed April 4, 2023. May 22-23, 2023: assets sold at bankruptcy auction for approximately
  $36 million total, split among three buyers: Rocket Lab ($16.1M, Long Beach HQ building, ~140,000 sq
  ft), Stratolaunch ($17M "stalking horse" bid, the Cosmic Girl 747 carrier aircraft), and Vast/Launcher
  ($2.7M, Mojave facility plus machinery/equipment/inventory). Firefly Aerospace separately acquired
  approximately $3.8M of additional assets in June 2023.
- Ratio: ~$36M against a $3.7B peak valuation is roughly 1% (0.97%) of peak value — the sharpest ratio
  in this comparator.
Source: verified in this channel's own
`031-Virgin-Orbit-3.7B-Valuation-Bankrupt-In-16-Months/sources-log.txt` — re-used here, not re-derived.

COMPANY 4: FRIENDSTER — sold outright, then its patents outsold the whole company
- IMPORTANT FRAMING NOTE: unlike the other four companies in this package, Friendster's 2009 sale was
  a normal, voluntary acquisition — NOT a bankruptcy fire sale. No Chapter 11 was ever filed. It
  qualifies for this package specifically because of what happened NEXT: its patent portfolio sold
  separately, for more than the whole company had sold for less than a year earlier. Flag this
  distinction explicitly on screen — do not imply Friendster went through the same distressed-auction
  process as Bird, Virgin Orbit, or Katerra.
- December 2009: Malaysian payments company MOL Global acquired 100% of Friendster for approximately
  $39 million.
- June 2010: MOL separately sold Friendster's patent portfolio (seven finalized patents, eleven
  pending applications) to Facebook for approximately $40 million — MORE than the whole company had
  sold for less than a year earlier.
Source: verified in this channel's own
`040-Friendster-The-Founder-Who-Wouldnt-Sell/sources-log.txt` — re-used here, not re-derived.

COMPANY 5: KATERRA — FRESHLY SOURCED THIS SESSION (2026-08-16), not previously stated in package 034
- Founded 2015 by Michael Marks. Raised approximately $2B, mostly from SoftBank (per this channel's
  own package 034, which covers Katerra's rescue-financing history but never states its final
  asset-sale total).
- Filed Chapter 11 June 7, 2021, in the Southern District of Texas. Remained in Chapter 11 for
  approximately four months before a Joint Chapter 11 Plan of Reorganization was confirmed.
- Combined asset sales and post-bankruptcy income totaled approximately $99 million, including:
  - Tracy, California facility: sold for $21.25 million
  - Spokane, Washington factory: sold for $50 million
  - Renovation business: sold to an ownership group operating Onx Homes for $1 million cash plus $17
    million in assumed liabilities
- *** [VERIFY]: one source in this fresh research pass states Katerra operated "in nine countries with
  more than $2.5B in capital and $1.75B in annual revenue" prior to filing — this $1.75B "annual
  revenue" figure is NOT corroborated elsewhere and looks likely to be a confusion with total capital
  deployed or a different metric; DO NOT use "$1.75B annual revenue" on screen. Default to package
  034's already-verified "~$2B raised, mostly SoftBank" for Katerra's funding figure. ***
- Ratio: ~$99M against ~$2B raised is roughly 5% of lifetime funding.
Source: Bloomberg Law ("SoftBank-Backed Katerra Seeks Liquidation After Selling Assets"),
https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/softbank-backed-katerra-seeks-liquidation-after-selling-assets;
Levelset ("Months After Bankruptcy Declaration, Katerra Still Faces Liens and Legal Claims As Major
Assets Are Sold Off for Millions"), https://www.levelset.com/news/after-bankruptcy-katerra-still-faces-liens/.
Katerra's rescue-financing history (the two $200M SoftBank infusions, the $35M DIP loan) remains
sourced to this channel's own `034-The-Bridge-Round-Death-Spiral-4-Companies/sources-log.txt` — only
the final $99M asset-sale total is new to this package.
