## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **The Fire Sale: What a Dead Startup's Assets Actually Fetch, Line by Line.** — the exact batch-
   title framing, promises concrete specificity, works as a series/format title if this comparator
   format gets revisited with a fresh set of five companies later
2. Five Startups. Five Bankruptcies. Here's Exactly What the Wreckage Sold For. — strong comparator-
   format hook, sets audience expectation for a multi-company structure immediately
3. This Company Was Worth $3.7 Billion. Its Assets Sold for $36 Million. — leads with the single
   sharpest ratio in the package (Virgin Orbit), extremely shareable on its own
4. One Company's Patents Sold for More Than the Whole Company Did. — sharp, self-contained irony hook
   (Friendster), doesn't require any other context to land
5. This Company's Own Lenders Ended Up Buying It Out of Bankruptcy. — strong structural-irony hook
   (Bird), good mid-video re-hook potential
6. $2 Billion Raised. $99 Million Recovered. Here's the Full Breakdown. — pure number-shock framing
   (Katerra), strong but slightly less dramatic ratio than Virgin Orbit's
7. This Scooter Company Went Public at $2.5 Billion. Its Lenders Bought It Back for $145 Million. —
   strong but company-specific, less useful as the umbrella title
8. This Rocket Company's Own Cargo Plane Sold for $17 Million. — vivid, specific, but a secondary
   detail rather than the package's core thesis
9. What Happens to a Bankrupt Startup's Assets? 5 Real Examples — best pure-SEO, lower CTR
10. Startup Bankruptcy Auctions Explained: 5 Real Cases — strong secondary search title for viewers
    specifically researching liquidation mechanics

## Recommended Title
**"Five Startups. Five Bankruptcies. Here's Exactly What the Wreckage Sold For."**

Reasoning: sets the comparator structure immediately (five companies, consistent format), promises
concrete specificity ("exactly what") that differentiates from vaguer "what happened to" recap
content, and works fully truncated on mobile at the first two sentences alone. Leaves room to reveal
the individual company names and their sharpest ratios (Virgin Orbit's ~1%, Friendster's patents-
outsold-the-company irony) as escalating reveals inside the video rather than spending them in the
title itself.
