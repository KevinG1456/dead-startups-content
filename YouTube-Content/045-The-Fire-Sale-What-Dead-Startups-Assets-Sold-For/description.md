## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: not why
five companies died, but what was actually left when they did.

**Quibi** raised $1.75 billion before launching a short-form premium video app in April 2020. Shutdown
was announced just six months later, in October 2020. In January 2021, its content library — roughly
75 shows — sold to Roku for a reported price under $100 million.

**Bird** went public via SPAC in November 2021 at a reported $2.3-2.5 billion valuation. After an
SEC-disclosed accounting restatement and an NYSE delisting, Bird filed Chapter 11 in December 2023. In
April 2024, its assets sold for approximately $145 million — to Third Lane Mobility, a company formed
by Bird's OWN lenders, converting debt into ownership.

**Virgin Orbit** went public via SPAC in December 2021 at a $3.7 billion valuation. After a failed UK
launch and mass layoffs, it filed Chapter 11 in April 2023 — about 16 months after going public. In May
2023, its assets were auctioned for roughly $36 million total, split three ways: Rocket Lab paid $16.1
million for the headquarters, Stratolaunch paid $17 million for the Cosmic Girl carrier plane, and
Vast/Launcher paid $2.7 million for the Mojave facility. That's about 1% of peak valuation.

**Friendster** is different — no bankruptcy was ever filed. In December 2009, it sold outright to
Malaysian payments company MOL Global for approximately $39 million. Then, in June 2010, MOL separately
sold Friendster's patent portfolio to Facebook for approximately $40 million — more than the whole
company had sold for less than a year earlier.

**Katerra** raised roughly $2 billion, mostly from SoftBank, before filing Chapter 11 in June 2021.
Its combined asset sales and post-bankruptcy income totaled approximately $99 million — including its
Tracy, California facility ($21.25 million), its Spokane, Washington factory ($50 million), and its
renovation business ($1 million cash plus $17 million in assumed liabilities).
