## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Olive AI, a healthcare startup that hit a $4 billion valuation and then spent 18 months selling itself off in pieces instead of collapsing all at once.

Founded in 2012 in Columbus, Ohio, by CEO Sean Lane and CTO Jeremy Yoder, the company started as CrossChx, doing patient identity verification for hospitals. In 2017 it rebranded to Olive with a new pitch: "the AI workforce for healthcare," automating administrative and revenue-cycle work. Funding climbed fast — a $225.5 million round in December 2020 valued Olive at $1.5 billion; seven months later, a $400 million round led by Vista Equity Partners valued it at $4 billion.

In April 2022, an investigation reported that Olive was overpromising its capabilities and that its claimed savings for hospital customers relied on rough estimates rather than delivered, audited results. What followed wasn't a single bad week. In July 2022, roughly 450 employees — about 35% of the company — were laid off; CEO Sean Lane cited "fast-paced growth and lack of focus." A second round of roughly 200 layoffs followed in February 2023. Then, rather than filing for bankruptcy outright, Olive sold off its own business units one at a time: utilization management tools to Availity in April 2023, then its clearinghouse and patient access businesses — described in coverage as "the heart of Olive's business" — to Waystar and Humata Health. On October 31, 2023, Olive formally announced it was winding down what remained.

Olive's real lesson: a company can be worth $4 billion, get investigated, lay off staff twice, and never file for bankruptcy at all — and still end up with nothing left, because the value simply got sold off in pieces before the shutdown was ever announced.
