# 024 — Olive AI: $4 Billion Valuation. Sold For Parts.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — brand-new topic, researched from scratch this session (not a candidate carried over from another package's research, unlike 021/022/023). Full citations in `sources-log.txt`.

**The real case:** Olive AI — founded 2012 in Columbus, Ohio, by CEO Sean Lane and CTO Jeremy Yoder, originally as CrossChx (patient identity verification), rebranded to Olive in 2017 around a new pitch: the "AI workforce for healthcare," automating hospitals' administrative and revenue-cycle work. Raised close to $900M over its lifetime, including a primary-sourced $225.5M round (Dec 2020, $1.5B valuation) and a primary-sourced $400M round (Jul 2021, $4 BILLION valuation — Olive's peak, led by Vista Equity Partners). In April 2022, an Axios investigation reported Olive was overpromising its savings and relying on rough estimates rather than delivered results. What followed was not a single dramatic collapse but an 18-month unwind: ~450 employees laid off (Jul 2022, "fast-paced growth and lack of focus" per CEO Sean Lane), a second layoff round of roughly 200 (Feb 2023), then Olive sold its own business units off piece by piece — utilization management tools to Availity (Apr 2023), then its clearinghouse and patient access businesses (described as "the heart of Olive's business") to Waystar and Humata Health — before formally winding down what remained on October 31, 2023.

**Important honesty note:** this is an overhype-and-burn-rate story, not a fraud story. Sean Lane has not been charged or convicted of anything. The strongest sourcing is the two funding press releases (Dec 2020, Jul 2021) — both primary, company/investor-issued. The "AI was mostly rules-based automation" characterization comes from retrospective industry analysis, not an on-record company admission — present it as analysts' view, not settled fact. The original Axios investigation could not be retrieved directly in this research pass — its findings are confirmed via multiple independent secondary summaries, but don't invent specific dollar figures or quotes beyond what `sources-log.txt` states. The February 2023 layoff headcount (200 vs. 215) and the exact date of the Waystar/Humata Health sales are both flagged as uncertain — see `sources-log.txt` before recording.

**Framing requirement:** lead with "sold for parts" — Olive's ending has no single dramatic week (unlike 022/Fast) and no single smoking-gun document (unlike 023/Munchery's bankruptcy filing). Its distinguishing shape is that the company sold off its own valuable pieces to competitors and industry buyers *while still operating*, before a final shutdown announcement that was closer to closing an empty shell than ending a going concern. That structural detail — liquidating yourself in installments — is the strongest, most differentiated hook available and should anchor the whole video.
