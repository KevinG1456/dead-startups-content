**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Stephen Elop's 2011 "Burning Platform"

**Mandatory exception flag:** Nokia is a large, public, multinational company — NOT a

**Framing correction, made during research — read before touching any file in this package:**
the batch title's literal premise doesn't match the real, sourced facts, and the real story is
sharper. Elop's memo was NOT an ignored warning — it was the explicit justification for the
drastic strategic pivot (the Windows Phone partnership with Microsoft) that Nokia announced just
three days later. The warning was heeded. The company still died anyway. That's a better story
than "ignored warning." Also: the batch title's "2 years early" framing is inaccurate — the real
gap between the memo (Feb 2011) and the effective death of the acquired business (Microsoft's
$7.6B write-off, Jul 2015, or the Lumia line's full discontinuation in 2016) is roughly four and
a half to five years, not two. This package uses the real, corrected numbers throughout.

**The real case:** Stephen Elop became Nokia's CEO in September 2010, arriving directly from
running Microsoft's Business Division — the first non-Finnish CEO in Nokia's history. On
February 8, 2011, he sent Nokia's entire staff an internal memo built around an extended
metaphor: a worker on a burning North Sea oil rig who has to choose between certain death by
fire or a 30-meter plunge into freezing water. The memo laid out, in blunt numbers, how badly
Nokia had fallen behind — Apple's share of the $300+ phone market had gone from 25% to 61% in
two years, and Android had just overtaken Nokia in smartphone volumes that same week. The memo
leaked publicly within about a day. Three days later, on February 11, 2011, Nokia announced the
pivot the memo was written to justify: abandoning its own Symbian and the unreleased MeeGo
operating systems in favor of Windows Phone. The bet did not work. Windows Phone never gained
durable market share. On September 3, 2013, Microsoft agreed to acquire substantially all of
Nokia's Devices & Services business for approximately $7.2 billion; the deal closed in 2014 with
about 30,000 employees transferring over. On July 8, 2015 — under new CEO Satya Nadella, who had
succeeded Steve Ballmer (the CEO who did the original 2013 deal) — Microsoft wrote off $7.6
billion, nearly the entire purchase price, and laid off 7,800 people, mostly from the former
Nokia phone business. Throughout 2016, Lumia production wound down to just nine phones before
the entire line was discontinued.

**A disputed detail, handled with care:** after the Microsoft deal, some employees, former
executives, and outside commentators floated a "Trojan horse" theory — that Elop had been placed
at Nokia specifically to engineer a Microsoft acquisition. Elop has explicitly and directly
denied this on record, and a later retrospective book reportedly concludes he wasn't a plant, just
an ineffective CEO — a different, still-unflattering, but distinct claim. This package never
states the Trojan horse theory as fact; it's always attributed explicitly and paired with Elop's
own denial in the same breath, the same standard this channel already applies to other disputed
claims about named, living individuals (see package 025/Sonder's handling of Marriott's litigation
allegations).

**Framing requirement:** lead with the memo's own vivid metaphor and its real, verified numbers
(Apple 25%→61%, Android overtaking Nokia "this week"), correct the "ignored warning" and "2
years" framings explicitly on screen, and never assert the Trojan horse theory as settled fact.
