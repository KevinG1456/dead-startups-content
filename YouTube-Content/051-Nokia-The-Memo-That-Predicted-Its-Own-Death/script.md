# Script Package

Anchored on Nokia/Stephen Elop — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT
RULE FOR THIS SCRIPT: Nokia is a large public company, NOT a funded startup — disclose this
plainly and early, same standard as package 003's Garmin/TomTom segment. THE SECOND MOST
IMPORTANT RULE: never state the memo was ignored — it was heeded, and the pivot it justified
happened three days later. Never state "2 years" for the memo-to-death gap — the real figure is
roughly 4.5-5 years. Never state the "Trojan horse" theory as fact — always attribute it
explicitly and pair it with Elop's on-record denial.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes (the memo, the pivot, the bet's failure, the acquisition, and
the write-off all covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "In 2011, a CEO sent his entire company a memo comparing it to a man standing on a burning
  oil rig. Everyone believed him. Three days later, they jumped. It still didn't save them."
- Visual: a slow-building flame graphic behind bold white text reading "OUR PLATFORM IS BURNING"
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This isn't a story about a
  warning nobody heard. Everybody heard it."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Nokia — and one honesty note up front: Nokia is a large public company, not a
  funded startup like almost everything else on this channel. This is a deliberate exception,
  because the story here is too good, and too instructive, to leave out."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand exactly what the memo said, why the
  company acted on it almost immediately, and exactly how long it actually took — years, not the
  '2 years' this story sometimes gets told with — for the warning to fully come true."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from Engadget's original coverage, Forbes, and a full-text
  retrospective compilation, cross-checked against contemporaneous 2011-2016 tech and business
  press — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "Two honesty notes before we start. First: this
  warning was not ignored — it was heeded almost immediately, and that's what makes this story
  different from the usual 'ignored warning' pattern. Second: the real gap between the warning and
  the company's actual end is roughly four and a half to five years, not two."

### Main Content — The Memo (1:40–5:00)
- **Segment 1 — The New CEO (1:40–2:15):** September 2010, Nokia hires Stephen Elop as CEO — the
  first non-Finnish CEO in the company's history, arriving directly from running Microsoft's
  Business Division. Visual: simple executive-bio title card.
- **Segment 2 — The Memo (2:15–3:30):** February 8, 2011, Elop sends an internal memo to all
  Nokia staff — roughly 1,300 words. It opens with an extended metaphor: a worker on a burning
  North Sea oil rig, facing an impossible choice between certain death by fire or a 30-meter
  plunge into freezing water. On-screen quote card, verified text: "stand on the platform, and
  inevitably be consumed by the burning flames. Or, he could plunge 30 meters in to the freezing
  waters." Visual: the quote card over a slow-building flame graphic.
- **Segment 3 — The Numbers (3:30–4:15):** the memo backs up the metaphor with real numbers:
  Apple's share of the $300+ phone market went from 25% in 2008 to 61% by 2010; that same week,
  Android had overtaken Nokia in smartphone sales volume. On-screen quote card, verified text:
  "In 2008, Apple's market share in the $300+ price range was 25 percent; by 2010 it escalated to
  61 percent." Visual: "25% → 61%" numeral graphic.
- **Segment 4 — It Leaks (4:15–5:00):** the memo was meant for internal staff only. It was public
  within about a day, becoming one of the most widely covered leaked internal memos in tech
  history. Visual: "LEAKED WITHIN 24 HOURS" title card.

### Main Content — The Pivot (5:00–8:00)
- **Segment 5 — Three Days Later (5:00–6:00):** February 11, 2011 — the drastic action the memo
  was written to justify. Nokia announces a strategic partnership with Microsoft: Windows Phone
  becomes its primary smartphone platform, replacing its own Symbian and the still-unreleased
  MeeGo. On-screen framing card, stated plainly: "This warning was not ignored. The company acted
  on it in three days." Visual: "WINDOWS PHONE PARTNERSHIP — FEBRUARY 11, 2011" title card.
- **Segment 6 — The Trojan Horse Theory (6:00–7:00):** after everything that followed, some
  employees, former executives, and outside commentators floated a theory — that Elop had been
  placed at Nokia specifically to engineer a future Microsoft acquisition. On-screen framing card:
  "Elop has directly and repeatedly denied this — 'I have only ever worked on behalf of and for
  the benefit of Nokia shareholders while at Nokia.' A later retrospective book reportedly
  concludes he wasn't a plant, just an ineffective CEO — a different, still unflattering, but
  distinct claim. Present this as disputed, not settled." Visual: neutral "DISPUTED THEORY" stamp
  card.
- **Segment 7 — The Bet Doesn't Work (7:00–8:00):** despite the fast, decisive pivot, Windows
  Phone never gains durable market share against iOS and Android. Nokia's position keeps
  deteriorating through 2011-2013. Visual: declining market-share line graph.

### Main Content — The Ending (8:00–10:30)
- **Segment 8 — Sold (8:00–9:00):** September 3, 2013, Microsoft agrees to acquire substantially
  all of Nokia's Devices & Services business for approximately $7.2 billion — roughly $5 billion
  for the business itself, roughly $2.17 billion to license Nokia's patents. The deal closes in
  2014; about 30,000 employees transfer to the new "Microsoft Mobile Oy" subsidiary. Visual:
  "SOLD FOR ~$7.2 BILLION — SEPTEMBER 2013" title card.
- **Segment 9 — The Write-Off (9:00–10:00):** July 8, 2015, under new CEO Satya Nadella —who had
  succeeded Steve Ballmer, the CEO who did the original deal — Microsoft writes off $7.6 billion,
  nearly the entire purchase price, and lays off 7,800 people, most from the former Nokia phone
  business. At the time, this was the largest write-down in Microsoft's corporate history. Visual:
  "$7.6 BILLION WRITE-OFF — JULY 2015" title card.
- **Segment 10 — The End (10:00–10:30):** through 2016, Lumia production winds down to just nine
  phones before the entire line is discontinued. Visual: "LUMIA DISCONTINUED — 2016" title card.
- Mid-video CTA (~10:15): "This channel usually covers funded startups that ran out of money.
  This is the rare one where the warning was heeded, the pivot happened fast, and it still wasn't
  enough. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Lesson (10:30–12:00)
- Plain-language comparison: most "warning" stories on this channel — and in business writing
  generally — are about warnings that got ignored. This one is different, and more useful,
  because it isolates a harder truth: heeding a warning and acting on it fast is necessary, but it
  is not sufficient. The response also has to actually work.
- The core lesson, stated directly: a correct diagnosis followed by fast, decisive action is not
  a guarantee of survival — it only removes one possible cause of death. Windows Phone was a real,
  serious attempt at the pivot the memo demanded, made almost immediately, and the market still
  didn't reward it.
- Visual: full-arc timeline graphic — Elop hired (Sep 2010) → memo leaks (Feb 8 2011) → Windows
  Phone partnership announced (Feb 11 2011) → market share keeps falling (2011-2013) → sold to
  Microsoft (Sep 2013) → deal closes, 30,000 employees transfer (2014) → $7.6B write-off, 7,800
  laid off (Jul 2015) → Lumia discontinued (2016).

### Final CTA + Outro (12:00–13:15)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — heeding a warning is necessary but not sufficient; the
  response also has to work, and sometimes it just doesn't, even when everyone did their job
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and this is the first one centered entirely
  on a warning that was heeded rather than ignored"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you ever own a Nokia phone? And do you think Elop was set up to fail,
  or did he just make the wrong bet with the information he had? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the $7.2B-to-$7.6B numeral contrast and the full-arc timeline, then works
backward through the memo, the pivot, and the failed bet as supporting detail. Measured,
graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine case study in the limits of "heeding a warning."

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the memo itself — the burning-oil-rig metaphor, read almost in full — before cutting
hard to the 2015 write-off, then rewinding to fill in everything in between. Slower pacing, holds
the "heeded, not ignored" correction until the pivot segment, resolving with the 2016
discontinuation as the closing beat. Strongest tonal-irony arc of the three versions.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "one memo, five years of consequences" leading into rapid-fire beats: Elop hired,
memo leaks, pivot announced, bet fails, sold to Microsoft, write-off, discontinued — landing on
the "heeded but not saved" lesson as the final, clarifying beat. Strong Shorts-extraction
potential, especially the burning-platform metaphor quote and the $7.2B-to-$7.6B contrast.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the "bet doesn't work" segment (7:00–8:00) if the 2011-2013 market-share
  decline feels like a dry recap without new specific detail
- **Fix:** frame this segment explicitly as "the response the memo demanded, actually happening,
  and still not being enough" — connect it directly forward to the write-off reveal
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general Windows Phone market-share context if audience
  familiarity with the platform's failure is already high
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "$7.6 billion" write-off figure even more
  explicitly in the cold open if early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 9 (9:00)
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small running "years since the warning" counter visible
  from Segment 5 onward, ticking upward toward "4.5 YEARS" at the write-off reveal
