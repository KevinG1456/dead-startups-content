# 058 — Quirky: $185 Million, 400 Products, Zero Focus

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built from Quirky's fully documented public history
(TechCrunch, Forbes, GE.com press releases, Fortune, Entrepreneur.com, and Wikipedia — full
citations in `sources-log.txt`). New, user-picked title, not from the dead-saas-30 batch.

**The real case:** Quirky was a crowdsourced invention platform founded by Ben Kaufman, publicly
launched June 2009 when Kaufman was 22 (he'd already sold a prior company, mophie, in 2007).
Quirky's community submitted and voted on product ideas; the company designed, manufactured, and
sold the winners, splitting revenue with the inventors and contributors. It raised roughly $185
million across four VC rounds, including a headline partnership with General Electric — first a
2011 product-collaboration deal, then a $30 million GE equity investment folded into a $79 million
Series D in November 2013. Out of that partnership came Wink, a connected-home platform Quirky
spun off as a standalone company in mid-2014.

The mechanism that killed Quirky wasn't a single bad decision — it was scale without focus. After
its 2012 Series C ($68M, Andreessen Horowitz), the company accelerated from its original goal of
one new product a week to three new products a week, shipping roughly 400 distinct, largely
unrelated products over its six years of operation, with thin retail margins and reportedly little
iteration once something launched. Layoffs began in late 2014; Kaufman stepped down as CEO on July
31, 2015 alongside a 111-person layoff; Quirky filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy September 22, 2015,
selling off its assets — including Wink, auctioned to Flextronics for $15 million.

**The Wink coda, and why it's NOT framed like package 049 (Parse):** Wink didn't disappear. Flex
resold it to will.i.am's company i.am+ for $59 million in 2017. But in May 2020, Wink imposed a
mandatory $4.99/month subscription on existing hardware owners, triggering real backlash, and the
platform has a documented history of outages continuing into 2024-2025. Wink is technically still
alive as of this research pass — but this is a messier, more ambiguous survival story than Parse
Server's clean, thriving afterlife. Keep that tonal distinction explicit on screen; don't reuse
Parse's triumphant framing here.

**Founder coda:** Ben Kaufman has no fraud or misconduct allegations found in this research —
a clean, undramatic executive career after Quirky: BuzzFeed Chief Commerce Officer (2016), CMO
(Jan 2019), stepped down Aug 2019 to focus on CAMP, a family-experience retail chain he founded in
2018 and still runs.
