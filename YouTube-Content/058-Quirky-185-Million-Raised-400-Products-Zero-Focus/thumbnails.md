# Thumbnail Strategy

One dominant idea per thumbnail — per this channel's standing rule. The obvious dominant visual for
this story is a wall/shelf of many small, random, unrelated gadgets — keep every version anchored
to that single idea rather than trying to also show GE, Wink, or the bankruptcy in the same frame.

## Thumbnail Intelligence — Winning Concept
- **Core visual:** a cluttered shelf/wall of dozens of small, generic, unrelated gadget silhouettes
  (no real product likenesses), with one single dollar-figure card overlaid
- **Main subject:** the "too many products, no focus" paradox
- **Emotion:** bewildered curiosity
- **Background:** dark, minimal, the gadget wall slightly desaturated/blurred at the edges
- **Lighting:** single spotlight on the dollar-figure card, gadgets left dim/cluttered
- **Camera angle:** N/A (graphic-led)
- **Composition:** gadget wall fills the frame, dollar-figure card centered/upper-third, bottom-
  third title-safe space reserved
- **Props:** generic, non-trademarked gadget silhouettes only
- **Thumbnail text:** `$185 MILLION. 400 PRODUCTS. NAME ONE.`
- **Text placement:** bottom third, bold sans-serif, high contrast
- **Color strategy:** bright white/red dollar-figure card, desaturated gray-blue gadget wall, near-
  black background
- **Contrast strategy:** the sheer density of small, indistinct gadgets against one sharp number
  communicates the entire thesis before any text is read

## Notes on What NOT to Do
- Do not render any real, identifiable photorealistic likeness of Ben Kaufman in any thumbnail —
  use generic avatar/host imagery or graphic-led concepts only
- Do not put real Quirky, GE, Wink, Home Depot, or i.am+ logos/wordmarks in generated thumbnail
  art — build the visual concept generically and add any verified real branding in the editor with
  proper sourcing
- Do not depict specific, recognizable real Quirky products (some had distinctive designs) —
  use generic, non-trademarked gadget silhouettes only
