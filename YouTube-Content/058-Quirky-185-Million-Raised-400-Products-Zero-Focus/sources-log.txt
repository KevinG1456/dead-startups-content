Sources Log — Package 058: Quirky
Filled in: 2026-08-16

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
Quirky is a new, user-picked title, not part of the dead-saas-30 batch. It's a "burned cash on too
many unfocused products" story — distinct in mechanism from package 012 (Pivot Graveyard, companies
that changed direction) and package 019 (open-source competing with its own paid product). Quirky's
mechanism is horizontal sprawl: a genuinely working crowdsourcing engine that never said no to an
idea, spread across hundreds of unrelated product categories with no real focus.

---

FOUNDING — precisely sourced
- Quirky was a crowdsourced invention platform: the community submitted product ideas, voted and
  commented on them, Quirky's in-house team designed/engineered/manufactured the winners, and
  inventors + contributors earned a royalty share of sales.
- Founded by Ben Kaufman, publicly launched June 2009, when Kaufman was 22.
- Kaufman's prior venture: founded mophie (iPod/accessory company) as a teenager, funded partly via
  a second mortgage on his parents' house; mophie was acquired in August 2007. This is the "sold his
  first company, immediately started a much bigger and stranger one" setup for the video.
Source: Center for Research Innovation (Northeastern) bio; Fortune (Jul 21 2014, "Counting Down
With Ben Kaufman"); Disruptor Awards 2013 honoree bio; Wikipedia "Quirky (company)."

THE GE PARTNERSHIP — precisely dated, two distinct phases
- Phase 1 (product collaboration, no equity): Quirky and GE partnered starting April 2011 to
  co-develop a line of connected/smart-home products based on GE patents — described in the GE.com
  Nov 2014 press release as debuting "a series of connected products... ranging from light switches
  to security sensors," working toward 30 GE-patent-based products total.
- Phase 2 (equity investment): November 13, 2013 — GE announced an expanded partnership including a
  $30 million strategic investment (minority equity stake) plus in-kind services, folded into
  Quirky's $79M Series D round.
- GE selected Wink as its preferred connected-home platform partner; Home Depot separately agreed to
  carry/integrate Wink-connected devices.
Source: GE.com press releases (Nov 13 2013; Nov 11 2014 "Quirky, GE Unveil Their Vision For The
Connected Home"); TechCrunch (Nov 11 2014); VentureBeat.

WINK — the connected-home spinoff, sourced with one date discrepancy flagged
- Wink was Quirky's software/hub platform for controlling smart-home devices from a single app.
- Spun off as a standalone company — sources give slightly different months: Wikipedia/Wink's own
  press release says "established as a standalone business" mid-2014; other secondary sources say
  June or July 2014. [VERIFY] exact month — state "mid-2014," do not pick a specific date without
  further confirmation.
- September 22, 2015 (Quirky's bankruptcy filing date): Quirky put Wink up for auction as part of
  the Chapter 11 process. Flextronics (an existing hardware/firmware supplier to Wink) placed the
  winning $15 million stalking-horse bid.
- December 14, 2015: a bankruptcy judge approved the sale of Quirky's assets, including Wink to
  Flextronics.
- July 2017: Flex (renamed from Flextronics) sold Wink onward to i.am+, the technology company
  founded by musician will.i.am, reportedly for $59 million — a real, sourced uptick from the $15M
  Flex paid less than two years earlier.
- May 2020: Wink (under i.am+) announced a mandatory $4.99/month subscription plan, restricting
  previously-free basic app/hub/API functionality behind the paywall — this triggered significant
  public backlash from existing device owners who felt locked out of hardware they already owned.
- Current status (checked during this research pass, 2026): Wink's service shows as operational,
  though with a documented history of repeated outages (most recently flagged Sept 2024 and Sept
  2025 by third-party status-monitoring sites) — a troubled, still-technically-alive status, NOT a
  clean "thriving independently" story like package 049 (Parse Server). Do not frame Wink's survival
  as an unambiguous happy ending — it changed owners three times, sparked real user backlash over
  the 2020 paywall, and its long-term stability remains genuinely uncertain even today.
Source: Fortune/TechCrunch (Sept 22 2015, Quirky bankruptcy + Flextronics $15M bid); PRNewswire
(Flex/i.am+ deal); VentureBeat (May 2020, Wink subscription backlash); How-To Geek ("Why We Can't
Recommend Wink Hubs Anymore"); StatusGator Wink status history (checked 2026).

THE SCALE PROBLEM — sourced, the video's core mechanism
- Kaufman's original stated goal at launch: introduce one new consumer product every week.
- After the 2012 Series C ($68M, Andreessen Horowitz), Quirky accelerated this further: the company
  scheduled THREE product launches per week, selecting 3 ideas out of 1,000+ weekly community
  submissions to move into development.
- Over its roughly six years of operation, Quirky brought approximately 400 distinct, largely
  unrelated products to market — everything from kitchen gadgets to smart-home hardware to office
  supplies — with little category focus and, per multiple retrospectives, little iteration once a
  product shipped.
- Retail-channel margin compression: Quirky relied heavily on big-box retailers to move volume,
  which reportedly squeezed margins on already thin-margin consumer hardware.
- Peak headcount: over 400 employees.
Source: CNBC (2012, "Inventing More Inventors at 'Quirky' Start-up"); Entrepreneur.com ("How a
'Quirky' 28-Year-Old Plowed Through $150 Million..."); Failory postmortem; Harvard Digital
Innovation and Transformation ("How Not to Crowdsource: The Demise of Quirky").

ROYALTY MODEL — sourced, with a real conflict flagged
- Quirky's stated model: inventors and community "influencers" who contributed to a product's
  development shared in its sales revenue as a royalty.
- Sources conflict sharply on the actual percentage structure: one describes a modest single-digit
  split (roughly 3% direct-to-consumer / 1.5% via retail, with Quirky keeping 5-10%); another
  describes a much larger split (30% of direct sales, 10% of wholesale revenue going to the
  inventor/community pool). [VERIFY] — THIS IS UNRESOLVED. Do not state a specific royalty
  percentage on screen; describe the model qualitatively ("inventors and contributors earned a share
  of sales revenue") without committing to disputed numbers.
Source: Vizologi business-model breakdown; The Penny Hoarder; Inventing Patents blog (conflicting
figures, not reconciled).

THE COLLAPSE — precisely dated
- Beginning in late 2014, Quirky underwent multiple rounds of layoffs and restructuring as it
  struggled to raise further funding.
- July 31, 2015: Ben Kaufman stepped down as CEO, the same period as a 111-person layoff, amid
  reported difficulty securing additional financing.
- September 22, 2015: Quirky filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, explicitly to facilitate an asset
  sale (including the Wink auction described above).
- December 14, 2015: bankruptcy judge approved the final asset sale.
Source: Forbes (Sept 22 2015, "Gadget Startup Quirky Goes The Way Of The Sharper Image: Bankruptcy");
SiliconANGLE (Dec 14 2015); Gizmodo (Sept 2015).

BEN KAUFMAN'S LATER CAREER — sourced, no fraud/misconduct found
- 2016: joined BuzzFeed as Chief Commerce Officer.
- January 2019: promoted to BuzzFeed CMO.
- August 2019: stepped down as BuzzFeed CMO (less than a year in the role) to focus on his own
  venture, CAMP.
- CAMP: a family-experience retail chain Kaufman founded in 2018 (retail stores blending play space
  + product shop); he remains founder/CEO as of the most recent sourcing found.
- 2025: public social-media posts describe Kaufman moving into new work connected to "eko," an
  AI-powered product-visualization company — described in his own words as "the perfect (and
  slightly unhinged) culmination" of his career across products, factories, retail, e-commerce,
  media, and technology. No fraud, lawsuit, or personal-misconduct allegations against Kaufman were
  found in this research pass — this is a clean, no-sensitivity-flag founder coda.
Source: Variety (Aug 15 2019, BuzzFeed CMO step-down); Tubefilter (Aug 2019); Crunchbase (Ben
Kaufman person profile, CAMP); Business of Fashion; Medium/Future of StoryTelling Q&A.
