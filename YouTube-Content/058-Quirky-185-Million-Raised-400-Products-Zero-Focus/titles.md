## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **$185 Million. 400 Products. Can You Name One?** — sharpest numeral contrast, built-in
   audience-participation hook, sets up the entire "scale without focus" thesis in one line
2. This Startup Launched 3 New Products a Week. It Still Went Bankrupt. — clean, countable,
   immediately legible pace claim with a strong contradiction
3. GE Backed This Startup With $30 Million. It Still Died in 2 Years. — leverages a recognizable
   brand (GE) for instant credibility/curiosity
4. Sold for $15 Million, Then Resold for $59 Million — After the Company That Built It Died —
   strong secondary hook for the Wink-afterlife angle specifically
5. The Crowdsourced Startup That Crowdsourced Its Own Collapse — clever wordplay, slightly softer
   CTR than the numeral-led options
6. This Startup's Smart-Home Spinoff Has Had 3 Owners Since It Died — strong for viewers who
   already know Wink, less legible cold
7. What Happens When a Startup Never Says No to an Idea — strong thesis statement, lower numeral
   shock value
8. They Had a Real Working Idea Machine. It Still Wasn't Enough. — good mid-funnel/retention title,
   softer cold-traffic CTR
9. What Happened to Quirky? The Full Story — best pure-SEO, lower CTR
10. Quirky Bankruptcy Explained: GE, Wink, and $185 Million — strong secondary search title

## Recommended Title
**"$185 Million. 400 Products. Can You Name One?"**

Reasoning: leads with two hard, verified numbers and closes with a direct, slightly uncomfortable
audience challenge — most viewers genuinely can't name a single Quirky product despite the
company's real scale and real GE partnership. Sets up the entire "too many ideas, no focus"
mechanism without needing the company name up front, and the challenge format is inherently
comment-baiting in a good way (viewers will answer in the comments). Matches the title used in
this package's README.
