## LinkedIn
"A real case study in what happens when a startup never learns to say no:

Quirky was founded in 2009 by Ben Kaufman, building a genuinely working crowdsourced invention
platform — community members submitted and voted on product ideas, Quirky designed and
manufactured the winners, everyone shared in the revenue.

It raised roughly $185 million, partnered with General Electric on connected-home products
(including a $30 million GE equity investment in 2013), and spun off a smart-home platform, Wink,
in 2014.

After its 2012 Series C, Quirky tripled its launch pace to three new products a week. Over six
years, it shipped around 400 distinct products — with little category focus and thin retail
margins. Kaufman stepped down as CEO in July 2015; the company filed Chapter 11 that September.

Wink itself has changed owners twice since — sold to Flextronics for $15M, resold to i.am+ for
$59M — and is technically still running today, though it's had real user backlash (a 2020
mandatory subscription requirement) and documented outages since.

Full breakdown: [video link]
