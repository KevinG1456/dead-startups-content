## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week:
Quirky — the crowdsourced invention platform that raised real money, landed a real General Electric
partnership, and still couldn't survive its own ambition.

Quirky was founded in June 2009 by Ben Kaufman, then 22, who'd already sold his first company
(mophie) in 2007. Quirky's model: the community submitted and voted on product ideas, the company
designed and manufactured the winners, and everyone who contributed shared in the sales revenue. It
worked — Quirky built a community of over a million engaged users and real hit products in real
stores.

It raised roughly $185 million across four rounds, including a $68 million Series C in 2012 led by
Andreessen Horowitz. That round funded an acceleration from Quirky's original goal — one new
product a week — to three new products a week. In 2011, Quirky partnered with General Electric to
build connected smart-home products from GE's own patents; by November 2013, GE had put $30 million
directly into the company as part of a $79 million Series D. Out of that partnership came Wink, a
smart-home platform Quirky spun off as its own standalone company in mid-2014.

But the underlying problem never went away: over roughly six years, Quirky shipped around 400
distinct, largely unrelated products — with thin retail margins and little iteration once something
launched. Layoffs began in late 2014. Kaufman stepped down as CEO on July 31, 2015, alongside a
111-person layoff. Quirky filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on September 22, 2015.

Here's the part most retrospectives skip: Wink didn't disappear. It was auctioned to Flextronics
for $15 million, then resold in 2017 to will.i.am's company i.am+ for $59 million. But in 2020, Wink
imposed a mandatory $4.99/month subscription on existing hardware owners, sparking real backlash —
and the platform has a documented history of outages continuing into 2024 and 2025. It's technically
still alive today. It's not a clean happy ending.
