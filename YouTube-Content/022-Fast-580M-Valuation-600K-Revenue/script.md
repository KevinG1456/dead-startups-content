# Script Package

⚠️ Read `sources-log.txt` in full before using any line below. Domm Holland has NOT been charged
or convicted of anything — every personal-history claim in this script is attributed on screen to
its actual source (primarily one NPR investigation), never stated as this channel's own
independently confirmed fact. Do not use "extorted" (the Qant.as item) or "boys' club" (not
sourced) anywhere. Include the balancing detail that investors reportedly knew about at least some
of this history and weren't uniformly deterred by it. The financial facts (funding, revenue, burn,
shutdown timeline) are solid and multi-sourced — state those plainly as fact.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 13–16 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "Fast raised $124 million at a $580 million valuation. Its real revenue that year was $600,000."
- Visual: "$580M" appears large, "$600K" appears small beneath it, a steep red downward arrow connects them
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "Every number in this video is sourced. Some of this story is more carefully attributed than usual — here's why."

### Hook (0:15–0:55)
- VO: "This is Fast — a real, well-funded checkout startup with one of the strangest revenue-to-valuation gaps in recent startup history. This video is built entirely from verified reporting, and where the sourcing gets more careful — specifically around some of the founder's personal history — I'll tell you exactly that, on screen, as it comes up."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand exactly how the numbers worked, what actually happened in the company's final six days, and a genuinely complicating detail most retellings of this story leave out."

### Introduction (0:55–1:45)
- Method statement: reconstructed from TechCrunch, The Information (via syndicated coverage), and one detailed NPR investigation — sourced on screen throughout, with an explicit note whenever a claim traces to that single NPR investigation specifically
- One-sentence framing: the financial story here is ironclad and multi-sourced; a secondary thread about the founder's earlier career is handled with more caution, because it largely traces to one piece of reporting about a person who has not been charged with anything

### Main Content — The Fast Case (1:45–9:00)
- Segment 1 — The Pitch (1:45–2:45): March 2019, San Francisco. Fast, founded by CEO Domm Holland and COO Allison Barr Allen, pitches a one-click checkout button any e-commerce site could add — competing directly with Shopify's Shop Pay, PayPal, and rival startup Bolt. Visual: simple checkout-button icon animating in.
- Segment 2 — The Raises (2:45–4:00): $20M Series A, led by Stripe, March 2020. $102M Series B, led by Stripe AGAIN, January 2021 — a payments company backing a startup in its own core category, twice. $124.5M raised total, valuation roughly $580M. Visual: funding timeline graphic, Stripe logo-style placeholder marker on both rounds.
- Segment 3 — The Real Numbers (4:00–6:00): 2021 revenue was approximately $600,000 — some of it from branded merchandise, not core transaction volume. Daily revenue was sometimes as low as $2,000. Burn was roughly $10 million a month. For comparison: rival checkout startup Bolt reportedly made around $40 million over the same period — roughly 65 times more. Visual: side-by-side revenue bar chart, Fast's bar barely visible next to Bolt's.
- Segment 4 — The Spending (6:00–7:00): Fast reportedly paid The Chainsmokers $1 million for a single conference performance — about 1.7 times the company's entire annual revenue. Headcount grew from 337 to 480 in the two months before the collapse became public. Visual: the $1M booking cost placed directly next to the $600K annual revenue figure.
- Segment 5 — The Collapse (7:00–9:00): by early 2022, hiring had slowed to a 13-month low. Late March 2022: The Information reports the real revenue figure; Fast tries to raise a new round at a lowered ~$450M valuation; fails; hires Morgan Stanley to find a buyer. April 5, 2022 — six days after the lowered-valuation attempt — Fast shuts down entirely. Roughly 450 employees, including about 150 engineers, are laid off, most with about an hour's notice. Holland's own statement: "difficult decision to close our doors... trailblazers don't make it all the way to the mountain top." Visual: six-day countdown graphic, March 30 to April 5.
- Mid-video CTA (~8:30): "If you're an investor or an operator trying to spot this kind of gap before it becomes a headline, the next part is exactly what you need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### A Carefully-Sourced Second Thread (9:00–11:30)
- On-screen disclosure, stated plainly before this section begins: "Everything in this section traces primarily to one investigative report, from NPR, published in February 2022. It's presented here exactly as reported, not as independently confirmed by this channel."
- NPR's investigation reported that Fast's original product prototype was built in 2019 by roughly a dozen engineers in Nigeria, paid around $800 a month, who were let go without notice after about three months — and that Holland is reported to have told investors he built it himself. Two engineers, quoted anonymously by NPR, described being "erased" from the story.
- Separately, NPR's investigation covered Holland's earlier venture, Tow.com.au, an Australian towing marketplace that collapsed in 2018 reportedly owing contractors around $5.7M AUD. Named creditor Joe Andriske, quoted on the record: "He made a promise, made a promise. We get a small payment then he disappeared, mate." NPR also reported Holland threatened to sell platform users' personal data before a Queensland court intervened.
- THE BALANCING DETAIL — do not cut this for pacing: a Fast spokesperson told NPR this history "was revealed to investors at every round," and two investors told NPR they knew about it and didn't consider it disqualifying. One separate investor said it would have discouraged them. This isn't a simple story of investors being blindsided — at least some of them reportedly knew.
- Visual: a clearly bordered/differently-colored graphic treatment for this entire section, visually distinct from the financial-story segments, with a persistent "Source: NPR investigation" lower third

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — a compelling story can outrun real product traction for a surprisingly long time, and the most useful diligence question isn't "is this founder likeable" but "does the revenue match the narrative," a question that, per the available reporting, at least some of Fast's investors either didn't ask hard enough or asked and proceeded anyway
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "What do you think should have caught this earlier — the revenue numbers, the spending, or something else? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the completed funding-to-revenue-to-shutdown graphic (all the real numbers at once), then works backward through the mechanism. Measured, graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine diligence resource for investors and operators — minimizes time spent on the founder-history segment, treating it as a brief, clearly-sourced coda rather than the emotional center.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the six-day collapse timeline in full (March 30 lowered-valuation attempt to April 5 shutdown) before any earlier numbers appear. Slower pacing, more narrative-driven.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "3 numbers that should have raised questions sooner," each illustrated with a beat from Fast's financial story only — deliberately excludes the founder-history segment entirely for a tighter, lower-risk Shorts-friendly cut.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the transition into "A Carefully-Sourced Second Thread" if the tonal shift and disclosure framing feels like a lecture rather than a natural continuation
- **Fix:** keep the on-screen disclosure brief and confident, not apologetic — state it once, clearly, and move into the material without over-explaining the caveat repeatedly
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** Script Version C (financial story only) is a legitimate, lower-risk alternative cut if the founder-history segment's sourcing requirements make it a poor fit for a given distribution channel
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** the $1M Chainsmokers booking next to the $600K revenue figure is one of the strongest single visual beats in the whole script — give it its own full graphic moment, don't rush past it
- **Recommended visual change:** keep the "Source: NPR investigation" lower third persistently visible throughout the entire second-thread segment, not just at its start
