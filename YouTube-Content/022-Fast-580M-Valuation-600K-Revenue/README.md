# 022 — Fast Raised $124M at a ~$580M Valuation. Its Real Revenue Was $600,000.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Fast, a real, extensively-reported case (TechCrunch, The Information via syndication, NPR — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This title was identified during the 001 (Sprig) sourcing pass and explicitly rejected there because Fast's real revenue (~$600K) directly contradicted that title's "$10M ARR" premise — flagged as "a different, still-tellable title" on its own. This is that package: the inverse story to Sprig (001) and Stay Alfred (021) — not real revenue that still wasn't enough, but a huge valuation built on almost no revenue at all.

**⚠️ ELEVATED CARE REQUIRED — read `sources-log.txt` in full before writing or recording any line from this package.** Unlike package 005 (HeadSpin), where the founder was criminally convicted and there is a court record to cite, Fast's founder, Domm Holland, has **not been charged or convicted of anything**. He is a living person currently running a new business. Several of the most serious personal-history claims in this package trace to **one underlying NPR investigation** (Feb 17, 2022), widely syndicated but not independently re-corroborated by a second investigation. Every such claim must be attributed on screen ("NPR reported...," "according to...") — never stated as this channel's own independently confirmed fact. Specific banned framings: do not use the word "extorted" (regarding the Qant.as domain item — NPR's own language is far more careful than that), and do not use "boys' club" (not supported by any source found). See `sources-log.txt` for the full list of required cautions before this package goes anywhere near a script read-through or a recording session.

**The real case:** Fast — a one-click e-commerce checkout startup founded March 2019 in San Francisco by CEO Domm Holland and COO Allison Barr Allen (a third co-founder, Joshua Abulafia, was reportedly pushed out about a year in) — raised $124.5M total, including a $102M Series B **led by Stripe** (announced January 26, 2021, following a $20M Series A also led by Stripe) at a valuation of roughly $580M. Its actual 2021 revenue was approximately $600,000 — a figure so low that rival checkout startup Bolt reportedly made roughly $40M over the same period, about 65x more. Fast was burning roughly $10M a month, including a reported $1M paid to The Chainsmokers for a single conference performance — about 1.7x Fast's entire annual revenue. After a failed attempt to raise at a lowered ~$450M valuation and a last-minute search for a buyer via Morgan Stanley, Fast shut down on April 5, 2022, laying off roughly 450 people, most with about an hour's notice.

**The distinct lesson (why this isn't a repeat of another package):** this is not a Stay Alfred-style "sound structure, fatal external shock" story, nor a Sprig-style "real revenue, broken unit economics" story. Fast's real revenue was negligible from the start — the actual mechanism here is narrative-driven fundraising outpacing product reality, with a second, carefully-handled thread: reporting that some of Fast's founder's own financial history was known to at least some investors and not universally treated as disqualifying, which complicates a simple "investors were deceived" narrative and is more interesting than a flat villain story.

**Framing requirement:** lead with the verified financial contrast ($124.5M raised, ~$580M valuation, ~$600K real revenue) as the hook — it needs no embellishment. Handle the founder-history material with the exact attribution discipline laid out in `sources-log.txt`, and include the balancing detail that investors reportedly knew about at least some of this history and weren't uniformly deterred by it.
