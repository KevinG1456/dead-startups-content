WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

*** ELEVATED CARE NOTICE — READ BEFORE WRITING OR RECORDING ANY SCRIPT LINE ***
Unlike package 005 (HeadSpin), where the founder was criminally convicted and there is
a court record and SEC/DOJ citations to point to, Domm Holland has NOT been charged or
convicted of anything. He is a living person currently running a new business
(Trady). Several of the most serious claims about his personal/founder history trace
to ONE underlying investigative piece (NPR, Feb 17, 2022, widely syndicated to ~10
partner outlets — that syndication is NOT independent corroboration, it's one
investigation republished many times). Every claim below must be presented as
"NPR reported," "according to [named source]," or "per [named creditor]'s quote" —
never as flat, independently-confirmed fact — anywhere this material is used: script,
blog, description, social posts, everywhere. Do not use characterizations that go
beyond what's directly sourced (e.g., do NOT say Holland "extorted" anyone — see the
Qantas item below for why that specific word must not be used). This is the single
most legally sensitive package this channel has produced; treat it accordingly.

FOUNDING

- Founded March 2019 in San Francisco by Domm Holland (CEO), Allison Barr Allen (COO,
  previously led Global Product Operations at Uber), and a third co-founder, Joshua
  Abulafia, who was reportedly pushed out roughly a year in following a dispute with
  Holland over finances/direction.
- Pitch: a one-click checkout button any e-commerce site could add, competing with
  Shopify's Shop Pay, PayPal, Amazon's one-click system, and rival startup Bolt.
Source: TechCrunch, kjlabuz.substack.com/p/fast (aggregation of primary reporting —
verify direct primary sources for any specific claim pulled from this aggregator

THE FINANCIAL REALITY — solid, multi-sourced, state as confirmed fact

- 2021 revenue: approximately $600,000 (a portion from branded merchandise sales,
  not core checkout transaction volume).
- Burn: approximately $10M/month.
- Daily revenue was typically under $300,000, with some days as low as $2,000.
- Headcount ballooned from 337 to 480 between October and November 2021, shortly
  before the company's collapse became public.
- Comparison point (useful on-screen contrast): rival checkout startup Bolt
  reportedly had roughly $40M in revenue over the same period — Fast's revenue was
  roughly 1.5% of a direct competitor's.
Source: The Information (via NPR/Morning Brew syndication), TechCrunch, Pragmatic
Engineer, Business of Business.

FOUNDER'S PRE-FAST HISTORY — SINGLE-SOURCED to one NPR investigation, extreme care
required, exact wording matters

- 2010, the Qant.as domain: Holland bought the domain for $20 and redirected its
  traffic to Qantas rival Virgin Blue. He later CLAIMED to have sold the domain for
  $1.3M but never named the buyer. NPR's OWN reporting uses careful, hedged language
  ("claimed to have sold it") — it does NOT say he "extorted" anyone.
  *** DO NOT USE THE WORD "EXTORTED" OR ANY SIMILAR CHARACTERIZATION ANYWHERE IN
  THIS PACKAGE. *** A secondary, non-primary source (thebrag.com) oversold this as
  "extorted $1.3M from Qantas" — that is NOT what NPR's primary reporting states,
  and using that framing would misrepresent the sourcing and create real legal
  exposure. Use NPR's own careful phrasing only: Holland "claimed" a sale occurred;
  no buyer was ever named; this is disputed/unconfirmed, not a settled fact of
  wrongdoing.
- Tow.com.au: an Australian "Uber for towing" venture Holland ran, with an
  exclusive contract with Queensland police, reportedly handling around 100
  vehicles a day at its peak. It collapsed in 2018, reportedly owing towing
  contractors approximately $5.7M AUD (roughly $4.1M USD). A named creditor, Joe
  Andriske of Harvey's Towing Services, is quoted on the record: "He made a
  promise, made a promise. We get a small payment then he disappeared, mate." —
  this specific quote IS attributable to a named, on-record source and can be used
  as such, clearly attributed to Andriske.
  Reporting further states Holland threatened to sell the driver's-license and
  bank data of more than 21,000 platform users on "the black market" to raise
  cash; Queensland's Supreme Court intervened and forced him to surrender the data
  instead of selling it. The Queensland government reportedly settled the
  resulting dispute for under $1M in September 2019.
  [Present all of this as "per NPR's investigation and named creditor Joe Andriske"
  — the Andriske quote is solidly attributable to him directly; the broader
  narrative (the data-sale threat, the court intervention, the settlement amount)
  traces to NPR's investigation and should be attributed to NPR specifically.]
- Holland began using "Domm" instead of "Dominic" coinciding with Fast's launch and
  early fundraising. Journalist Kristian Silva is quoted characterizing this as a
  deliberate rebrand: "trying to bury what happened in Brisbane and start afresh."
  [Attribute this interpretation explicitly to Silva — it is his characterization,
  not an established fact about Holland's intent.]
- BALANCING DETAIL, IMPORTANT TO INCLUDE: a Fast company spokesperson (Jason
  Alderman) is quoted saying the Tow.com.au history "was revealed to investors at
  every round." Two unnamed VCs told NPR they knew about this history and did not
  see it as disqualifying to investing; one separate, unnamed "prominent investor"
  told NPR the history would have discouraged them from investing had they known.
  [This is a genuinely balanced detail — investors were reportedly NOT deceived
  about this specific history, even if separate questions exist about metrics
  presented for Fast itself. Include this balancing context; do not present the
  founder-history material one-sidedly.]
Source: NPR (Feb 17, 2022 investigation) — this is, again, ONE underlying
investigation for nearly all of this section; present accordingly.

THE COLLAPSE — precisely dated

- January-February 2022: Fast's job postings decline, hitting a 13-month low by
  February 17, 2022.
- February 17, 2022: NPR publishes its Domm Holland background investigation.
- March 29, 2022: The Information reveals the ~$600K 2021 revenue figure.
- March 30, 2022: The Information reports Fast is planning mass layoffs and trying
  to lower its own valuation to roughly $450M in an attempt to raise a new $100M
  round.
- March 31, 2022: The Information reports Fast has hired Morgan Stanley to urgently
  find a buyer.
- April 1, 2022: an early engineer departs and posts cryptically about it.
- April 5, 2022: Fast announces total shutdown. Approximately 450 employees
  (including roughly 150 engineers) are laid off, reportedly given notice roughly
  one hour before the public announcement.
  [Strong, fully verifiable script beat: from a ~$580M-adjacent valuation
  conversation (the ~$450M markdown attempt, March 30) to total shutdown in six
  days, March 30 to April 5.]

  "difficult decision to close our doors"; "trailblazers don't make it all the way

Source: The Information (via syndication), TechCrunch.
