## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **The Open-Source Project That Killed Its Own Commercial Company** — original; clean, high-clarity paradox, states the thesis directly
2. This Company Gave Away the Thing That Ended Up Killing It — strong irony framing, slightly more conversational
3. Docker Was Everywhere. Docker Inc. Was Dying. — extremely strong for developer audiences who recognize Docker instantly; two-sentence structure creates immediate tension
4. They Raised $100M+. Their Own Free Software Beat Them. — punchy, specific-number framing drives curiosity
5. The Open-Core Trap That Killed a $100M Company — names the reusable business-model concept, strong for founder/investor audience
6. The Free Fork That Outlived the Company That Made It — strong curiosity, slightly more oblique without prior context
7. How Giving Away Your Product for Free Can Kill the Company Behind It — clean, practical, slightly less punchy
8. What Actually Happened to the Company Behind CyanogenMod — best pure-nostalgia hook for Android/LineageOS audience specifically
9. What Is the Open-Core Dilemma? Full Explanation — best pure-SEO framing, lower CTR
10. They Built Something Everyone Loved — and It Still Wasn't Enough — best for the emotional/human-angle alt cut

## Recommended Title
**"The Open-Source Project That Killed Its Own Commercial Company."**

Reasoning: it states the paradox directly and completely in one line, works as both a search-friendly and a curiosity-friendly title, and doesn't require the viewer to already know what Cyanogen or Docker are to feel the hook. Keep "Docker Was Everywhere. Docker Inc. Was Dying." and "They Raised $100M+. Their Own Free Software Beat Them." in reserve as strong A/B test challengers, since both lean harder on specific, recognizable proof points that could outperform with a developer-heavy audience segment.
