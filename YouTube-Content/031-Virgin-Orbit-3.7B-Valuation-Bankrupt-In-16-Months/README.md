# 031 — Virgin Orbit Went Public at a $3.7 Billion Valuation. It Filed for Bankruptcy 16 Months Later — After One Rocket Failure and a Fake $200 Million Bailout Offer.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Virgin Orbit's fully documented public
history (Wikipedia, CNBC, Fortune, CNN Business, Space.com, SpaceNews, FlightGlobal, TechCrunch, The
Register, BusinessWire/SEC EDGAR earnings filings, SEC.gov Litigation Release No. 26031, Bloomberg —
full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This is the first title on the channel chosen directly from the
"Dead SaaS & Software Graveyard" Batch 2 idea list (Cluster 1, idea #1: "The startup that IPO'd and
died within 24 months") rather than from a candidate flagged during earlier research.

**The real case:** Virgin Orbit was founded March 2, 2017, as a spin-off from Virgin Galactic under
Richard Branson's Virgin Group, building LauncherOne — a small-satellite rocket carried to altitude
by a modified 747 ("Cosmic Girl") and air-launched. For nearly five years it operated as a private
company, backed by roughly $1 billion from Virgin Group and Abu Dhabi's Mubadala Investment Company.
It went public via SPAC merger on December 30, 2021, at a reported $3.7 billion valuation. Public-
company SEC filings then showed what private funding had kept hidden: full-year 2021 revenue of just
$7.4 million, and a single-quarter net loss of $42.9 million in Q3 2022 — even after a fourth
successful launch. On January 9, 2023, Virgin Orbit's first UK orbital launch attempt failed,
destroying 9 customer satellites; the cause, confirmed weeks later, was a $100 dislodged fuel filter.
With no more launches to sell and a funding gap it could no longer hide, the company paused
operations March 15, 2023, was the target of a fabricated $200 million rescue offer from an unrelated
Texas man (later sued by the SEC for it), laid off 675 employees (85% of staff) March 30, 2023, and
filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy April 4, 2023 — 16 months after going public. Its assets, once part of a
$3.7 billion company, sold at auction for about $36 million in pieces to Rocket Lab, Stratolaunch,
and Vast/Launcher.

**Framing requirement:** Never conflate Virgin Orbit with Virgin Galactic — two separate, easily
confused Branson/Virgin Group companies with different tickers, business models, and litigation
histories (Virgin Galactic's 2021 shareholder suit is not Virgin Orbit's). The only fraud in this
story belongs to Matthew Brown, an unrelated third party who faked a rescue offer — not to Virgin
Orbit, Dan Hart, or Richard Branson, none of whom were charged with anything. Keep that distinction
as clean as this channel's other fraud-adjacent packages (022/Fast, 029/Ozy Media, 030/MoviePass).

**Stronger angle used:** most existing coverage treats the January 2023 launch failure as the cause

Batch 2 content brief directly: going public in Dec 2021 forced quarterly SEC disclosures that
revealed the real revenue-to-loss ratio ($7.4M revenue vs. a single $42.9M quarterly loss) a full
year *before* the launch failure — the failure was the trigger, not the underlying cause. The fake
$200 million bailout subplot (Matthew Brown, later sued by the SEC) is the added twist almost no
existing coverage connects to the main collapse narrative in one piece.
