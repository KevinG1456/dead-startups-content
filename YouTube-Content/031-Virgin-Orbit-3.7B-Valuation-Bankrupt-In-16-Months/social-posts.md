## LinkedIn
"A real case study in what going public actually reveals — and how fast a funding gap can end a
company once the rocket stops flying:

Virgin Orbit went public via SPAC merger on December 30, 2021, at a reported $3.7 billion valuation.
For nearly five years before that, it operated privately, backed by roughly $1 billion from Virgin
Group and Mubadala. Quarterly SEC disclosures then showed what private funding had kept invisible:
$7.4 million in 2021 revenue, and a $42.9 million net loss in a single Q3 2022 quarter — even after a
fourth successful launch.

On January 9, 2023, the company's first UK orbital launch attempt failed, destroying 9 customer
satellites. The confirmed cause: a dislodged fuel filter. Operations paused March 15, 2023. Days
later, a Texas man submitted a fabricated $200 million rescue offer — his real bank balance was less
than $1; the SEC sued him for it in June 2024. Virgin Orbit laid off 675 employees (85% of staff)
March 30, 2023, and filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy April 4, 2023 — 16 months after going public. Its
assets sold at auction in May 2023 for about $36 million.
