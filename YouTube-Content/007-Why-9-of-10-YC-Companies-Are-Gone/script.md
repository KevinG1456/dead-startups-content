# Script Package

Anchored on a full audit of YC's Summer 2006 (S06) batch — full citations in `sources-log.txt`.
Present BOTH ratios on screen: 10/11 (91%, counting acquired-then-discontinued as gone) and 8/11
(73%, counting any real acquisition as a success). Do not claim S06 was YC's first batch (S05
was). Flag Zanbazaar's status as single-sourced. Present OMGPOP's and Xobni's acquisition prices
as ranges, not single precise figures.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 13–16 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "I tracked down every single company from a real Y Combinator batch. Only one of them still exists."
- Visual: 11-square grid animates in, 10 squares dim to grey with an X one by one, 1 stays lit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This is a full, checkable audit. Every company, every source, on screen."

### Hook (0:15–0:55)
- VO: "'9 out of 10 startups fail' gets repeated constantly, but almost nobody actually checks it against a real batch, company by company. I did — Y Combinator's Summer 2006 class, 11 real companies. And the honest answer is more interesting than a single number."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll know exactly what happened to all 11 companies, and why the real 'gone' ratio depends on a definition most people skip past."

### Introduction (0:55–1:45)
- Method statement: reconstructed from TechCrunch's contemporaneous Nov 2006 coverage, cross-checked against YCDB and company-specific reporting — sourced on screen throughout, full audit table linked in the description
- One-sentence framing: define "gone" precisely before the count starts — fully shut down, acquired-then-discontinued, or still independently operating — and explain why the batch chosen (S06, not YC's famous first batch) was a deliberate choice for full-audit integrity, not recency

### The Audit — All 11 Companies (1:45–9:30)
- Segment 1 — The Quiet Shutdowns (1:45–4:00): six companies — Jamglue, Jumpchat, Likebetter, Pollground, Shoutfit, Talkito, Thinkature — shut down with little to no lasting trace. Cover briefly, as a group, with the audit table populating company by company on screen: what each did, when it disappeared, sourced to TechCrunch's 2006 coverage and YCDB. Visual: audit table filling in row by row, each row landing with a soft SFX.
- Segment 2 — The Company That Got Bought Back Twice, Sort Of: OMGPOP (4:00–6:00): started as a dating site ("i'minlikewithyou"), pivoted to casual social gaming under CEO Dan Porter, became a breakout hit with Draw Something. Acquired by Zynga in March 2012 for a reported $180-200M. Zynga shut it down roughly 14 months later, June-August 2013 — and reportedly refused when the original team tried to buy the site back. Visual: acquisition headline, then shutdown headline roughly a year later, timeline compressed on screen.
- Segment 3 — The Smaller Repeat: Xobni (6:00–7:30): an Outlook plugin and email analytics tool built by Adam Smith and Matt Brezina. Acquired by Yahoo in July 2013 for a reported $30-60M+. Yahoo shut it down about a year later, July 2014, folding a few features into Yahoo Mail. Same pattern as OMGPOP, smaller scale. Visual: acquisition-then-shutdown timeline, same visual template as OMGPOP's for pattern recognition.
- Segment 4 — The One Survivor: Scribd (7:30–9:00): pitched in 2006 under the working name "Social Documents," built by Trip Adler, Tikhon Bernstam, and Jared Friedman, raised $105M total over its life. Still independently operating today, with a CEO transition to Tony Grimminck in October 2023 and reported recent growth. Visual: a 20-year timeline compressing into "still here" on the audit table's final row.
- Segment 5 — Zanbazaar, Honestly Flagged (9:00–9:30): the eleventh company, Zanbazaar, appears to have shut down — but this is confirmed only by a single aggregator source, not independently corroborated. On-screen lower third: "Single-sourced — flagged, not stated as confirmed fact with the same confidence as the rest of this table." Visual: this row of the table appears with a distinct "unconfirmed" visual marker.
- Mid-video CTA (~8:30): "If you're evaluating an accelerator or trying to understand real startup odds, the next part is the actual number you need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Finding — Two Honest Numbers (9:30–12:30)
- Plain-language reveal: counting every company that no longer exists in any form today — the 8 shutdowns plus OMGPOP and Xobni, both acquired and later discontinued — that's 10 of 11, roughly 91%. Close enough that "9 out of 10" genuinely holds up, under this specific definition.
- But counting OMGPOP's and Xobni's acquisitions as what they actually were for their founders and investors — real, multi-million-dollar cash exits — regardless of what the acquirer did with the product afterward, the ratio drops to 8 of 11, roughly 73%.
- The practical lesson: "startup success rate" isn't one honest number — it depends entirely on whether you're asking "does the product still exist" or "did the people who built it get a real outcome," and those two questions can have very different answers for the same company.
- Visual: the two-ratio comparison graphic (91% vs. 73%), both shown simultaneously with their definitions labeled

### Final CTA + Outro (12:30–14:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — "9 out of 10 startups fail" is close to true under one honest definition and meaningfully overstated under another, and a real audit is the only way to know which one you're actually looking at
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and I might audit a more recent YC batch next"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you recognize any of these 11 companies? Do you think OMGPOP and Xobni should count as 'gone,' or as real successes for their founders? Tell me in the comments — I want to hear the disagreement on this one."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the completed 11-row audit table, then works through each row briskly in order, saving the two-ratio reveal for the final third. Measured, table-forward, treats the video as a genuine reference resource.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the OMGPOP buy-back-refusal story in full before any aggregate numbers appear — a specific human moment (a team trying and failing to buy their own company back) as the entry point into the larger audit.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "11 companies, 1 batch, here's what happened to every single one," rapid-fire through each company in under 60 seconds before the two-ratio reveal. Strong Shorts-extraction potential — the whole audit segment could work as a standalone Short.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Segment 1 (the six quiet shutdowns) if it becomes a flat, monotone list read
- **Fix:** keep pacing brisk here, let the audit table itself carry visual interest, and treat this segment as necessary setup for the more interesting OMGPOP/Xobni/Scribd stories that follow
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim any individual quiet-shutdown company's coverage to a single sentence if retention data shows drop-off in this section specifically
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "two honest numbers" finding in the Hook (already done) and pay it off fully in the final third
- **Recommended visual change:** keep the 11-row audit table visible in a corner throughout the entire video, filling in progressively, so viewers always have the full picture building
