## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **I Audited All 11 Companies From a Real YC Batch. Here's What "9 Out of 10 Gone" Actually Means.** — promises a checkable, rigorous audit and sets up the video's actual nuance (two honest numbers) rather than a flat claim
2. Only 1 of These 11 Y Combinator Companies Still Exists — sharp, specific, verifiable
3. They Sold Their Company for $200 Million. Then Tried to Buy It Back. — strong emotional hook using the OMGPOP standout story
4. 9 Out of 10 YC Companies From This Batch Are Gone. Here's the Actual List. — closest to the original title, now honestly earned
5. Two of These Companies Sold for Real Money. Both Got Killed Within a Year. — strong curiosity, sets up the acquired-vs-survived nuance directly
6. What Happened to YC's Summer 2006 Batch? — clean SEO, lower CTR
7. This Startup Sold for $200M. It Still Got Killed a Year Later. — strong standalone hook, works as a Shorts anchor too
8. What Actually Happens to Startups After an Accelerator — good general-curiosity framing, less specific
9. Y Combinator Success Rate: A Full Batch Audit — best pure-SEO, lowest CTR
10. 11 Companies. One Batch. One Survivor. — punchy, best for thumbnail text pairing

## Recommended Title
**"I Audited All 11 Companies From a Real YC Batch. Here's What '9 Out of 10 Gone' Actually Means."**

Reasoning: promises exactly what the video delivers — a full, checkable, named-company audit — and
sets up the actual finding (two honest, defensible ratios depending on definition) rather than a
flat statistic that invites easy pushback. Works fully truncated on mobile, and the specificity
("11 companies," "a real batch") signals rigor to this channel's fact-check-heavy audience before
they even click.
