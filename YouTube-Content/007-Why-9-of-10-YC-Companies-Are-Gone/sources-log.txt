Sources Log — Package 007: Y Combinator Summer 2006 (S06) Batch Audit
Filled in: 2026-08-13

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
This title required an actual company-by-company audit of one real, named YC batch —
not a single anchor company. The package's own README correctly warned against
conflating "acquired" with "died." This log is the full roster, with each company's
status individually sourced, and the real computed ratio under two different, both
legitimate, definitions of "gone."

BATCH SELECTION REASONING
YC's very first batch (Summer 2005, 8 companies: Reddit, Kiko, TextPayMe, Loopt,
ClickFacts, Infogami, Memamp, Simmery) was considered and rejected — it's YC's own
origin-myth batch (Reddit's success, the famous "Kiko sold on eBay" story), which is
exactly the "known for one mega-success" cherry-picking the package's own README says
to avoid, and it's already heavily covered elsewhere. Summer 2006 (S06) — YC's third
batch, after S05 and W06 — was selected instead: 11 companies, small enough to fully
and honestly audit (not sample or estimate), with no single dominating mega-success
story. This deliberately trades some of the package's own "8-15 years prior to
filming" suggestion (S06 is closer to 20 years old) for something more valuable to
this video's actual premise: a complete, checkable audit instead of a partial one.
One low-quality source claims Scribd/S06 was YC's "inaugural class" — this is WRONG,
do not repeat it on screen; S05 was YC's first batch.

FULL ROSTER — YC Summer 2006 (11 companies)

1. JAMGLUE — online music remixing/mixing community.
   STATUS: (a) Shut down. No trace beyond a 2006-era Wayback Machine snapshot.

2. JUMPCHAT — bridged SMS/email/IM messaging.
   STATUS: (a) Shut down. Raised $125K from YC; did not achieve significant market
   traction.

3. LIKEBETTER — online photo-comparison personality-test game.
   STATUS: (a) Shut down.

4. OMGPOP — founded as "i'minlikewithyou" (a dating site), pivoted to casual social
   gaming under CEO Dan Porter, best known for Draw Something.
   STATUS: (b) Acquired by Zynga in March 2012 for a reported $180M-$200M [VERIFY:
   figures conflict across sources]. Zynga shut down OMGPOP.com and closed its NYC
   office approximately 14 months later, June-August 2013. STANDOUT STORY: reportedly,
   the OMGPOP team tried to buy the site back from Zynga and was refused.

5. POLLGROUND — online polls.
   STATUS: (a) Shut down.

6. SCRIBD — document-sharing platform, pitched under the working name "Social
   Documents" in Nov 2006, later branded "the YouTube of documents." Founders: Trip
   Adler, Tikhon Bernstam, Jared Friedman.
   STATUS: (d) Still independently operating (as of 2026). $105M raised total.
   CEO transition to Tony Grimminck in October 2023, with reported usage/margin
   growth since. THE BATCH'S SOLE SURVIVOR.

7. SHOUTFIT — "fashion-related community."
   STATUS: (a) Shut down.

8. TALKITO — chat/meet-people-on-shared-sites service.
   STATUS: (a) Shut down. Appears to have barely launched.

9. THINKATURE — online collaboration/whiteboard tool.
   STATUS: (a) Shut down.

10. XOBNI — Outlook plugin / email analytics and inbox tool. Founders: Adam Smith and
    Matt Brezina.
    STATUS: (b) Acquired by Yahoo in July 2013 for a reported $30M-$60M+ [VERIFY:
    figures conflict across sources]. Yahoo shut it down approximately one year later,
    July 2014, folding some features into Yahoo Mail. Same acquire-then-kill pattern
    as OMGPOP, on a smaller scale.

11. ZANBAZAAR — community-curated product-recommendation catalogs.
    STATUS: (a) Shut down. [FLAG: single-sourced — confirmed only via YCDB (ycdb.co),
    an aggregator, not corroborated by a second primary source. Present with an
    explicit "unable to fully corroborate" caveat rather than as confirmed fact with
    the same confidence as the other 10 entries.]

SOURCING BASIS
9 of these 11 companies are independently confirmed via TechCrunch's contemporaneous
Nov 9, 2006 article "Who Are The Y Combinator Companies" (techcrunch.com/2006/11/09/
the-y-combinator-companies/), cross-checked against YCDB (ycdb.co)'s batch/status
data. OMGPOP and Zanbazaar are not in that TechCrunch piece: OMGPOP is confirmed
separately via Wikipedia/Grokipedia and later Zynga-acquisition TechCrunch coverage;
Zanbazaar is YCDB-only, flagged above as needing a second source before recording.

THE REAL RATIO — present BOTH definitions on screen, per the package's own
"don't conflate acquired with died" requirement

DEFINITION 1 — "No longer exists in any form today" (inclusive count):
8 outright shutdowns + 2 acquired-then-discontinued by the acquirer (OMGPOP, Xobni)
= 10 of 11 gone ≈ 91%. Only Scribd survives as an independent operating company.
This is close enough to round to "9 out of 10" — the original title's number DOES
hold up, but only under this specific, disclosed definition.

DEFINITION 2 — "Founders/investors got a real outcome" (strict count):
If OMGPOP (~$180-200M) and Xobni (~$30-60M+) are counted as successful outcomes for
their founders and investors — real cash exits — rather than as failures, regardless
of what the acquirer did with the product afterward, the ratio drops to 8 of 11 gone
≈ 73%.

RECOMMENDATION: present both numbers explicitly rather than picking one. This is
exactly the nuance the package's own README demanded, and it's a stronger, more
defensible teaching moment than either number alone — "9 out of 10" survives as an
honest on-screen claim only when the video is explicit about which definition of
"gone" it's using.

BEFORE RECORDING
1. Do not claim S06 was YC's first/inaugural batch — S05 was first; S06 is the third.
2. Present both the 10/11 (91%) and 8/11 (73%) ratios, with their definitions stated
   plainly on screen — do not pick one number and hide the other.
3. Corroborate Zanbazaar's status with a second source before stating it as
   confirmed fact with the same confidence as the other 10 companies.
4. Confirm exact OMGPOP ($180M vs $200M) and Xobni ($30M vs $60M+) acquisition prices
   before citing a single precise figure on screen — sources conflict on both.
5. Do not conflate "acquired" with "failed" without qualification — OMGPOP and Xobni
   were real, successful cash exits for their founders/investors even though the
   products themselves were later killed by their acquirers.
