## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week is different — instead of one company, it's a full audit of an entire cohort: all 11 companies from Y Combinator's Summer 2006 batch (Jamglue, Jumpchat, Likebetter, OMGPOP, Pollground, Scribd, Shoutfit, Talkito, Thinkature, Xobni, Zanbazaar).

8 of the 11 shut down outright with little lasting trace. OMGPOP — which pivoted from a dating site to become the studio behind Draw Something — was acquired by Zynga in March 2012 for a reported $180-200M, then shut down by Zynga roughly 14 months later; reportedly, the original team tried to buy the site back and was refused. Xobni, an Outlook plugin and email-analytics tool, was acquired by Yahoo in July 2013 for a reported $30-60M+, and shut down about a year later. Only Scribd, pitched in 2006 as "Social Documents," still operates independently today.

Counting every company that no longer exists in any form — the 8 shutdowns plus OMGPOP and Xobni — that's 10 of 11, roughly 91%, which is close enough that "9 out of 10" genuinely holds up. But counting OMGPOP's and Xobni's acquisitions as the real, multi-million-dollar exits they were for their founders and investors, the ratio drops to 8 of 11, roughly 73%. Both numbers are presented — this video's actual finding is that "startup success rate" depends on which honest question you're asking.
