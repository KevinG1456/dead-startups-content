# 007 — I Audited All 11 Companies From a Real YC Batch. Here's What "9 Out of 10 Gone" Actually Means.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — a full, individually-verified company-by-company audit of YC's Summer 2006 (S06) batch (TechCrunch's contemporaneous Nov 9, 2006 coverage, YCDB, Wikipedia, Grokipedia — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This is the first package on this channel built as a complete audit table rather than a single-company or matched-pair anchor.

**The real batch:** YC Summer 2006 — 11 companies: Jamglue, Jumpchat, Likebetter, OMGPOP, Pollground, Scribd, Shoutfit, Talkito, Thinkature, Xobni, Zanbazaar. YC's actual first batch (Summer 2005: Reddit, Kiko, etc.) was deliberately not used — it's YC's own origin-myth batch, and using it would be exactly the "known for one mega-success" cherry-picking this package's own original README warned against. S06 is small enough (11 companies) to fully audit rather than sample, even though it's closer to 20 years old than the "8-15 years prior to filming" the original framework suggested — full-audit integrity was weighted above hitting that exact window.

**The real ratio — two honest numbers, not one:** 8 of the 11 companies shut down outright. 2 more (OMGPOP, acquired by Zynga for a reported $180-200M in March 2012; Xobni, acquired by Yahoo for a reported $30-60M+ in July 2013) were acquired and then killed by their acquirers within about a year to 14 months. Only 1 — Scribd — still operates independently today. Counting "no longer exists in any form" (the 8 shutdowns plus the 2 acquired-then-discontinued companies), that's **10 of 11, roughly 91%** — close enough that "9 out of 10" genuinely holds up. But counting acquisitions as real, successful outcomes for the founders and investors who received actual money for them, regardless of what the acquirer later did with the product, the ratio is **8 of 11, roughly 73%**. Both numbers are presented on screen — this is exactly the nuance the original framework demanded, and picking only one would misrepresent the audit.

**Important honesty note:** one company in the roster, Zanbazaar, is confirmed only by a single aggregator source (YCDB), not independently corroborated — flagged on screen with an explicit "unable to fully corroborate" caveat rather than presented with the same confidence as the other 10. The OMGPOP and Xobni acquisition prices are both reported inconsistently across sources ($180M vs. $200M; $30M vs. $60M+) — present as ranges, not single precise figures.

**Framing requirement:** lead with the full, visible audit table — all 11 companies, all statuses, all sources — as the primary evidence, not a voiceover claim. The video's credibility rests on this table being checkable, not on the presenter's assertion.
