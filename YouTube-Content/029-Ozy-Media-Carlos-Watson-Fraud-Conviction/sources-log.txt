FOUNDING
- Ozy Media launched September 2013, founded by Carlos Watson (a former CNN and
  MSNBC anchor) and Samir Rao (co-founder/COO).
- Business: a digital media and entertainment company (website, newsletters, a
  video/TV production arm, live events) positioned as covering "the new and the
  next" — people, ideas, and trends outside the mainstream.
Source: Wikipedia ("Ozy Media"); IMDb/Hollywood Reporter contemporaneous coverage.

FUNDING
- Ozy raised more than $70 million in total funding between 2012 and 2019, from
  investors including Ron Conway, Axel Springer, Marc Lasry, Interlock Partners,
  LionTree, Atinum Investment, iHeartMedia, Venture Souq, Clayton Dubilier & Rice,
  and GSV Capital (per aggregated investor-list reporting — this list of named
  investors is well-corroborated across sources, though a single precise
  round-by-round breakdown was not fully reconstructed in this research pass).
  *** Use "raised more than $70 million" as the safe cumulative figure; do not
  state a more precise single total without further sourcing. ***
- SEPARATELY, and NOT the same figure: the SEC's securities-fraud case alleges
  Watson and co-defendants raised approximately $50 million FROM VICTIM
  INVESTORS specifically "on the basis of fraudulent documents and repeated
  misrepresentations" — this is the SEC's characterization of the FRAUD-TAINTED
  portion of fundraising, not a claim that all $70M+ of Ozy's total lifetime
  funding was fraudulently obtained. *** Keep these two figures explicitly
  distinct on screen — "$70M+ total funding over the company's life" vs. "the SEC
  says roughly $50M was raised through fraudulent misrepresentations" — do not
  merge them into one number. ***
Source: Development Corporate (investor-list summary); DOJ/SEC case materials as
reported by Courthouse News and Forbes.

THE SEPTEMBER 2021 COLLAPSE — precisely detailed
- September 26, 2021: The New York Times published a column by Ben Smith
  revealing that Samir Rao (co-founder/COO) had impersonated a YouTube executive
  during a conference call with Goldman Sachs, in an attempt to help secure a
  $40 million investment from the bank. Goldman Sachs contacted Google/YouTube
  directly to verify, confirmed no such YouTube executive had participated in
  the call, and did not proceed with the investment as a result.
- A former Ozy editor later publicly suggested, on video, that it may actually
  have been Carlos Watson himself impersonating the executive rather than Rao —
  *** this specific claim (Watson, not Rao, on the call) is a single-sourced
  allegation from a former employee, not an established, court-confirmed fact —
  flag as disputed/unconfirmed if used, do not state it as settled. The
  established, multiply-sourced fact is that RAO was identified as the
  impersonator in the original NYT reporting and in subsequent coverage. ***
- Fallout was immediate: on-camera talent and producers associated with Ozy's TV
  ventures (e.g., journalist Katty Kay) publicly resigned from Ozy-affiliated
  roles within days of the NYT story.
- October 1-2, 2021: Ozy Media announced it would shut down entirely, citing the
  mounting allegations about its business practices. *** However, the company did
  NOT actually fully cease operations at this point — it continued in limited
  operation under Watson's direction for roughly another 16 months. Present this
  explicitly as a real reversal, not a clean shutdown — Ozy said it was closing
  in October 2021 and then didn't, which is itself a notable, quotable beat. ***
Source: NYT (Ben Smith column, Sept 26 2021, as summarized/cited by Wikipedia,
Variety, Hollywood Reporter, TheWrap); NPR (Oct 2, 2021, "Ozy shuts down");
Yahoo/Katty Kay resignation coverage.

THE REAL, FINAL COLLAPSE AND CRIMINAL CASE — precisely dated
- February 23, 2023: Carlos Watson was arrested and criminally indicted on
  federal fraud charges (conspiracy to commit securities fraud, conspiracy to
  commit wire fraud, and aggravated identity theft) in the Eastern District of
  New York.
- Ozy Media formally, finally shut down at this point — following the indictment,
  roughly 16 months after its initial (not-actually-final) October 2021
  shutdown announcement.
- The SEC separately alleged that, over several years, Watson and co-defendants
  raised approximately $50 million from victim investors based on fraudulent
  documents and repeated misrepresentations (see funding section above for how
  this figure relates to Ozy's total funding).
- July 2024: Watson was convicted by a jury in Brooklyn on charges including
  conspiracy to commit securities fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, and
  aggravated identity theft. *** This is an ACTUAL JURY CONVICTION — unlike 022
  (Fast/Domm Holland) or 024 (Olive AI/Sean Lane), this channel is factually
  accurate in describing Watson as convicted of fraud, because he was. ***
- December 2024: Watson was sentenced to 116 months (approximately 9 years and 8
  months, commonly rounded to "over 9 years") in federal prison, and separately
  ordered to pay approximately $96 million in restitution and forfeiture.
Source: Forbes (Feb 23 2023, arrest coverage); Variety (Sept 2023, Ozy shuts down
following indictment); Courthouse News Service (Dec 2024 sentencing coverage, "9
years in prison"); DOJ press release (U.S. Attorney's Office, EDNY, official
sentencing announcement, "116 months").

THE COMMUTATION — precisely dated, present factually and without editorializing
- March 28, 2025: President Trump commuted Carlos Watson's prison sentence,
  doing so shortly before Watson was scheduled to surrender to begin serving his
  sentence. As a direct result of the commutation, Watson also no longer owes the
  approximately $96 million in restitution and forfeiture that had been ordered
  at sentencing.
- *** This channel's standard for this specific fact: state the who/what/when
  plainly (President Trump commuted the sentence on March 28, 2025, voiding both
  the prison term and the restitution obligation) and stop there. Do not
  speculate on motive, do not characterize the commutation as deserved or
  undeserved, do not draw a contrast with other commutation/pardon decisions, and
  do not frame this as a statement about the broader justice system. This is a
  documented legal event relevant to "what actually happened to this person," on
  the same factual footing as any other verifiable outcome covered on this
  channel — treat it with the same neutral, sourced tone as any other fact, not
  as a special editorial moment. ***
Source: CBS News (Mar 28 2025); CNBC (Mar 28 2025); Variety (Mar 28 2025,
Watson's own statement about the commutation); Deadline (Mar 28 2025, "hours
before his surrender").
