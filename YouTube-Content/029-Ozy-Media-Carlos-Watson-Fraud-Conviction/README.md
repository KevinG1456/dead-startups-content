# 029 — His Co-Founder Impersonated a YouTube Executive on a Call With Goldman Sachs. He Was Convicted of Fraud. His Sentence Was Commuted.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Carlos Watson and Ozy Media's full documented history and criminal case (NYT, DOJ press releases, court reporting from Courthouse News, Forbes, Variety, CBS News — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). **Different sensitivity profile than 022 (Fast) or 024 (Olive AI):** those packages require careful "not charged or convicted" caution. This package is the opposite — Carlos Watson WAS criminally convicted by a jury, making this channel's second real-conviction fraud story after 005 (HeadSpin). Read `sources-log.txt` in full before touching this package, especially the section on how to handle the 2025 presidential commutation.

**The real case:** Ozy Media — founded 2013 by Carlos Watson (a former CNN/MSNBC anchor) and Samir Rao — raised more than $70 million as a digital media company. On September 26, 2021, the New York Times revealed that co-founder Samir Rao had impersonated a YouTube executive on a conference call with Goldman Sachs, attempting to secure a $40 million investment; Goldman verified with Google that no such executive existed and pulled out. Ozy announced it was shutting down days later — but didn't actually stop operating, continuing under Watson for another 16 months. On February 23, 2023, Watson was arrested and indicted on federal fraud charges; Ozy finally, actually shut down. The SEC alleged Watson and co-defendants raised roughly $50 million from investors "on the basis of fraudulent documents and repeated misrepresentations." In July 2024, a Brooklyn jury convicted Watson of conspiracy to commit securities fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, and aggravated identity theft. He was sentenced in December 2024 to 116 months in prison and ordered to pay roughly $96 million in restitution. On March 28, 2025 — hours before he was due to surrender — President Trump commuted his sentence, also voiding the restitution order.

**Framing requirement:** This is the channel's second real-fraud-conviction story, and it's allowed to say "fraud" and describe the conviction as settled fact, the same way 005 (HeadSpin) does. The commutation must be presented with strict neutrality: state what happened and when, sourced to contemporaneous reporting, with zero editorializing about motive or political dimension — this channel is not a political-commentary channel. The Samir Rao impersonation call is the strongest cold-open material this channel has ever had.
