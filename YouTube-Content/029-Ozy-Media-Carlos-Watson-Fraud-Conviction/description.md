## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Ozy
Media — a digital media and entertainment company launched in September 2013 by Carlos Watson (a
former CNN and MSNBC anchor) and Samir Rao (co-founder/COO), positioned around covering "the new
and the next." Ozy raised more than $70 million in total funding between 2012 and 2019 from
investors including Ron Conway, Axel Springer, Marc Lasry, LionTree, and Clayton Dubilier & Rice,
among others.

On September 26, 2021, the New York Times revealed that Samir Rao had impersonated a YouTube
executive during a conference call with Goldman Sachs, in an attempt to help secure a $40 million
investment. Goldman Sachs contacted Google/YouTube directly to verify, confirmed no such executive
had participated, and did not proceed. Within days, on-camera talent tied to Ozy's TV ventures
publicly resigned. On October 1–2, 2021, Ozy announced it would shut down entirely — but it didn't.
The company continued operating under Watson's direction for roughly another 16 months, until
February 23, 2023, when Watson was arrested and indicted on federal fraud charges in the Eastern
District of New York. That's when Ozy Media actually, finally shut down.

The SEC separately alleged that Watson and co-defendants raised approximately $50 million from
investors on the basis of fraudulent documents and repeated misrepresentations — a figure that
describes the fraud-tainted portion of fundraising specifically, distinct from Ozy's $70M+ total
lifetime funding; this video keeps both figures explicitly separate throughout.

In July 2024, a jury in Brooklyn convicted Watson on charges including conspiracy to commit
securities fraud, conspiracy to commit wire fraud, and aggravated identity theft. In December 2024,
he was sentenced to 116 months (roughly 9 years and 8 months) in federal prison and ordered to pay
approximately $96 million in restitution and forfeiture. On March 28, 2025 — hours before he was
due to surrender — President Trump commuted his sentence, also voiding the restitution order. This
video states that fact plainly, sourced to contemporaneous reporting, without speculating on
motive or taking a position on the decision.

This is the second real, adjudicated startup-fraud conviction on this channel, after package 005
(HeadSpin/Manish Lachwani) — cross-referenced explicitly. Both cases involve an actual jury
conviction or guilty plea, not just an accusation; their final outcomes are very different.
