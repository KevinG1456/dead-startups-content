# 059 — Better Place Raised $850 Million, Peaked at $2.25 Billion. It Sold For $450,000.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Better Place's public funding/press
history (TechCrunch, Fast Company, Quartz, MIT Technology Review, GreenCarReports, Times of Israel —
full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This channel's first EV/battery-swap INFRASTRUCTURE moonshot —
distinct from Bird (028)'s mobile-fleet hardware story, this is a fixed-network capital-intensity
story tied to one exclusive automaker partnership.

**The real case:** Better Place — founded 2007 by Shai Agassi, a former SAP executive who left a
likely future path to SAP's own CEO role to build it — set out to solve electric-vehicle "range
anxiety" and battery cost with a battery-SWAP network instead of charging: robotic stations would
swap a depleted EV battery for a charged one in minutes, with Better Place owning the batteries and
selling "miles" like a mobile-phone plan. It raised approximately $850 million (Israel Corp, HSBC,
General Electric, and Morgan Stanley among its backers) and reached a peak valuation of $2.25
billion in November 2011. The entire network depended on a single exclusive automaker partner,
Renault, which built one purpose-made car — the Fluence Z.E. — compatible with Better Place's swap
stations; no second automaker ever signed on. Launched in Israel and Denmark, the company deployed
fewer than 1,500 vehicles combined (some sources cite as few as ~950) against much larger
projections, while its robotic swap stations cost roughly 4x their original budget (~$2M actual vs.
~$500K budgeted). Founder Shai Agassi was ousted as CEO in October 2012; the company filed for
liquidation in an Israeli court on May 26, 2013, having lost more than $800 million of what it
raised — one of the largest failures in Israeli tech history. A July 2013 deal to sell the remains
for ~$12 million fell through when the buyer failed to make its first payment; the actual final sale,
in November 2013, was for just $450,000.

**Framing requirement:** No fraud or criminal allegations anywhere in this story — the spine is
capital-intensity infrastructure risk plus total dependency on a single automaker's single car
model. The headline arc is the three verified numbers in sequence: $850M raised -> $2.25B peak
valuation -> $450,000 final sale price. Keep the canceled $12M Sunrise Group deal explicitly
separate from the real $450,000 Gnrgy sale that actually closed four months later — don't let the
$12M figure read as the ending.
