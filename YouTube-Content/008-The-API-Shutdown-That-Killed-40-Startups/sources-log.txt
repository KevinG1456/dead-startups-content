Sources Log — Package 008: Meerkat (Twitter) / Viddy (Facebook)
Filled in: 2026-08-13

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
This title was originally a structural framework built around an unsourced "40 startups
in one day" claim — the package's own README explicitly warned not to record that
figure without a citation. Research (multiple targeted searches, see below) found no
real, citable count of "40 startups" killed by any single API/platform event, and no
named, VC-funded startup casualty tied to either the 2018 Google Maps Platform pricing
change or the 2023 Twitter API paywall. What research DID find: two real, independently
verified, VC-funded startups — Meerkat and Viddy — each killed or crippled by a platform
decision, on two different platforms, through two structurally different mechanisms
(a deliberate access cutoff vs. an incidental algorithm change). This package is now
built as a matched pair around those two verified cases instead of an invented number.

MEERKAT (Twitter)

COMPANY / FOUNDER
- Meerkat — live video-streaming app, made by Life On Air, Inc.
- Founder/CEO: Ben Rubin.
Source: TechCrunch, CNN Money.

FUNDING
- $14M raised, announced March 26, 2015. Led by Greylock Partners (partner Josh Elman
  joined the board). Other investors: Aleph, Broadway Video Ventures, CAA Ventures,
  Comcast Ventures, Entree Capital, Raine Ventures, Sherpa, Slow Ventures, Sound
  Ventures, Universal Music Group, UTA, Vayner/RSE, WME.
- IMPORTANT SEQUENCE DETAIL: this $14M closed AFTER the Twitter API cutoff below, not
  before — investors funded Meerkat roughly two weeks after Twitter had already choked
  off its access. Do not imply in the script that Twitter cut off a startup that hadn't
  yet proven it could raise money; the raise happened despite/because of the public
  drama, not before it.
Source: TechCrunch ("Greylock Confirms Its Rumored Investment In Meerkat," Mar 26 2015,
techcrunch.com/2015/03/26/greylock-confirms-its-rumored-investment-in-meerkat/), CNN
Money (money.cnn.com/2015/03/26/technology/meerkat-funding/index.html).

THE API CUTOFF
- Friday, March 13, 2015: Twitter cut off Meerkat's access to Twitter's social graph
  (the feature that let Meerkat users automatically find their Twitter friends already
  using the app). Same day: Twitter announced it was acquiring Meerkat's direct
  livestreaming rival, Periscope. Same day: SXSW 2015 began — the festival where
  Meerkat had just broken out as the buzziest app in tech.
Source: VentureBeat (venturebeat.com/2015/03/13/twitter-cripples-meerkat-by-cutting-off-
access-to-its-social-graph/), TechCrunch (techcrunch.com/2015/03/13/twitter-starts-
breaking-meerkat-features-by-limiting-social-graph-access/), BuzzFeed News
(buzzfeednews.com/article/mathonan/twitter-chokes-off-meerkats-access-to-its-social-
graph), Engadget (engadget.com/2015/03/14/meerkat-cut-off-from-twitter/).

THE OUTCOME
- Meerkat kept operating for another 18 months after the cutoff.
- October 4, 2016: Meerkat app officially removed/killed; Life On Air pivoted the team
  and company to a new product, Houseparty.
Source: TechCrunch ("Meerkat, Star Of 2015, Is Officially Dead," Sep 30 2016,
techcrunch.com/2016/09/30/meerkat-star-of-2015-is-officially-dead/).

THE FORENSIC TWIST — founder's own account complicates the popular narrative
- Ben Rubin told TechCrunch the decision to pivot away from Meerkat was made roughly
  six months after launch, and attributed it primarily to the livestreaming/broadcast
  category itself never becoming "a daily habit" for users — not primarily to Twitter's
  API cutoff.
  [FLAG: this is a single-sourced founder quote, not independently corroborated by a
  second outlet. Present it clearly as "Rubin's own account," not as an independently
  confirmed cause — and note that a founder explaining a shutdown after the fact has
  an obvious incentive to downplay a competitor's role in it. Both readings (API
  cutoff mattered a lot / founder says it wasn't the main reason) should be presented,
  not resolved into a single confident claim.]
Source: TechCrunch (same Sep 30 2016 piece cited above).

VIDDY (Facebook)

COMPANY / FOUNDERS
- Viddy — social video-sharing app ("Instagram for video").
Source: TechCrunch, Recode.

FUNDING
- $2.8M seed: New Enterprise Associates (NEA), Battery Ventures.
- $6M Series A: February 2012.
- $30M Series B: confirmed May 11, 2012. Investors: NEA, Goldman Sachs, Khosla
  Ventures, Battery Ventures. Valued Viddy at $370M.
- Total lifetime funding: reported as roughly $36M–$39.3M across sources — figures
  are inconsistent between outlets. [VERIFY exact total before stating one precise
  lifetime figure on screen; safe to say "more than $36M" without a false-precision
  single number.]
Source: TechCrunch ("Viddy Officially Closes $30M Series B... At A $370M Valuation,"
May 11 2012, techcrunch.com/2012/05/11/viddy-30m-confirmed).

THE MECHANISM — an algorithm/visibility change, NOT an API access revocation
- Viddy grew by riding Facebook's frictionless-sharing Open Graph integration, reaching
  27M+ registered members via automatic Facebook Timeline posts.
- May 31, 2012: Facebook introduced a "10-second rule" crackdown on auto-sharing spam
  (a policy/algorithm change affecting how visible auto-shared Open Graph posts were in
  the News Feed), reducing the visibility of Viddy's (and similar apps') automated
  shares.
  [IMPORTANT DISTINCTION FOR THIS SCRIPT: this was Facebook changing News Feed
  visibility/ranking rules broadly, not Facebook revoking Viddy's API keys or blocking
  Viddy's access outright the way Twitter did to Meerkat. Do not describe this as an
  "API shutdown" in the literal sense — the title's contrast between Meerkat (hard
  access cutoff) and Viddy (visibility/algorithm change) is the actual point being
  made, and collapsing that distinction would misstate what happened.]
Source: TechCrunch ("Facebook Finally Cracks Down On Auto-Sharing Spam With '10-Second
Rule'," May 31 2012, techcrunch.com/2012/05/31/facebook-auto-sharing/).

THE COLLAPSE — real numbers
- Daily active users: ~5M (late April 2012) fell to ~1M (end of May 2012) — a drop of
  more than 80% in about a month.
Source: Los Angeles Business Journal ("Video App Lost Facebook, Focus,"
labusinessjournal.com/media/video-app-lost-facebook-focus/).
- February 25, 2013: Viddy cut 12 staff — more than a third of headcount — following a
  CEO departure.
Source: TechCrunch ("After CEO Departure, Mobile Video Startup Viddy Cuts Staff By More
Than A Third," techcrunch.com/2013/02/25/viddy-cuts-deep).

THE OUTCOME
- Rebranded as "Supernova," acquired by Fullscreen for $20M, announced January 15,
  2014 — down from the $370M valuation just 20 months earlier.
Source: Recode (recode.net/2014/1/15/11622370/viddy-the-instagram-for-video-that-wasnt-
acquired-by-web-video), Variety (variety.com/2014/digital/news/fullscreen-buys-
supernova-mobile-video-startup-formerly-known-as-viddy-1201059711/).
- Final shutdown: December 15, 2014, under Fullscreen ownership.
Source: TechCrunch ("Viddy, Once Touted As 'The Instagram For Video,' Will Shut Down On
December 15," techcrunch.com/2014/11/04/viddy-once-touted-as-the-instagram-for-video-
will-shut-down-on-december-15).

CANDIDATES CHECKED AND DELIBERATELY NOT USED

- Socialcam: same Facebook frictionless-sharing ecosystem, but this is NOT a platform-
  kill story — Socialcam sold to Autodesk for $60M (deal completed Aug 1, 2012, per SEC
  filings) while it was still the #1 Facebook app, then ran three more years under
  Autodesk before shutting down Oct 28, 2015 for unrelated strategic reasons. Do not
  present Socialcam as a casualty of the same mechanism that hit Viddy — if referenced
  at all, use only as a one-line contrast ("the one that cashed out before the
  crackdown caught it").
- Google Maps Platform pricing overhaul (announced May 2018, effective July 16, 2018):
  real, well-documented pricing shock (free tier cut from 25,000 to ~933 map-loads/day;
  some accounts saw effective price increases reported up to ~9,000%), but no specific,
  named, VC-funded startup was found in reliable press whose death is directly
  attributed to it — coverage is diffuse "many small businesses/developers affected."
  Do not use as a primary case; usable only as one-line background context on the
  broader theme if needed.
- 2023 Twitter API paywall (third-party API access blocked ~Jan 12-13, 2023 without
  warning; formalized Feb 2023 with $100–$42,000/month paid tiers): real casualties
  (Tweetbot/Tapbots, Twitterrific/Iconfactory, Echofon, Fenix, Talon) are all small
  indie/bootstrapped developer apps, not VC-funded startups — doesn't fit this
  channel's "funded company" niche. The widely-repeated "25+ third-party clients
  killed overnight" figure traces to a single X/Twitter post (Aakash Gupta), not
  primary journalism — do not cite that count as confirmed.
