# 008 — Twitter Cut One Startup Off On Purpose. Facebook Buried Another By Accident.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around two real, independently verified cases (TechCrunch, VentureBeat, BuzzFeed News, Engadget, CNN Money, Los Angeles Business Journal, Recode, Variety — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). The original "40 startups in one day" framing had no sourceable figure behind it and has been dropped, per the package's own README caveat and channel precedent (see packages 005 and 017). This is a matched-pair mechanism piece, not a single company autopsy: two VC-funded startups, two platforms, two structurally different ways a platform decision can end a company.

**The real cases:** **Meerkat** — a live-streaming app that raised $14M (led by Greylock Partners) — had its access to Twitter's social graph cut off on Friday, March 13, 2015, the exact same day Twitter announced it was acquiring Meerkat's direct rival Periscope, and the same day SXSW began, the festival where Meerkat had just broken out. That's a targeted, deliberate access cutoff. **Viddy** — a video-sharing app that hit a $370M valuation on a $30M Series B (NEA, Goldman Sachs, Khosla Ventures, Battery Ventures) — grew explosively by riding Facebook's frictionless-sharing feature, then watched its daily actives collapse from 5M to 1M in about a month after Facebook's May 2012 "10-second rule" crackdown on auto-sharing spam made its posts far less visible in the News Feed. That's a broad anti-spam algorithm change that hit Viddy as a side effect, not a decision aimed at Viddy. Meerkat shut down 18 months after its cutoff (Oct 2016, pivoting to Houseparty); Viddy fire-sold for $20M in Jan 2014 — down from that $370M peak — and fully shut down in Dec 2014.

**Important honesty note — the forensic twist that makes this a two-part story, not a straight platform-villain narrative:** Meerkat's own founder, Ben Rubin, later told TechCrunch that the pivot decision came roughly six months after launch and that the real reason was the livestreaming category itself never becoming "a daily habit" for users — not primarily Twitter's API cutoff. That's a single-sourced founder account, not independently corroborated, and a founder has an obvious incentive to downplay a rival platform's role after the fact. Present both the documented timeline (cutoff same day as the Periscope deal) and Rubin's own competing explanation — let viewers sit with the contradiction rather than resolving it for them. Separately, do not describe what happened to Viddy as an "API shutdown" in the literal sense — Facebook changed a visibility/ranking algorithm broadly; it did not revoke Viddy's API access the way Twitter revoked Meerkat's. That distinction is the actual content of the video's title contrast ("on purpose" vs. "by accident"), not a detail to gloss over.

**Framing requirement:** Neither company needs an invented number to land. Meerkat: $14M raised, cut off from Twitter's social graph the same day Twitter announced acquiring its direct rival, 18 months to shutdown. Viddy: $370M valuation to a $20M fire sale in 20 months, DAUs down 80%+ in a single month. Lead with the real figures and the real timeline collision (Meerkat) and the real number collapse (Viddy) — the "40 startups" claim is gone and does not need to be replaced with a different invented number.
