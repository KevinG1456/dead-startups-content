## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **Twitter Cut One Startup Off On Purpose. Facebook Buried Another By Accident.** — sets up the video's actual thesis (two failure modes) in one line, works fully truncated on mobile, doesn't require naming companies to create curiosity
2. Twitter Cut Off Meerkat the Same Day It Bought Its Rival — strong, specific, fully sourced, names the company directly
3. Viddy Was Worth $370M. Then Facebook Changed One Rule. — clean number-driven hook for the second case
4. This Startup Hit a $370M Valuation. An Algorithm Change Erased It In a Month. — strong contradiction-driven hook, no name needed for curiosity
5. Meerkat's Own Founder Says Twitter Isn't Why It Really Died. Here's the Timeline. — leads with the forensic twist, strong for retention promise
6. Platform Risk, Told Two Ways: One On Purpose, One By Accident — clean SEO/curiosity hybrid, good secondary cut title
7. What Happened to Meerkat? The Full Story — best pure-SEO, lower CTR
8. They Broke Out at SXSW. Twitter Cut Them Off the Same Weekend. — best for the emotional/nostalgia-driven alt cut
9. How Do You Kill a Startup Without Even Trying? Ask Facebook. — punchy but slightly misleading in isolation (needs the pairing with Meerkat to land the actual thesis)
10. What Happened to Viddy? The Full Story — dry, best for search-only sub-cut

## Recommended Title
**"Twitter Cut One Startup Off On Purpose. Facebook Buried Another By Accident."**

Reasoning: states the video's real thesis — two distinct, verifiable failure modes of platform
dependency — without needing an invented casualty count, works fully truncated on mobile, and
creates curiosity about which companies and platforms are involved without overclaiming a detail
that isn't sourced. Name Meerkat and Viddy directly once the video opens; both carry real
recognition value (Meerkat's SXSW breakout, Viddy's "Instagram for video" era) once viewers are
already watching.
