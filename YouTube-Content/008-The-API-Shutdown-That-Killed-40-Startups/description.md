## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: two real, funded startups — Meerkat and Viddy — killed by two different platforms, through two structurally different mechanisms.

Meerkat, a live-streaming app from Life On Air (founder Ben Rubin), broke out at SXSW 2015 almost entirely by piggybacking on Twitter's social graph. On March 13, 2015 — the same day Twitter announced acquiring Meerkat's direct rival, Periscope — Twitter cut off Meerkat's access to that graph. Two weeks later, Meerkat still raised $14 million led by Greylock Partners. It operated for another 18 months before shutting down in October 2016. But Meerkat's own founder later gave a more complicated account of what really killed it — we cover that directly, flagged clearly as his account, not an independently confirmed fact.

Viddy, a video-sharing app, hit a $370 million valuation on a $30 million Series B in May 2012 (NEA, Goldman Sachs, Khosla Ventures, Battery Ventures) by riding Facebook's frictionless-sharing feature to 27 million+ members. A broad Facebook anti-spam algorithm change in May 2012 — never aimed at Viddy specifically — cut its News Feed visibility, and its daily active users collapsed from roughly 5 million to roughly 1 million in about a month. Viddy fire-sold for $20 million in January 2014 and fully shut down in December 2014.

We close with the actual lesson: platform dependency risk has at least two distinct failure modes — deliberate access cutoffs you can sometimes see coming, and incidental collateral damage from a policy change that was never aimed at you at all.
