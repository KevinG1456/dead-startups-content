# Thumbnail Strategy

One dominant idea per thumbnail — per this channel's standing rule. Never state the $100M price or the
37-day figure as unqualified exact facts in any thumbnail text — treat them as the reported, sourced
numbers they are (the thumbnail text itself can still use them as headline numbers; this note is about
matching the script/description framing, not about avoiding the numbers).

## Thumbnail Intelligence — Winning Concept
- **Core visual:** a bold "$100M" price tag graphic next to a small, grayed-out, faded generic app icon
- **Main subject:** the mismatch between the size of the deal and the size/age of what was actually
  bought
- **Emotion:** irony
- **Background:** dark, minimal, faint envelope/email-icon texture at low opacity
- **Lighting:** single spotlight on the price tag, the app icon left dimmer
- **Camera angle:** N/A (graphic-led)
- **Composition:** price tag large and central-left, faded app icon smaller to the right, bottom-third
  title-safe space reserved
- **Props:** generic price-tag graphic, generic grayed app-icon shape — no real Mailbox or Dropbox logos
- **Thumbnail text:** `$100 MILLION. 37 DAYS OLD. GONE IN 3 YEARS.`
- **Text placement:** bottom third, bold sans-serif, high contrast
- **Color strategy:** gold price tag, desaturated gray app icon, near-black background
- **Contrast strategy:** the visual mismatch between a bold, bright price tag and a small, fading icon
  communicates the entire thesis before any text is read

## Notes on What NOT to Do
- Do not render a real, identifiable photorealistic likeness of Gentry Underwood, Scott Cannon, or Drew
  Houston in any thumbnail — use generic avatar/host imagery or graphic-led concepts only
- Do not put real Mailbox, Dropbox, Orchestra, Carousel, or competitor (Yahoo Mail, Outlook, Google
  Inbox) logos/wordmarks in generated thumbnail art — build the visual concept generically and add any
  verified real branding in the editor with proper sourcing
- Do not present the $100M price or 37-day figure with more certainty in thumbnail text than the script
  gives them — they're reported, sourced numbers, not Dropbox-confirmed exact figures
