# 046 — Dropbox Paid $100 Million for an App That Was 37 Days Old. Under Three Years Later, It Was Gone.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Mailbox's fully documented public history
(TechCrunch, Forbes, Engadget, Fortune, CNN Business, YourStory retrospective — full citations in
`sources-log.txt`). This package replaces the retired dead-saas-30 batch title #19 ("the acquisition
integration that killed both companies") — that pattern was researched twice this session (2026-08-16)
and never landed a real anchor where a funded software company died alongside its acquirer; every real
disaster-merger case found (Mattel/Learning Company, eBay/Skype, News Corp/Myspace) has the acquirer
surviving. Instead of forcing that pattern, this package pulls a fresh, well-documented title from the
SAME batch file's untouched second half: title #27, "Acqui-hired into oblivion: the 'we're thrilled to
announce' posts, revisited 5 years later."

**Relationship to package 003 — read before touching any file in this package:** this channel's package
003 ("The Company Google Killed, Platform Risk 5 Ways") already researched Mailbox once and explicitly
rejected it as an anchor for THAT package's platform-risk pattern, because Mailbox's 2015 shutdown was
Dropbox's own stated strategic pivot, not a platform/competitor action. That finding stands and is not
contradicted here. This package uses Mailbox for a genuinely different question: not "did a platform
kill this company," but "what did the acquirer actually do with what it bought, and how long did the
enthusiasm last."

**The real case:** Gentry Underwood and Scott Cannon originally built a shared to-do-list app called
Orchestra (raising a $5.3M Series A, Nov 2011, Charles River Ventures + SV Angel + Kapor Capital +
CrunchFund), then pivoted to Mailbox — an iOS email-triage app with swipe-to-archive, swipe-to-delete,
and "snooze" gestures nobody had combined before. A viral demo video and a "velvet rope" virtual
waitlist (800,000+ signups, a live position counter) built massive pre-launch hype; the app opened to
the public App Store in February 2013. On March 15, 2013 — reported as just 37 days after launch —
Dropbox acquired Mailbox for a reported ~$100 million, all 13 employees joining Dropbox, with Drew
Houston's blog post framing the deal enthusiastically. An Android version and a Mac app were promised in
2014, but real development on Mailbox effectively stalled after that, while competitors (Yahoo Mail,
Outlook, Google's own Inbox by Gmail) copied its signature gestures into their own products. On December
7, 2015, Dropbox announced it was shutting down both Mailbox and its separate Carousel photo app to
refocus on collaboration products. Mailbox actually closed February 26, 2016 — just under three years
after the acquisition that once made headlines for its enthusiasm and speed.

**Framing requirement:** This is NOT a "both companies died" story — Dropbox is alive and independently
operating; never imply otherwise. Present the ~$100M price and the "37 days" launch-to-acquisition gap
as reported figures (TechCrunch; YourStory retrospective), not company-confirmed exact numbers. No
fraud/misconduct framing anywhere — this is a neutral product-strategy and acquisition-integration
story.
