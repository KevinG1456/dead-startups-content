## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Mailbox —
one of the fastest, most hyped acquisitions of 2013, and what actually happened to the product in the
years after the deal closed.

Gentry Underwood and Scott Cannon originally built Orchestra, a shared to-do-list app, raising a $5.3
million Series A in November 2011. The team pivoted to Mailbox — an iOS email app with swipe-to-archive,
swipe-to-delete, and "snooze" gestures nobody had combined before. A December 2012 demo video went viral,
and instead of releasing immediately, Mailbox used a "velvet rope" virtual waitlist — over 800,000
people signed up just to try it. The app opened to the public App Store in February 2013.

Reportedly just 37 days after that launch, Dropbox announced it was acquiring Mailbox for a reported
price around $100 million. All 13 employees joined Dropbox; Mailbox kept running as a standalone app at
first, and Drew Houston's blog post framed the deal enthusiastically.

Here's what happened next: an Android version and a Mac app were promised in 2014, but real development
on Mailbox stalled. Competing products — Yahoo Mail, Microsoft Outlook, and Google's own new "Inbox by
Gmail" — adopted swipe-and-snooze gestures similar to Mailbox's own signature feature, while Mailbox
itself sat largely un-updated.

On December 7, 2015, Dropbox announced it was shutting down both Mailbox and its separate Carousel photo
app, to refocus on collaboration products. Mailbox actually closed February 26, 2016 — just under three
years after the acquisition that once made headlines for its speed and enthusiasm.
