THE FOUNDING AND PRE-ACQUISITION HISTORY — precisely sourced
- The company behind Mailbox was originally called Orchestra, Inc., co-founded by Gentry Underwood and
  Scott Cannon. Orchestra's first product was a shared to-do list app (also called "Orchestra"),
  intended to get people to stop using email as a to-do list.
- Orchestra raised a $5.3 million Series A round in November 2011, led by Charles River Ventures, with
  participation from SV Angel, Kapor Capital, and CrunchFund.
- The team pivoted from the Orchestra to-do app to Mailbox, an iOS email-triage client, in 2012.
Source: TechCrunch ("Dropbox Buys Mailbox, All 13 Employees Joining And App Will Remain Separate," Mar
15 2013); Fierce Network retrospective ("Dropbox shutters Mailbox, exposing the perils of mobile app
acquisition"); YourStory retrospective ("$100M in 37 Days: Mailbox's Journey to Fame, Failure & Startup
Lessons," 2023).

THE LAUNCH — precisely sourced
- Mailbox previewed via a viral demo video in December 2012, showcasing swipe-to-archive, swipe-to-
  delete, and "snooze" (defer a message to reappear later — Later Today, This Evening, This Weekend)
  gestures never seen together in a mainstream email app before. The video drew 100,000 views within
  four hours of release.
- Instead of releasing immediately, Mailbox used a "velvet rope" virtual waitlist: downloading the app
  placed a user in a live queue, showing their position and the people ahead/behind them. Over 800,000
  people signed up for the waitlist; reported waitlist/reservation totals reached 1.3 million within
  about a month, with the app said to be handling up to 60 million messages/day even before full public
  availability.
- The app opened to the public App Store in February 2013, with the virtual queue continuing to admit
  waitlisted users gradually rather than releasing everyone at once.
Source: Fortune ("How Mailbox Became an Instant Sensation," Feb 12 2013); TechCrunch ("Mailbox's
Virtual Queue Succeeds In The Waiting Game...," Feb 9 2013); YourStory retrospective.

THE ACQUISITION — precisely sourced, this package's central hook
- March 15, 2013: Dropbox announced it was acquiring Mailbox. Dropbox co-founder Drew Houston's official
  blog post framed the deal enthusiastically, saying the goals of the two companies aligned closely —
  both aimed to improve on legacy technology people use every day. *** [VERIFY]: the specific phrase
  "we're thrilled" was not confirmed verbatim in this research pass in the primary Dropbox blog post
  itself — multiple retrospectives paraphrase enthusiastic language, and Drew Houston is quoted as
  saying Dropbox was "excited to welcome the Mailbox team" in secondary coverage, but the exact primary-
  source wording should be re-verified against Dropbox's original 2013 blog post before using a direct
  quotation on screen. ***
- The reported price was approximately $100 million (TechCrunch: "Mailbox Cost Dropbox Around $100
  Million," Mar 15 2013 — a reported figure, not a company-confirmed exact number).
- All 13 Mailbox/Orchestra employees joined Dropbox as part of the deal; Mailbox continued operating as
  a standalone app under Dropbox ownership rather than being immediately absorbed.
- THE CENTRAL HOOK, PRECISELY SOURCED: multiple retrospectives (most directly, YourStory's "$100M in 37
  Days" headline) report that only 37 days passed between Mailbox's public launch and the announced
  acquisition. *** [VERIFY]: the exact start-date basis for "37 days" (public App Store launch in early
  February 2013 vs. the December 2012 preview video) was not independently re-derived in this research
  pass from primary sources — present as "reported as 37 days" or "about five weeks," attributed to the
  retrospective source, rather than as an independently re-verified exact day-count. ***
- Gentry Underwood became head of product and design for Dropbox's collaboration products; Scott Cannon
  became head of engineering for Dropbox's Paper product — both integrated into Dropbox's broader
  product organization rather than continuing to run Mailbox as a discrete team.
Source: TechCrunch ("Mailbox Cost Dropbox Around $100 Million," Mar 15 2013; "Dropbox Buys Mailbox, All
13 Employees Joining And App Will Remain Separate," Mar 15 2013); CNN Business ("Dropbox acquires
buzzworthy app Mailbox," Mar 15 2013); YourStory retrospective; Crunchbase acquisition record.

THE STALL — precisely sourced
- An Android version and a Mac desktop app were announced/promised in 2014. Development on new Mailbox
  features and platforms effectively stalled after that point, according to retrospective coverage.
- During this stall, competing email products — Yahoo Mail, Microsoft Outlook, and Google's own "Inbox
  by Gmail" — incorporated Mailbox-style swipe and snooze gestures into their own apps, eroding
  Mailbox's core differentiation while it sat largely un-updated.
- *** [VERIFY]: no on-record internal Dropbox or Mailbox-team explanation for the specific internal
  reasons development slowed was found in this research pass — the "competitors copied the features
  while Mailbox stalled" framing is a retrospective/journalistic characterization (Fierce Network,
  TechRadar), not a company admission of neglect. Present as reported analysis, not confirmed internal
  cause. ***
Source: Fierce Network retrospective; TechRadar ("Dropbox is dropping its Carousel and Mailbox apps");
YourStory retrospective.

THE SHUTDOWN — precisely dated
- December 7, 2015: Dropbox announced it was shutting down both Mailbox and its separate Carousel photo
  app, publicly stating the decision was to refocus company resources on collaboration and file-sharing
  products — most notably the then-new Paper app. This was Dropbox's own stated strategic rationale, not
  attributed to any external platform action (confirms and does not contradict package 003's earlier
  finding on this point).
- Mailbox actually closed February 26, 2016. Carousel closed separately, March 31, 2016. These are two
  distinct dates for two distinct products — do not conflate them or state a single shutdown date for
  both.
- Total elapsed time from the March 15, 2013 acquisition to the app's actual February 26, 2016 closure:
  just under three years (about 2 years, 11 months).
Source: Forbes (Brian Solomon, "Dropbox Gives Up On Mailbox, Carousel," Dec 7 2015); Engadget ("Dropbox
is killing its Carousel and Mailbox apps," Dec 7 2015); GSMArena coverage; TechRadar coverage.
