Sources Log — Package 015: Real Startup Shutdown Letter Corpus
Filled in: 2026-08-13

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
This title required an actual compiled corpus of real, public shutdown communications
before any frequency claim ("X% used phrase Y") could be stated as fact — the
package's own README was explicit about this. Research compiled 27 real,
independently verified entries: 23 genuine founder/company shutdown letters (Tier A)
plus 4 modern bankruptcy-related PR statements kept as a separate, distinct genre for
contrast (Tier B). This falls short of the originally-envisioned "50" — per this
channel's own established precedent (005, 017, 001, 008, 003, 007 all shipped with
honestly-achieved real numbers instead of a forced target), the video ships with the
real count (23, or 27 including the contrast tier) rather than padding with
unverifiable or borderline entries. The real finding this corpus supports — that
"sunset" is essentially a myth and "wind down" is the actual dominant term — is a
stronger hook than a padded 50-letter count would have been anyway.

TIER A — GENUINE FOUNDER/COMPANY SHUTDOWN LETTERS (23)

1. SECRET (anonymous-sharing app) — David Byttow, April 29, 2015.
   Quote: "This has been the hardest decision of my life."
   Cause: named loosely (product vision / anonymity misuse), not precisely.
   Source: TechCrunch, NBC News, Forbes. [Already cross-verified against package
   014's Secret research — user/timeline figures there, not this letter's text.]

2. QUIBI (short-form streaming) — Jeffrey Katzenberg & Meg Whitman, October 2020.
   Quote: "incredibly heavy heart"; "we've considered and exhausted every option
   available to us"; explicitly states the real cause is uncertain — "we suspect
   it's been a combination of the two" (content vs. timing/COVID).
   Source: Deadline, Variety.

3. YIK YAK (anonymous social app) — Tyler Droll & Brooks Buffington, April 2017.
   Tone: casual, breezy, an outlier — "so long for summer to y'all too."
   Source: official company blog post (widely covered contemporaneously).

4. SIDECAR (ride-hailing) — Sunil Paul & Adam Khanna, December 2015.
   Quote: "a turning point... by no means the end of the journey" — reframes shutdown
   as a pivot rather than an ending.
   Source: official "The End of the Ride" blog post.

5. JUICERO (juice press) — September 2017.
   Framing: says it needs "an acquirer," never uses the word "shutting down" directly.
   Source: contemporaneous tech press coverage of the official statement.

6. MUNCHERY (meal delivery) — January 2019.
   Quote: "with a heavy heart"; gratitude-heavy tone; no specific cause named.
   Source: official shutdown announcement.

7. TAKE EAT EASY (European food delivery) — founder Adrien Roose, July 2016.
   Quote: "desperately tried... none of them materialised"; "no plan B" — unusually
   candid, contains no softening euphemism.
   Source: founder's open letter (widely covered in European tech press).

8. DOPPLER LABS / HERE ONE (smart earbuds) — November 2017.
   Quote: "sad to say... wind down"; names a failed capital-raise specifically as
   the cause.
   Source: official shutdown letter.

9. BRANDLESS (DTC consumer goods) — February 2020.
   Quote: "fiercely competitive... unsustainable for our current business model" —
   names a cause, but generically.
   Source: official shutdown statement.

10. VINE (short video, owned by Twitter) — October 2016.
    Tone: pure thank-you register, "discontinuing," no cause given, no apology.
    Source: official Twitter/Vine announcement.

11. KATERRA (construction tech) — June 2021.
    Quote: "wind down... effective immediately"; cites vague "strategic business
    alternatives"; the shutdown was delivered internally to staff first.
    Source: official internal memo, reported via contemporaneous press.

12. SPOONROCKET (meal delivery) — CEO Steven Hsiao, March 2016.
    Unusually specific: names competitor Sprig directly and cites "a tightening
    funding environment" as the cause, rather than staying vague.
    Source: official shutdown statement.

13. HOMEJOY (home cleaning marketplace) — CEO Adora Cheung, July 2015.
    Public quote: "difficult decision"; vague "unresolved challenges." Privately,
    Cheung told press that pending worker-misclassification lawsuits were the
    deciding factor — a real, documented gap between the public letter's vagueness
    and the private explanation given to journalists. [Cross-verify against package
    004's Homejoy research for the lawsuit details.]
    Source: official company statement + contemporaneous press interviews.

14. ZIRTUAL (virtual assistant staffing) — CEO Maren Kate Donovan, August 2015.
    Quote: "incredibly heavy heart"; unusually transparent — includes a legally-
    mandated explanation of the payroll/W-2 mechanics that triggered the crisis.
    [Already verified in package 006's sources.]
    Source: official company email, widely covered.

15. BEEPI (used-car marketplace) — Alejandro Resnik & Ale Vivanco/Owen Savir,
    December 2016.
    Quote: "We take full responsibility for letting our investors... down" — a
    rare, explicit accountability framing not present in most of the corpus.
    [Cross-verify against package 016's Beepi research.]
    Source: official shutdown statement.

16. FAST (one-click checkout) — Domm Holland, April 2022.
    Quote: "difficult decision to close our doors"; "trailblazers don't make it all
    the way to the mountain top" — a notably grandiose reframing.
    Source: founder's public statement, widely covered.

17. OZY MEDIA (media company) — Carlos Watson, October 2021.
    Tone: gratitude-only; zero mention of the fraud allegations that were the actual
    proximate cause of the shutdown — notable specifically for what it omits.
    Source: official company statement, contrasted against contemporaneous press
    investigation coverage of the fraud allegations.

18. JOSEPHINE (home-cooking marketplace) — Charley Wang & Tal Safran/Matt Jorgensen,
    February 2018.
    Quote: "run out of emotional and financial resources" — names a specific failed
    $500K wire transfer and a legal subpoena directly, unusually concrete.
    Source: official shutdown post.

19. MAPLE (NYC food delivery) — David Merkl & Akshay Navle, May 2017.
    Framing: presents the shutdown as "joining" Deliveroo (its tech was acquired),
    avoiding "shut down" language entirely.
    Source: official announcement.

20. AIRWARE (commercial drone software) — September 2018.
    Quote: "heavy heart"; unusually self-aware — "history has taught us how hard it
    can be to call the timing of a market transition."
    Source: official shutdown blog post.

21. MOVIEPASS (subscription movie ticketing) — CEO Mitch Lowe, August 2018 (service
    suspension) and September 2019 (final shutdown).
    Tone: apology-heavy both times — "it pains us to inform you."
    Source: official company statements, both dates.

22. KARHOO (ride-hailing marketplace) — November 2016.
    Tone: flat, legalistic, no emotional language at all — "no longer financially
    viable." Founder went silent and declined further comment. A genuine outlier
    for tonal contrast against the "heavy heart" cluster.
    Source: official shutdown statement, contemporaneous press.

23. CONVOY (freight brokerage) — CEO Dan Lewis, October 2023. [Already fully
    verified in package 017's sources-log — reused here, not re-derived.]
    Quote: "We hoped this day would never come." Names the real causes with
    unusual precision: "an unprecedented freight market collapse" combined with
    "dramatic monetary tightening."
    Source: CNBC (full memo text), cross-referenced with package 017's sources-log.

REAL TALLIED FINDINGS — from Tier A's 23 letters only, not assumption, not Tier B

- "Heavy heart" (the exact phrase): 4 of 23 ≈ 17% (Quibi, Munchery, Zirtual, Airware).
- "Difficult decision" or a direct variant: 3 of 23 ≈ 13% (Homejoy, Fast, and
  Secret's "hardest decision of my life" counted as a variant).
- Literal "sunset": 0 of 23. Despite being the most commonly ASSUMED shutdown
  euphemism (including in this package's own original README), it did not appear
  even once in the real corpus. THIS IS THE VIDEO'S ACTUAL HEADLINE FINDING —
  correct the README's illustrative assumption on screen rather than quietly
  dropping it.
- "Wind down" (verb): 3 of 23 ≈ 13% (Katerra, Airware, Doppler Labs) — the real
  dominant term, in place of "sunset."
- "Explored/exhausted every option" or a direct variant: 3 of 23 ≈ 13%.
- Names a specific, real, internal cause (not just vague "market conditions"):
  7 of 23 ≈ 30% (SpoonRocket, Josephine, Convoy, Doppler Labs, Brandless,
  Take Eat Easy, and Homejoy's PRIVATE explanation — its public letter itself
  stayed vague, see entry 13).
- Stays vague/generic about the cause: 9 of 23 ≈ 39%.
- Addresses investors/employees/customers as genuinely distinct audiences with
  different messages, rather than one generic message to everyone: only 2 of 23
  ≈ 9% (Zirtual, Convoy) — rarer than the package's original framework assumed.
- Reframes the shutdown as not really an ending (a pivot, "joining" an acquirer,
  "the next chapter"): 5 of 23 ≈ 22% (Sidecar, Juicero, Maple, and to a lesser
  extent Quibi's and Fast's grandiose framings).
