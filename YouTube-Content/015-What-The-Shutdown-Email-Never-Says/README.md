# 015 — I Read 23 Real Startup Shutdown Letters. Nobody Actually Said "Sunset."

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around a real, independently verified corpus of 23 genuine startup shutdown letters (Tier A) plus 4 modern bankruptcy-statement letters kept separate for contrast (Tier B). Full letter-by-letter citations in `sources-log.txt`. The originally-envisioned "50 farewell letters" was never achievable with real, verifiable sources — per this channel's own established precedent (005, 017, 001, 008, 003, 007 all shipped with honestly-achieved real numbers instead of a forced target), this package ships with the real count instead of padding it.

**The real corpus:** 23 genuine founder/company shutdown communications, spanning Secret (2015) through Convoy (2023) — including Quibi, Yik Yak, Sidecar, Juicero, Munchery, Take Eat Easy, Doppler Labs, Brandless, Vine, Katerra, SpoonRocket, Homejoy, Zirtual, Beepi, Fast, Ozy Media, Josephine, Maple, Airware, MoviePass, Karhoo, and Convoy. A separate set of 4 modern bankruptcy-filing PR statements (WeWork, Bird, 23andMe, and the weaker-fit Managed by Q) is kept distinct — different genre, different euphemism patterns, not folded into the same percentages.

**The real headline finding:** "sunset" — the single most commonly assumed startup-shutdown euphemism, including in this package's own original research framework — appears **zero times** across the real 23-letter corpus. The actual dominant term is "wind down" (3 of 23). Other real findings: "heavy heart" appears in 4 of 23 (17%); a specific, real internal cause is named in 7 of 23 (30%) while 9 of 23 (39%) stay vague; only 2 of 23 (9%) address investors, employees, and customers as genuinely distinct audiences rather than one generic message to everyone. This is a stronger, more surprising hook than a padded "50 letters, X% used Y phrase" video would have delivered — the video should lead with the correction, not bury it.

**Important honesty note:** Homejoy's public shutdown letter stayed vague ("difficult decision," "unresolved challenges"), while CEO Adora Cheung privately told journalists that pending worker-misclassification lawsuits were the real deciding factor — a documented gap between the public letter and the private explanation that is itself a perfect illustration of this video's own thesis. Present this distinction explicitly rather than implying the lawsuits were named in the public letter itself. Managed by Q (Tier B) is the weakest-fit entry in the whole corpus — a post-acquisition mass layoff, not really a farewell letter — flagged for possible cutting rather than used to pad the count.

**Framing requirement:** lead with the corrected "sunset never appears" finding as the video's actual hook, not a generic "let's mock corporate euphemisms" framing — this is a genuine, checkable research correction, and stating it plainly is what makes the whole video defensible to this channel's fact-check-heavy audience.
