# Script Package

Anchored on a real, verified corpus of 23 genuine startup shutdown letters (Tier A) plus 4
bankruptcy-statement letters kept separate for contrast (Tier B) — full citations in
`sources-log.txt`. Do not claim "50 letters." Lead with the real finding: "sunset" appears 0/23
times; "wind down" is the actual dominant term (3/23). Keep Tier A's percentages separate from
Tier B throughout. Present Homejoy's public/private gap accurately — the lawsuits were named
privately to press, not in the public letter itself.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 13–16 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "Everyone assumes startups say 'we're sunsetting the product.' I read 23 real shutdown letters. Not one of them said it."
- Visual: the word "SUNSET" appears large, a hard red strikethrough slashes through it, "WIND DOWN" fades in below
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This is a real, counted corpus. Every letter, every source, on screen."

### Hook (0:15–0:55)
- VO: "'Sunset' is the joke everyone makes about corporate shutdown-speak. I actually went and compiled real startup shutdown letters — not a guess, not a vibe, an actual counted corpus — to find out what these letters really say. The answer corrects an assumption I had too."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll know the real, counted patterns across 23 real shutdown letters, the specific gap between what one founder said publicly and privately, and what actually works if you ever have to write one of these yourself."

### Introduction (0:55–1:45)
- Method statement: this video is built from 23 independently verified, real founder/company shutdown letters, spanning 2015 to 2023 — full source list on screen and linked in the description
- One-sentence framing: this started as a plan for 50 letters; 23 real, verifiable ones is the honest number, and it's enough to find a real pattern — padding it further would have meant using unverifiable examples

### The Real Corpus, In Motion (1:45–7:00)
- Segment 1 — The "Heavy Heart" Cluster (1:45–3:00): Quibi, Munchery, Zirtual, and Airware all use some version of "heavy heart" — 4 of 23, about 17%. Show all four quotes on screen together. Visual: four quote cards stacking.
- Segment 2 — The Reframe Cluster (3:00–4:15): Sidecar calls its shutdown "a turning point... by no means the end of the journey." Juicero frames it as needing "an acquirer," never says "shutting down." Maple frames it as "joining" Deliveroo. 5 of 23 letters do some version of this — reframing an ending as not really an ending. Visual: "ending" text morphing into "the next chapter" text.
- Segment 3 — The Outliers (4:15–5:30): Yik Yak's breezy, casual tone ("so long for summer to y'all too") and Karhoo's flat, legalistic "no longer financially viable" sit at opposite ends of the emotional spectrum from everything else in the corpus. Visual: a tone spectrum graphic with Yik Yak and Karhoo at opposite ends.
- Segment 4 — The Ones That Actually Said Something (5:30–7:00): Take Eat Easy's founder wrote "desperately tried... none of them materialised... no plan B" — no euphemism at all. Beepi's founders wrote "we take full responsibility for letting our investors... down." SpoonRocket and Josephine both named specific, real causes (a competitor, a failed wire transfer) instead of staying vague. Convoy's Dan Lewis named the actual causes precisely. Visual: these quotes displayed with visibly less euphemistic language highlighted.

### The Homejoy Gap (7:00–8:30)
- Homejoy's public 2015 letter said "difficult decision" and cited vague "unresolved challenges." Privately, CEO Adora Cheung told journalists that pending worker-misclassification lawsuits were the real deciding factor. On-screen lower third: "This detail was NOT in the public letter — it came out in separate press interviews." This is the clearest single example in the corpus of the gap between what a shutdown letter says and what actually happened. Visual: split screen, public letter text on one side, private quote on the other.
- Mid-video CTA (~8:00): "If you ever have to write one of these yourself, or you just want to see the full real data, the next part is the actual finding. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Real Numbers — Counted, Not Assumed (8:30–11:30)
- Reveal the actual tallies from the real 23-letter corpus, one at a time, building a bar-chart graphic: "sunset" (0/23), "wind down" (3/23), "heavy heart" (4/23), "difficult decision" or a variant (3/23), "explored/exhausted every option" or a variant (3/23), names a specific real cause (7/23, 30%), stays vague about the cause (9/23, 39%), addresses investors/employees/customers as genuinely distinct audiences (2/23, 9%).
- The headline correction, stated plainly: "sunset" — the word everyone assumes, including the word this video's own original research plan assumed — appears zero times in this real corpus. "Wind down" is the actual dominant term.
- Visual: the full bar-chart graphic, "sunset" bar visibly at zero next to the others

### A Different Genre — Bankruptcy Statements (11:30–12:30)
- Briefly contrast Tier A's founder farewell letters against 4 modern bankruptcy-filing statements — WeWork ("no change to the way we operate"), Bird ("a significant milestone in Bird's transformation"), 23andMe ("I equally take accountability... my belief in the company... is unwavering") — a structurally different genre that minimizes the event rather than mourning it. Kept explicitly separate from the Tier A percentages above. Visual: a visually distinct "different genre" graphic treatment.

### Final CTA + Outro (12:30–14:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — the corporate-euphemism cliché everyone assumes about startup shutdowns turns out to be mostly wrong when you actually check, and the letters that say the least ("unresolved challenges," vague market language) usually have the most interesting private story behind them
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and I'll keep adding real letters to this corpus over time"
- Engagement prompt: "Know a real startup shutdown letter that should be added to this list? Drop it in the comments with a source — I'll pin the best additions."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the completed bar-chart graphic (all the real tallies at once), then works backward through specific letter examples to illustrate each number. Measured, graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine reference resource.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the Homejoy public/private gap in full before any aggregate numbers appear — a specific human moment (a founder's letter vs. her private explanation) as the entry point into the larger corpus.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "23 real shutdown letters in under 90 seconds," rapid-fire through the most quotable lines before the data reveal. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Segment 1-4 of "The Real Corpus, In Motion" if it starts to feel like a flat list read across too many companies
- **Fix:** group letters by pattern (heavy-heart cluster, reframe cluster, outliers, candid ones) rather than going company-by-company in a flat sequence — pattern-grouping keeps it feeling like a real analysis, not a list
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim any individual letter's coverage to a single quote card if retention data shows drop-off in this section
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "sunset never appears" finding in the cold open (already done) and hold the full numeric reveal for the final third
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small "23 REAL LETTERS" counter visible in a corner throughout, so viewers always know the scale of the real corpus behind the claims
