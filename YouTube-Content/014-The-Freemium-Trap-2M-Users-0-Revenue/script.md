## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–14 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "How does an app get 15 million users and still go out of business?"
- Visual: large user-count figure counts up rapidly, hard cut to a bright red "$0" with a heavy SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This actually happened. In about 15 months."

### Hook (0:15–0:45)
- VO: "This app reached roughly 15 million users and significant cultural buzz. It never built a working revenue model. About 15 months after launch, it was gone."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand exactly why massive growth never turned into a business — and what actually separates freemium apps that make it from the ones that don't."

### Introduction (0:45–1:30)
- Method statement: reconstructed from press coverage, funding records, and shutdown reporting — sourced on screen
- One-sentence framing: this is the freemium trap — growth that never converts to revenue before the money runs out

### Story — Rise (1:30–4:00)
- Founding story, the product concept, the viral growth phase, press coverage and cultural moment
- Visual: press clippings, generic app-interface mockups, growth-chart animation
- Curiosity gap: tease the specific reason monetization never happened without fully explaining it yet

### Main Content — The Autopsy (4:00–9:30)
- Segment 1: the growth story — how fast the user base grew, what drove it
- Segment 2: the monetization gap — why no revenue model was ever built (reveal the teased reason: e.g., product type resistant to obvious monetization, cultural/product reasons ads or paid features felt wrong, or simply lack of prioritization)
- Segment 3: the burn rate — how quickly funding ran out relative to the growth curve
- Mid-video CTA (~6:30): "If you're building a consumer product with a 'we'll monetize later' plan, the next part is exactly what you need. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- Graphics: growth-vs-revenue chart contrast, burn-rate countdown

### Story — The Fall (9:30–11:30)
- The shutdown, what happened to remaining funds/investors, what users experienced when the app disappeared
- Slow the pacing deliberately here for the nostalgia/human element

### Final CTA + Outro (11:30–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — user growth and revenue are different problems requiring different plans, and "we'll figure out monetization later" needs a real deadline and thesis, not just optimism
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you use this app? What do you remember about it? Comment below."
- End screen: two related autopsies + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the growth-vs-revenue chart contrast fully populated, then rewinds to explain the monetization gap and burn rate. Measured, graphics-forward.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the nostalgia moment — what using the app was actually like at its peak — before any numbers appear. Slower pacing, warmer tone, cinematic.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "3 signs this app was heading toward the freemium trap," counting down using the same facts. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

---

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** Main Content section (4:00–9:30) if the monetization-gap explanation becomes overly abstract
- **Fix:** ground the explanation in specific, concrete product decisions rather than general business theory; cut every 20–30 seconds between talking head, B-roll, and animated graphic
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim the Rise section if press coverage of the growth phase is thin
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** move the "why no revenue model" tease earlier if early-retention data shows drop-off before 2:00
- **Recommended visual change:** show the growth-vs-revenue chart partially in the cold open, fully revealed only at the end
