## What "Freemium Trap" Means
The freemium trap describes a specific failure pattern: a product achieves genuine user-growth success under a free model, with an implicit or explicit plan to introduce monetization (premium features, advertising, or another revenue stream) later — but that plan never materializes in time, or at all, before the company's funding runs out.

## What Separates Freemium Success From the Freemium Trap
Some of the most successful companies in tech (Slack, Dropbox, Spotify, among others) also began with free-first models. What distinguishes them is typically: a clear, tested thesis for what premium value looks like, willingness to experiment with monetization relatively early (even in small ways) rather than deferring it entirely, and a product with natural upgrade paths (more storage, more seats, more features) that don't compromise the core free experience.

## The Lesson
Massive user growth is a real achievement, but it is not, on its own, a business. Founders pursuing a "growth first, monetize later" strategy should treat monetization as an explicit, timed experiment — not an assumption to revisit once growth slows down, by which point runway is often already critically short.
