# 014 — The Freemium Trap: 15M Users, $0 Revenue, Dead in 15 Months

**Package status:** Production-ready with a confirmed real candidate. **Secret** — an anonymous social-sharing app — is the strongest fit: it launched January 30, 2014, grew to roughly **15 million registered users/downloads** at peak with significant media buzz, never built a monetization/revenue model, and shut down April 29, 2015 — **roughly 15 months** after launch, with the company publicly returning remaining investor capital. Figures confirmed against TechCrunch, CNN, and Fortune coverage; the title has been corrected from earlier draft figures ("2M users," "18 months") which did not match the sourced record.

**Alternative candidates to consider:** Yik Yak (larger scale, but operated closer to 4 years before shutting down in 2017 — doesn't fit the "15 months" timeframe as cleanly), Meerkat (livestreaming app, viral then rapidly overtaken by Twitter's Periscope, pivoted rather than shut down outright).

**Framing note:** the "~15M users" figure refers to reported registered users/downloads — press coverage of apps like Secret sometimes conflates installs, active users, and peak concurrent users, so keep the on-screen framing to the defensible "registered users/downloads" metric rather than implying a specific daily-active-user number.

## 2. Viewer Psychology
- **Curiosity triggers:** whether the viewer remembers/used the app; how something with such massive engagement never built a business model
- **Emotional triggers:** nostalgia, disbelief at the scale-to-zero-revenue gap, validation of monetization-planning instincts
- **Viewer objections:** "growth-first, monetize-later is a legitimate strategy that works for some companies" — address directly by distinguishing cases where it works from cases where it doesn't
- **Motivation to watch:** founders building consumer apps want monetization-timing lessons; general audience wants the nostalgia payoff plus the "what actually happened" reveal
- **Expected comments:** "I used to use this app," comparisons to other free-then-dead apps, debate over whether the app could have been monetized successfully with different choices

## 3. Competitor Analysis
- **Similar creators:** "remember this app" nostalgia content covers apps like Secret and Yik Yak, but rarely with a monetization-focused financial breakdown
- **Content gap:** almost nobody pairs the nostalgia hook with a rigorous "why didn't this convert to revenue" analysis
- **How to outperform:** build a reusable "freemium conversion framework" graphic (what separates freemium products that monetize from those that don't) as the video's practical payoff
