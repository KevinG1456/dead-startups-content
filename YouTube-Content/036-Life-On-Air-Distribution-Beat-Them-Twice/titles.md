## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **This Team Built the Best-Loved App in Its Category. Twice. They Lost Both Times.** — states the
   core "lost twice" premise plainly, matches the batch title's own framing exactly
2. Houseparty Had a 4.9-Star Rating From 500,000 Reviews. It Still Lost. — the single most concrete,
   most quotable acclaim statistic in the sources-log
3. Twitter Cut Off One App's Access in Two Hours. Five Years Later, the Same Team Lost Again. — strong
   dual-hook, sets up both halves of the story in one sentence
4. This App Exploded to 17 Million Downloads in a Month. It Still Couldn't Beat Zoom. — pure
   number-shock framing for the Houseparty half
5. Epic Games Paid $35 Million for This App. Two Years Later, They Shut It Down. — strong curiosity/
   irony hook
6. 47 Million Downloads to 8 Million in One Year. Here's What Actually Happened. — precise, dated,
   dramatic decline statistic
7. This Founder's First App Died to Twitter. His Second App Died to Zoom. — clean "same person, twice"
   framing, strong nostalgia pull for both eras
8. The App That Beat Zoom on Every Review Site Still Lost to Zoom. — sharp, ironic, punchy
9. What Happened to Meerkat and Houseparty? The Full Story — best pure-SEO, lower CTR
10. Distribution vs Product Explained With Real Companies — strong secondary search title for the
    startup-strategy-education search audience

## Recommended Title
**"This Team Built the Best-Loved App in Its Category. Twice. Distribution Beat Them Both Times."**

Reasoning: states the exact pattern this title was chosen to illustrate, without overclaiming Meerkat's
specific product-quality edge (the word "loved" covers cultural momentum honestly, without claiming a
clean product-quality win the sourcing doesn't fully support for Meerkat specifically). Works fully
truncated on mobile at the first two sentences, and sets up the "same team, twice" reveal as the
video's core differentiator before naming either app. Matches the title already used in this package's
README.
