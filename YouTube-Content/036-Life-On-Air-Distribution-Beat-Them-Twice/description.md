## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: two apps
most people remember separately, built by the exact same team, that lost the exact same way.

Life On Air was founded in 2012 in Israel by Ben Rubin and Itai Danino. Their first product, Meerkat,
launched February 2015 and became the breakout star of SXSW that year, raising $12 million from
Greylock Partners on that momentum. The same day Twitter confirmed acquiring rival Periscope, Twitter
cut Meerkat's access to its follower-graph API with about two hours' notice — eliminating Meerkat's
main distribution channel while Periscope kept its native Twitter integration. Worth being honest
here: some contemporaneous reporting describes Periscope as the more polished product at the time.
Meerkat won the SXSW moment, not necessarily a clean product-quality comparison. Meerkat shut down
October 4, 2016.

The team pivoted to Houseparty, a group-video-chat app built around casually "dropping in" on friends
already online. Epic Games acquired Houseparty in June 2019 for a reported $35 million. In March 2020,
amid COVID lockdowns, downloads spiked from about 533,000 in February to roughly 17.2 million — over
2,900% growth. As of August 2020, Houseparty held a 4.9-out-of-5-star rating from more than 500,000 App
Store reviews, widely and specifically praised in period coverage for its playful design compared
against Zoom's more utilitarian interface. This is the clean version of "best product in the category"
— quantified and real.

It still lost. Zoom's default-at-work status, Apple's built-in Group FaceTime, and Facebook's Messenger
Rooms — none requiring a separate download for most users — ate into Houseparty's userbase. Installs
fell from about 47 million (Jan-Aug 2020) to roughly 8 million (Jan-Aug 2021), an 83% year-over-year
collapse. On September 9, 2021, Epic Games announced Houseparty would shut down, fully discontinuing
it that October, citing a pivot toward "meaningful and authentic social interactions at metaverse scale
across the Epic Games family."

This channel has covered Meerkat's collapse before — package 008 — through the lens of platform API
dependency risk. This video covers the same event more briefly, then tells the fuller, connected story:
the same team, losing the same way, in two completely different categories, five years apart.
