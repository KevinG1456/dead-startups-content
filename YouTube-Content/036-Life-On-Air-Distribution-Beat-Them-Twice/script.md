# Script Package

Anchored on Life On Air (Meerkat + Houseparty) — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. THE SINGLE MOST
IMPORTANT RULE FOR THIS SCRIPT: never claim Meerkat was objectively the better product versus
Periscope — some contemporaneous reporting describes Periscope as more polished (better UI, stream
replay). Present Meerkat's edge honestly as cultural momentum/first-mover hype, not a confirmed
product-quality win. Houseparty's acclaim (4.9 stars, 500K+ reviews, explicit UX comparisons favoring
it over Zoom) is well-quantified and should carry the "best product" half of the title's claim.
Cross-reference package 008 explicitly for Meerkat's fuller platform-API-dependency story rather than
re-covering it in full depth. Distinguish Sima Sistani (Houseparty co-founder/leader) from Ben Rubin
and Itai Danino (Life On Air's 2012 co-founders).

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 13–16 minutes (Meerkat's rise and fall, the pivot, Houseparty's rise and fall, and
the shared lesson, all covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "This team built the most-loved app in its category. Then they did it again, in a completely
  different category, five years later. Both times, they lost the exact same way."
- Visual: two glowing five-star icons fading to gray, side by side
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This isn't two separate stories. It's
  the same team. Twice."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Life On Air — founded in Israel in 2012 by Ben Rubin and Itai Danino. Their first
  product, Meerkat, became the breakout star of SXSW 2015. Their second product, Houseparty, became
  one of the most downloaded apps in the world during COVID lockdown. Both are gone."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll see exactly how distribution beat this team twice —
  once from a company that could cut off their access in two hours, and once from three separate
  competitors that didn't even require a download."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from CNN Money, TechCrunch, Variety, Washington Post, Axios,
  NoCamels, Sequoia Capital, and NFX — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "One honesty note up front: Meerkat's specific product
  quality compared to its rival is genuinely disputed — some reporting says the rival was actually more
  polished. Meerkat won the moment, not necessarily the product comparison. Houseparty's case is
  cleaner, and we'll get to the receipts."
- Cross-reference tease: "This channel has covered Meerkat's collapse before — package 008 — through a
  different lens: platform API dependency. This video covers the same event more briefly, then tells
  the fuller, connected story most coverage never makes."

### Act One — Meerkat (1:40–5:30)
- **Segment 1 — SXSW 2015 (1:40–2:30):** Meerkat launches February 2015, becomes the breakout star of
  SXSW that year — widely covered as having stolen attention from Twitter itself. March 2015: $12
  million from Greylock Partners. Visual: "SXSW 2015 — MEERKAT'S BREAKOUT" title card.
- **Segment 2 — The Cutoff (2:30–3:45):** the same day Twitter confirms acquiring rival Periscope,
  Twitter cuts Meerkat's access to its follower-graph API — about two hours' notice. On-screen framing
  card: "Periscope kept its native Twitter integration. Meerkat lost the exact distribution channel it
  depended on." Visual: countdown-style "2 HOURS' NOTICE" title card.
- **Segment 3 — The Honest Caveat (3:45–4:30):** on-screen framing card, held for a beat: "Some
  reporting says Periscope was actually the more polished product — better UI, and the ability to
  replay streams, which Meerkat's live-only format didn't have. Meerkat won the moment. The
  distribution loss is what ended it either way." Visual: neutral side-by-side feature comparison card.
- **Segment 4 — The Shutdown (4:30–5:30):** Meerkat's usage collapses; the app is officially shut down
  October 4, 2016 — about 19 months after SXSW. Ben Rubin told investors early on Meerkat "was not
  going to work," and the team began developing a new concept. Visual: "SHUT DOWN — OCTOBER 4, 2016"
  title card.

### Act Two — Houseparty (5:30–11:00)
- **Segment 5 — The Pivot and the Sale (5:30–6:30):** the team builds Houseparty, a group-video-chat
  app centered on casually "dropping in" on friends already online. Life On Air raises $52 million tied
  to Houseparty's growth (2016). June 2019: Epic Games acquires Houseparty for a reported $35 million.
  Visual: "ACQUIRED BY EPIC GAMES — $35M — JUNE 2019" title card.
- **Segment 6 — The Lockdown Explosion (6:30–8:00):** March 2020, downloads spike from about 533,000 in
  February to roughly 17.2 million in March — over 2,900% growth — making Houseparty one of the most
  downloaded apps in the world. Visual: steep download-spike graph.
- **Segment 7 — The Real Acclaim (8:00–9:15):** as of August 2020, Houseparty holds a 4.9-out-of-5-star
  rating from over 500,000 App Store reviews — widely praised in period coverage for its playful,
  social design, explicitly contrasted against Zoom's more utilitarian, work-oriented interface.
  On-screen framing card: "This is the clean version of 'best product in the category' — quantified,
  documented, real." Visual: "4.9★ — 500,000+ REVIEWS" title card.
- **Segment 8 — The Collapse Anyway (9:15–10:15):** despite the acclaim, Zoom's default-at-work status,
  Apple's built-in Group FaceTime, and Facebook's Messenger Rooms — all requiring no separate download
  for most users — eat into Houseparty's userbase. Installs fall from roughly 47 million (Jan-Aug 2020)
  to about 8 million (Jan-Aug 2021) — an 83% year-over-year collapse. Visual: "47M → 8M — DOWN 83%"
  title card.
- **Segment 9 — The Shutdown (10:15–11:00):** September 9, 2021, Epic Games announces Houseparty will
  shut down; the app is fully discontinued in October 2021. Epic's own stated reason, attributed
  directly: pivoting toward "meaningful and authentic social interactions at metaverse scale across the
  Epic Games family." Visual: "SHUTDOWN ANNOUNCED — SEPTEMBER 9, 2021" title card.
- Mid-video CTA (~10:45): "Same team. Two products. Two categories. The exact same ending. Subscribe
  for a new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Lesson (11:00–13:00)
- Plain-language comparison: Meerkat lost to a competitor with a built-in social graph it could switch
  off in two hours. Houseparty lost to three competitors that didn't require a separate download at
  all. Both times, the product itself wasn't really the deciding factor.
- The core lesson, stated directly: distribution isn't just a growth channel — it's a structural
  advantage that can outlast genuine product acclaim, and building something people love doesn't
  neutralize it.
- Visual: dual-timeline graphic, Meerkat's arc (2015-2016) and Houseparty's arc (2016-2021) shown
  stacked, both ending the same way.

### Final CTA + Outro (13:00–14:15)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — a genuinely loved product can still lose to a competitor whose
  real advantage is simply being already installed, already integrated, or already the default
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and if you want the deeper platform-dependency
  story behind Meerkat's collapse specifically, check out package 008"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you use Meerkat, Houseparty, or both? And do you think there was anything
  this team could have done differently the second time around, knowing what happened the first time?
  Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: package 008 (Meerkat/Viddy) + most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the dual-timeline graphic and the download/rating statistics for both apps, then
works through each app's story as supporting detail. Measured, graphics-forward, treats the video as a
genuine case study in distribution-vs-product strategy.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on Ben Rubin at SXSW 2015, the peak of Meerkat's moment, before cutting hard to Meerkat's
shutdown eighteen months later — then follows the same emotional arc for Houseparty, ending on the
explicit "same team, same lesson" reveal as the closing beat. Strongest tonal-irony arc of the three
versions.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "one team, two collapses, in order" leading into rapid-fire beats for each app (launch →
acclaim → distribution-advantaged competitor → collapse → shutdown), landing on the shared lesson as
the final, clarifying beat. Strong Shorts-extraction potential, especially the download-spike-to-
collapse numbers for Houseparty.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the transition from Meerkat to Houseparty (~5:30) if the pivot feels like a
  separate video starting over
- **Fix:** explicitly frame the transition as "round two" using the same visual template that will
  repeat for Houseparty, so viewers recognize the pattern building rather than a fresh start
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general 2015 live-streaming-era or 2020 COVID-culture context if
  audience familiarity with either period is already high
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "same team, twice" reveal even more explicitly in the
  cold open if early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 5 (5:30) — many viewers won't expect
  the connection
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small running "ROUND 1 / ROUND 2" corner tag visible throughout
  each app's segment, reinforcing the repeated-pattern structure
