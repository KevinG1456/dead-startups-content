## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 10–13 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:20)
- VO: "What does a down round actually do to your equity, beyond the headline valuation number?"
- Visual: asymmetric bar-chart animates in, one bar shrinking dramatically, red downward arrow overlays with a heavy SFX hit
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "It's worse than the headline. Here's the real math."

### What Is a Down Round (0:20–1:30)
- Plain-language definition: a funding round priced at a lower valuation than the company's previous round
- Preview: this video annotates 3 cap tables — clearly labeled as illustrative teaching tools — to show the real mechanics, grounded in real examples (Klarna, Instacart) for context

### Cap Table 1: Dilution (1:30–3:30)
- Walk through a simple illustrative cap table showing how a down round's new share issuance dilutes existing common-stockholders (founders, employees) more than it might first appear
- Visual: cap table graphic builds row by row, clearly labeled "ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE"

### Cap Table 2: Liquidation Preference Stacking (3:30–5:30)
- Walk through how each funding round typically comes with its own liquidation preference, and how multiple stacked preferences from prior rounds plus the new down round can consume most or all of an exit's proceeds before common stockholders see anything
- Visual: second cap table graphic, layered preference-stack visualization

### Cap Table 3: Pay-to-Play (5:30–7:30)
- Walk through how a pay-to-play provision can force existing investors (and sometimes require difficult choices for smaller shareholders) to participate in the new round or face significant dilution/conversion penalties
- Visual: third cap table graphic showing the pay-to-play mechanic

### Why This Matters (7:30–9:30)
- Mid-video CTA (~7:30, opening this segment): "If you're a founder or an employee at a company that's raising — or has raised — at a lower valuation, the next part is for you. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."
- Real-world grounding: reference Klarna's reported ~85% valuation cut and Instacart's pre-IPO markdowns as real examples of the scale this can happen at, clearly sourced

### What to Ask If Your Company Takes a Down Round (9:30–10:30)
- Practical segment: what employees should ask (how does this affect my option strike price/value, what does the new preference stack look like, is there a re-pricing or refresh planned) and what founders should communicate proactively

### Final CTA + Outro (10:30–11:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — a down round's real impact lives in the cap table mechanics, not just the headline valuation number, and everyone holding equity should understand the difference
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these"
- Engagement prompt: "Has your company been through a down round? What did you learn? Comment below."
- End screen: two related autopsies + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on all 3 completed cap table graphics side by side, then works backward through each mechanic in detail. Measured, graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine financial-education resource.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the human moment — an employee learning what a down round meant for their options — before any cap table appears. Slower pacing, more narrative-driven.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "3 things a down round does that the headline doesn't tell you," each mechanic covered in under 90 seconds. Strong Shorts-extraction potential.

---

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the three cap-table segments (1:30–7:30) if any one becomes too dense or technical without enough visual pacing
- **Fix:** keep each cap table visually clean and build it incrementally rather than showing a fully complete table all at once
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** if audience feedback suggests strong familiarity with dilution basics, compress Cap Table 1 and spend more time on the less-understood liquidation preference and pay-to-play mechanics
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the Klarna/Instacart real-world scale examples early to sustain interest through the more technical middle segments
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a persistent "illustrative vs. real example" label visible throughout to maintain clarity and trust
