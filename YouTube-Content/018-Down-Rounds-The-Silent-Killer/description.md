## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week is a finance explainer: down rounds, and why they're a genuinely underexplained "silent killer" of startup equity value.

A down round means a company raises its next round of funding at a lower valuation than its previous round. That sounds simple, but the real mechanical impact — through dilution, liquidation preference stacking, and provisions like pay-to-play — is usually much larger and much more unevenly distributed than the headline valuation number suggests, often landing hardest on founders and employees holding common stock rather than the investors holding preferred stock.

We walk through 3 annotated cap table examples to show exactly how this works, grounded in real, reported down-round events (Klarna's roughly 85% valuation cut, Instacart's pre-IPO markdowns) for context — clearly distinguishing those real examples from the illustrative teaching cap table we build to show the mechanics step by step.
