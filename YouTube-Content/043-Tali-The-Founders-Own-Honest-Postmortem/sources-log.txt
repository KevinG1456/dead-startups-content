FOUNDING — precisely sourced
- Tali was founded in February 2017 in Beaverton, Oregon (Portland metro area) by three coworkers at a
  healthcare company: Matthew (Matt) Volm, Matt Anthes-Washburn, and Matt Hoiland. All three co-founders
  are named Matt; the company's actual registered legal entity name was "Three Matts Inc."
- Volm served as CEO.
Source: Gust company profile ("Three Matts Inc."); Above the Law (Oct 17, 2018, "Meet Matt Volm, CEO
And Co-Founder Of Tali"); Legal Tech Founders (Oct 2018 interview).

THE PRODUCT — precisely sourced
- Tali was a voice-powered timekeeping assistant for lawyers: users spoke their billable time to Amazon
  Alexa or Google Assistant, and the system synced that time automatically into legal billing platforms
  including Clio, Rocket Matter, and PracticePanther.
- Pronunciation detail: "Tali" is an actual word (plural of "talus," an anklebone), pronounced
  "TAIL-eye" — not "Tally," which is how it was commonly mispronounced/misheard, creating friction for a
  voice-first product. Source for this detail: Legal Cheek's founder interview, where Volm discusses the
  naming issue directly as a contributing factor.
Source: Above the Law; Legal Cheek (May 30, 2019); Failory founder interview.

FUNDING — precisely sourced
- First investment received June 2017; over roughly 1.5 years, Tali raised nearly $1.0 million on a
  rolling pre-seed structure using SAFEs and convertible notes, typically at a 15-20% discount and a
  $3.5M-$4.0M valuation cap.
- Named investors: Cascade Angels, Mucker Capital, Geekdom Fund, and SoFi Ventures.
- October 2018: Tali won $100,000 in Clio's "Launch//Code" pitch competition.
- *** [VERIFY]: the Failory interview headline references "$750K burned... over 2.5 years," while other
  sources state "nearly $1.0M raised... over 1.5 years." These are not necessarily contradictory (raised
  vs. burned are different figures, and the operating window may be measured differently across
  sources), but the precise reconciliation was not fully confirmed in this research pass. Use "raised
  nearly $1 million" as the primary, best-corroborated figure (repeated across Legal Cheek and Failory
  independently) and avoid stating a precise "burned" figure on screen unless further verified. ***
Source: Legal Cheek; Failory interview; Above the Law.

THE LAUNCH AND THE CHURN DISASTER — precisely sourced
- September 2017: paid beta launched publicly at the Clio Cloud Conference.
- December 2017: 101 paid users at $30/user/month, totaling $3,030 in monthly recurring revenue.
- Critical product gap: the subscription auto-renewal billing system had not been built before launch.
  When the first billing cycle ended, Volm had to personally contact all 101 users to manually resubmit
  payment information. Only 22 of the 101 did so — a 78% churn rate directly attributable to this
  missing feature, not to product dissatisfaction.
Source: Failory founder interview (most detailed on this specific mechanism); Legal Cheek (corroborates
the 101-user, $30/month, 78%-churn figures independently).

THE FIVE FAILED ACQUISITION CHANNELS — precisely sourced, direct founder quotes
Throughout 2018, Tali tried five distinct customer-acquisition channels. Founder Matthew Volm's own
words, from his Medium postmortem, describing each:
1. Outbound/inside sales — "unit economics were not sustainable."
2. Digital marketing (general campaigns) — "results were poor."
3. Google AdWords / SEM (paid search) — "this was expensive and conversion rates were low."
4. Facebook Ads (paid social) — included among the channels tried per Failory's interview summary;
   grouped with digital marketing failures.
5. Reseller / referral partnerships — "these weren't driving any volume."
Source: Matthew Volm, "I just shut down my startup. Here's what I learned." (Medium/The Startup/SWLH,
direct founder-authored postmortem); corroborated by Failory's separate founder interview, which lists
the same channel set (inside sales, Google AdWords, Facebook Ads, resellers) as having failed to drive
meaningful growth.

THE PRICE CUT AND FLAT REVENUE — precisely sourced
- During 2018, Tali cut its price from $30/user/month to $12/user/month, attempting to remove price as
  an adoption barrier.
- Paid user count grew to roughly 250 by the end of 2018 — but monthly recurring revenue remained flat
  at approximately $3,000, since the price cut offset the user growth almost exactly.
- Free-trial-to-paid conversion never exceeded 3.0%. Daily active usage never exceeded 10% of the paid
  user base, even after continued 2018 product improvements.
Source: Failory founder interview; Legal Cheek.

THE RUNWAY AND THE SHUTDOWN DECISION — precisely sourced, with one date discrepancy flagged
- By December 2018, Tali had approximately 2.5 months of runway remaining; at the company's
  then-current growth rate, reaching cash-flow-positive would have required roughly 26 more months.
- Volm returned to existing investors, then angel investors, then later-stage investors, requesting
  bridge funding. All declined.
- Mentors advised against running the company in "zombie mode" (continuing to operate with no credible
  path to survival) — Volm is quoted (via Failory) describing this advice as: "Putting things in zombie
  mode is a dumb idea."
- Volm and his co-founders chose to shut Tali down rather than continue in zombie mode.
- *** [VERIFY]: the exact shutdown month is not firmly confirmed. Legal Cheek's article, published May
  30, 2019, discusses the shutdown as already having occurred by publication — implying a shutdown in
  the first half of 2019, likely Q1-Q2 2019 (consistent with the December 2018 "2.5 months of runway"
  figure). One automated extraction of that same article separately surfaced an "end of June 2019"
  reference that cannot be reconciled with the article's May 30, 2019 publication date, and should NOT
  be used. On screen: state "shut down in 2019" without a specific month unless a firmer primary source
  is found before recording. ***
Source: Legal Cheek (May 30, 2019); Failory founder interview.

Source: Medium (Matthew Volm, direct author); Legal Cheek; Failory.

POST-TALI — Matthew Volm's later career, for context/closing note only, precisely sourced

  CEO/co-founder of Funnel IQ, then CEO/co-founder of Atomic (April 2022-September 2023), and is
  currently CEO/co-founder of RevOps Co-op (a community of 10,000+ RevOps professionals), alongside
