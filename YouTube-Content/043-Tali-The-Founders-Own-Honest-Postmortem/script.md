# Script Package

Anchored on Tali — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. IMPORTANT FRAMING RULE FOR THIS SCRIPT: never
inflate the ~$1 million raised or the ~250 peak paid users — the honesty and specificity of the real
numbers is the entire point of this video. Present founder Matthew Volm's candor as admirable
transparency, never as mockery. Flag the exact shutdown month as approximate ("2019") per the
[VERIFY] note in `sources-log.txt` — do not state a specific month as fact.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 11–13 minutes (the founding, the launch and churn disaster, all five failed
acquisition channels, the shutdown decision, and the actual lesson, all covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "He tried five different ways to get customers. All five failed. Most founders would bury that.
  He wrote it down, in detail, and published it."
- Visual: five channel icons (sales call, ad, search bar, social icon, handshake) appearing one at a
  time, each getting a red X
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to black, white text: "This isn't a story about a scandal. It's a story
  about a founder who told the truth."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Tali — a voice-powered timekeeping assistant for lawyers, built by three coworkers who
  all happened to be named Matt. They raised nearly a million dollars. They won a $100,000 pitch
  contest. And within two years, it was over."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll know exactly which five channels they tried to get
  customers, exactly why each one failed, and the one missing feature that cost them almost four out of
  every five customers they'd already signed up."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from the founder's own Medium postmortem, Legal Cheek, and Failory's
  founder interview — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "One honesty note up front: this is a smaller company than
  most of what this channel covers — about $1 million raised, not tens of millions. That's not the
  point. The point is that almost no failed startup is this specific about exactly what didn't work.
  That specificity is what makes this useful."

### Main Content — The Founding and the Launch (1:40–4:00)
- **Segment 1 — Three Matts (1:40–2:15):** February 2017, Beaverton, Oregon. Matthew Volm, Matt
  Anthes-Washburn, and Matt Hoiland, coworkers at a healthcare company, leave to start Tali — the
  company's actual legal name was "Three Matts Inc." Visual: three silhouette icons, each labeled "Matt."
- **Segment 2 — The Product (2:15–3:00):** Tali is a voice-powered timekeeping assistant for lawyers —
  speak your billable time to Amazon Alexa or Google Assistant, and it syncs directly into billing
  platforms like Clio, Rocket Matter, and PracticePanther. On-screen framing card: "Tali is pronounced
  'TAIL-eye' — it's the plural of 'talus,' an anklebone. Almost everyone who saw it written said
  'Tally' instead." Visual: "TALI (TAIL-eye)" pronunciation card next to a microphone icon.
- **Segment 3 — Real Money, Real Validation (3:00–4:00):** June 2017, first investment lands; over the
  next 1.5 years, Tali raises nearly $1 million on SAFEs and convertible notes from Cascade Angels,
  Mucker Capital, Geekdom Fund, and SoFi Ventures. In October 2018, Tali wins $100,000 in Clio's
  Launch//Code pitch competition. Visual: "$1M RAISED · $100K PITCH-CONTEST WIN" title card.

### Main Content — The Churn Disaster (4:00–5:30)
- **Segment 4 — The Launch (4:00–4:45):** September 2017, paid beta launches at the Clio Cloud
  Conference. By December 2017: 101 paid users at $30/month — $3,030 in monthly recurring revenue.
  Visual: "101 USERS · $3,030 MRR — DECEMBER 2017" title card.
- **Segment 5 — The Missing Feature (4:45–5:30):** the subscription auto-renewal system was never built
  before launch. When the first billing cycle ended, Volm had to personally contact all 101 users to
  manually resubmit their payment information. Only 22 did. On-screen framing card: "78% churn — not
  from a bad product. From a feature that didn't exist yet." Visual: "101 → 22 USERS — 78% CHURN" title
  card, users icon collapsing.

### Main Content — The Five Failed Channels (5:30–9:30)
- **Segment 6 — Outbound Sales (5:30–6:15):** throughout 2018, Tali tries inside/outbound sales
  directly to law firms. Founder's own words, on screen: "unit economics were not sustainable." Visual:
  "CHANNEL 1: OUTBOUND SALES — FAILED" title card.
- **Segment 7 — Digital Marketing (6:15–6:45):** general digital marketing campaigns. Founder's own
  words, on screen: "results were poor." Visual: "CHANNEL 2: DIGITAL MARKETING — FAILED" title card.
- **Segment 8 — Google AdWords / SEM (6:45–7:15):** paid search advertising. Founder's own words, on
  screen: "expensive and conversion rates were low." Visual: "CHANNEL 3: GOOGLE ADWORDS / SEM — FAILED"
  title card.
- **Segment 9 — Facebook Ads and Resellers (7:15–7:45):** paid social ads and reseller/referral
  partnerships. Founder's own words on resellers, on screen: "these weren't driving any volume." Visual:
  "CHANNEL 4: FACEBOOK ADS — FAILED · CHANNEL 5: RESELLERS — FAILED" title card.
- **Segment 10 — The Price Cut That Didn't Help (7:45–8:45):** in 2018, Tali cuts its price from
  $30/month to $12/month, trying to remove price as the barrier. Paid users roughly double, reaching
  about 250 by the end of 2018 — but monthly recurring revenue stays flat at roughly $3,000, because the
  lower price offsets the user growth almost exactly. Trial-to-paid conversion never exceeds 3%; daily
  active usage never exceeds 10% of the paid base. Visual: "250 USERS · STILL $3K MRR" title card, flat
  revenue line despite rising user-count line.
- **Segment 11 — The Runway Runs Out (8:45–9:30):** by December 2018, Tali has 2.5 months of runway
  left, and at its current growth rate would need 26 more months to reach cash-flow positive. Volm
  returns to existing investors, then angels, then later-stage funds, asking for a bridge. All decline.
  Visual: "2.5 MONTHS OF RUNWAY — DECEMBER 2018" title card.

### Main Content — The Ending (9:30–10:30)
- **Segment 12 — Zombie Mode or Shutdown (9:30–10:00):** mentors advise Volm against running the
  company in "zombie mode" — limping forward on fumes with no real path to survival. Volm and his
  co-founders choose to shut Tali down rather than fake continued viability. On-screen framing card:
  "[VERIFY] — exact shutdown month not firmly confirmed across sources; shut down in 2019." Visual:
  "SHUTDOWN — 2019" title card.
- Mid-video CTA (~10:00): "This channel usually pieces failures together from press coverage. This
  founder already did that work himself, in public, on purpose. Subscribe for a new autopsy every
  week."
- **Segment 13 — The Postmortem (10:00–10:30):** Volm publishes a detailed public postmortem on Medium,
  naming every failed channel and every real number — one of the most specific, most honest startup
  failure writeups publicly available. Visual: Medium article screenshot mockup.

### The Actual Lesson (10:30–12:00)
- Plain-language comparison: Tali didn't fail from one dramatic mistake — it failed because every
  standard customer-acquisition channel a startup can reach for, tried in sequence over an entire year,
  came back negative, and a preventable early product gap (no auto-renewal) made the math even harder
  than it needed to be from day one.
- The core lesson, stated directly: "everyone knows CAC has to beat LTV" is easy to say in the abstract
  — this is what it actually looks like to test that in practice, channel by channel, with real numbers
  attached to each failure, and the discipline to stop rather than limp forward once the answer is
  clearly no.
- Visual: full-arc timeline graphic — founding (Feb 2017) → first investment (Jun 2017) → paid beta
  launch (Sep 2017) → 101 users/78% churn (Dec 2017) → five channels tried and failed (2018) → $100K
  pitch win (Oct 2018) → price cut, 250 users, flat MRR (2018) → 2.5 months runway, investors decline
  (Dec 2018) → shutdown (2019) → public postmortem published.

### Final CTA + Outro (12:00–13:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — trying every channel and getting a clear "no" from all of them is
  a real, valid answer, and having the discipline to publish that answer honestly is rarer than the
  failure itself
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and this is the smallest company I've covered so
  far, because sometimes the most useful lesson doesn't come from the biggest number"
- Engagement prompt: "Which of these five channels would you have tried first? And have you ever tried
  a channel that looked promising and just quietly failed on you? Tell me in the comments."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the five-channel scorecard (all five marked FAILED) and the flat-MRR-despite-rising-
users graph, then works backward through the founding and the churn disaster as supporting detail.
Measured, graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine GTM-channel case study for founders currently
choosing an acquisition strategy.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the December 2018 moment — 2.5 months of runway, investors saying no — before cutting hard to
the optimistic 2017 launch, then working forward through the year of failed channels toward the
shutdown decision and the postmortem. Slower pacing, holds the "78% churn from a missing feature" reveal
until Segment 5, resolving with the founder's own choice to publish rather than stay quiet.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured explicitly as "5 Channels We Tried. All 5 Failed." with each channel getting its own rapid,
numbered beat (sales, digital marketing, SEM, paid social, resellers), bookended by the founding and the
shutdown as opening/closing context. Strongest Shorts-extraction potential — each channel segment works
as a standalone 30–45 second clip on its own.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the five-channel sequence (5:30–7:45) if each channel feels like a repetitive list
  item rather than an escalating pattern
- **Fix:** frame each channel explicitly as "one more door closing" with an on-screen running tally
  (1 of 5 failed → 5 of 5 failed), building tension toward the runway crisis rather than just listing
  facts
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general legal-tech market context if audience is more
  founder/marketer-focused than legal-industry-focused
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the naming-pronunciation irony in the cold open if
  early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 2
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small running "channels failed: X of 5" corner counter visible
  from Segment 6 through Segment 9, ticking up toward "5 of 5"
