## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **He Tried 5 Different Ways to Get Customers. All 5 Failed. Here's Exactly Why.** — leads with the
   structural hook that doesn't require brand recognition, promises a checklist payoff, sets up the
   entire video's spine
2. This Startup's Own Name Broke Its Own Voice Recognition. — sharp, self-contained irony, strong
   curiosity, works without any other context
3. Sales Failed. Ads Failed. SEO Failed. Resellers Failed. He Named Every Single One. — rapid-fire
   structural preview, promises specificity immediately
4. This Founder Forgot to Build One Feature. It Cost Him 78% of His Customers. — strong single-number
   shock hook, very concrete and checkable
5. He Cut His Price in Half. His Customers Doubled. His Revenue Didn't Move. — counterintuitive numbers
   hook, strong analytical curiosity
6. Three Guys Named Matt Started a Company Called "Three Matts Inc." — strong quirky-detail hook, good
   for driving initial curiosity even without failure context
7. 2.5 Months of Runway Left. Every Investor Said No. — strong tension hook, sets up the shutdown
   decision cleanly
8. He Wrote the Most Honest Startup Postmortem I've Ever Read. — strong authority/curation framing,
   positions the video as a curated discovery rather than just a recap
9. What Happened to Tali? The Full Story — best pure-SEO, lowest CTR of the set
10. Startup CAC LTV Failure Case Study: Tali — strong secondary search title for the batch title's
    original CAC/LTV framing, useful for viewers arriving via that search intent

## Recommended Title
**"He Tried 5 Different Ways to Get Customers. All 5 Failed. Here's Exactly Why."**

Reasoning: Tali carries essentially no brand recognition, so the title needs to sell entirely on
structure and promise rather than name recognition — this framing does that, sets up a clean five-part
chapter structure (sales, digital marketing, SEM, paid social, resellers), and promises the exact
specificity that differentiates this video from generic "why startups fail" content. The company name
and its ironic pronunciation problem become a strong mid-video beat rather than the opening hook, since
neither carries enough pull to open on alone.
