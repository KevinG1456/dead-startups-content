# 043 — He Tried Every Channel a Startup Can Try. None of Them Worked. Then He Wrote Down Exactly Why.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Tali, a real, named, fully-documented startup
death (Medium, Legal Cheek, Failory — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This package replaces
skipped batch title #17 ("The sales-led startup where CAC never once beat LTV — and everyone knew"),
one of seven titles from the "dead-saas-30" batch's #11–#20 half that had no real single-company anchor
after the original research pass (see `project_channel_overview.md` memory). Tali was sourced fresh in
a second, harder research pass (2026-08-16) specifically to fill this slot.

**The real case:** Matthew Volm, Matt Anthes-Washburn, and Matt Hoiland — three coworkers at a
healthcare company in Portland, Oregon, all coincidentally named Matt — founded Tali in February 2017
(the company's actual legal name was "Three Matts Inc."). Tali was a voice-powered timekeeping assistant
for lawyers: speak your billable time to Alexa or Google Assistant, and it synced automatically into
billing platforms like Clio, Rocket Matter, and PracticePanther. They raised nearly $1 million over 1.5
years on SAFEs and convertible notes from Cascade Angels, Mucker Capital, Geekdom Fund, and SoFi
Ventures, and won $100,000 in Clio's Launch//Code pitch contest. Then, over 2018, they tried outbound
sales, digital marketing, Google AdWords, Facebook Ads, and reseller partnerships to acquire customers —
and every single channel failed to produce sustainable unit economics. By December 2018, with 2.5 months
of runway left, existing investors, angels, and later-stage investors all declined to fund a bridge.
Rather than run the company in "zombie mode," Volm shut it down in 2019 — and then wrote an unusually
specific, named-numbers public postmortem on Medium explaining exactly which acquisition channel failed
and why.

**Why this replaces #17's exact premise:** the batch title imagined a company where "everyone knew" CAC
never beat LTV — implying insider knowledge kept quiet until after the fact. Tali's real story is more
useful and better sourced: the founder didn't wait for a journalist or a post-mortem site to dig this up
— he published the specific failed CAC math himself, on purpose, while it was still recent. That
voluntary candor, not a concealed truth revealed later, is this package's actual hook.

**Framing requirement:** this is a smaller-scale story than most of this channel's anchors (~$1M raised,
not tens or hundreds of millions) — never inflate the funding figure or invent a larger one. The video's
value is the founder's own specificity and honesty, not the dollar amount. Treat Volm's candor as
admirable transparency, not a target for mockery — this is a rare case where the subject chose to be
this exposed publicly, and the tone should reflect that.

## 2. Viewer Psychology
- **Curiosity triggers:** which channel failed first; whether any channel showed early promise before
  collapsing; what the founder would do differently; the irony of a voice-product whose own name
  confused voice recognition
- **Emotional triggers:** respect for a founder who published this level of detail voluntarily; dread at
  the completely preventable 78% churn (a missing auto-renewal feature, not a market problem); quiet
  recognition for anyone in the audience who has tried the same five channels
- **Viewer objections:** "$1M and 250 customers isn't a real failure story" — pre-empt by reframing the
  value proposition up front: this isn't about scale, it's about specificity — five real, named
  acquisition channels and their real, documented failure reasons
- **Motivation to watch:** founders and marketers currently choosing a GTM channel who want a real,
  checkable answer instead of a generic listicle
- **Expected comments:** founders sharing their own "we tried five channels too" stories, product people
  reacting to the missing-auto-renewal detail, debate over whether voice UI was ever the right bet for
  legal timekeeping

## 3. Competitor Analysis
- **Similar creators:** generic "top 10 reasons startups fail" content is extremely common; specific,
  single-company, founder-sourced CAC/LTV failure breakdowns are much rarer
- **Content gap:** most startup-failure content either stays abstract (no named company) or picks
  already-famous failures (Theranos, WeWork) where the CAC/LTV mechanics aren't the actual story — a
  small, honestly-documented, channel-by-channel failure case like Tali is underused
- **How to outperform:** treat the five channels as a structured checklist the viewer can apply
  immediately — sales, digital marketing, SEM, paid social, resellers — each with Tali's real number
  attached, rather than treating Tali as merely a curiosity

## 5. Improvement Suggestions
- Lead the cold open with the structural hook, not the company name — Tali has no brand pull
- Keep the founder's own quotes verbatim and attributed throughout — his specific words ("unit
  economics were not sustainable," "results were poor," "expensive and conversion rates were low") are
  the differentiator versus generic failure content
- Do not inflate the ~$1M raise or the 250-user peak — the honesty of the real numbers is the point
- [VERIFY] exact shutdown month before recording — sources conflict slightly (see `sources-log.txt`);
  use "2019" without a specific month unless a firmer source is found
- Present the missing-auto-renewal detail and the naming-pronunciation problem as real, documented,
  slightly ironic operational misses — not as mockery of the founders
