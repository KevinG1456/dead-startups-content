# Video Description

He tried five different ways to get customers. All five failed. Most founders would bury that. He
wrote it down, in detail, and published it.

This is the story of Tali — a voice-powered timekeeping assistant for lawyers, built by three coworkers
who all happened to be named Matt. They raised nearly $1 million. They won a $100,000 pitch contest.
They launched to real, paying customers. And over the following year, they tried outbound sales,
digital marketing, Google AdWords, Facebook ads, and reseller partnerships to grow — and every single
channel failed to produce sustainable unit economics.

This video reconstructs exactly what happened: the missing feature that cost them 78% of their first
customers, the five acquisition channels tried and the founder's own words on why each one failed, the
price cut that doubled their users without moving their revenue, and the December 2018 moment when
every investor they asked said no.

Most startup-failure content stays abstract, or picks already-famous collapses where the actual
customer-acquisition math isn't the real story. This is different: a real, named company, and a
founder who chose to publish exactly what didn't work, while it was still recent, in his own words.
