# 052 — The $9 Billion Blood-Testing Company That Was Mostly Lying About Its Own Technology

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Elizabeth Holmes and Theranos's full
documented history and criminal case (SEC press releases, DOJ press releases, court reporting from
CBS News, NBC News, CNN, Fortune — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). **Third real-conviction
fraud story on this channel**, after 005 (HeadSpin) and 029 (Ozy Media/Carlos Watson) — Elizabeth
Holmes WAS criminally convicted by a federal jury, so "fraud" language is accurate here. **Live,
unresolved sensitivity unique to this package:** Holmes has a clemency/commutation petition
currently PENDING with the Trump administration as of the most recent reporting (Jan 2026) — this
is a REQUEST, not a decision. Read `sources-log.txt` in full before touching this package, especially
the section on how to handle the pending petition.

**The real case:** Theranos — founded 2003 by 19-year-old Stanford dropout Elizabeth Holmes — raised
more than $700 million from investors (SEC's own figure) and hit a peak valuation of roughly $9
billion in 2013-2014, built on the claim that its "Edison" devices could run more than 200 standard
blood tests from a few drops of finger-stick blood. In reality, per Wall Street Journal reporting
that broke October 15, 2015, the devices could reliably run only a small fraction of those tests —
the large majority of patient results at Walgreens "Wellness Centers" were quietly produced on
modified samples run through conventional third-party lab machines, while Theranos told investors,
partners, and regulators otherwise. Safeway's $350 million in-store-clinic buildout never went live
and the partnership ended quietly in November 2015; Walgreens froze and then ended its own
partnership in 2016 after a federal regulator found deficiencies posing "immediate jeopardy to
patient health and safety." The company dissolved in September 2018 — not through bankruptcy, but
through an out-of-court wind-down after defaulting on a loan — with $0 returned to shareholders. The
SEC's 2018 civil case ended in a settlement (a fine and a 10-year officer/director bar, no admission
of wrongdoing). The separate CRIMINAL case did not: on January 3, 2022, a federal jury convicted
Holmes of conspiracy to commit wire fraud and three counts of wire fraud against investors — while
acquitting her on every patient-related count. She was sentenced in November 2022 to 11 years, 3
months in federal prison and, with co-defendant Ramesh "Sunny" Balwani (convicted on all 12 counts he
faced, a broader conviction than Holmes's, sentenced to nearly 13 years), ordered in May 2023 to pay
$452 million in restitution. Holmes reported to prison in May 2023 and remains there as of this
package's build date — with a clemency petition now pending, not yet decided.

**Framing requirement:** This channel is allowed to say "fraud" and describe Holmes's conviction as
settled fact, the same way 005 and 029 do — a jury decided it. The 2018 SEC settlement (civil) must
stay explicitly distinct from the 2022 criminal conviction. The pending clemency petition must be
presented as exactly that — pending, undecided, a request — with the same strict neutrality this
channel applied to 029's already-decided commutation, adjusted for the fact that nothing has actually
happened here yet.
