## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week:
Theranos — a blood-testing company founded in 2003 by 19-year-old Stanford dropout Elizabeth Holmes.
The company raised more than $700 million (the SEC's own figure) and reached a peak valuation of
roughly $9 billion in 2013-2014, on the strength of a single extraordinary claim: its "Edison"
devices could run more than 200 standard lab tests from just a few drops of finger-stick blood. The
board included Henry Kissinger, George Shultz, retired general James Mattis, and Sam Nunn — statesmen,
not scientists. Rupert Murdoch alone invested $125 million.

The technology mostly couldn't do what it claimed. Per Wall Street Journal reporting that broke on
October 15, 2015, the large majority of patient tests run through Theranos's Walgreens "Wellness
Centers" were actually processed on modified samples run through conventional third-party lab
machines — while Theranos told investors, partners, and regulators the results came from its own
technology. Safeway had spent approximately $350 million building in-store clinics for a rollout that
never launched at scale; the deal quietly ended in November 2015. Walgreens froze and then ended its
own partnership in 2016, after federal inspectors found deficiencies posing "immediate jeopardy to
patient health and safety."

In March 2018, the SEC charged Theranos, Holmes, and former president Ramesh "Sunny" Balwani with an
"elaborate, years-long fraud." Holmes settled — without admitting or denying wrongdoing — for a
$500,000 fine, a 10-year bar from serving as a public-company officer or director, and the surrender
of her voting control. This video keeps that CIVIL settlement explicitly distinct from what came
next: a separate CRIMINAL case. In September 2018, Theranos dissolved — not through bankruptcy, but
through an out-of-court wind-down after defaulting on a loan — with $0 returned to shareholders.

Holmes and Balwani were criminally indicted in 2018 and tried separately. On January 3, 2022, a
federal jury convicted Holmes of conspiracy to commit wire fraud and three individual investor
wire-fraud counts — and ACQUITTED her on every patient-related fraud count, a detail most retellings
of this story leave out. Balwani, tried separately, was convicted on all 12 counts he faced, both
investor and patient-related — a broader conviction than Holmes's. In November 2022, Holmes was
sentenced to 11 years, 3 months in federal prison; Balwani received nearly 13 years. In May 2023, a
judge ordered $452 million in restitution, including $125 million to Rupert Murdoch and $40 million
to Walgreens.

Holmes reported to Federal Prison Camp Bryan, Texas, on May 30, 2023. As of the most recent
reporting available when this video was built (January 2026), her legal team has filed a formal
clemency petition with the Trump administration — the Office of the Pardon Attorney lists it as
PENDING. No decision has been made. This video states that fact plainly, as exactly what it is: a
request, not an outcome, with zero speculation about the result.

This is the third real, adjudicated startup-fraud conviction on this channel, after package 005
(HeadSpin/Manish Lachwani) and package 029 (Ozy Media/Carlos Watson) — cross-referenced explicitly.
