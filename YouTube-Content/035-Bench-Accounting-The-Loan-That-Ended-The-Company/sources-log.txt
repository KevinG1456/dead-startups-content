Sources Log — Package 035: Bench Accounting
Filled in: 2026-08-15

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
Bench Accounting is the anchor for this channel's "Money Mechanics" batch title #7: "The startup that
got margin-called: venture debt's ugliest quarter." Pattern: MAC clauses and covenant breaches. This
case is real, dated, and well-documented by BetaKit (a respected Canadian tech outlet, the deepest
reporting found), though — same honest caveat as this channel's package 017 (Convoy) — the specific
lender's identity and the exact covenant/MAC trigger were never publicly confirmed; Bench itself
declined to confirm details. Present the general mechanism (a bank called in Bench's venture debt
loan, forcing an immediate, unannounced shutdown) as real and sourced, while flagging the unconfirmed
specifics clearly. Distinct from package 017 (Convoy): Convoy's lender (Hercules Capital) took control
and wound the company down over time; Bench's lender calling the loan caused an immediate, overnight
platform shutdown with zero customer warning — a sharper, faster version of the same underlying risk.

---

FOUNDING AND BUSINESS — precisely sourced
- Bench was founded in 2012 as "10sheet Inc." by Ian Crosby and Adam Saint, accepted into the
  TechStars NYC accelerator that year. The company rebranded to "Bench" in 2013 after relocating to
  Vancouver, British Columbia, and launched its product publicly.
- Business model: software-based bookkeeping-as-a-service for small and medium-sized businesses —
  human bookkeepers supported by proprietary software, aimed at businesses too small for a full-time
  accountant.
Source: Wikipedia ("Bench Accounting"); BetaKit.

FUNDING HISTORY — [VERIFY] total raised varies slightly by source ($100M+ vs. $113-115M)
- 2013: $2 million seed round.
- 2014: $1 million from VCs and angel investors.
- 2015: $7 million Series A, led by Altos Ventures and Contour Venture Partners.
- 2016: $16 million Series B, led by Bain Capital Ventures.
- 2018: $18 million Series B-1, led by iNovia Capital.
- 2021: $60 million Series C, led by Contour Venture Partners, with backing that included Shopify,
  Bain Capital Ventures, and Sage. This round was reportedly structured as $37 million in equity plus
  $23 million in debt — the debt component that ultimately mattered most to the company's ending.
- Total lifetime funding by December 2024: reported as "over $100 million" (Wikipedia) or "$113-115
  million" (other aggregator sources) — treat as "over $100 million" if a single round figure is
  needed on screen, and flag the discrepancy if a precise total is required.
- Post-money valuation after the 2021 Series C: approximately $232 million.
Source: Wikipedia; Tracxn (aggregator); BetaKit (Series C debt/equity split).

THE FOUNDER'S OUSTER — precisely dated, presented as his own account where attributed
- November 2021 — within days of the Series C round closing — Bench's board removed co-founder Ian
  Crosby from the CEO role. *** Per Crosby's own account (his own public statements, attributed as
  such, not independently verified by this research pass): the board told him a "professional CEO"
  was needed to "take the company to the next level," and he claims he had turned down what he
  described as "a highly lucrative acquisition offer" before his removal. Present this explicitly as
  Crosby's own claim, since this research pass found no independent corroboration of the specific
  acquisition-offer detail. ***
Source: BetaKit ("Bench had a crazier holiday break than your startup").

THE POST-2021 STRUGGLE — precisely sourced
- After the 2021 Series C and the CEO change, Bench reportedly closed two additional bridge financing
  rounds, per BetaKit's sourcing, while struggling with scalability and customer retention, and
  burning cash to the point of insolvency.
Source: BetaKit.

THE SHUTDOWN — precisely dated, the core "margin call" mechanism
- December 27, 2024: Bench notified customers that its platform would become immediately
  inaccessible — an abrupt shutdown with no advance warning, locking out an estimated 35,000+ U.S.
  small-business customers from their own bookkeeping and tax records.
- The same day, Bench laid off 450 employees.
- Per BetaKit's sourcing, the shutdown was forced by a bank calling in Bench's venture debt loan —
  i.e., the lender exercised its right to demand immediate repayment, a classic venture-debt "margin
  call" / accelerated-repayment event. *** The identity of the specific lender, and the specific
  covenant or Material Adverse Change (MAC) clause invoked to justify calling the loan, were never
  publicly confirmed in any source found for this research pass — Bench itself declined to confirm
  these details when asked by reporters. Present the general mechanism (a lender called in venture
  debt, forcing immediate shutdown) as real and sourced; do not name a specific lender or cite a
  specific covenant term as confirmed fact. ***
Source: BetaKit; Yahoo Finance/Associated Press coverage (Dec 2024); multiple accounting-industry
trade outlets (EisnerAmper, AccountingProse, EcomBalance — secondary coverage, consistent with
BetaKit's core reporting).

THE BANKRUPTCY FILING — precisely dated, exact figures
- January 7, 2025: Bench filed for bankruptcy protection in Canada, disclosing $2.8 million in cash
  against $65.4 million in total liabilities at the time of filing.
Source: Multiple outlets citing the Canadian bankruptcy filing (Jan 2025).

THE ACQUISITION — precisely dated
- December 30, 2024 — three days after the shutdown — Employer.com, a California-based HR technology
  company with no prior specialization in accounting, announced it would acquire Bench. Employer.com's
  CEO, Jesse Tinsley, said he learned of Bench's shutdown from news reports, not directly from Bench.
  Financial terms of the acquisition were not disclosed.
- January 1, 2025: the acquisition closed; Bench resumed operations under the Bench Accounting brand
  as an Employer.com subsidiary, and rehired more than half of the 450 employees laid off days
  earlier.
- Employer.com's own characterization of itself at the time: "bootstrapped, profitable, and on track
  to hit $100 million in annual recurring revenue in 2025" — presented as the acquirer's own claim
  about its own business, not independently audited.
Source: GeekWire; CBC News; Banking Dive; BetaKit; Tech Startups.
