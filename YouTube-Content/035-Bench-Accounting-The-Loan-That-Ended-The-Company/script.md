# Script Package

Anchored on Bench Accounting — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RULE FOR
THIS SCRIPT: never name a specific lender or covenant term for Bench's venture debt — this research
found no public confirmation of either, and Bench itself declined to confirm them. State only the
general, sourced fact: a bank called in Bench's venture debt loan, forcing an immediate shutdown.
Present Ian Crosby's account of his own 2021 ouster as his own claims, attributed, not verified fact.
Cross-reference package 017 (Convoy) explicitly, keeping the two lender mechanisms distinct — Convoy's
named lender took control over time; Bench's unnamed lender calling in the loan caused an immediate,
overnight shutdown.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes (founding, the founder's ouster, the buildup, the shutdown, the
bankruptcy, and the acquisition all covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "A bank called in one loan. The next morning, 35,000 small businesses couldn't access their own
  financial records. This is exactly what happened, and exactly why."
- Visual: blank laptop screen graphic, small storefront icons around it dimming one by one
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "There was no warning. Not to
  customers. Not to most employees."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Bench Accounting — a bookkeeping company founded in 2012 in Vancouver, built to give
  small businesses real human bookkeepers backed by real software. It raised over $100 million. On
  December 27, 2024, its platform simply stopped working, locking out an estimated 35,000-plus small-
  business customers with zero warning."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll understand the exact mechanism that ended this company
  overnight — a bank calling in a loan — and you'll see just how fast the entire rest of the story
  happened: shutdown, layoffs, bankruptcy filing, and acquisition, all within about eleven days."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from BetaKit, Wikipedia, GeekWire, CBC News, and Banking Dive —
  sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "One detail before we start: the specific lender behind
  this story, and the exact contract term that let them call in the loan, have never been publicly
  confirmed. Bench itself declined to confirm them. This video states only what's actually sourced —
  and that's still more than enough to understand what happened."

### Main Content — The Rise and the Ouster (1:40–5:00)
- **Segment 1 — The Founding (1:40–2:20):** Bench founded 2012 as "10sheet Inc." by Ian Crosby and
  Adam Saint, through the TechStars NYC accelerator; rebranded Bench in 2013 after moving to Vancouver.
  Visual: generic startup-office establishing shot, no real branding.
- **Segment 2 — The Climb (2:20–3:15):** funding progression from a $2M seed (2013) through a $60
  million Series C in 2021 — $37 million in equity, $23 million in debt — valuing the company at $232
  million. Visual: "$232M VALUATION — 2021" title card.
- **Segment 3 — The Ouster (3:15–4:15):** days after that round closed, Bench's board removed
  co-founder Ian Crosby as CEO. On-screen attribution card: "Per Crosby's own account: the board said
  they wanted a 'professional CEO.' He says he'd turned down a lucrative acquisition offer beforehand
  — his own claim, not independently confirmed." Visual: "FOUNDER REMOVED — NOVEMBER 2021" title card.
- **Segment 4 — The Slow Struggle (4:15–5:00):** over the next three years, Bench reportedly closed
  two more bridge financing rounds while struggling with scalability and burning cash. Visual: fading
  timeline graphic, 2021 → 2024.

### Main Content — The Shutdown (5:00–8:00)
- **Segment 5 — December 27, 2024 (5:00–6:30):** Bench's platform goes dark with zero advance notice.
  An estimated 35,000-plus small-business customers lose access to their own bookkeeping and tax
  records. The same day, Bench lays off 450 employees. On-screen framing card: "No warning to
  customers. Very little warning to most staff." Visual: blank-screen graphic, storefront icons
  dimming, "450 LAID OFF — SAME DAY" title card.
- **Segment 6 — The Mechanism (6:30–8:00):** per BetaKit's reporting, the shutdown was forced by a
  bank calling in Bench's venture debt loan — the lender exercising its right to demand immediate
  repayment. On-screen framing card, held for a beat: "The specific lender's name, and the exact
  covenant they used, have never been publicly confirmed. What IS confirmed: a bank called in the
  loan, and the company went dark the same day." Visual: generic "LOAN CALLED" document icon, no
  lender name or logo anywhere on screen.

### Main Content — The Aftermath (8:00–11:00)
- **Segment 7 — The Bankruptcy Filing (8:00–9:00):** January 7, 2025, Bench files for bankruptcy in
  Canada, disclosing $2.8 million in cash against $65.4 million in total liabilities. Visual: "$2.8M
  CASH / $65.4M LIABILITIES" side-by-side card.
- **Segment 8 — The Rescue (9:00–10:15):** December 30, 2024 — three days after the shutdown —
  Employer.com, an HR-tech company with no prior accounting specialization, announces it's acquiring
  Bench. Employer.com's own CEO says he learned of the shutdown from news reports, not from Bench
  directly. The deal closes January 1, 2025; Bench resumes operating under its own brand, and more
  than half of the 450 laid-off employees are rehired. Visual: "ACQUIRED BY EMPLOYER.COM — JANUARY 1,
  2025" title card.
- **Segment 9 — The Full Timeline (10:15–11:00):** Dec 27 shutdown → Dec 30 acquisition announced →
  Jan 1 deal closes → Jan 7 bankruptcy filing — the entire sequence compressed into about eleven days.
  Visual: compact four-point date-marker timeline graphic.
- Mid-video CTA (~10:45): "This channel has covered venture debt taking over a company before —
  package 017, Convoy. That story unfolded over months. This one happened in a single day. Subscribe
  for a new autopsy every week."

### The Actual Lesson (11:00–12:15)
- Plain-language comparison: Convoy's lender took control and wound the company down gradually. Bench's
  lender calling in a loan ended the company's operations instantly, with real people — 35,000-plus
  small-business owners, 450 employees — finding out the same way everyone else did.
- The core lesson, stated directly: venture debt isn't just a financing choice — it's a structural risk
  that can end a company's operations in a single day, with no warning built in for the people who
  depend on it.
- Visual: full-arc timeline graphic — founding (2012) → funding climb (2013-2021) → founder ouster
  (Nov 2021) → bridge financings (2022-2024) → shutdown (Dec 27, 2024) → acquisition announced (Dec 30)
  → deal closes (Jan 1, 2025) → bankruptcy filing (Jan 7, 2025).

### Final CTA + Outro (12:15–13:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — a venture debt lender's decision to call in a loan can end a
  company's operations overnight, with zero warning built in for customers or employees
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and if you want the other venture-debt-mechanics
  story on this channel, check out package 017 on Convoy"
- Engagement prompt: "Were you or someone you know affected by this shutdown? And do you think a

## Alternative Scripts

Opens directly on the $2.8M-cash-vs-$65.4M-liabilities graphic and the compact four-point timeline,
then works backward into the founding, the ouster, and the mechanism. Measured, graphics-forward,

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on the morning of December 27, 2024, from a small-business owner's perspective — logging in,
finding nothing there — before pulling back to the company history and the mechanism. Slower pacing,
holds the "who called the loan" mystery until Segment 6, resolving with the rescue and the full
timeline as the closing beats. Strongest human-impact arc of the three versions.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "11 days, in order" leading into rapid-fire beats: founding, funding climb, ouster,
struggle, shutdown, mechanism, bankruptcy, rescue — landing on the venture-debt-risk lesson as the
final, clarifying beat. Strong Shorts-extraction potential, especially the "35,000 businesses locked
out overnight" and "11 days, shutdown to acquisition" contrasts.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the founder-ouster segment (3:15–4:15) if it feels like a tangent before the main
  shutdown story
- **Fix:** frame Crosby's ouster explicitly as an early, visible instability signal that connects
  directly to the company's later struggle to raise money on favorable terms, rather than a standalone
  detour
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general bookkeeping-industry context if audience familiarity
  with the business model is already high
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "who called the loan" mystery more explicitly in the
  cold open if early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 6 (6:30) — most viewers will expect
  a named party to blame
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small running "days since shutdown" corner counter visible
  from Segment 5 onward, landing on "11 DAYS" at the bankruptcy-filing title card, to keep the speed of
  the full sequence visually present throughout
