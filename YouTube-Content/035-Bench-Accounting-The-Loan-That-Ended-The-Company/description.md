## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Bench
Accounting — a real, dated, fully-sourced case study in what happens when a venture debt lender calls
in a loan, and how fast the rest of a company's story can unfold once that happens.

Bench, a bookkeeping-as-a-service company for small businesses, was founded in 2012 in Vancouver by
Ian Crosby and Adam Saint, through the TechStars NYC accelerator. It raised over $100 million,
including a $60 million Series C in 2021 — $37 million in equity, $23 million in debt — that valued
the company at $232 million. Days after that round closed, Bench's board removed Crosby as CEO. Per
his own account, he was told the company needed a "professional CEO," and he says he'd turned down a
lucrative acquisition offer beforehand — his own claim, not independently confirmed by this research.

Over the next three years, Bench reportedly closed two more bridge financing rounds while struggling
with scalability and burning cash. On December 27, 2024, the platform simply stopped working — no
advance notice to customers, locking out an estimated 35,000-plus small-business owners from their own
bookkeeping and tax records. The same day, Bench laid off 450 employees.

Per BetaKit's reporting — the deepest sourcing found for this story — the shutdown was forced by a bank
calling in Bench's venture debt loan. This video states that mechanism plainly, and just as plainly
states what's NOT known: the specific lender's identity, and the exact covenant they invoked, were
never publicly confirmed. Bench itself declined to confirm these details to reporters.

On January 7, 2025, Bench filed for bankruptcy in Canada, disclosing $2.8 million in cash against
$65.4 million in total liabilities. But three days after the shutdown — on December 30, 2024 —
Employer.com, an HR-tech company with no prior accounting specialization, announced it was acquiring
Bench. Employer.com's own CEO said he learned of the shutdown from news reports. The deal closed
January 1, 2025; Bench resumed operating under its own brand, rehiring more than half of the 450
employees laid off days earlier.

Shutdown to acquisition announcement to closed deal to bankruptcy filing — the entire sequence took
about eleven days.

This channel has covered venture debt taking over a company before — package 017, Convoy. That story
unfolded over months. This one happened in a single day.
