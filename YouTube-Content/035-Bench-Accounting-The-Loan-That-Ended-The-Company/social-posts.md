## LinkedIn
"A real, dated case study in what happens when a venture debt lender calls in a loan:

Bench Accounting, a bookkeeping-as-a-service company founded in 2012, raised over $100 million and
reached a $232 million valuation after its 2021 Series C. Days after that round closed, the board
removed co-founder Ian Crosby as CEO. On December 27, 2024, Bench's platform went dark with zero
advance warning — locking out an estimated 35,000+ small-business customers from their own financial
records, and laying off 450 employees the same day.

Per reporting from BetaKit, the shutdown was forced by a bank calling in Bench's venture debt loan.
The specific lender and the exact covenant invoked were never publicly confirmed — Bench declined to
disclose them. On January 7, 2025, Bench filed for bankruptcy in Canada, listing $2.8 million in cash
against $65.4 million in liabilities. But three days after the shutdown, HR-tech company Employer.com
announced it was acquiring Bench — closing the deal January 1, 2025, and rehiring more than half the
laid-off staff.

Shutdown to acquisition: about 11 days.

Full breakdown: [video link]
