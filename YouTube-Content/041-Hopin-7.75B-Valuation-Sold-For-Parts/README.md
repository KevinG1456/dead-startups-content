**Framing correction, made during research — read before touching any file in this package:** the
popular shorthand ("raised $1 billion, sold for $15 million") is real but incomplete. The $15 million
was only the upfront cash portion of the August 2023 RingCentral deal for Hopin's Events + Session
product lines; the deal also included up to $35 million more in contingent consideration (up to $50
million total). And that deal was NOT the end of the company — Hopin retained StreamYard, Streamable,
and Superwave, continued operating under a new CEO, and only fully ceased to exist as an independent
company in April 2024, when Bending Spoons acquired what remained (terms undisclosed). This package
uses the complete, two-deal ending rather than stopping at the more famous "$15 million" headline.

**The real case:** Johnny Boufarhat founded Hopin in London in June 2019, building a virtual-events
platform while housebound with an autoimmune illness. The product launched in March 2020, almost
exactly as COVID-19 lockdowns began, and pandemic-driven demand for virtual events sent the company
into a growth rocket: from about 8 employees in early 2020 to roughly 1,200 at its peak in about two
years (~15,000% headcount growth), and from a $6.5 million seed round (Feb 2020) through a $450 million
Series D at a $7.75 billion valuation (Aug 2021) — over $1 billion raised in total, alongside five
acquisitions in 2021 including a $250 million purchase of StreamYard. As in-person events resumed
through 2022, demand for virtual events collapsed, and Hopin cut staff in three rounds within a single
year — February (12%), July (29%), and November (roughly 17% of what remained) — roughly halving
headcount from ~1,200 to ~500. In August 2023, Hopin sold its core Events and Session product lines to
RingCentral for up to $50 million total, founder Boufarhat stepped down as CEO, and the remaining
company (StreamYard, Streamable, Superwave) continued under new leadership. In April 2024, Bending
Spoons acquired what remained of Hopin outright, on undisclosed terms, ending its run as an independent
company less than five years after founding and about three years after its $7.75 billion peak.

**Framing requirement:** Never state the ending as simply "sold for $15 million" — use the full,
accurate two-deal ending (up to $50M to RingCentral in Aug 2023, then full acquisition by Bending
Spoons in Apr 2024, terms undisclosed). Do not use the unverified "$500 million share buyback" claim —
use the sourced, accurate partial-investor-liquidity framing instead. Keep the tone analytical, not
mocking — this is a demand-evaporation and overhiring story, not a fraud story, and Hopin built
something real that briefly mattered enormously.
