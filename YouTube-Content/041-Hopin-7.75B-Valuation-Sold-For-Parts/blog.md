# Blog Article: 8 Employees. 1,200 Employees. $7.75 Billion. Then It Was Gone in Two Deals.

*All figures sourced to TechCrunch, Axios, Sifted, CNBC, BusinessWire, and RingCentral's own SEC
filings — see `sources-log.txt` for the full citation map. This article corrects the popular but
incomplete "raised $1 billion, sold for $15 million" version of this story to the real, complete
figures: up to $50 million (not a flat $15 million) for the core product in 2023, and a second,
undisclosed-terms deal in 2024 that most retellings never reach.*

## Introduction
Hopin is one of the most frequently cited "pandemic unicorn" cautionary tales in startup media. Most
versions of the story stop at a single, famous headline: raised a billion dollars, sold for $15
million. That headline is real, but it's only half the story — and the missing half is arguably the
more instructive part.

## The Founding
Johnny Boufarhat, Australian-born, founded Hopin in London in June 2019 while largely housebound with
an autoimmune illness, which pushed him toward building a virtual-events platform. The product launched
in March 2020 — almost exactly as COVID-19 lockdowns began across much of the world.

## The Headcount Rocket
The timing turned out to be extraordinary. Hopin grew from about 8 employees in early 2020 to roughly
1,200 at its peak, about two years later — an increase widely reported at approximately 15,000%. Few
startups in history have scaled headcount this fast, in either direction.

## The Funding Rocket
Investors moved just as fast. A $6.5 million seed round in February 2020 was followed by a $40 million
Series A in June 2020, a $125 million Series B in November 2020 that pushed the valuation past $2
billion, a $400 million Series C at a $5.65 billion valuation in March 2021, and a $450 million Series D
at a $7.75 billion valuation in August 2021 — over $1 billion raised in total, at a pace reportedly
faster than any other European startup had reached that scale.

## Buying Everything
Hopin didn't just hire — it acquired. In 2021 alone, it made five acquisitions, including a $250 million
purchase of livestreaming-studio startup StreamYard in January. At the time, StreamYard looked like one
acquisition among several. It would later turn out to be the one that mattered most.

## The World Reopens
As in-person events resumed through 2022, the specific, pandemic-driven demand Hopin had been built and
staffed around began to evaporate. This wasn't a story about a product nobody wanted — the demand had
been completely real for about two years. It simply hadn't been permanent, and the company's headcount
and spending had scaled as if it were.

## Three Rounds of Layoffs
Hopin cut staff three separate times within a single year: February 2022 (138 people, about 12% of
staff), July 2022 (242 people, about 29% of staff), and November 2022 (a third round, roughly 17% of
what remained, including senior product and data leadership). Combined, headcount roughly halved across
the year, from about 1,200 down to about 500.

## The RingCentral Deal — and the Part Most Coverage Skips
In August 2023, Hopin sold its core Events and Session product lines to RingCentral. Here's the detail
that gets flattened in most retellings: the deal was not a flat $15 million. It was $15 million upfront
plus up to $35 million more contingent on performance targets — up to $50 million in total potential
consideration, according to RingCentral's own SEC filing. Founder Johnny Boufarhat stepped down as CEO
at the time of the deal; Badri Rajasekar, previously CTO and Chief Product Officer, took over.

Just as importantly: this deal was not the end of Hopin. The company retained StreamYard, Streamable,
and Superwave — by this point its actual primary revenue drivers — and continued operating as a smaller,
independent company.

## The Real Ending
The real ending came eight months later. In April 2024, Bending Spoons — the Italian acquisition-
focused tech company — acquired the entire remaining Hopin entity outright, on undisclosed terms. That
deal, not the RingCentral headline, is what actually ended Hopin's run as an independent company: less
than five years after it was founded, and about three years after its $7.75 billion peak.

## The Actual Lesson
The demand Hopin was built around was real — genuinely, substantially real, for about two years.
The mistake wasn't building the product. It was scaling headcount, spending, and acquisitions as though
a demand spike tied to a specific, dateable event — a global pandemic and the lockdowns that came with
it — were the company's new permanent baseline, rather than a temporary window that needed to be
managed toward whatever came after it.

The speed of the rise didn't protect the company from the speed of the fall. If anything, it made the
fall faster and harder to manage, because spending and headcount had scaled to match the peak of demand,
not any sustainable baseline beneath it.

## What Founders and Investors Should Take From This
- **A demand spike tied to a specific external event is not the same as durable product-market fit** —
  distinguishing between the two, in real time, is one of the hardest and most consequential judgment
  calls a founder can make
- **Headcount that scales with peak demand, not baseline demand, creates a second crisis on top of the
  first** — Hopin's three separate 2022 layoff rounds reflect just how far ahead of sustainable demand
  its headcount had grown
- **Popular shorthand versions of a story often flatten the real ending** — "sold for $15 million" was
  real, but incomplete; the actual conclusion took another eight months and a second, larger deal
- **A single "good" acquisition can end up mattering more than the core business it was acquired
  alongside** — StreamYard, a comparatively overlooked 2021 purchase, ended up being what survived
  longest
- **An acquisition doesn't have to be the end of a company — but it can still be a step toward it** —
  the RingCentral deal preserved Hopin's existence for another eight months, not indefinitely

## The Lesson
Hopin's story is usually told as a simple, single-headline cautionary tale: raised a billion, sold for
$15 million. The more precise, more useful version is about the actual mechanics of overhiring against
a temporary demand curve — what it looked like while it was happening, and how long it actually took,
in two separate deals, for the consequences to fully play out.

# FAQ (30 Questions)

**1. What was Hopin?**
A virtual-events platform founded in 2019, letting organizers run large-scale online conferences and
events, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic.

**2. Who founded Hopin?**
Johnny Boufarhat, in London, in June 2019.

**3. Did Hopin really grow that fast?**
Yes — from approximately 8 employees in early 2020 to roughly 1,200 at its peak about two years later,
an increase widely reported at approximately 15,000%.

**4. How much did Hopin raise in total?**
Just over $1 billion across its seed round through Series D (Feb 2020–Aug 2021).

**5. What was Hopin's peak valuation?**
$7.75 billion, reached with its $450 million Series D in August 2021.

**6. Why did Hopin's growth reverse?**
As in-person events resumed through 2022, demand for the virtual-events product Hopin was built around
declined sharply.

**7. How many layoff rounds did Hopin have?**
Three within a single year: February 2022 (~12%), July 2022 (~29%), and November 2022 (~17% of what
remained).

**8. How much did Hopin's headcount fall?**
From roughly 1,200 at peak to approximately 500 by the end of 2022.

**9. Did Hopin really sell for $15 million?**
That figure is real but incomplete — it was the upfront cash portion of the August 2023 RingCentral
deal, which also included up to $35 million more in contingent consideration, up to $50 million total.

**10. What did RingCentral actually buy?**
Hopin's Events and Session product lines — the technology, customer relationships, and the team behind
them.

**11. Did Hopin cease to exist after the RingCentral deal?**
No — Hopin retained StreamYard, Streamable, and Superwave and continued operating as a smaller company.

**12. Who ran Hopin after the RingCentral deal?**
Badri Rajasekar, previously CTO and Chief Product Officer, became CEO; founder Johnny Boufarhat stepped
down.

**13. What happened to Hopin after that?**
In April 2024, Bending Spoons acquired the entire remaining company outright, on undisclosed terms.

**14. So when did Hopin actually stop existing as an independent company?**
April 2024 — not August 2023, as the popular "$15 million" headline implies.

**15. Was the $500 million share buyback claim real?**
It could not be independently verified in this video's research and was not used. What is verified:
Hopin planned to provide partial liquidity to Series B+ investors from deal proceeds plus cash,
expected to recoup roughly half their investment.

**16. What was StreamYard, and why does it matter to this story?**
A livestreaming-studio startup Hopin acquired for $250 million in January 2021 — by 2023, it had become
Hopin's primary revenue driver and the part of the company that outlasted the original flagship
product.

**17. Was this a fraud story?**
No — this is an overhiring and demand-durability risk story, not a fraud story.

**18. Was the demand for Hopin's product ever real?**
Yes — genuinely and substantially real for about two years during the pandemic; the issue was treating
that demand as permanent rather than temporary.

**19. How many acquisitions did Hopin make?**
Five in 2021 alone: StreamYard, Streamable, Boomset, Attendify, and Jamm.

**20. What is Bending Spoons?**
An Italian acquisition-focused technology company that has acquired numerous apps and platforms; it
acquired the remaining Hopin entity in April 2024.

**21. Were the Bending Spoons deal terms disclosed?**
No — financial terms were not made public.

**22. How fast did Hopin reach unicorn status?**
It surpassed a $2 billion valuation in its Series B round in November 2020, less than a year after
launch.

**23. Was Hopin the fastest-growing European startup ever?**
It was reported at the time as reaching its valuation scale faster than any other European startup had
before.

**24. What is the core lesson of this video?**
That a demand spike tied to a specific, dateable event isn't the same as durable product-market fit —
and scaling as if it is turns an eventual correction into a collapse.

**25. How many years passed between Hopin's founding and its full absorption by Bending Spoons?**
Under five years — from June 2019 to April 2024.

**26. Did Hopin's founder start another company afterward?**
This video does not track Johnny Boufarhat's activities after stepping down as CEO in detail.

**27. Why do most retellings stop at the RingCentral deal?**
It's the more widely reported, more dramatic-sounding headline; the Bending Spoons acquisition eight
months later received comparatively little coverage.
