# Script Package

Anchored on Hopin — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RULE FOR THIS
SCRIPT: never state the ending as simply "sold for $15 million" — the real, complete ending is a two-
deal outcome: up to $50 million total (not just $15M) for the Events/Session lines to RingCentral in
August 2023, followed by Bending Spoons acquiring the entire remaining company (StreamYard, Streamable,
Superwave) outright in April 2024, terms undisclosed. Do not use the unverified "$500 million share
buyback" claim — use the sourced partial-investor-liquidity framing instead. Keep the tone analytical,
not mocking — the underlying demand for virtual events during the pandemic was real; the mistake was
scaling headcount and spend as if that demand were permanent.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 12–14 minutes (the founding, the growth rocket, the layoffs, and the complete
two-deal ending all covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "Eight employees. Then twelve hundred. Then a $7.75 billion valuation. Then it was gone — in two
  separate deals."
- Visual: rapid numeral sequence — "8" → "1,200" → "$7.75B" → "GONE" — each snapping in fast
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "You already know the '$15 million'
  headline. That's not actually where this story ends."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Hopin — a virtual-events platform founded in 2019, and one of the fastest-growing
  startups Europe had ever produced. It launched almost the exact week COVID lockdowns began."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll know exactly how fast this company grew, exactly how it
  fell apart, and exactly what happened to it — including the part that happened eight months after the
  headline most people already know."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from TechCrunch, Axios, Sifted, CNBC, BusinessWire, and RingCentral's
  own SEC filings — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "One honesty note up front: you've probably heard 'Hopin
  raised a billion dollars and sold for $15 million.' That's real, but it's not the whole story — and
  this video covers the whole story, including the part most retrospectives skip."

### Main Content — The Rocket (1:40–5:30)
- **Segment 1 — The Founding (1:40–2:15):** June 2019, Johnny Boufarhat founds Hopin in London while
  largely housebound with an autoimmune illness. The platform launches in March 2020 — almost exactly
  as COVID-19 lockdowns begin. Visual: generic virtual-conference-platform UI graphic.
- **Segment 2 — The Headcount Rocket (2:15–3:00):** Hopin grows from about 8 employees in early 2020 to
  roughly 1,200 at its peak — approximately two years later — an increase widely reported at around
  15,000%. Visual: headcount line graph, rocketing upward.
- **Segment 3 — The Funding Rocket (3:00–4:15):** $6.5M seed (Feb 2020) → $40M Series A (Jun 2020) →
  $125M Series B, valuation past $2B (Nov 2020) → $400M Series C at $5.65B valuation (Mar 2021) → $450M
  Series D at $7.75B valuation (Aug 2021) — over $1 billion raised in total, at a pace reportedly faster
  than any other European startup had reached that scale. Visual: valuation line graph, stacked beneath
  the headcount graph.
- **Segment 4 — Buying Everything (4:15–5:30):** in 2021 alone, Hopin makes five acquisitions, including
  a $250 million purchase of livestreaming-studio startup StreamYard in January — a product that would
  later turn out to matter far more than anyone expected at the time. Visual: "$250 MILLION —
  STREAMYARD ACQUISITION, JAN 2021" title card.

### Main Content — The Reversal (5:30–8:30)
- **Segment 5 — The World Reopens (5:30–6:15):** through 2022, in-person events resume worldwide, and
  demand for the core product Hopin was built and staffed around — virtual events — collapses. On-
  screen framing card: "The demand had been real. It just hadn't been permanent." Visual: crossover
  graph, virtual-event demand declining as in-person events resume.
- **Segment 6 — Three Rounds of Layoffs (6:15–7:45):** February 2022 — 138 people, about 12% of staff.
  July 2022 — 242 people, about 29% of staff. November 2022 — a third round, roughly 17% of what
  remained, including senior product and data leadership. Combined, headcount roughly halves across the
  year, from about 1,200 down to about 500. Visual: "3 LAYOFF ROUNDS — ONE YEAR" title card, with the
  headcount graph now falling.
- **Segment 7 — What $1 Billion Actually Bought (7:45–8:30):** despite the funding, the acquisitions,
  and the peak $7.75 billion valuation, none of it changes the fundamental math once the demand curve
  the company was built around reverses. On-screen framing card: "This isn't a story about running out
  of money before the layoffs — it's a story about what happens after." Visual: "$1.02 BILLION RAISED"
  title card.

### Main Content — The Complete Ending (8:30–11:00)
- **Segment 8 — The RingCentral Deal (8:30–9:45):** August 2023, Hopin sells its core Events and
  Session product lines — the technology, the customers, and the team behind them — to RingCentral, for
  $15 million upfront plus up to $35 million more contingent on performance targets: up to $50 million
  total, not the flat $15 million most coverage cites. Founder Johnny Boufarhat steps down as CEO;
  Badri Rajasekar, previously CTO and Chief Product Officer, becomes CEO. Visual: "UP TO $50 MILLION —
  RINGCENTRAL DEAL, AUG 2023" title card.
- **Segment 9 — What Was Left (9:45–10:15):** Hopin keeps StreamYard, Streamable, and Superwave — by
  this point its actual primary revenue drivers, ironically led by the 2021 acquisition most people had
  forgotten about — and continues operating as a smaller, independent company. On-screen framing card:
  "This is the part most retrospectives stop before reaching." Visual: "WHAT REMAINED" title card
  listing StreamYard, Streamable, Superwave.
- **Segment 10 — The Real Ending (10:15–11:00):** April 2024, less than eight months after the
  RingCentral deal, Bending Spoons acquires the entire remaining company outright — terms undisclosed —
  ending Hopin's run as an independent company less than five years after founding, and about three
  years after its $7.75 billion peak. Mid-video CTA folded in here (~10:45): "This channel usually
  covers companies that took years to unravel. This one grew and ended faster than almost anything else
  I've covered. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week." Visual: "ACQUIRED BY BENDING SPOONS — APR 2024,
  TERMS UNDISCLOSED" title card.

### The Actual Lesson (11:00–12:30)
- Plain-language comparison: the demand Hopin was built around was completely real — virtual events
  genuinely mattered for about two years. The mistake wasn't building the product; it was scaling
  headcount, spend, and acquisitions as if a pandemic-specific demand spike were the company's permanent
  baseline, rather than a temporary window to be managed toward whatever came after it.
- The core lesson, stated directly: growth driven by a specific, dateable external event is not the
  same thing as durable product-market fit — and the speed of the rise doesn't protect you from the
  speed of the fall; if anything, it can make the fall faster, because the spending and hiring scaled to
  match the peak, not the baseline.
- Visual: full-arc timeline graphic — founding (Jun 2019) → launch (Mar 2020) → seed through Series D
  (2020–2021) → $7.75B peak valuation (Aug 2021) → five acquisitions (2021) → in-person events return
  (2022) → three layoff rounds (2022) → RingCentral deal (Aug 2023) → Bending Spoons acquisition (Apr
  2024).

### Final CTA + Outro (12:30–13:30)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — a demand spike tied to a specific, dateable event isn't the same
  as durable product-market fit, and building as if it is turns the eventual correction into a collapse
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and this is the fastest full rise-and-fall arc I've
  covered on this channel, start to finish, in under five years"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you ever attend a Hopin-powered event during the pandemic? And did you already
  know the real ending — or did you think it stopped at the '$15 million' headline too? Tell me in the
  comments."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the paired headcount and valuation line graphs (rocketing up, then both falling) and
the full-arc timeline, then works through the founding and each deal as supporting detail. Measured,
graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine case study in overhiring against a temporary demand
curve.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on Boufarhat building the company from his sickbed in 2019, then cuts hard to the April 2024
Bending Spoons acquisition before rewinding to fill in everything in between. Slower pacing, holds the
"$7.75 billion" reveal until Segment 3, resolving with the two-deal ending as the closing beats.
Strongest tonal-irony arc of the three versions — sickbed origin to full company dissolution.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "one demand spike, five years of consequences," leading into rapid-fire beats: founding,
launch, headcount rocket, funding rocket, acquisitions, reopening, three layoff rounds, RingCentral
deal, what remained, Bending Spoons acquisition — landing on the demand-durability lesson as the final,
clarifying beat. Strong Shorts-extraction potential, especially the 8-to-1,200-to-500 headcount contrast
and the "the $15 million headline isn't the real ending" reveal.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the acquisitions segment (4:15–5:30) if the five 2021 deals feel like a dry
  business-news list
- **Fix:** frame the StreamYard acquisition explicitly as "the one deal from this list that actually
  outlives the rest of the company" — connects it forward to Segment 9's payoff
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general early-pandemic virtual-events context if audience
  familiarity with the period is already high
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the "the $15 million headline isn't the real ending" reveal
  even more explicitly in the cold open if early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 8 (8:30)
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small running headcount counter visible from Segment 2 onward,
  climbing to "1,200," then falling across Segments 5-6 to "~500," reinforcing the human scale of the
  story alongside the dollar figures
