Sources Log — Package 041: Hopin
Filled in: 2026-08-16

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
Hopin is the replacement anchor for this channel's "dead-saas-30" Batch 2 title #15: "14 months of
runway, 19 months of roadmap: reconstructing a burn-rate suicide from job postings." The original
pattern (reconstructing overhiring purely from job-posting cadence) had no sourceable single-company
anchor after two research passes. Hopin fits the underlying THEME — hiring and spending far ahead of a
demand curve that then collapsed — with a fully documented, on-the-record headcount and funding
trail, so it replaces #15's slot with a real, verifiable story rather than a forced reconstruction.

CRITICAL FRAMING CORRECTION — READ BEFORE WRITING ANY FILE IN THIS PACKAGE
The popular shorthand version of this story ("raised $1 billion, sold for $15 million") is real but
incomplete in two ways this package must correct:
1. The $15 million was only the upfront cash portion of the August 2023 RingCentral deal for Hopin's
   Events + Session product lines — the deal also included up to $35 million more in contingent,
   performance-based consideration (SEC filing: RingCentral 8-K, Sept 2023), i.e. up to $50 million in
   total potential deal value, not a flat $15 million.
2. Hopin did NOT cease to exist in August 2023. It retained StreamYard, Streamable, and Superwave and
   continued operating as a smaller company under new CEO Badri Rajasekar (former CTO/CPO; founder
   Johnny Boufarhat stepped down as CEO at the time of the RingCentral deal). Hopin's independent
   existence actually ended in April 2024, when Bending Spoons acquired the remaining company (legally
   "StreamYard Top Corp") outright — terms undisclosed. Use BOTH endpoints (Aug 2023 asset sale, Apr
   2024 full acquisition) as the real, complete ending — not just the more famous "$15M" headline.
Never state "sold for $15 million" as the full outcome — state "up to $50 million for the events
business in Aug 2023, then the remaining company fully absorbed by Bending Spoons in Apr 2024, terms
undisclosed."

FOUNDING — precisely sourced
- Johnny Boufarhat, Australian-born, founded Hopin in London in June 2019 after an autoimmune illness
  kept him largely housebound, prompting the idea for a virtual-events platform. The product launched
  in March 2020 — almost exactly as COVID-19 lockdowns began.
Source: CNBC (Aug 5, 2021, "Two-year-old events start-up Hopin boomed in the pandemic"); Wikipedia
("Johnny Boufarhat"); Grokipedia ("Johnny Boufarhat").

THE FUNDING ROCKET — precisely sourced, dated
- Feb 2020: $6.5M Seed round.
- Jun 2020: $40M Series A.
- Nov 2020: $125M Series B — valuation surged past $2 billion in this round, making Hopin a unicorn
  less than a year after launch.
- Jan 2021: acquires livestreaming-studio startup StreamYard for $250 million (cash + stock) — the
  first of five acquisitions in 2021 (also Streamable, Boomset, Attendify, and Jamm).
- Mar 2021: $400M Series C at a $5.65 billion valuation.
- Aug 2021: $450M Series D at a $7.75 billion valuation — at the time, reported as the fastest a
  European startup had ever reached that scale.
- Total raised across all rounds: just over $1 billion (~$1.02B summed across seed through Series D).
Source: CNBC (Aug 5, 2021); UNLEASH ("UK scaleup Hopin valued at $7.75bn after $450m Series D");
BusinessWire (Aug 5, 2021 Series D release; Jan 7, 2021 StreamYard acquisition release); TechCrunch
(Jan 7, 2021, "Hopin buys livestreaming startup Streamyard for $250M"; Mar 4, 2021, Series C
confirmation).

THE HEADCOUNT ROCKET — precisely sourced
- Hopin grew from approximately 8 employees in early 2020 to approximately 1,200 at its peak
  (roughly early-to-mid 2022) — an increase widely cited as approximately 15,000% in about two years.
Source: Sifted ("Was Hopin really such a failure?"); Headcount Coffee ("The Collapse of Hopin").

THE COLLAPSE IN DEMAND AND THE LAYOFFS — precisely sourced, dated
- As in-person events resumed through 2022 and pandemic-driven demand for virtual events shrank,
  Hopin cut staff in three separate rounds within a single year:
  - Feb 10-11, 2022: 138 employees laid off, approximately 12% of staff, "citing goal of sustainable
    growth."
  - Jul 11, 2022: 242 employees laid off, approximately 29% of staff — the second round that year.
  - Nov 2022: a third round, reported at approximately 17% of remaining staff, including the VP of
    product, head of data science, and several directors.
  - Combined effect: headcount roughly halved across 2022, from about 1,200 at peak down to
    approximately 500 by the end of the year.
Source: TechCrunch (Feb 10, 2022, "Virtual events platform Hopin cuts 12% of staff"; Jul 11, 2022,
"Hopin cuts 29% of its staff, just months after its last layoffs"); Conference News ("Hopin announces
new layoffs and a 'transformation'"); Skift Meetings (Feb 11, 2022; Jul 11, 2022).

THE RINGCENTRAL DEAL — Aug 2023, precisely sourced
- Jul 31 / Aug 2, 2023: RingCentral agreed to acquire Hopin's Events and Session product lines —
  including the underlying technology, customer relationships, and the engineering, product, and
  go-to-market teams behind them — for $15 million upfront cash plus up to $35 million more contingent
  on performance targets (up to $50 million total potential consideration, per RingCentral's SEC 8-K
  filing).
- Hopin planned to use a portion of the deal proceeds, plus existing cash, to provide partial liquidity
  to its venture backers; investors who had participated from the Series B round onward were reported
  to expect to recoup roughly half of what they had put in.
- Founder Johnny Boufarhat stepped down as CEO at the time of this deal; Badri Rajasekar, previously
  CTO and Chief Product Officer, became CEO.
- Hopin retained StreamYard, Streamable, and Superwave — by this point its primary revenue drivers —
  and continued operating as a smaller company, initially describing a pivot toward a "Community Suite"
  for creators.
- *** [VERIFY]: the earlier-referenced "$500 million share buyback" claim could NOT be independently
  confirmed in this research pass and should NOT be used — the accurate, sourced fact is the partial
  investor-liquidity plan described above (Series B+ investors expected to recoup roughly half their
  investment from deal proceeds plus cash), not a specific $500M buyback figure. ***
Source: TechCrunch (Aug 2, 2023, "Hopin, the struggling virtual conference unicorn, sells events and
engagement units to RingCentral"); Axios (Aug 3, 2023, "Hopin, once valued at $7.6 billion, makes some
major changes"; Aug 12, 2023, "Virtual meetings software flameout"); RingCentral SEC 8-K (Sept 2023);
Sifted ("Johnny Boufarhat steps down as CEO of Hopin").

THE FULL END — Apr 2024, precisely sourced
- Apr 9, 2024: Bending Spoons (the Italian acquisition-focused tech company) agreed to acquire the
  remaining Hopin entity — legally "StreamYard Top Corp," covering StreamYard, Streamable, and
  Superwave — ending Hopin's independent existence entirely, less than five years after founding and
  about three years after its $7.75 billion peak valuation. Deal financial terms were not disclosed.
Source: Skift Meetings (Apr 15, 2024, "Hopin To Be Acquired by Bending Spoons"); BusinessWire (Apr 9,
2024, "Bending Spoons to Acquire StreamYard"); AKM ("Italian Bending Spoons acquires StreamYard Top
(Hopin)").
