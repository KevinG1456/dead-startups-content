## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Hopin —
one of the pandemic's most famous unicorns, and a company whose real ending most retrospectives get
only half right.

Johnny Boufarhat founded Hopin in London in June 2019, building a virtual-events platform while largely
housebound with an autoimmune illness. The product launched in March 2020 — almost exactly as COVID-19
lockdowns began — and pandemic-driven demand sent the company into a growth rocket: from about 8
employees in early 2020 to roughly 1,200 at its peak about two years later, an increase widely reported
at around 15,000%.

Funding grew just as fast: a $6.5 million seed round in February 2020, a $40 million Series A in June
2020, a $125 million Series B in November 2020 that pushed the valuation past $2 billion, a $400 million
Series C at a $5.65 billion valuation in March 2021, and a $450 million Series D at a $7.75 billion
valuation in August 2021 — over $1 billion raised in total. Along the way, Hopin made five acquisitions
in 2021 alone, including a $250 million purchase of livestreaming-studio startup StreamYard.

As in-person events resumed through 2022, demand for virtual events collapsed, and Hopin cut staff in
three separate rounds within a single year: February (12%), July (29%), and November (roughly 17% of
what remained) — roughly halving headcount from about 1,200 to about 500.

Here's the part most coverage stops before reaching: in August 2023, Hopin sold its core Events and
Session product lines to RingCentral — not for a flat $15 million, but for $15 million upfront plus up
to $35 million more contingent on performance targets, up to $50 million total. Founder Johnny Boufarhat
stepped down as CEO, and Hopin kept StreamYard, Streamable, and Superwave, continuing to operate as a
smaller company under new leadership. Then, in April 2024 — less than eight months later — Bending
Spoons acquired the entire remaining company outright, on undisclosed terms, ending Hopin's run as an
independent company less than five years after it was founded and about three years after its $7.75
billion peak.
