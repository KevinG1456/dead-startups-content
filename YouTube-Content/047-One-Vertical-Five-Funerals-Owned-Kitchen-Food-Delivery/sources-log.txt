COMPANY 1: SPRIG — reused directly from package 001's sources-log.txt, not re-derived
- Founded 2013 by CEO Gagan Biyani, San Francisco. Vertically integrated: owned and operated its own
  kitchens rather than partnering with restaurants.
- Raised roughly $56-58M total (Series A $10M Mar 2014 Greylock; $45M round Apr 2015 led by
  Social+Capital and Greylock, Battery Ventures and Accel also participating).
- Hit a founder-confirmed run rate of "roughly $21-22 million" (Biyani, Mixergy interview, Feb 2016
  context) while losing approximately $850,000/month (Bloomberg, via Fortune).
- Uber Eats' major product relaunch around February 2016 was the precisely dated trigger — Biyani:
  "instead of going up by 2%, we were going down by 2%."
- Shut down May 26, 2017. ~200 staff affected at shutdown (distinct from "over 1,300" peak
  operational headcount — two different measures, not a contradiction). ~$8M returned to investors
  out of ~$56-58M raised.
- Biyani's own hindsight, on record: "I would have shut it down within three months" (of the
  negative trend becoming clear, not literally three months after the 2013 launch).
Source: this channel's own `001-The-Startup-That-Hit-10M-ARR-And-Still-Died/sources-log.txt`
(Mixergy, The Full Ratchet, TechCrunch, Fortune, Wikipedia — full citations there).

COMPANY 2: MUNCHERY — reused directly from package 023's sources-log.txt, not re-derived
- Founded 2010 (idea stage), launched May 2011, San Francisco, by CEO Tri Tran and Conrad Chu.
  Vertically integrated: industrial/commissary kitchens, in-house "Resident Chefs" and outside "Chef
  Partners," Munchery's own delivery drivers.
- Raised over $120M total (commonly cited around $125M) across 5 rounds; a major 2015 round valued
  Munchery at approximately $300M.
- Bloomberg reported Munchery losing up to $5M/month and wasting ~16% of food prepared (reporting
  clustered Nov 2016, alongside Tri Tran stepping down to Chief Strategy Officer, James Beriker
  installed as CEO).
- January 2017: ~30% of HQ staff laid off (a separate, earlier event — do not conflate with the 2019
  shutdown). May 2018: retreated from LA/NY/Seattle to San Francisco only.
- January 21, 2019: shut down abruptly, notifying customers same day; vendors reportedly got no
  notice; had been actively marketing gift cards in December 2018.
- February 28, 2019: Chapter 11 filed. CEO James Beriker named causes ON THE RECORD in the court
  filing itself: "increased competition, over-funding, aggressive expansion efforts, and Blue Apron's
  failed IPO" — a rare, court-filed causal statement, stronger sourcing than a press quote.
- This channel's package 023 already establishes the category-arc framing this package expands:
  SpoonRocket ($13.5M raised) collapsed March 2016 → Sprig ($56-58M, package 001) collapsed May 2017
  → Munchery ($120M+, most capital, most time) held on until January 2019.
Source: this channel's own `023-Munchery-Outlasted-Every-Rival-Still-Bankrupt/sources-log.txt`
(Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Wikipedia, Inc.com — full citations there).

COMPANY 3: SPOONROCKET — freshly researched for this package (previously only a one-line citation
in package 015's shutdown-letter corpus)
- Founded 2013, Berkeley CA, by co-founders Steven Hsiao and Anson Tsui.
- Raised $13.5M total: a 2013 seed round (Y Combinator + angels) and a 2014 Series A (Foundation
  Capital, Base Ventures, Sherpa Capital).
- Business: on-demand, pre-made (not made-to-order) meal delivery — cars stocked with pre-cooked
  meals, aiming for sub-10-minute delivery.
- The FIRST of the three core companies in this category to die. CEO Steven Hsiao confirmed the
  shutdown to TechCrunch, March 15, 2016 — unusually specific for a shutdown announcement, per
  package 015's corpus entry, naming competitor Sprig directly and citing "a tightening funding
  environment," rather than staying vague.
- Company stated it had reached positive contribution margin (selling meals for more than it cost to
  cook, package, and deliver) but could not raise further capital in a colder fundraising climate on
  top of other operating costs.
- After shutting down U.S. operations, SpoonRocket's assets/brand were acquired by Brazil-based
  delivery platform iFood, which used it to expand into the Latin American on-demand market — a
  genuinely different ending than Sprig's or Munchery's straight shutdowns, worth flagging on screen.
Source: Bloomberg (Mar 15, 2016, "Food Delivery Startup SpoonRocket to Close Shop"); TechCrunch (Mar
15, 2016, "SpoonRocket shuts down amongst on-demand apocalypse"; Mar 16, 2016, "SpoonRocket finds a
home with Brazil-based iFood"); Inc.com ("How This Startup Blew $13.5 Million and Ended in
Bankruptcy"); Berkeleyside (Mar 14, 2016).

COMPANY 4: MAPLE — freshly researched for this package (previously only a one-line citation in
package 015's shutdown-letter corpus)
- Founded 2014, New York City, by co-founders Caleb Merkl and Akshay Navle, with CTO Dan Cowgill —
  notably backed by celebrity chef David Chang (Momofuku), who was a co-founder/investor and lent the
  concept culinary credibility and press attention.
- Raised approximately $25-26M in a March 2015 Series A led by Greenoaks Capital; total lifetime
  funding reported around $50M+ ([VERIFY] — sources give a range, "$50M+" rather than one precise
  figure). Valued at approximately $115M after the 2015 Series A.
- Business: delivery-only "restaurant of the future" — Maple cooked its own food in its own kitchens
  and delivered it directly, no third-party restaurant partners, positioned as a premium, chef-driven
  alternative to Sprig/Munchery's model.
- May 8-9, 2017: shut down after fewer than 3 years in business, framed explicitly as "joining"
  Deliveroo (a London-based food-delivery company) rather than using "shut down" language — Deliveroo
  acquired Maple's technology and team (Merkl, Navle, and Cowgill all joined Deliveroo); acquisition
  terms undisclosed. This is a genuinely different ending than SpoonRocket's, Sprig's, or Munchery's —
  an acqui-hire into a much larger company, not a bankruptcy or straight shutdown.
- David Chang's backing did not prevent the collapse — the Inc.com headline itself frames this
  directly: "Not Even David Chang Could Save Maple From the Vagaries of the Food Business."
Source: Inc.com ("Not Even David Chang Could Save Maple..."); Recode (May 8, 2017, "The food-delivery
startup Maple has shut down"); Crain's New York Business (May 9, 2017); UKTech.news (May 9, 2017,
Deliveroo acquisition coverage); PYMNTS.com.

  015, 025, and 034 on this channel.
- Four of the five (Sprig, Munchery, SpoonRocket, Maple) share the SAME core failure mechanism: an
  owned-kitchen, vertically-integrated cost structure that could never produce sub-restaurant-margin
  meals at delivery-app prices and delivery-app scale. Present these four as genuine proof points of
  one repeatable pattern.

  acquired by iFood for Latin American expansion), Maple (acqui-hired into Deliveroo, framed as
  "joining" not shutting down), Josephine (wound down after a regulatory fight it couldn't afford to
