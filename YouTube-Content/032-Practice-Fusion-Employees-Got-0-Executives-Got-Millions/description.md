## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Practice
Fusion — a $100 million sale that shows exactly what a liquidation preference stack and an executive
carve-out actually do to real people, with dollar figures and names attached.

Practice Fusion, a free cloud-based electronic health records company for small medical practices, was
founded in 2005 in San Francisco by Ryan Howard, Alan Wong, Jonathan Malek, and Matthew Douglass. By
2015, it had raised roughly $157 million and hired JPMorgan to explore an IPO — JPMorgan's own
estimate put a possible 2016 listing near a $1.5 billion valuation. On August 14, 2015, Practice
Fusion's board removed Howard as CEO, replacing him with chief commercial officer Tom Langan. This
video does not claim to know what Howard was ultimately paid, or not paid, when the company sold three
years later — no source found in this research addresses his specific outcome, and we're not going to
guess.

In March 2016 — after Howard's departure — Practice Fusion signed a contract with Purdue Pharma, the
maker of OxyContin, accepting nearly $1 million to build a clinical alert into its software that
pushed physicians toward prescribing more extended-release opioids. That alert fired an estimated 230
million times between 2016 and 2019. Ryan Howard has told reporters directly that he was not in charge
of the company when that contract was signed — his own on-record statement, consistent with the
timeline.

In March 2017, federal prosecutors opened an investigation. Allscripts had offered $225-250 million
for Practice Fusion in May 2017 — after the investigation had already begun — then pulled the offer in
June, partly over an unrelated EHR-industry fraud settlement that spooked the sector. In January 2018,
Allscripts returned with a $100 million cash offer, which closed. Allscripts' own president later
confirmed to shareholders the company knew about the DOJ investigation before acquiring the business.

Here's the part almost no coverage connects clearly: CNBC's reporting weeks after the sale found that
ordinary employees and common stockholders received $0 from the deal — a direct result of stacked
liquidation preferences that paid preferred investors first. But a separate, pre-arranged management
bonus pool paid CEO Tom Langan $7 million and co-founder/executive Jonathan Malek $2.25 million, with
other executives receiving millions more. Three named former employees told CNBC they'd each lost
between $40,000 and over $100,000 — money they'd already spent exercising options, and paying tax on,
years earlier — on stock that ended up worth nothing.

In January 2020, two years after the sale, the DOJ announced a $145 million settlement — Practice
Fusion admitted criminal responsibility for the Purdue Pharma kickback scheme, the first-ever criminal
case against an EHR vendor. Separately, in 2021, former sales director Steven Mack pleaded guilty to
obstructing that investigation by deleting relevant files — sentenced in 2024 to probation and a fine.
Mack was not a founder or part of the executive carve-out; keep him in his own category.
