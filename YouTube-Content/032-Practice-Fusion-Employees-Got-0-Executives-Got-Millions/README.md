# 032 — Practice Fusion Sold for $100 Million. Employees Got $0. Executives Got Millions.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Practice Fusion's fully documented public
history (TechCrunch, CNBC, Fortune, Forbes, Wikipedia, DOJ/Oversight.gov settlement and litigation
records, NPR, Fierce Healthcare, VTDigger — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). Second title on the
channel sourced from the "dead-saas-30-Content Titles new-video-ideas.md" batch, filling Cluster 1
idea #3: "Liquidation preferences: the $100M exit where founders got $0."

**Framing correction, made during research — read before touching any file in this package:** the
batch title's literal premise ("founders got $0") does not survive sourcing. What's actually
documented: ordinary employees and common stockholders got $0 from the $100 million sale, while a
pre-arranged management carve-out paid CEO Tom Langan $7 million and co-founder/executive Jonathan
Malek $2.25 million. Founder Ryan Howard — ousted as CEO in 2015, gone from the company by the 2018
sale — is NOT confirmed either way in available reporting; no source states his specific payout. This
package is built on the real, sourced version of the story, which is sharper than the original title:
the preference stack didn't wipe out "the founders" as a group, it wiped out ordinary shareholders
while insiders with negotiated protection still got paid.

**The real case:** Practice Fusion, a free cloud-based EHR company for small medical practices, was
founded in San Francisco (2005, per Wikipedia) by Ryan Howard, Alan Wong, Jonathan Malek, and Matthew
Douglass, and raised roughly $157 million. In 2015, JPMorgan was hired to explore an IPO that could
have valued the company near $1.5 billion. On August 14, 2015, the board removed Howard as CEO,
replacing him with Tom Langan. In March 2016 — after Howard's departure — Practice Fusion signed a
deal with Purdue Pharma, accepting nearly $1 million to embed a clinical alert in its software that
pushed doctors toward more extended-release opioid prescriptions. In March 2017, a federal grand-jury
investigation opened. Allscripts offered $225-250 million for Practice Fusion in May 2017, pulled out
in June, and returned in January 2018 with a $100 million offer — which closed. CNBC's reporting weeks
later found that employees and common stockholders got nothing from the sale, while Langan, Malek,
and other executives split millions via a carve-out. In January 2020, Practice Fusion admitted
criminal responsibility and paid a $145 million DOJ settlement — the first-ever criminal case against
an EHR vendor. In 2021, a former sales director, Steven Mack, pleaded guilty to obstructing that
investigation (sentenced 2024 to probation and a fine) — a separate matter from the executive carve-
out and from Howard.

**Framing requirement:** Keep three groups clearly, repeatedly distinct throughout: (1) Ryan Howard,
the ousted founder whose 2018 payout is unconfirmed either way; (2) Tom Langan and Jonathan Malek, the
executives who received the carve-out payouts; (3) Steven Mack, the sales director later convicted of
obstruction — a separate matter unrelated to the sale proceeds. Never state Howard received $0; never
imply Malek (a co-founder) represents "the founders" broadly when he specifically was paid millions.
