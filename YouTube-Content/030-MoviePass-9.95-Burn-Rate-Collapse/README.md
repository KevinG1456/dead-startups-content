# 030 — MoviePass Charged $9.95 a Month for Unlimited Movies. It Lost Money on Almost Every Ticket. Then Its Fired Founder Bought It Back and Fixed It.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around MoviePass/Helios and Matheson's full documented history (Wikipedia, SEC filings, Bloomberg, Variety, Hollywood Reporter, DOJ/SEC litigation records, Time, CNBC — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). The single most viral, most quotable unit-economics catastrophe in this genre, paired with a genuinely rare happy ending: the ousted original founder bought the brand back and, per the most recent reporting found, built a real, profitable comeback.

**The real case:** MoviePass was co-founded in 2011 by Stacy Spikes. In 2017, Helios and Matheson Analytics bought a 51% controlling stake for $25 million — and in January 2018, Spikes was fired by the new owners after disputing their pricing direction. Freed of its founder's caution, the company launched an unlimited-movies-for-$9.95-a-month plan in August 2017, paying theaters close to full price per ticket while collecting a flat monthly fee — a structure that lost money on nearly every heavy user, on purpose, as a growth strategy. Subscribers grew to almost 3 million; Helios and Matheson reported an estimated $329.2 million net loss in the worst year. As the company scrambled to survive in 2018, it resorted to changing subscriber passwords to throttle heavy users. Subscribers collapsed to about 225,000 by April 2019; the service shut down September 14, 2019; Helios and Matheson filed Chapter 7 bankruptcy January 28, 2020. Two Helios and Matheson-era executives — CEO Ted Farnsworth and MoviePass CEO Mitch Lowe (NOT Spikes, who was long gone by then) — were criminally charged with securities and wire fraud in November 2022; Lowe pleaded guilty to securities fraud conspiracy in 2024. Meanwhile, Stacy Spikes bought MoviePass's brand and assets out of bankruptcy in 2021, relaunched it in September 2022 with a tiered, non-unlimited pricing model that fixed the exact flaw that killed the original — and, per the most recent reporting found (early 2024), the relaunched company posted its first profitable year in 2023, growing to $14 million in annual recurring revenue.

**Framing requirement:** Keep Stacy Spikes (founder, fired before the crash, no charges, later comeback) completely and repeatedly distinct from Farnsworth/Lowe/Itum (the executives who ran the company into the ground and were criminally charged) — this is the single biggest fact-accuracy risk in the package. The spine is unit-economics-as-slapstick (a company that paid more for tickets than it charged, on purpose) followed by the rare, genuine redemption arc.
