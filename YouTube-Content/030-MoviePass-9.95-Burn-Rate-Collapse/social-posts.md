## LinkedIn
"A real case study in deliberate unsustainable growth economics — and in what happens when the
person who warns against it gets removed first:

MoviePass co-founder Stacy Spikes was fired in January 2018 after disagreeing with new majority
owner Helios and Matheson's pricing direction. Seven months earlier, that direction had already been
set: an unlimited-movies-for-$9.95-a-month plan that paid theaters close to full price per ticket
while charging subscribers a flat fee. Subscribers grew to almost 3 million. Helios and Matheson
reported an estimated $329.2 million net loss in the worst year. Subscribers collapsed to roughly
225,000 by April 2019. The company shut down in September 2019 and filed Chapter 7 bankruptcy in
January 2020.

Two of the executives who ran the company through that collapse — CEO Ted Farnsworth and MoviePass
CEO Mitch Lowe — were criminally charged with wire fraud and securities fraud in November 2022; Lowe
pleaded guilty to securities fraud conspiracy in 2024. Spikes was not one of them — he was already
gone.

In 2021, Spikes bought MoviePass's brand and assets out of bankruptcy and relaunched it in 2022 with
a tiered, non-unlimited pricing model — correcting the exact structural flaw that killed the original
company. Per his own public statements (most recent found: early 2024), the relaunch posted its
first profitable year in 2023 and grew to $14 million in annual recurring revenue.

Full breakdown, with the founder kept clearly, repeatedly distinct from the charged executives
throughout: [video link]
