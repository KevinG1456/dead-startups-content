Sources Log — Package 030: MoviePass
Filled in: 2026-08-15

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
MoviePass is arguably the single most viral, most quotable unit-economics
catastrophe in this entire genre — a company that, for over a year, literally paid
more for movie tickets than it charged its own subscribers, on purpose, as a
strategy. It also has real criminal consequences for two named executives
(distinct from the original founder, who was pushed out BEFORE the reckless
pricing decision was made — an important, exculpatory distinction) and a genuinely
rare happy-ending twist: the ousted original founder bought the brand back out of
bankruptcy and, per the most recent reporting found, has built a real, profitable
company under the same name. This channel has covered brand-afterlife twists
before (025/Lyric's Wheelhouse-and-Black-Swan-Holdings ending) — MoviePass's is the
most complete and most positive version of that pattern yet.

---

FOUNDING AND THE FOUNDER'S OUSTER — important distinction, keep separate from the
$9.95-era executives below
- MoviePass was co-founded in 2011 by Stacy Spikes (and others).
- 2017: data-analytics firm Helios and Matheson Analytics acquired a 51%
  controlling stake in MoviePass for $25 million.
- January 2018: Stacy Spikes was FIRED from MoviePass by its new parent company,
  Helios and Matheson, after disagreeing with the direction the new owners wanted
  to take the pricing model. *** Critical framing point: Spikes was OUT of the
  company before the reckless $9.95 unlimited-plan strategy was locked in and
  before the worst of the financial collapse happened — he is not the executive
  responsible for the burn-rate catastrophe described below, and was not one of
  the individuals later criminally charged. Keep this distinction explicit and
  clean throughout the package — conflating Spikes with the Helios and Matheson-
  era executives would be a factual error. ***
Source: Hollywood Reporter/Time/Inc.com profiles of Spikes (multiple, corroborating
the 2011 founding and Jan 2018 ouster); SEC filings (Helios and Matheson 2017
424B3/424B5 forms, re: the 51% acquisition).

THE $9.95 PLAN AND THE BURN — precisely detailed
- The $9.95-a-month unlimited-movies plan originated, per Spikes' own later
  account, as what was initially framed as a short-term "promotional thing" to
  mark Helios and Matheson's acquisition of the company — not intended by Spikes
  himself to become the permanent price point.
- Spikes has stated on record that he knew from the start the $9.95 rate was
  never going to be profitable for the company at that price.
- Under Helios and Matheson's leadership (CEO Ted Farnsworth of Helios and
  Matheson; Mitch Lowe installed as MoviePass CEO), the $9.95 unlimited plan was
  launched and aggressively marketed starting August 2017, driving explosive
  subscriber growth — MoviePass grew to almost 3 million subscribers at its
  peak. Because MoviePass was paying theaters close to full price for each
  ticket while charging subscribers a flat $9.95/month regardless of how many
  movies they saw, heavy users generated massive losses per-subscriber.
- Helios and Matheson reported an estimated net loss of $329.2 million for the
  fiscal year covering the worst of the burn (per company financial disclosures).
- 2018: as losses mounted, the company took increasingly desperate measures to
  survive, including reportedly changing subscriber passwords to block heavy
  users from using the service and experiencing repeated outages — this is
  contemporaneously reported operational behavior, not disputed. *** Note: this
  research pass found this described in secondary retrospective summaries; if a
  specific primary-source article/date is needed on screen for this exact claim,
  verify against a contemporaneous 2018 news report before final recording;
  otherwise present as a well-established, widely-corroborated pattern rather
  than citing one single precise date. ***
- Subscriber count collapsed from almost 3 million at peak to approximately
  225,000 by April 2019, as service quality degraded and the company scrambled
  to survive.
Source: Wikipedia ("MoviePass"); Inc.com (Spikes' own account, "I Spent 13 Years
Building MoviePass"); IndieWire (Spikes interview on the company's demise); SEC
filings (Helios and Matheson financial disclosures).

THE SHUTDOWN AND BANKRUPTCY — precisely dated
- September 14, 2019: MoviePass notified remaining subscribers it would shut
  down, stating its "efforts to recapitalize MoviePass have not been successful
  to date."
- January 28, 2020: Helios and Matheson Analytics (MoviePass's parent) filed a
  Chapter 7 bankruptcy petition (full liquidation, not reorganization) in the
  U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York.
Source: NBC News (Sept 2019 shutdown notice); Deadline (Jan 2020 bankruptcy
filing); Bloomberg (Jan 29 2020, "MoviePass Owner Collapses Into Liquidation,
Leaders Quit"); Variety (Jan 2020).

THE CRIMINAL CASE — precisely dated, involves Farnsworth and Lowe, NOT Spikes
- The SEC and federal prosecutors alleged that from August 2017 through at
  least March 2019, Ted Farnsworth (Helios and Matheson CEO) and Mitch Lowe
  (MoviePass CEO) repeatedly made materially false or misleading public
  statements about MoviePass's business model and path to profitability, and
  that when the true, dire finances became undeniable internally, they chose to
  covertly throttle/degrade the service for heavy users rather than disclose the
  problem publicly.
- A third executive, Khalid Itum (a MoviePass business-development executive),
  was also named in the SEC's civil action.
- November 2022: Farnsworth and Lowe were criminally charged with wire fraud and
  securities fraud. The government estimated total investor/consumer losses
  from the scheme at approximately $303 million.
- 2024: Mitch Lowe pleaded guilty to a securities fraud conspiracy charge,
  admitting he had conspired to deceive the public and investors about the
  sustainability of the $9.95-a-month unlimited plan. He faces a maximum
  sentence of five years in federal prison under the plea. *** [VERIFY] before
  recording: confirm Lowe's final sentencing outcome and date, and the current
  status of Farnsworth's case (charged/awaiting trial vs. resolved), as of
  closer to the actual recording date — this research pass confirmed the guilty
  plea itself but did not fully confirm final sentencing for either
  Farnsworth or Lowe. Do not state a specific sentence for either man beyond
  the plea's stated maximum exposure without rechecking. ***
Source: Hollywood Reporter (SEC civil suit coverage); Variety (Sept 2024, Lowe's
guilty plea); SEC.gov litigation release LR-25525 (Farnsworth, Lowe, and Itum);
IndieWire (Nov 2022, criminal charges); Fortune (Nov 2022, Farnsworth's arrest).

THE COMEBACK — precisely dated, a genuinely rare positive ending for this channel
- 2021: Stacy Spikes — the ORIGINAL founder, fired in Jan 2018 and uninvolved in
  the $9.95-era executives' later conduct — acquired MoviePass's brand and
  remaining assets out of the Helios and Matheson bankruptcy proceeding.
- August 2022: Spikes announced MoviePass would relaunch under an entirely
  different model: three subscription tiers, NONE of them unlimited, with
  pricing varying by market — a direct structural correction of the flaw that
  killed the original company. Roughly 775,000 people joined a waitlist.
- September 5, 2022: the relaunched MoviePass began a beta rollout in Chicago,
  Dallas, and Kansas City.
- By the most recent reporting found in this research pass (early 2024): Spikes
  stated the relaunched service's users had collectively watched over 1 million
  movies since the relaunch, that 2023 was the company's first profitable year,
  and that the company had grown from $0 to $14 million in annual recurring
  revenue while powering 2.8 million movie trips. *** Present these growth/
  profitability figures as Spikes' own public statements (his own claims about
  his own company), attributed as such, rather than as independently audited
  facts — consistent with how this channel handles founder self-reported figures
  elsewhere. Given this data point is from early 2024 reporting, note on screen
  that more recent performance was not independently reconfirmed in this
  research pass — flag as the most recent available figure, not necessarily
  current as of recording. ***
Source: Variety (2021, Spikes reacquires assets); Time ("Everything You Need to
Know About the MoviePass Relaunch", 2022); CNBC (Aug 2022 relaunch coverage);
Screen Rant, TheWrap (relaunch details); Earnest Analytics (relaunch performance
analysis).
