# Video Editing Notes

## Zoom Suggestions
- Slow push-in on avatar during explainer beats; hard snap-zoom on the cold-open "$9.95/MONTH" vs.
  stacked-ticket-price-icons reveal, and again on the "FOUNDER OUT — JANUARY 2018" title card
- Slow, deliberate push-in (not a snap) on every "who's who" framing card — this is the one visual
  beat in the video that should never feel rushed

## Motion Graphics
- Cold-open "$9.95/MONTH" text with a hard cut to a stack of ticket-price icons visibly taller, red
  "LOSS" stamp landing between them
- "The founder didn't make this decision. He was fired seven months before it launched." pattern-
  interrupt card — white text on black, held for a full beat
- Clean "FOUNDER OUT — JANUARY 2018" title card closing Segment 2, with a visual thread/connector
  that reappears and closes the loop at the "2021 — SPIKES BUYS BACK THE BRAND" card in Segment 9
- Subscriber-count graph climbing steeply toward almost 3 million during Segment 3, then collapsing
  sharply toward 225,000 during Segment 5 — same graph, same axes, so the collapse reads as a direct
  visual payoff of the climb
- Side-by-side "$9.95 charged" vs. "actual per-ticket cost" comparison graphic (Segment 4), loss
  figure climbing to $329.2M
- Generic "PASSWORD CHANGED" login-lock icon (Segment 5) — do not fabricate or reconstruct an actual
  MoviePass UI; keep it a generic representative icon
- Dated timeline graphic for shutdown (Sept 14, 2019) and Chapter 7 filing (Jan 28, 2020), landing
  on a "CHAPTER 7 — LIQUIDATION" title card
- Three-name "who's who" graphic for the criminal case: Farnsworth and Lowe visually grouped
  together, Itum marked distinctly as the SEC civil-action-only defendant, Spikes' name kept
  spatially separate at all times with a standing "FIRED JAN 2018 — NOT INVOLVED" tag
- "GUILTY PLEA — 2024" title card paired with the [VERIFY]-sourced disclosure text ("this research
  did not confirm final sentencing for either man...") — held on screen a full beat, not a quick
  flash, never trimmed for pacing
- "2021 — SPIKES BUYS BACK THE BRAND" title card, connector line back to "FOUNDER OUT — 2018"
- "NO UNLIMITED TIER" callout placed directly beside a small ghosted repeat of the Segment 4
  "$9.95 vs. real cost" graphic — visually shows the fix landing on the exact original flaw
- Growth-figure graphic (1M+ movies watched, first profitable year 2023, $0-to-$14M ARR, 2.8M
  trips) locked in the same frame as its attribution card ("Spikes' own public statements...") —
  never shown alone, never separated even for a single cut
- Full-arc timeline graphic for The Actual Lesson: founding (2011) → ouster (2018) → $9.95 launch
  (Aug 2017) → collapse (2019) → bankruptcy (2020) → criminal charges (2022) → guilty plea (2024) →
  comeback (2021-2022) → current standing (2023-2024) — Spikes' timeline thread in a visually
  distinct color from the Farnsworth/Lowe/Itum thread throughout this graphic and reappearing in a
  small corner-graphic across Segments 2 through 11
- Package 025 cross-reference graphic: small side-by-side card, Lyric/Black Swan Holdings (brand
  revived by an unrelated third party) vs. MoviePass/Spikes (brand revived by the original founder)
  — label MoviePass's version explicitly as "the more complete version"

## Lower Thirds
- Source citation lower-third whenever a number, date, or term is referenced (Wikipedia, SEC
  filings, Inc.com, IndieWire, NBC News, Deadline, Bloomberg, Variety, Hollywood Reporter, SEC.gov
  LR-25525, Fortune)
- "FIRED JAN 2018 — NOT INVOLVED" standing tag on Spikes' name any time he appears on screen near
  Farnsworth, Lowe, or Itum — no exceptions, this is the single most important lower-third rule in
  the package
- "Per Spikes' own account" lower third whenever his own statements about the $9.95 plan's origin or
  the relaunch's performance are on screen
- "Presented as a well-corroborated pattern, not one single dated source" lower third during the
  password-changing detail in Segment 5
- "[VERIFY] — sentencing/case status not confirmed" lower third held for a full beat during the
  guilty-plea disclosure card in Segment 8 — do not shorten or rush this
- "Spikes' own public statements about his own company — not independently audited" standing lower
  third whenever the 2023-2024 relaunch performance figures ($14M ARR, first profitable year, 2.8M
  trips, 1M+ movies) are on screen

## Animations
- Text-reveal (not fade) for all key numbers, dates, and quotes
- Ken Burns pan/zoom on any static press photos or generic movie-theater/ticket-stub imagery — no
  real readable logos/trademarks (MoviePass, Helios and Matheson, AMC, or any specific theater
  chain) without rights clearance
- The "who's who" three-name graphic animates in with Farnsworth and Lowe sliding in together from
  one side and Spikes sliding in from a visually distant, separate direction — the physical motion
  itself should reinforce the separation, not just the labels
- The founder-out-to-founder-returns connector line (Segment 2 to Segment 9) draws itself across the
  screen slowly when it reappears, so the "loop closing" reads as a deliberate payoff
- The subscriber-count graph's climb (Segment 3) and collapse (Segment 5) should animate at
  noticeably different speeds — the climb steady and confident, the collapse fast and visually
  abrupt — to match the tonal shift

## Camera Movement (HeyGen/live-action hybrid)
- Static avatar frame during explanatory beats; reserve any movement for the who's-who graphic, the
  subscriber-count graph, and the full-arc timeline in The Actual Lesson
- Hold the avatar completely still and centered during the [VERIFY] sentencing-disclosure card in
  Segment 8 — no camera movement competing with that text for attention

## Background Music
- Cold open: tense, minimal pulse, cutting to near-silence on the pattern-interrupt card
- The Founding / The Acquisition and the Ouster: measured, slightly somber under the firing beat
- The $9.95 Plan / The Math That Never Worked: brisk and confident during the growth climb, curdling
  into something more clinical and sparse as the per-ticket-cost math lands
- The Desperate Measures: uneasy, declining energy, mirroring the subscriber collapse
- Shutdown and Bankruptcy: quiet, funereal, minimal
- The Criminal Case (Allegations + Charges/Plea): even-handed, investigative — never triumphant or
  punitive in tone, and explicitly not warm; hold this tone through the [VERIFY] disclosure card
- The Comeback (Buying It Back / Fixing the Flaw): a clear, deliberate tonal lift — warmer, hopeful,
  first genuinely upbeat cue in the video
- Where It Stands Today: bright but not triumphant — the attribution caveat should keep this from
  feeling like a victory lap
- The Actual Lesson: neutral, clear, slightly reflective
- Outro: return to a softened version of the cold-open motif

## Sound Effects
- Soft "date stamp" SFX on each key date reveal throughout (Jan 2018 firing, Aug 2017 launch, Sept
  2019 shutdown, Jan 2020 bankruptcy, Nov 2022 charges, 2024 plea, 2021 buyback, Sept 2022 relaunch)
- Sharp "cash register / loss" SFX as the $329.2M figure climbs in Segment 4
- Distinct "lock" SFX on the "PASSWORD CHANGED" icon in Segment 5
- Soft "gavel" or "case file" SFX (not overly dramatic) on the criminal-charges title cards in
  Segment 8 — avoid anything that reads as celebratory or vindictive
- Soft "unlock" or "revive" SFX at the "2021 — SPIKES BUYS BACK THE BRAND" reveal, and a warmer
  variant at the "NO UNLIMITED TIER" fix reveal
- No comedic stingers anywhere in the criminal-case segment (9:00–11:30) — tone stays investigative
  and precise, especially around the [VERIFY] disclosure
- Light, satisfying "chime" SFX on the full-arc timeline's final beat in The Actual Lesson, timed to
  the moment Spikes' colored thread and the Farnsworth/Lowe/Itum thread are shown side by side one
  last time
