# Thumbnail Strategy

One dominant idea per thumbnail — per this channel's standing rule. Do not try to cram the 1989
conviction, the funding figure, the performer lineup, the failed stream, AND the confession into a
single image — those live in the video, not the thumbnail. Each version below picks exactly one.

## Thumbnail Intelligence — Winning Concept
- **Core visual:** a lavish concert stage under bright lights, packed with the silhouette of a
  crowd — with a single bold "FAKE" stamp graphic overlaid dead-center
- **Main subject:** the contradiction between the real spectacle and the fake product behind it
- **Emotion:** shock/disbelief
- **Background:** a warm, bright concert-stage-lighting glow, blown out at the edges
- **Lighting:** stage-light flare, high contrast against the dark stamp graphic
- **Camera angle:** N/A (graphic-led)
- **Composition:** stage/crowd silhouette fills the frame, "FAKE" stamp centered and slightly
  rotated, title-safe space reserved at the bottom edge
- **Props:** one stamp graphic only
- **Thumbnail text:** `$16 MILLION PARTY. FAKE PRODUCT.`
- **Text placement:** bottom third, bold sans-serif, high contrast
- **Color strategy:** warm gold/amber stage light against a red stamp graphic
- **Contrast strategy:** the stage's real glamour against one blunt word communicates the entire
  thesis before any text is read

## Thumbnail Version A — Curiosity (the lineup)
- **Image concept:** a row of generic silhouetted "rockstar" microphone/guitar icons on a dark
  background, no real likenesses
- **Subject:** the scale of the performer lineup, kept generic/iconographic
- **Expression:** N/A (no faces, no real likenesses — no rights clearance)
- **Objects:** microphone icon, guitar icon, spotlight beam icons
- **Background:** dark stage-lit gradient
- **Colors:** warm gold icons on near-black
- **Lighting:** single spotlight sweep across the icons
- **Angle:** N/A (graphic-led)
- **Composition:** icons arranged in a row, dominant and centered, bottom-third text space
- **Thumbnail text:** `THE BIGGEST LAUNCH PARTY EVER THROWN`
- **Why it works:** sells the scale of the real event without naming any specific artist or using
  any real likeness, avoiding rights-clearance risk while still hooking on spectacle
- **CTR prediction:** high — scale-driven curiosity performs well on this channel
- **Best audience:** cold/browse traffic

## Thumbnail Version C — Authority (face-led)
- **Image concept:** face-led (avatar/host), serious/measured expression, small red "FUGITIVE CEO"
  tag in the corner — no reference to the party or performers in this version, keeping it to one idea
- **Subject:** host/avatar, three-quarter crop
- **Expression:** serious, measured, direct eye contact
- **Objects:** small "WANTED"-style tag icon in the corner
- **Background:** dark, minimal
- **Colors:** desaturated, single warm key light on the face, red accent only on the corner tag
- **Lighting:** dramatic single top-down light
- **Angle:** slight low angle, three-quarter crop
- **Composition:** face fills most of frame, small tag in the corner, bottom-third text space
- **Thumbnail text:** `HE WAS RUNNING FROM THE LAW`
- **Why it works:** face-led performs best with subscribed audience; the "wanted" framing signals a
  real, adjudicated criminal history rather than a vague allegation
- **CTR prediction:** medium-high with returning viewers
- **Best audience:** subscribers, suggested placement

## Notes on What NOT to Do
- Do not render any real performer's likeness, name, or logo (KISS, The Who, MGM Grand branding)
  without proper rights clearance — build every visual generically and add verified real imagery in
  the editor only
- Do not use a real photo of David Kim Stanley/"Michael Fenne" without rights clearance
- Do not combine the funding figure and the party cost into one thumbnail number — if a dollar
  figure appears, use only one, clearly labeled
- Do not imply Stanley's post-2000 fate — his outcome after his April 2000 surrender is unconfirmed
  in this research; no thumbnail should state or imply what happened to him afterward
