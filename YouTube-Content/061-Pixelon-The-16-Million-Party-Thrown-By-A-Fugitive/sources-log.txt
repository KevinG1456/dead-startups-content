WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
This is a real-fraud story, same category as 029 (Ozy Media) and 052 (Theranos) —
"fraud" language is accurate and sourced throughout. The company's founder,
"Michael Fenne," was a false identity used by a real fugitive, David Kim Stanley,
who had a real 1989 fraud conviction PREDATING Pixelon entirely. Keep these two
frauds distinct in every file: (1) Stanley's 1989 conviction (defrauding ~$1.25M
from elderly victims in Virginia and Tennessee, unrelated to Pixelon) and (2) the
Pixelon-specific fraud (raising investor money under a false identity, on the
basis of streaming technology that did not work). Do not blur them into one
event — they are two distinct frauds by the same person, roughly a decade apart.
Stanley's current whereabouts/legal status beyond his April 2000 surrender were
NOT confirmed in this research pass — state this plainly as unconfirmed, do not
speculate about his life today.

FOUNDING
- Pixelon, Inc. was founded in 1998, based in San Juan Capistrano, California.
- Founder and CEO presented himself as "Michael Fenne." He was, in reality, David
  Kim Stanley — a fugitive who had fled Virginia in 1996 and was, per multiple
  sources, "living out of the back of his car" when he arrived in California and
  began building Pixelon under the false identity.
Source: Wikipedia ("Pixelon"); StreamingMedia.com, "Pixelon: The True Story of
Streaming Video's Greatest Fraud"; Seattle Times (archived, Apr 16 2000, "Criminal
past, jail rewrite a dot-com success story").

STANLEY'S 1989 CONVICTION — PRE-DATES PIXELON, KEEP DISTINCT
- In 1989, David Kim Stanley pleaded guilty to 55 fraud-related counts, admitting
  to taking roughly $750,000 from Virginia residents and roughly $500,000 from
  Tennessee victims (~$1.25M combined) — reported as a scheme targeting seniors
  ("the man who fleeced his father's flock," per the sentencing judge's own
  description at Wise County Circuit Court, Virginia).
- He was sentenced to 36 years in prison, with 28 years suspended on the
  condition that he repay his victims — effectively a deferred/probationary
  arrangement contingent on restitution.
- In 1996, Stanley fled before completing restitution and was placed on
  Virginia's "most wanted" list. This is the fraud/flight he was still evading
  when he founded Pixelon two years later.
*** This 1989 case has NOTHING to do with Pixelon's technology or investors — it
is the reason "Michael Fenne" was a fugitive, not a Pixelon-era crime. State this
distinction explicitly on screen every time it's mentioned. ***
Source: Seattle Times (archived, Apr 16 2000); Pollstar News (Apr 18 2000, "Rock
'n' Roll Swindler").

THE TECHNOLOGY CLAIM AND THE FRAUD
- Pixelon marketed a proprietary video-streaming technology it claimed could
  deliver full-screen, TV-quality video over standard internet connections — a
  breakthrough claim relative to the real streaming technology of 1999.
- Multiple retrospective sources (StreamingMedia.com's two features, including
  one written by a former Pixelon executive; Wikipedia; Sunset) describe the
  underlying technology as fake, non-functional, and never having actually
  worked as advertised. Wikipedia's summary specifically states Fenne "stole the
  video streaming patent he used to create Pixelon" — this specific patent-theft
  claim is carried in secondary/retrospective sourcing rather than a named,
  linked primary legal filing; flag as [VERIFY] on the precise mechanics of the
  patent claim, while the broader "the technology did not work as claimed" point
  is well-corroborated across multiple independent sources.
Source: Wikipedia ("Pixelon"); StreamingMedia.com (both articles); Sunset.

THE LAUNCH — iBASH '99
- October 29, 1999: Pixelon staged "iBASH '99" at the MGM Grand in Las Vegas,
  intended as a live global demonstration of its streaming technology.
- Reported cost: $16 million — repeated consistently across StreamingMedia.com,
  Sunset, and an iHeart podcast episode built on the same reporting. [VERIFY] some
  older secondary retellings cite a lower ~$12M figure; this package uses $16M as
  the best-corroborated number.
- Confirmed performer lineup, corroborated across multiple sources: KISS, Tony
  Bennett, Sugar Ray, Faith Hill, Dixie Chicks, Natalie Cole, LeAnn Rimes, The
  Brian Setzer Orchestra, and The Who (reunited specifically for this show).
- The live stream failed. Viewers who tried to watch remotely were shown error
  messages; most attendees/viewers who did see any stream saw it via Microsoft's
  competing streaming software, not Pixelon's own technology — a direct,
  public-facing demonstration that the core product did not work.
Source: StreamingMedia.com (both articles); Sunset; Wikipedia; iHeart ("200: Tech
Tales Found," episode "Pixelon's Illusion: The $16 Million Party That Streamed
Lies").

THE EXPOSURE AND COLLAPSE — precisely dated
- April 16-18, 2000: Stanley's true identity and criminal history became public.
  The Seattle Times (Apr 16, 2000, archived) and Pollstar News (Apr 18, 2000)
  are the two strongest contemporaneous sources for this exact window; The
  Register also covered it (Apr 17, 2000, "Pixelon.com founder confesses he is
  fugitive embezzler").
- Fearing he had been tracked to his California home by Virginia State Police,
  Stanley returned to Virginia and turned himself in at the Wise County jail,
  confessing his real identity.
- Following the exposure, Pixelon's management team was ousted, the company laid
  off most of its employees, and it collapsed into bankruptcy. Multiple sources
  state the bankruptcy filing came in June 2000 — roughly eight months after
  iBash '99 and about two months after Stanley's exposure/surrender.

  any additional time he served, or his subsequent whereabouts/status. Do not
  state or imply an outcome here; if this comes up on screen, say plainly that
  his ultimate legal outcome after 2000 was not confirmed in this research.
Source: Seattle Times (archived, Apr 16 2000); Pollstar News (Apr 18 2000); The
Register (Apr 17 2000); Wikipedia; StreamingMedia.com.
