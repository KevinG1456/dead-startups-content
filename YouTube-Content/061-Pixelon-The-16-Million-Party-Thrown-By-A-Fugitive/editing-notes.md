# Video Editing Notes

## Zoom Suggestions
- Slow push-in on avatar during explainer beats; hard snap-zoom only on the "FAKE" stamp reveal and
  the error-message/stream-failure reveal
- No snap-zoom during the "UNCONFIRMED AFTER THIS POINT" beat — keep that moment visually flat and
  static, consistent with the honest-uncertainty framing required

## Motion Graphics
- Courthouse graphic + "1989" date stamp with a persistent "UNRELATED TO PIXELON" lower third for
  the first-fraud segment
- Car silhouette + name-change graphic for the escape/new-identity segment
- Funding figure building toward "$30.9M" for the raise segment
- Lineup graphic (generic icons only — microphone, guitar, spotlight — no real likenesses) + "$16,000,000" figure held on screen for the party segment
- Buffering/error-icon graphic hard-cutting to "MOST VIEWERS WATCHED IT ON A COMPETITOR'S SOFTWARE"
  for the failure segment
- Newspaper-headline treatment + calendar graphic landing on April 2000 + "UNCONFIRMED AFTER THIS
  POINT" lower third for the exposure segment — this lower third must hold, not flash
- Layoff graphic + bankruptcy date stamp for the collapse segment
- Two-event comparison graphic (1989 vs. 1998-2000), clearly separated and dated, for the "Two
  Frauds, Not One" segment
- "Autopsy Report Card" end-graphic (full timeline: 1989 Conviction → Flight → Founding → Raise →
  Party → Failure → Exposure → Collapse)

## Lower Thirds
- Source citation lower-third whenever a number, date, or claim is referenced (Seattle Times,
  Pollstar News, The Register, Forbes, Wikipedia, StreamingMedia.com)
- "UNRELATED TO PIXELON" lower third held for the full 1989-conviction segment
- "UNCONFIRMED AFTER THIS POINT" lower third held for the full post-exposure segment — no exceptions
- "$30.9M raised" and "$16M party cost" as standing lower-third phrasings whenever either figure is
  referenced — never let one appear without being clearly labeled

## Animations
- Text-reveal (not fade) for all key numbers, dates, and claims
- Ken Burns pan/zoom on any static press-era imagery or generic stage/office/courthouse imagery (no
  real readable logos/text — KISS, The Who, MGM Grand — without rights clearance, and no real photos
  of David Kim Stanley without rights clearance)
- The stream-failure beat gets a distinct, slightly deflating visual treatment — different pacing
  than the high-energy party segment before it, since it's the story's clearest "wait, it didn't
  work?" moment
- The "UNCONFIRMED AFTER THIS POINT" card animates in and holds — no bounce, no dramatic sting; treat
  it visually the same as any other honest-uncertainty moment on this channel

## Camera Movement (HeyGen/live-action hybrid)
- Static avatar frame during explanatory beats; reserve any movement for the timeline graphic and the
  two-event comparison graphic layers

## Background Music
- Cold open: bright, celebratory pulse (mirroring the party's real energy) that curdles slightly
  under the pattern-interrupt line
- The First Fraud / The Escape: tense, minimal
- The Pitch and the Raise: brisk, building energy, optimistic
- The Party: full, bright, celebratory — let the scale feel genuinely impressive before the turn
- The Failure: the music should visibly deflate/thin out under the error-message reveal
- The Exposure: quiet, matter-of-fact, no dramatic sting
- The Collapse: sparse, clinical
- The Actual Lesson: neutral, clear, slightly reflective educational tone
- Outro: return to cold-open motif, slightly wry

## Sound Effects
- Soft "date stamp" SFX on each key date reveal throughout the timeline
- Distinct "record scratch" or "power-down" SFX on the stream-failure reveal only
- No SFX during the "UNCONFIRMED AFTER THIS POINT" card — let it land plainly
- Avoid comedic stingers during the 1989-conviction segment specifically — that beat involves real
  victims and should stay matter-of-fact, not played for laughs
