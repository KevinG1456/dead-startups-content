# 061 — He Threw a $16 Million Party With KISS and The Who. He Was a Fugitive the Whole Time.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around David Kim Stanley/"Michael Fenne" and
Pixelon's full documented history (Seattle Times, Pollstar News, The Register, Forbes, Wikipedia,
StreamingMedia.com — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). Real-fraud story, same category as 029
(Ozy Media) and 052 (Theranos), but with a distinct wrinkle: the founder's criminal record predates
the company entirely. Read `sources-log.txt` in full before touching this package — especially the
section on keeping the 1989 conviction and the Pixelon-era fraud explicitly separate.

**The real case:** Pixelon, Inc. — founded 1998 in San Juan Capistrano, California, by a man calling
himself "Michael Fenne." He was, in reality, David Kim Stanley, a fugitive who had fled Virginia in
1996 to avoid completing restitution on a 1989 conviction for 55 fraud-related counts (roughly $1.25
million taken from elderly victims in Virginia and Tennessee). As "Fenne," Stanley raised over $30
million from investors (Advanced Equities, a Chicago investment bank, raised $30.9M specifically) on
the strength of a proprietary video-streaming technology that, per multiple independent sources,
never actually worked. On October 29, 1999, Pixelon staged "iBASH '99" at the MGM Grand in Las
Vegas — a $16 million launch party featuring KISS, Tony Bennett, Faith Hill, the Dixie Chicks, and a
one-off reunion of The Who — meant to demonstrate the technology live to a global audience. The
stream failed. Viewers got error messages; most who saw anything saw it through Microsoft's
competing software, not Pixelon's. In April 2000, Stanley's real identity became public. Fearing
he'd been tracked down, he turned himself in at a Virginia jail. Pixelon's management was ousted,
most employees were laid off, and the company collapsed into bankruptcy by June 2000 — about eight
months after the most expensive product demo in dot-com history proved the product didn't exist.

**Framing requirement:** This is a real-fraud story — the false identity, the fabricated technology
claims, and the fundraising built on both are well-corroborated across independent, largely
contemporaneous sources. But the two frauds involved are NOT the same event: Stanley's 1989
conviction predates Pixelon by nearly a decade and has nothing to do with its technology or
investors; the Pixelon-era fraud is a separate act built on the false identity that conviction
forced him into. Keep them distinct on screen every time. No confirmed information exists in this
research pass about what happened to Stanley after his April 2000 surrender — state that plainly
rather than guessing.
