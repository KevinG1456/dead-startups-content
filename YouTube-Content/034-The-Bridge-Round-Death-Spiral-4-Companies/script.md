# Script Package

Anchored on four companies — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT RULE FOR
THIS SCRIPT: keep each company's specific "bridge" mechanism distinct — Katerra (repeat emergency
equity infusions from one investor), Veev (a named, failed bridge loan), Plastiq (a failed SPAC merger
functioning as a public-market bridge, then real DIP bankruptcy financing), Enjoy Technology (DIP
financing from its eventual buyer). Do not blur these into one generic "ran out of money" narrative.
Precisely state that SoftBank's Dec 2020 Katerra infusion was its final rescue attempt BEFORE
bankruptcy, not literally the last money Katerra ever received (a further DIP loan followed). No fraud
or criminal allegations apply to anyone across all four companies — keep the tone analytical.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 16–19 minutes (pattern setup, four company segments, and the shared lesson, all
covered in full)

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "Four companies. Four different industries. Every single one tried the same move when things
  got bad — one more round, framed as a bridge to survival. None of them made it across."
- Visual: cracked bridge icon, four small building/company icons falling into darkness beneath it
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This isn't a coincidence. It's a
  pattern. Watch it repeat four times."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This video covers four real companies — a construction-tech company, a prefab home builder, a
  B2B payments company, and a mobile-retail company — that each hit a moment of real financial
  distress, and each tried to solve it the same way: one more raise, specifically framed as a bridge
  to survival or to a bigger round. In every single case, the bridge either didn't close, or didn't
  actually save the company."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll be able to spot this pattern yourself — and you'll see
  the moment, in each company's story, where the bridge round stopped being a rescue plan and started
  being a delay."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from Bloomberg, CNBC, TechCrunch, Construction Dive, Forbes,
  Bloomberg Law, The Information, and Retail Dive — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early and plainly: "Each of these four companies used a different kind of
  bridge — an emergency equity infusion, a bridge loan, a SPAC merger, bankruptcy financing. Watch how
  differently each one is structured, and how identically each one ends."

### Company 1 — Katerra (1:40–5:00)
- **Segment 1 — The Rise (1:40–2:30):** founded 2015 by Michael Marks (former Flextronics CEO), a
  vertically integrated construction company. January 2018: an $865 million SoftBank Vision Fund round
  valued Katerra at over $3 billion; SoftBank's cumulative investment reached roughly $2 billion.
  Visual: "$2 BILLION RAISED" title card.
- **Segment 2 — Rescue One (2:30–3:15):** May 2020, SoftBank provides a $200 million emergency
  infusion, alongside a new CEO brought in to fix the company's finances. Visual: "RESCUE #1 — $200M —
  MAY 2020" title card.
- **Segment 3 — Rescue Two (3:15–4:00):** December 2020, just seven months later, SoftBank provides a
  SECOND $200 million infusion — reported as SoftBank's final cash infusion before what comes next —
  taking a majority stake. Its lender, Greensill Capital, cancels $435 million of Katerra's debt for a
  5% stake. Visual: "RESCUE #2 — $200M — DECEMBER 2020" title card.
- **Segment 4 — The Collapse (4:00–5:00):** in early 2021, Greensill Capital itself collapses in an
  unrelated scandal, cutting off Katerra's working-capital lifeline. June 7, 2021, Katerra files
  Chapter 11 — and receives a THIRD infusion, a $35 million bankruptcy-only loan from SoftBank, just to
  fund a 60-day asset sale. On-screen framing card: "Two rescues. A third, bankruptcy-only loan. Still
  gone." Visual: "CHAPTER 11 — JUNE 7, 2021" title card.

### Company 2 — Veev (5:00–8:00)
- **Segment 5 — The Rise (5:00–6:00):** founded 2008 as a real estate developer, pivoted to prefab,
  tech-forward home construction around 2018, under Amit Haller. March 2022: a $400 million round
  pushes Veev to unicorn status, total funding around $600-650 million. Visual: "UNICORN STATUS —
  MARCH 2022" title card.
- **Segment 6 — The Failed Bridge (6:00–7:30):** by late 2023, Veev tries to raise a new round. It
  fails. Veev then specifically tries to secure a bridge loan as a fallback plan. That fails too.
  On-screen framing card: "Not a vague 'ran out of money' story — a specifically named bridge loan
  that nobody would give them." Visual: "BRIDGE LOAN ATTEMPTED — LATE 2023" title card, red X stamp.
- **Segment 7 — The Shutdown (7:30–8:00):** November 27, 2023, Veev shuts down, laying off roughly 250
  employees. Visual: "SHUTDOWN — NOVEMBER 27, 2023" title card.

### Company 3 — Plastiq (8:00–11:15)
- **Segment 8 — The Rise (8:00–8:45):** founded 2012 by Eliot Buchanan and Dan Choi, a B2B bill-
  payment platform. January 2022: a funding round values Plastiq at $940 million. Visual: "$940M
  VALUATION — JANUARY 2022" title card.
- **Segment 9 — The SPAC as a Bridge (8:45–10:00):** August 2022, Plastiq announces a SPAC merger with
  Colonnade Acquisition Corp II at a $480 million valuation, expected to add over $300 million to its
  balance sheet — effectively its "big last raise." September 2022, in the middle of this, Plastiq
  acquires Nearside for $130 million. Late 2022, Plastiq determines the deal isn't viable and backs
  out; February 2023, Colonnade threatens litigation. On-screen framing card: "A public-market bridge,
  not a private one — and it collapsed the same way." Visual: "SPAC DEAL COLLAPSES" title card.
- **Segment 10 — SVB and the End (10:00–11:15):** March 2023, Silicon Valley Bank — Plastiq's own bank
  — collapses, compounding the crisis. May 24, 2023, Plastiq files Chapter 11, having missed a $2
  million interest payment on a $43 million loan despite extensions. It survives on $7.1 million in
  DIP financing before its assets sell for $27.5 million. Visual: "SOLD FOR $27.5M" title card.
- Mid-video CTA (~11:00): "One pattern, three completely different industries so far. Subscribe for a
  new autopsy every week — and stay for the fourth."

### Company 4 — Enjoy Technology (11:15–14:00)
- **Segment 11 — The Rise (11:15–12:00):** founded 2014 by Ron Johnson, former Apple SVP of Retail and
  former JCPenney CEO — mobile "retail-as-a-service" vans, with AT&T as a major partner. October 15,
  2021, Enjoy completes a SPAC merger at a roughly $1.2 billion implied valuation, raising up to $454
  million. Visual: "SPAC COMPLETE — OCTOBER 2021" title card.
- **Segment 12 — The Crisis (12:00–13:00):** early 2022, an iPhone supply shortage guts Enjoy's AT&T-
  driven sales, right as the company expands staffing and warehouses for roughly 100 new markets. June
  30, 2022 — barely eight months after going public — Enjoy files Chapter 11 with just $523,000 in
  cash on hand, not enough for the next weekly payroll. Visual: "$523,000 IN THE BANK" title card.
- **Segment 13 — The Buyer as the Bridge (13:00–14:00):** to survive bankruptcy, Enjoy takes a $2.5
  million bridge loan and a $55 million financing commitment from Asurion — which becomes the
  stalking-horse bidder and ultimately buys Enjoy's mobile-retail business for $110 million. On-screen
  framing card: "The company that bridged them through bankruptcy is the same company that bought what
  was left." Visual: "SOLD TO ASURION — $110M" title card.

### The Actual Lesson (14:00–15:30)
- Plain-language comparison: four companies, four industries, four differently-structured "bridges" —
  emergency equity, a bridge loan, a SPAC merger, bankruptcy DIP financing — and every single one
  either failed to close or failed to actually save the company as an independent business.
- The core pattern, stated directly: a bridge round is a bet that the company's situation will improve
  before the bridge runs out. In all four of these cases, it didn't — and in two of the four (Enjoy,
  and arguably Katerra's asset-sale financing), the "rescuer" ended up as the buyer of the wreckage.
- Visual: four-column side-by-side timeline graphic, each company's distress-to-bridge-to-outcome
  sequence shown in parallel.

### Final CTA + Outro (15:30–17:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — a bridge round is only as good as what's on the other side of
  it, and in each of these four cases, nothing was
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these, and if you want more cap-table and financing-

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the four-column side-by-side timeline graphic from The Actual Lesson, then works
through each company in the same order, using the graphic as a recurring visual anchor after each
segment. Measured, graphics-forward, treats the video as a genuine pattern-recognition case study.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on Enjoy Technology's $523,000 bank balance as the most viscerally specific moment, then works
backward through all four companies chronologically by collapse date (Katerra 2021 → Enjoy 2022 →
Plastiq 2023 → Veev 2023), closing with the shared-pattern reveal. Strongest tonal build of the three
versions.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "four companies, four bridges, four collapses" with rapid-fire beats per company (rise →
bridge attempt → failure → outcome), landing on the shared-pattern lesson as the final, clarifying
beat. Strong Shorts-extraction potential — each company segment can stand alone as its own Short.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the transition between companies (end of each segment) if the pacing flags before
  the next company's hook lands
- **Fix:** end each company segment on its most dramatic single fact (the $200M double rescue, the
  failed bridge loan, the SPAC lawsuit threat, the $523,000 balance) immediately before cutting to the
  next company, so momentum carries across the transition
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general industry-context setup (construction tech, prefab
  housing, B2B payments, mobile retail) if audience is more interested in the financing pattern than
  the business models themselves
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease all four companies' names or industries in the cold open if
  early-retention data shows drop-off before the fourth segment — signal early that this is a build-up
  across four proof points, not four unrelated stories
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small running "bridge #1 / #2 / #3 / #4" counter visible in a
  corner graphic throughout, so viewers always know how far through the comparator they are
