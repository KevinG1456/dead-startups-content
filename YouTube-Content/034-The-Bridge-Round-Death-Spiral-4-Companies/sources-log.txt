Sources Log — Package 034: The Bridge Round Death Spiral (Katerra, Veev, Plastiq, Enjoy Technology)
Filled in: 2026-08-15

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
This is the anchor for this channel's "Money Mechanics" batch title #2: "The bridge round death
spiral: 4 companies, 4 'one last raise' stories." Pattern: bridge-to-nowhere financing — a company in
distress takes (or tries to take) one more round of capital specifically framed as a bridge to
survival or to a bigger raise, and that bridge either fails to close or fails to actually save the
company. Four real, independently-verified companies anchor this package, chosen for having a clean,
well-documented "one last raise" moment and for not having been used elsewhere on this channel:
Katerra (construction tech), Veev (prefab home construction), Plastiq (B2B bill payment), and Enjoy
Technology (mobile retail). Same multi-company comparator format as packages 007, 015, and 025 on this
channel — each company gets its own sourced section below.

---

COMPANY 1: KATERRA — the clearest, most literal "one more bridge" story; TWO separate rescue
infusions from the same investor before collapse
- Founded 2015 by Michael Marks (former CEO of Flextronics, former interim CEO of Tesla). Business:
  a vertically integrated construction company aiming to control design, manufacturing, and building
  under one roof, reducing reliance on traditional contractors/subcontractors.
- January 2018: an $865 million Series D round led by SoftBank Vision Fund valued Katerra just over
  $3 billion.
- SoftBank's cumulative investment in Katerra is widely reported at approximately $2 billion.
- May 2020: SoftBank provided a $200 million emergency infusion — the first "bridge" — coinciding
  with a new CEO, Paal Kibsgaard, brought in to fix the company's finances.
- December 2020: SoftBank provided a SECOND $200 million infusion just seven months later — explicitly
  reported by Bloomberg as SoftBank's "final cash infusion" to the company — and took a majority
  stake as part of the deal. As part of the same restructuring, Greensill Capital (a SoftBank-backed
  financial services firm that had been financing Katerra's working capital) canceled approximately
  $435 million of Katerra's debt in exchange for a 5% equity stake.
- Q1 2021: Greensill Capital itself collapsed in an unrelated, separate financial scandal — this
  eliminated the working-capital financing lifeline Katerra had been relying on, and Katerra was
  unable to raise further capital to replace it.
- June 7, 2021: Katerra filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, explicitly citing both the COVID-19 pandemic
  and its inability to raise new capital following Greensill's collapse.
- After filing, SoftBank offered Katerra a $35 million debtor-in-possession (DIP) loan — a THIRD
  "bridge," this one explicitly to fund a 60-day asset-sale process during bankruptcy, not to save
  the company as a going concern.
Source: Bloomberg (Jun 7, 2021, "SoftBank Throws Final Cash Infusion to Bankrupt Startup Katerra" —
note: despite this headline referring to the Dec 2020 round as "final," a further DIP loan followed
bankruptcy, so use "final infusion before bankruptcy" precisely, not "final money Katerra ever
received"); Construction Dive (multiple 2020-2021 articles); TechCrunch (Jun 1, 2021); The Real Deal
(Jun 7, 2021); Architect Magazine ("Katerra's $2 Billion Legacy").

COMPANY 2: VEEV — a literal, named "bridge loan" attempt that failed
- Founded 2008 (originally a real estate development company) by Amit Haller, Ami Avrahami, and Dafna
  Akiva; pivoted toward prefabricated, tech-forward home construction starting around 2018.
- March 2022: a $400 million Series D round — backed by LenX (formerly Lennar Ventures), Zeev
  Ventures, Fifth Wall Climate Tech, JLL Spark Global Ventures, and led by Bond, with Khosla Ventures
  also among its backers — pushed Veev to unicorn status at a reported valuation of approximately
  $1-1.5 billion (sources vary on the exact figure; use "unicorn status, roughly $1 billion+" if a
  single precise number is needed). Total funding by this point: approximately $600-650 million.
- Late 2023: Veev attempted to raise a new financing round. When that failed, the company specifically
  tried to secure a BRIDGE LOAN from investors as a fallback — and that bridge loan also failed to
  materialize.
- November 27, 2023: Veev informed staff it was shutting down, citing that "it was not possible to
  secure additional funding" given market conditions. Approximately 250 employees were laid off.
Source: TechCrunch (Nov 27, 2023, "Prefab home builder Veev reportedly shutting down after reaching
unicorn status last year"); The Information (briefing, "Khosla-Backed Construction Tech Startup Veev
to Shut Down"); GM Today/Business (citing AP/wire coverage); Builder Online ("Explainer: Why Did Veev
Fail, and What Happens Next?").

COMPANY 3: PLASTIQ — a failed SPAC deal as the "big last raise," compounded by SVB's collapse
- Founded 2012 by Eliot Buchanan and Dan Choi. Business: a B2B bill-payment platform letting
  businesses pay vendors, rent, taxes, and other bills by credit card for a flat fee, even when the
  recipient didn't accept cards directly.
- Total funding raised: approximately $141.8 million per Crunchbase (other figures cited around
  $150M) — investors included Khosla Ventures, Atlas Venture, and Flybridge Capital Partners.
- January 2022: a funding round valued Plastiq at $940 million.
- August 2022: Plastiq announced plans to go public via a SPAC merger with Colonnade Acquisition Corp
  II, at an implied $480 million valuation, expected to add more than $300 million to Plastiq's
  balance sheet — this de-SPAC was effectively Plastiq's "big last raise."
- September 2022: in the middle of pursuing the SPAC deal, Plastiq acquired Nearside, a digital
  banking product for small businesses, for a reported $130 million.
- Late 2022: Plastiq determined the de-SPAC transaction was no longer viable and moved to back out.
- February 2023: Colonnade sent Plastiq a letter alleging breach of the merger agreement and
  threatening litigation.
- March 2023: Silicon Valley Bank — Plastiq's primary bank — collapsed, compounding Plastiq's cash
  crisis at the worst possible moment.
- May 24, 2023: Plastiq filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy. Court filings showed the company had missed
  $2 million in interest payments on a $43 million loan, despite receiving multiple due-date
  extensions from its lender.
- Plastiq secured $7.1 million in debtor-in-possession (DIP) financing to fund itself through the
  bankruptcy process — its actual final "bridge."
- A Delaware bankruptcy judge confirmed a plan to sell Plastiq's assets to stalking-horse bidder
  Priority Technology Holdings Inc. for $27.5 million; the sale completed August 1, 2023.
Source: Bloomberg Law (2023, "Payment Company Plastiq Goes Bankrupt in Wake of SVB's Collapse");
Forbes (May 25, 2023, "Plastiq Files For Bankruptcy As Fintech Troubles Mount"); ION Analytics/Debtwire
("Case Profile: Business payment platform Plastiq files for Chapter 11 following failed de-SPAC
transaction"); Law360 (2023).

COMPANY 4: ENJOY TECHNOLOGY — a bankruptcy-stage bridge loan from the company that ultimately bought
it
- Founded 2014 by Ron Johnson, formerly Apple's SVP of Retail and later CEO of JCPenney. Business:
  "retail-as-a-service" — mobile stores (vans/staff) bringing a retail-store experience to customers
  at their homes or offices, with AT&T as a major retail partner selling phones this way.
- October 15, 2021: Enjoy completed a SPAC merger with Marquee Raine Acquisition Corp., at an implied
  pro forma enterprise value of approximately $1.06-1.2 billion, delivering up to $454 million in
  growth capital (approximately $374 million from the SPAC trust plus an $80 million PIPE). Began
  trading on Nasdaq under ticker ENJY.
- Early 2022: Enjoy experienced a rapid cash-flow crisis, driven substantially by a shortage of
  Apple's latest iPhone products (Enjoy relied heavily on iPhone sales through its AT&T retail
  partnership) alongside recent staffing and warehouse expansion undertaken in anticipation of
  entering roughly 100 new markets.
- June 30, 2022: Enjoy filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy — just over eight months after going public —
  with only $523,000 in cash on hand, not enough to make its next weekly payroll.
- To fund itself through the bankruptcy process, Enjoy received a $2.5 million bridge loan and a $55
  million financing commitment from Asurion, a technology-insurance/protection company, which became
  the stalking-horse bidder for Enjoy's assets.
- Enjoy's mobile retail business was ultimately sold to Asurion for $110 million, court-approved later
  in 2022.
Source: CNBC (Jun 30, 2022, "Former Apple, JC Penney exec Ron Johnson's Enjoy Technology files for
bankruptcy months after it went public"); Bloomberg Law (2022, "Enjoy Technology $110 Million
Bankruptcy Sale to Asurion Approved"); Retail Dive (2022, multiple articles); PR Newswire (2021 SPAC
completion announcement); DealFlow's SPAC News (2021).
