# Thumbnail Strategy

One dominant idea per thumbnail — per this channel's standing rule. This package covers four
companies, but each thumbnail still leads with ONE dominant idea (the shared pattern, or one company's
single most dramatic fact) — never all four company names/logos crammed into one image.

## Thumbnail Intelligence — Winning Concept
- **Core visual:** a cracked/collapsing bridge icon, four small generic building/company icons falling
  from it into darkness below
- **Main subject:** the shared "bridge that doesn't hold" pattern across four companies
- **Emotion:** dread/inevitability
- **Background:** dark, minimal, faint chasm/gap texture at low opacity
- **Lighting:** single spotlight on the cracked bridge
- **Camera angle:** N/A (graphic-led)
- **Composition:** cracked bridge dominant and centered, four small falling icons beneath it,
  bottom-third title-safe space reserved
- **Props:** generic bridge icon, four generic building/company silhouette icons
- **Thumbnail text:** `4 COMPANIES TRIED THE SAME "LAST RAISE." NONE MADE IT.`
- **Text placement:** bottom third, bold sans-serif, high contrast
- **Color strategy:** red crack lines on the bridge, white/gray falling icons, near-black background
- **Contrast strategy:** the visual of a bridge literally breaking under four falling shapes
  communicates the entire thesis before any text is read

## Notes on What NOT to Do
- Do not render a real, identifiable photorealistic likeness of Michael Marks, Amit Haller, Eliot
  Buchanan, Dan Choi, or Ron Johnson in any thumbnail — use generic avatar/host imagery or graphic-led
  concepts only; attribute real names verbally/on-screen in the video itself
- Do not put real company logos/wordmarks (Katerra, Veev, Plastiq, Enjoy Technology, SoftBank,
  Asurion) in generated thumbnail art — build the visual concept generically and add any verified real
  branding in the editor with proper sourcing
- Do not cram all four company names or logos into a single thumbnail — the comparator format lives
  inside the video, not the thumbnail
