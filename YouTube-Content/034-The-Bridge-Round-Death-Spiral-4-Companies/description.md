## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week is
different — instead of one company, it's four, chosen to prove one shared pattern is real across
completely unrelated industries: the bridge round that doesn't actually bridge anything.

**Katerra** (construction tech, founded 2015 by Michael Marks) raised roughly $2 billion from
SoftBank, including an $865 million round that valued it above $3 billion. As distress set in,
SoftBank provided TWO separate $200 million emergency infusions — May 2020 and December 2020, the
second reported as SoftBank's final cash infusion before what came next. When SoftBank-backed lender
Greensill Capital collapsed in early 2021, Katerra lost its working-capital lifeline. It filed Chapter
11 on June 7, 2021 — and received a third, bankruptcy-only $35 million loan from SoftBank just to fund
an asset sale.

**Veev** (prefab home construction, founded 2008, pivoted 2018 under Amit Haller) raised roughly $600-
650 million and hit unicorn status with a $400 million round in March 2022. By late 2023, after a new
funding round failed to materialize, Veev specifically tried to secure a bridge loan as a fallback.
That failed too. On November 27, 2023, Veev shut down, laying off about 250 employees.

**Plastiq** (B2B bill payments, founded 2012 by Eliot Buchanan and Dan Choi) hit a $940 million
valuation in January 2022. Its "big last raise" was a SPAC merger announced in August 2022 — which
Plastiq backed out of, triggering a breach-of-agreement threat from its SPAC partner. Silicon Valley
Bank's March 2023 collapse — Plastiq's own bank — compounded the crisis. Plastiq filed Chapter 11 on
May 24, 2023, survived on $7.1 million in bankruptcy financing, and sold its assets for $27.5 million.

**Enjoy Technology** (mobile retail, founded 2014 by former Apple retail chief and JCPenney CEO Ron
Johnson) went public via SPAC in October 2021 at a roughly $1.2 billion valuation. An iPhone supply
shortage gutted its AT&T-driven sales just as the company expanded into new markets. On June 30, 2022
— eight months after going public — Enjoy filed Chapter 11 with just $523,000 in the bank. It survived
on a bridge loan from Asurion, which then bought Enjoy's mobile-retail business for $110 million.

Four companies. Four differently-structured bridges — emergency equity, a bridge loan, a SPAC merger,
bankruptcy financing. All four failed to save the company as an independent business.
