# 034 — The Bridge Round Death Spiral: 4 Companies, 4 "One Last Raise" Stories

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around four independently-verified companies
(Bloomberg, CNBC, TechCrunch, Construction Dive, Forbes, Bloomberg Law, The Information, Retail Dive —
full citations in `sources-log.txt`). Fourth title on the channel sourced from the "dead-saas-30-
Content Titles new-video-ideas.md" batch (Cluster 1, idea #2: "The bridge round death spiral"). Same
multi-company comparator format as packages 007, 015, and 025 on this channel — none of the four
companies here (Katerra, Veev, Plastiq, Enjoy Technology) has been used as a full anchor elsewhere.

**The four real cases:**
1. **Katerra** — construction tech, founded 2015 by Michael Marks. Raised ~$2B from SoftBank,
   including an $865M round valuing it at $3B+. Got TWO separate $200M emergency infusions from
   SoftBank (May 2020, Dec 2020 — the second explicitly called SoftBank's "final cash infusion" by
   Bloomberg) before Greensill Capital's own unrelated collapse cut off its financing lifeline. Filed
   Chapter 11 June 7, 2021 — then got a third, bankruptcy-only $35M DIP loan from SoftBank just to
   fund the asset sale.
2. **Veev** — prefab home construction, founded 2008, pivoted 2018. Raised ~$600-650M, hit unicorn
   status (~$1B+) in a $400M March 2022 round. Tried to raise a new round in late 2023 — failed. Tried
   a named, explicit "bridge loan" as a fallback — also failed. Shut down Nov 27, 2023, ~250 laid off.
3. **Plastiq** — B2B bill payment, founded 2012. Raised ~$142M, hit a $940M valuation in Jan 2022.
   Its "big last raise" was a SPAC merger announced Aug 2022 — which it backed out of, triggering a
   breach threat from the SPAC. SVB's March 2023 collapse (its own bank) compounded the crisis. Filed
   Chapter 11 May 24, 2023, survived on $7.1M DIP financing, sold for $27.5M.
4. **Enjoy Technology** — mobile retail, founded 2014 by Ron Johnson (ex-Apple, ex-JCPenney). Went
   public via SPAC Oct 2021 at a ~$1.2B valuation. An iPhone supply shortage triggered a cash crisis;
   filed Chapter 11 June 30, 2022 with just $523,000 in the bank. Survived on a bridge loan from
   Asurion — which then bought the company for $110M.

**Framing requirement:** Each company's "bridge" mechanism is distinct — state this precisely rather
than treating all four as the same story. Katerra: repeated emergency equity infusions from one
dominant investor. Veev: a specifically named, failed bridge loan. Plastiq: a failed SPAC merger
functioning as a public-market "bridge," followed by real bankruptcy DIP financing. Enjoy: DIP
financing supplied directly by the company's eventual buyer. No individual across all four companies
was charged with fraud or any crime — keep the tone analytical, not accusatory.
