# 026 — WeWork Hit a $47 Billion Valuation. Its Founder Got a $1.7 Billion Exit Package. The Company Still Filed for Bankruptcy.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around WeWork's full public history (Britannica Money, SEC filings, TechCrunch, Forbes, CNBC, Orrick, Davis Polk — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This is the most famous overhype/governance-collapse story in the genre, and was already name-checked as a direct comparison in this channel's own package 025 research (an analyst called Sonder "the WeWork of apartments"). This is that story told in full for the first time on this channel.

**The real case:** WeWork — founded 2010 in NYC by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey, leasing office space long-term and re-renting it flexibly and short-term — raised roughly $13 billion and hit a $47 billion valuation in 2019, the single highest valuation of any company on this channel. Its August 2019 IPO prospectus collapsed within six weeks under scrutiny of Neumann's control structure, a self-invented "community-adjusted EBITDA" metric, and related-party dealings; Neumann resigned September 24, 2019 with an exit package reported at up to $1.7 billion, and SoftBank stepped in with a roughly $9.5 billion rescue. WeWork tried again — a SPAC merger in October 2021 valued it at $9 billion, an 80% discount from its private peak. It still filed Chapter 11 on November 6, 2023, with $18.6B in liabilities against $15B in assets. Unlike most of this channel's bankruptcy stories, this was a reorganization, not a liquidation: WeWork emerged June 11, 2024, roughly $4 billion lighter on debt, and continues operating today — now worth about $750 million, a fraction of any of its previous valuations. Neumann himself, meanwhile, launched a new company (Flow) backed by Andreessen Horowitz, reportedly worth $2.5 billion by 2025.

**Framing requirement:** The spine is the valuation arc — $47B private peak → $9B SPAC → ~$750M post-bankruptcy — paired with the fact that the company's founder personally walked away with up to $1.7 billion *before* any of the worst outcomes hit employees and later shareholders, and is now running a new, well-funded company again. No crime, no conviction — this is a governance and incentive-structure story, distinct in kind from 005 (HeadSpin) or 029 (Ozy Media), which involve real fraud convictions.

- No "fraud/scam/lied" language — Neumann has not been charged with any crime; this is a governance story
