## LinkedIn
"A real, underused case study in the gap between valuation and business health:

WeWork reached $47 billion in 2019 — the highest valuation of any company I've covered on this
channel. Its August 2019 IPO filing collapsed within weeks under scrutiny of founder control
(super-voting shares), related-party transactions, and a self-invented metric called
'community-adjusted EBITDA.' Adam Neumann resigned that September with an exit package reported at
up to $1.7 billion. SoftBank rescued the company for roughly $9.5 billion.

WeWork went public a second time in October 2021, via SPAC, at a $9 billion enterprise value —
already an 80% discount from its private peak. It still filed for Chapter 11 in November 2023, with
$18.6 billion in liabilities against $15 billion in assets.

Here's the part most coverage skips: this was a reorganization, not a liquidation. WeWork emerged in
June 2024, eliminated roughly $4 billion in debt, and continues operating today under new ownership
— now valued at approximately $750 million.

Neumann, meanwhile, founded Flow, backed by Andreessen Horowitz at launch in 2022, reportedly valued
at $2.5 billion by 2025. No fraud, no conviction — I present that fact plainly, as what happened
next, not as vindication or condemnation.
