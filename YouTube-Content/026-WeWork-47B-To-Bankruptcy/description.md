## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week:
WeWork — founded in 2010 in New York City by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey, opening its first
location on Grand Street in SoHo in April 2011. WeWork's model: lease large blocks of commercial
office space on long-term leases, subdivide and redesign it, then re-rent it to individuals and
companies on flexible, short-term membership terms — the same fixed-obligation-versus-flexible-
revenue mismatch this channel has already covered in its Stay Alfred (package 021) and Sonder/Zeus
Living (package 025) videos, just applied to commercial real estate instead of residential
short-term rental.

WeWork raised roughly $13 billion across its funding history. In 2019, SoftBank led a Series H round
investing $1 billion, part of a financing history that valued WeWork at $47 billion at its peak — the
highest valuation of any company this channel has covered. SoftBank's cumulative debt-and-equity
exposure to WeWork eventually reached approximately $18.5 billion; its reported cumulative loss by
2023 was approximately $16 billion — two different, both-real numbers that describe two different
things, never conflated in this video.

In August 2019, WeWork filed its S-1 IPO prospectus and met immediate, intense backlash: scrutiny
fell on Neumann's outsized control through super-voting shares, numerous related-party transactions
(including WeWork paying Neumann personally for the trademark rights to the word "We"), and a
self-invented non-GAAP metric — "community-adjusted EBITDA" — that excluded most of the company's
actual operating costs. The IPO was shelved by mid-October 2019 and formally withdrawn shortly after.
On September 24, 2019, Neumann resigned as CEO under board pressure. His exit package was reported at
up to $1.7 billion total: up to $970 million for his shares, a $185 million "consulting fee," and
$500 million in credit facilities to help him repay personal loans he'd taken out partly against
WeWork stock. That October, SoftBank stepped in with a rescue package reported at roughly $9.5
billion — a mix of new equity, debt financing, and an accelerated warrant/tender offer — taking
effective control of the company.

On October 20, 2021, WeWork completed a SPAC merger with BowX Acquisition Corp., beginning to trade
on the NYSE under ticker "WE" at an enterprise value of approximately $9 billion — already roughly an
80% discount from the 2019 peak. WeWork's Class A stock declined more than 98% over 2023 alone. On
November 6, 2023, WeWork Inc. and 517 affiliated entities filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, entering a
restructuring support agreement with SoftBank and other major debt holders. At filing: approximately
$15 billion in assets against approximately $18.6 billion in liabilities. Adam Neumann's public
statement on the filing was notably muted — he called it "disappointing."

Unlike most bankruptcy stories on this channel, WeWork's Chapter 11 was a reorganization, not a
liquidation. WeWork emerged on June 11, 2024, with ownership restructured to Yardi Systems' affiliate
Cupar Grimmond at approximately 60%, SoftBank at approximately 20%, and other lenders holding the
remaining 20%. Roughly $4 billion in pre-petition debt was eliminated. WeWork continues operating
today, now valued at roughly $750 million — the third and final point in a valuation arc that ran
from $47 billion (2019 private peak) to $9 billion (2021 SPAC) to approximately $750 million
(post-2024 emergence).

Adam Neumann, meanwhile, founded Flow — a residential real-estate company built around shared-
amenity rental housing — after his 2019 exit. Andreessen Horowitz invested in Flow at its 2022
launch. As of 2025, Flow was reportedly valued at approximately $2.5 billion. This video presents
that fact plainly, as "what happened next" — not as vindication, not as condemnation.

No one named in this video has been charged with or convicted of any crime. This is a governance
and incentive-structure story, distinct in kind from this channel's fraud-conviction stories
(005/HeadSpin, 029/Ozy Media).
