# Title Options — Ranked

Anchored on WeWork: founded 2010 in NYC by Adam Neumann and Miguel McKelvey, raised roughly $13
billion lifetime, hit a $47 billion valuation in 2019 (the highest of any company on this channel),
its August 2019 S-1 collapsed within weeks over Neumann's control structure and "community-adjusted
EBITDA," Neumann resigned September 24, 2019 with an exit package reported at up to $1.7 billion,
SoftBank rescued the company for roughly $9.5 billion, WeWork went public a second time via SPAC in
October 2021 at a $9 billion enterprise value, filed Chapter 11 on November 6, 2023 ($18.6B
liabilities vs. $15B assets), emerged as a reorganization (not a liquidation) on June 11, 2024 at
roughly $750 million, and continues operating today under new ownership (Cupar Grimmond/Yardi 60%,
SoftBank 20%, other lenders 20%). Neumann founded Flow post-exit, backed by Andreessen Horowitz,
reportedly valued at $2.5 billion by 2025. No fraud/scam language — Neumann has not been charged
with any crime; this is a governance and incentive-structure story.

## Recommended Title
**"WeWork Hit a $47 Billion Valuation. Its Founder Got a $1.7 Billion Exit Package. The Company Still Filed for Bankruptcy."**

Reasoning: leads with the two best-corroborated, most citable figures in the sources-log ($47
billion peak valuation, up to $1.7 billion exit package), states the video's real irony without
inventing or exaggerating anything, and works fully truncated on mobile. Names the company directly
— WeWork carries the highest brand recognition of any company on this channel — and sets up both
the specific WeWork story and its comparison points to this channel's other funded-startup failures
(025/Sonder, 021/Stay Alfred) without overpromising. Avoids stating any single "the" WeWork valuation
by anchoring the title on the peak figure only, with the full three-point arc ($47B → $9B → ~$750M)
reserved for the video itself and its own titles/hooks.
