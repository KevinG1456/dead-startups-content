## LinkedIn
"A real case study in feature-dependency risk — a company forced to remove the exact thing that made
it popular, and dying anyway:

Yik Yak, founded by Furman University students Tyler Droll and Brooks Buffington, launched November
2013 as an anonymous, hyperlocal social feed. A November 2014 funding round valued it at $400.5
million on roughly $73.5 million raised.

The same anonymity that made the app popular also enabled real threats and harassment, leading to
arrests at multiple campuses. In August 2016, the company required handles and photos to curb abuse —
removing the anonymity that was the entire premise. Users left immediately. The founders publicly
apologized in November 2016 and reversed the change, but downloads were already down 76% year over
year.

In April 2017, Square paid roughly $1 million for a handful of engineers and an IP license. The app
shut down days later — a fall from $400.5 million to roughly $1 million in under two and a half
years.
