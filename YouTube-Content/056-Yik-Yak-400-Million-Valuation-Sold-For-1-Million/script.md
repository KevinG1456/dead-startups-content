# Script Package

Anchored on Yik Yak — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. Framing requirement: no fraud/misconduct
framing toward Tyler Droll or Brooks Buffington — this is a content-moderation and product-pivot
failure story. Keep the campus-threat material factual and brief, naming only the three
independently-verified individuals from `sources-log.txt`. Keep the original 2013-2017 company
explicitly separate from the unrelated 2021 revival team and the 2023 Sidechat acquisition.

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 10–13 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "This app was worth $400.5 million. Twenty-nine months later, it sold for about $1 million."
- Visual: two title cards, "$400.5M — NOV 2014" then hard cut to "~$1M — APR 2017"
- Pattern Interrupt: white text on black: "The thing that made it a phenomenon is the exact thing
  that killed it."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "This is Yik Yak — built by two Furman University students, Tyler Droll and Brooks Buffington,
  launched in November 2013. The idea: post anything, see only what's posted nearby, no name
  attached. No profile. No followers. Just your campus, anonymously."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video you'll know exactly how a $400 million anonymous app collapsed
  — and why the fix for its biggest problem is the thing that actually ended it."

### Introduction (1:00–1:40)
- Method statement: reconstructed from TechCrunch, Forbes, The Washington Post, CBS News, and
  Fortune's contemporaneous reporting — sourced on screen throughout.
- Explicit framing, stated early: "This isn't a story about fraud — no one here was charged with
  anything. This is a story about what happens when a product's core feature becomes its biggest
  liability."

### Main Content — The Rise (1:40–4:00)
- **Segment 1 — The Idea (1:40–2:20):** Nov 2013 launch, hyperlocal anonymous feed, rapid campus
  adoption. Visual: "LAUNCHED NOVEMBER 2013" title card.
- **Segment 2 — The Money (2:20–3:15):** $73.4-73.5M raised from Sequoia Capital, DCM Ventures, Azure
  Capital Partners; Nov 2014 round values the company at $400.5 million. Visual: "$400.5 MILLION —
  NOVEMBER 2014" title card.
- **Segment 3 — A Real Fracture Underneath (3:15–4:00):** brief mention of the Douglas Warstler
  co-founder lawsuit (Nov 2014, settled Apr 2016, undisclosed terms) — a business dispute, not fraud,
  framed as an early sign of strain. Visual: generic "founding dispute" title card, no dramatization.

### Main Content — The Problem (4:00–6:30)
- **Segment 4 — What Anonymity Enabled (4:00–5:30):** the same feature that made the app popular
  also enabled sustained threats and harassment. Cite three named, sourced incidents only: Emily
  Sakamoto (Emory, Oct 2015), Hunter Park (Missouri S&T, Nov 2015, threats targeting Black students
  and faculty at Mizzou), Christian Pryor (Fresno State). Visual: neutral "REAL ARRESTS FOLLOWED"
  title card — no dramatized reenactment.
- **Segment 5 — The Fix (5:30–6:30):** August 2016, the company requires handles and photos to curb
  abuse — directly removing the anonymity that was the entire premise of the app. Visual: "REQUIRED:
  NAME + PHOTO — AUGUST 2016" title card.

### Main Content — The Collapse (6:30–9:00)
- **Segment 6 — The Backlash (6:30–7:15):** users revolt immediately; some local feeds drop to as few
  as 5 posts a day. Visual: "5 POSTS A DAY" stat card.
- **Segment 7 — The Apology (7:15–8:00):** Nov 2016, Droll and Buffington release a public video
  admitting "we messed this one up," reverse the change, make handles optional again. Visual:
  attributed quote card, held on screen.
- **Segment 8 — Too Late (8:00–8:45):** by the end of 2016, downloads are down 76% year over year;
  most staff laid off. Visual: "-76% DOWNLOADS" title card.
- Mid-video CTA (~8:45): "This channel has covered platform-dependency deaths before — this one is
  different: the company killed itself trying to save itself. Subscribe for a new autopsy every
  week."

### The End, and the Twist (9:00–10:15)
- **Segment 9 — The Sale (9:00–9:45):** April 2017, Square pays roughly $1 million for a handful of
  engineers and an IP license; the app shuts down days later. Visual: "$400.5M → ~$1M" split-screen
  card.
- **Segment 10 — Not the Same Company (9:45–10:15):** a completely unrelated team bought the Yik Yak
  brand in Feb 2021 and relaunched it in Aug 2021; that revived app was itself acquired by a rival,
  Sidechat, in March 2023, and still runs today. Explicitly state: different founders, different
  company, no continuity with Droll and Buffington's original decisions. Visual: "A DIFFERENT COMPANY
  — 2021" title card.

### The Actual Lesson (10:15–11:00)
- Plain-language lesson: when your entire product is built on one feature, you can't safely remove
  that feature to fix its downside — you either solve the downside within the feature, or the product
  stops being the product. Yik Yak tried to keep the app while removing what made it the app.
- Visual: full-arc timeline graphic — launch (Nov 2013) → $400.5M valuation (Nov 2014) → threats/
  arrests (2015) → handles required (Aug 2016) → apology, reversal (Nov 2016) → -76% downloads (end
  2016) → sold for ~$1M (Apr 2017) → unrelated 2021 revival → 2023 Sidechat acquisition.

### Final CTA + Outro (11:00–11:45)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — the feature that makes you matters most protects your company
  right up until it doesn't, and there's often no safe way to remove it later
- "Subscribe — I'm building a full library of these autopsies"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you use Yik Yak in college? Do you remember the handles change? Tell me in
  the comments."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the $400.5M-to-$1M numbers and the -76% download stat, then works backward through
the anonymity mechanism and the 2016 pivot as supporting detail. Measured, graphics-forward.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on two college students launching an app from a dorm in Nov 2013, builds forward chronologically
through the rise, the threats, the pivot, and the apology video before landing on the sale price as
the closing gut-punch number. Slower pacing, holds the $1 million figure as a late reveal.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as rapid-fire beats: launch, funding, valuation, threats, handles pivot, backlash, apology,
downloads collapse, sale, 2021 revival, 2023 acquisition — landing on the "removed feature killed the
company" lesson as the final clarifying beat. Strong Shorts-extraction potential, especially the
apology-video clip description and the $400.5M-to-$1M number.

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the threats/harassment segment (4:00-5:30) if it feels heavy or dwelt-on
- **Fix:** keep it factual, brief, and clearly sourced — state the pattern and move to the pivot
  quickly rather than lingering
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim general campus-culture context if audience nostalgia is already
  high
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease the $1 million sale figure even earlier if retention data
  shows drop-off before Segment 9
- **Recommended visual change:** keep a small "valuation counter" graphic ticking down in a corner
  from $400.5M toward $1M across the whole video, landing exactly at the sale reveal
