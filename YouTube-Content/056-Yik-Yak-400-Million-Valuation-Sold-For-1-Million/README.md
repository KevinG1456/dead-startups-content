# 056 — Yik Yak Hit a $400 Million Valuation. The Feature That Made It Popular Also Killed It.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built from independent WebSearch verification against
TechCrunch, Forbes, The Washington Post, CBS News, Fortune, and EdSurge; full citations in
`sources-log.txt`. Fresh, cold-researched title (not from the dead-saas-30 batch) — picked as a
famous, well-documented case with a failure mechanism distinct from everything else on this channel.

**The real case:** Yik Yak, founded by Furman University students Tyler Droll and Brooks Buffington,
launched in November 2013 as an anonymous, hyperlocal social feed — post anything, see only what's
nearby, no name attached. It became a genuine campus phenomenon: a November 2014 funding round valued
it at $400.5 million, on $73.4–73.5M raised from investors including Sequoia Capital, DCM Ventures,
and Azure Capital Partners. But the anonymity that made it popular also made it a magnet for threats
and harassment — real arrests followed at Emory, Missouri S&T, and Fresno State, among others. In
August 2016, the company tried to fix this by requiring handles and photos, killing the anonymity
that was the entire point. Users revolted; usage collapsed so hard that some local feeds saw as few
as 5 posts a day. The founders publicly apologized and reversed the change in November 2016, but it
was too late — downloads were down 76% year over year by the end of 2016. In April 2017, Square
(later renamed Block) paid roughly $1 million for a handful of engineers and a license to some IP.
The app shut down days later. Two and a half years earlier, it had been worth $400.5 million.

**Twist not to skip:** a completely different, unrelated team bought the Yik Yak brand in Feb 2021
and relaunched the app in Aug 2021 — this is NOT the same company or the same founders. That revived
app was itself acquired by a rival app, Sidechat, in March 2023, and continues operating today. Never
blur the original 2013–2017 company with this unrelated later chapter.

**Framing requirement:** No fraud/misconduct framing toward Droll or Buffington — this is a
content-moderation and product-pivot failure story. Handle the campus-threat material factually,
citing only the three named, independently-verified individuals in `sources-log.txt`; do not
sensationalize or name anyone beyond what mainstream reporting already made public.
