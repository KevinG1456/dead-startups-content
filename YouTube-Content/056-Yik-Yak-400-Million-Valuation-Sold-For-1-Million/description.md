## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Yik
Yak, one of the defining campus apps of the mid-2010s, and a company that had to remove its own core
feature to survive — and died anyway.

Founded by Furman University students Tyler Droll and Brooks Buffington, Yik Yak launched in November
2013: post anything, see only what's nearby, no name attached. It grew fast. A November 2014 funding
round valued the company at $400.5 million, on $73.4-73.5 million raised from Sequoia Capital, DCM
Ventures, and Azure Capital Partners.

But the anonymity that made Yik Yak popular also made it a magnet for threats and harassment. Real
arrests followed at real campuses — including Emory University, Missouri University of Science and
Technology, and Fresno State. In August 2016, the company tried to fix this by requiring users to add
a handle and photo — directly removing the anonymity that was the entire point of the app. Users
revolted immediately; some local feeds saw as few as 5 posts a day. In November 2016, Droll and
Buffington released a public video admitting "we messed this one up" and reversed the change. It was
too late — downloads were down 76% year over year by the end of 2016.

In April 2017, payments company Square paid roughly $1 million for a handful of Yik Yak's engineers
and a license to some of its intellectual property. The app shut down days later — a fall from a
$400.5 million peak valuation to a roughly $1 million asset sale in under two and a half years.

One important twist: a completely different, unrelated team bought the Yik Yak brand in February 2021
and relaunched the app that August. That revived app was itself acquired by a rival, Sidechat, in
March 2023, and continues operating today. This video is about the original 2013-2017 company only —
never to be confused with what runs under the Yik Yak name now.
