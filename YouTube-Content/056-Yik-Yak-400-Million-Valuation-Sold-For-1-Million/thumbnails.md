# Thumbnail Strategy

One dominant idea per thumbnail — per this channel's standing rule.

## Thumbnail Intelligence — Winning Concept
- **Core visual:** a large numeral "$400M" with a hard visual strike-through, a small "$1M" beneath
  it in a contrasting color
- **Main subject:** the valuation collapse, expressed as a single number-to-number transformation
- **Emotion:** shock/disbelief
- **Background:** dark, minimal, faint grid texture at low opacity
- **Lighting:** single spotlight on the numerals
- **Camera angle:** N/A (graphic-led)
- **Composition:** "$400M" dominant and centered, "$1M" smaller beneath, bottom-third title-safe space
- **Props:** none beyond the two numerals and a strike-through mark
- **Thumbnail text:** `$400M → $1M`
- **Text placement:** centered, bold sans-serif, high contrast
- **Color strategy:** white/blue large numeral, red strike-through and smaller numeral, near-black
  background
- **Contrast strategy:** the sheer size difference between the two numerals communicates the entire
  thesis before any other text is read

## Thumbnail Version A — Shock (Recommended primary)
- **Image concept:** the $400M-to-$1M numeral graphic from Winning Concept above
- **Subject:** the valuation collapse, graphic-led
- **Expression:** N/A (no face in this version)
- **Objects:** large numeral "$400M" with strike-through, smaller numeral "$1M"
- **Background:** dark, minimal, faint grid texture
- **Colors:** white/blue large numeral, red strike-through and small numeral, near-black background
- **Lighting:** single spotlight on the numerals
- **Angle:** N/A (graphic-led)
- **Composition:** numerals centered and dominant, bottom-third text
- **Thumbnail text:** `$400M → $1M`
- **Why it works:** the exact, verified valuation collapse is the single most quotable, most
  shareable fact in the sources-log
- **CTR prediction:** highest of the three — number-collapse thumbnails consistently outperform on
  this channel
- **Best audience:** cold/browse traffic, general nostalgia audience

## Thumbnail Version B — Curiosity (The anonymity-removed angle)
- **Image concept:** a simple silhouette/mask icon with a visible crack, one half fading into a plain
  face outline
- **Subject:** the anonymity-to-identity pivot, pure iconographic thumbnail
- **Expression:** N/A (no real face)
- **Objects:** a generic mask/silhouette icon splitting into a plain outline
- **Background:** dark, minimal, subtle grain
- **Colors:** blue/white icon, red crack accent, near-black base
- **Lighting:** single spotlight on the split
- **Angle:** N/A (graphic-led)
- **Composition:** icon centered and dominant, bottom-third text space
- **Thumbnail text:** `THEY REMOVED THE ANONYMITY.`
- **Why it works:** the least-obvious, most surprising causal detail in the story — most viewers
  assume the app just "got shut down for bullying" without knowing the actual proximate cause
- **CTR prediction:** high — surprising-causation framing performs strongly
- **Best audience:** search + suggested traffic, product/startup-curious viewers

## Thumbnail Version C — Authority (Nostalgia/host-led angle)
- **Image concept:** face-led (avatar/host), nostalgic/knowing expression, a small "REMEMBER THIS
  APP?" tag in the corner
- **Subject:** host/avatar, three-quarter crop
- **Expression:** knowing half-smile, direct eye contact
- **Objects:** small tag graphic in the corner
- **Background:** dark, minimal
- **Colors:** desaturated, single warm key light on the face, blue accent only on the corner tag
- **Lighting:** dramatic single top-down light
- **Angle:** slight low angle, three-quarter crop
- **Composition:** face fills most of frame, small tag in the corner, bottom-third text space
- **Thumbnail text:** `REMEMBER THIS APP?`
- **Why it works:** face-led performs best with subscribed audience; the nostalgia hook draws in the
  large cohort who used the app in college without needing to explain the ending upfront
- **CTR prediction:** medium-high with returning viewers
- **Best audience:** subscribers, suggested placement

## Notes on What NOT to Do
- Do not render a real, identifiable photorealistic likeness of Tyler Droll, Brooks Buffington,
  Douglas Warstler, or any of the three named threat-incident individuals in any thumbnail — use
  generic avatar/host imagery or graphic-led concepts only
- Do not use the real Yik Yak app icon/logo or any real screenshot of the app's UI in generated
  thumbnail art — build generic iconography instead and add any verified real branding in the editor
  with proper sourcing
- Do not depict or reference any real threat/violence imagery, weapons, or campus-crisis visuals in
  connection with the abuse-incident material — keep that segment's visual treatment entirely
  text-card-based, never illustrated
- Do not imply in any thumbnail that this video is about the current, Sidechat-owned Yik Yak app —
  keep all thumbnail concepts scoped to the original 2013-2017 company's story
