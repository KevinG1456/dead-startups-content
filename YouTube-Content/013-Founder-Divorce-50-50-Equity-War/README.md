# 013 — Founder Divorce: Reconstructing a 50/50 Equity War From the Court Docket

**Package status:** Production-ready with two strong, extensively documented real candidates. Both involve actual litigation with public court records, making them safe to reconstruct factually (state claims as "alleged in the complaint" or "per the settlement" where appropriate, and clearly distinguish allegations from proven facts).

**Strongest candidates:**
1. **Snapchat — Reggie Brown vs. Evan Spiegel and Bobby Murphy.** Brown, a third co-founder, sued in 2013 alleging he was cut out of equity and credit for the app's original concept after a falling-out. The case settled in 2014 for a reported sum widely cited around $157–158 million `[VERIFY exact figure and terms — settlement amounts are frequently reported inconsistently across outlets]`. Extensively covered by Business Insider, Vanity Fair, and court filings.
2. **Facebook — the Winklevoss twins (ConnectU) vs. Mark Zuckerberg.** The Winklevosses alleged Zuckerberg stole their idea for a social network while they had hired him to build ConnectU. The case settled in 2008 for a reported ~$65 million in cash and stock `[VERIFY exact figure and terms`]. One of the most publicly documented founder disputes in tech history, including significant court record availability and the basis for the film "The Social Network" (note: the film is a dramatization, not a primary source — do not cite it as fact).

**Recommendation:** either case works well; Snapchat is more recent and may resonate more with a younger core audience, while Facebook/Winklevoss is more universally recognizable. Consider covering one as the main case study with the other referenced as a comparison point, or building a two-part structure.

**Legal-sensitivity note:** since this involves real, named individuals and litigation, distinguish clearly on screen between allegations made in a complaint and facts established by settlement or judgment. Avoid stating disputed claims as settled fact, and avoid speculation about individuals' motives beyond what's directly documented.

- **Viewer objections:** "this is old news / already covered in a movie" — counter by promising primary-document specifics beyond the popular narrative
- **Motivation to watch:** practical equity-structuring lessons for co-founders; general interest in a well-documented tech-industry legal drama
- **Expected comments:** strong opinions on who was "right," comparisons to other founder disputes, requests to cover additional cases (Tinder, WeWork, others)

- **Similar creators:** these stories have been covered extensively in text journalism and some documentary/film treatment; dedicated document-driven video breakdowns specifically for a startup-education audience are less common
