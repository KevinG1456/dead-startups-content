## LinkedIn
"A real case study in timing risk — proven, on both ends, by two real companies:

Webvan, founded 1996, raised $396M+, and went public in November 1999 at a $4.8 billion valuation —
despite roughly $395,000 in cumulative revenue. It built its own $1 billion warehouse network via a
Bechtel construction deal rather than partnering with existing grocery stores. In 2000, it acquired
competitor HomeGrocer for $1.2 billion in stock while reporting $178.5 million in sales against $525.4
million in expenses. On July 9, 2001, it filed Chapter 11, having lost over $800 million.

In 2012, Apoorva Mehta founded Instacart — solving the exact same customer problem with an
asset-light model: existing grocery stores' inventory, paired with gig-economy shoppers, made possible
by smartphone GPS and payments infrastructure that didn't exist in Webvan's era. In September 2023,
Instacart filed a real S-1 and completed its IPO at a $9.9 billion valuation. Business press covering
it explicitly called Instacart a company that had "transformed from a Webvan clone" into the category
leader.

The idea wasn't wrong. It was eleven years too early.

Full breakdown: [video link]
