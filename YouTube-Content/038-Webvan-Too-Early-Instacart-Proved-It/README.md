# 038 — Webvan Burned $800 Million Proving Online Grocery Delivery Would Work. It Just Worked for Someone Else, 11 Years Later.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Webvan and its real successor, Instacart
(Wikipedia, CNN Money, Seattle Times, Forbes, Bloomberg Law — full citations in `sources-log.txt`).
Eighth title on the channel sourced from the "dead-saas-30-Content Titles new-video-ideas.md" batch
(Cluster 2, idea #11: "Too early twice: the company that built the right product 8 years before the
market"). Note: the real, sourced gap between Webvan's collapse and Instacart's founding is 11 years
(16 years from Webvan's founding) — presented accurately rather than forced to match the title's "8
years" framing.

**The real case:** Webvan, founded 1996 by Louis Borders (co-founder of the Borders bookstore chain),
raised $396M+ privately and IPO'd November 1999 at a $4.8 billion valuation — despite roughly $395,000
in cumulative revenue. It built its own $1 billion warehouse network (via a Bechtel construction deal)
rather than partnering with existing grocery stores, hired CEO George Shaheen away from Andersen
Consulting on a contract reportedly including $375,000/year for life, and acquired competitor
HomeGrocer for $1.2 billion in stock in 2000 — while spending $525.4 million against $178.5 million in
sales that same year. It filed Chapter 11 on July 9, 2001, having lost over $800 million and laying off
2,000 employees. In 2012, Apoorva Mehta founded Instacart, solving the same customer need with an
asset-light model — existing grocery stores, gig-economy shoppers, smartphone GPS and payments
infrastructure that didn't exist in Webvan's era. Instacart filed a real S-1 and went public in
September 2023 at a $9.9 billion valuation; business press explicitly described it as having
"transformed from a Webvan clone" into the category leader.

**Framing requirement:** Present the timing gap accurately (11-16 years, not literally 8). George
Shaheen's lifetime-compensation contract is a colorful, real, sourced business detail — present it
neutrally, not as misconduct. Attribute the "Webvan clone" framing to business press coverage of
Instacart's IPO, not as this channel's own characterization.
