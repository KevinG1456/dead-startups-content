Anchored on Pets.com: founded 1998 (Greg McLemore/Eva Woodsmall), CEO Julie Wainwright joined 1999;
~$95.5M raised pre-IPO (Amazon.com, Hummer Winblad, Bowman Capital); $11.8M 1999 ad spend incl. ~$1.2M
Super Bowl XXXIV ad + 1999 Macy's parade balloon; IPO Feb 2000 at $11/share, raised $82.5M; lost
$3-4/order shipping 50lb dog food free, sold ~27% below cost; board approved liquidation Nov 4 2000,
ceased ops Nov 9 2000 — commonly reported as 268 days from IPO to complete liquidation, ~$300M burned.
Sock puppet later sold for $125,000. Wainwright later founded The RealReal, IPO'd 2019.

## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **The Sock Puppet That Burned $300 Million in 268 Days** — instant recognition (the puppet) fused
   with a precise, shocking number and timeframe; needs zero setup to land
2. They Spent $2 to Make $1 — For Two Years Straight — the sharpest, most quotable summary of the
   actual root cause, distinct from the well-worn "puppet" angle
3. This Company Sued Saturday Night Live Over a Sock Puppet — And Lost — the freshest beat in the
   whole package; almost no existing coverage uses this angle
4. They Shipped Dog Food for Free. It Bankrupted Them. — concrete, specific, immediately
   understandable mechanism
5. IPO to Complete Liquidation in Under a Year — Here's the Real Math — strong for the
   business/founder-focused segment of the audience
6. A 36-Foot Balloon at Macy's. Bankrupt Nine Months Later. — strong juxtaposition hook
7. This CEO's Company Died in 2000. Her Next One Went Public in 2019. — strong redemption-arc hook,
   differentiator vs. nearly all existing Pets.com coverage
8. Pets.com Raised $95 Million. It Lost Money on Every Single Sale. — clean, direct, high-clarity
9. This Company's Whole Business Model Lost 27 Cents on Every Dollar — precise but slightly dry
10. The Most Famous Mascot of the Dot-Com Bubble Cost the Company Everything — good but closest to
    the already-crowded generic framing

## Recommended Title
**"The Sock Puppet That Burned $300 Million in 268 Days"**

Reasoning: leads with the single most recognizable image in the entire genre (near-universal
recognition even outside the startup niche), pairs it with a precise, sourced number and timeframe
instead of vague "dot-com bust" framing, and sets up the whole video's real differentiator — that most
retellings never get past the puppet to the actual math. Matches the folder name and README title.
