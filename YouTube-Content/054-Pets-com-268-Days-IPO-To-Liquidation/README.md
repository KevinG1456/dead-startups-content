# 054 — Pets.com: The $300 Million Sock Puppet, 268 Days From IPO to Liquidation

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around the most heavily-retold story in this
channel's genre, so extra care was taken to separate confirmed facts from the inflated pop-culture
version. Full citations in `sources-log.txt`.

**Correction, read before touching any file:** the initial research brief for this package assumed
co-founders "Greg McLeod and Josh Stevens." Both names are wrong. The real founders are **Greg
McLemore and Eva Woodsmall** (launched November 1998 as a spinoff of McLemore's company WebMagic,
incorporated February 1999). **Julie Wainwright** bought the company and became CEO in early 1999 —
she is the CEO who built it into what became famous, but she did not co-found it. Every file uses
this corrected structure.

**The real case:** Pets.com raised roughly $95.5M across three pre-IPO venture rounds (Mar/Jun/Nov
1999) from Amazon.com, Hummer Winblad Venture Partners, and Bowman Capital — Amazon became the
largest shareholder, reportedly holding as much as half or more of the company (exact percentage
varies by source, 30%-54% — flagged [VERIFY]). The company spent $11.8M on marketing in 1999 alone,
including a ~$1.2M Super Bowl XXXIV ad ("Please Don't Go," sock puppet voiced by Michael Ian Black)
and a 36-foot balloon in the 1999 Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade. It IPO'd February 2000 at $11/share
(ticker IPET), raising $82.5M. The underlying business, though, was structurally broken: shipping
heavy, bulky, low-margin pet supplies (a 50-lb bag of dog food shipped free lost $3-4 per order),
selling merchandise at roughly 27% below cost, and spending about $2 in marketing/fulfillment for
every $1 of sales. The board approved full liquidation November 4, 2000; the company ceased
operations November 9 — commonly reported as 268 days from IPO to complete liquidation, having
burned roughly $300M. The sock puppet's rights later sold for $125,000 and reportedly went on to
appear in ads for an auto lender. A genuinely funny real footnote: SNL's Robert Smigel accused the
puppet of ripping off his character Triumph the Insult Comic Dog; Pets.com preemptively sued him for
$20M, and lost a month later. Closing beat: CEO Julie Wainwright went on to found The RealReal in
2011 and take it public in 2019 — a real redemption arc, this channel's second after 030/MoviePass's
Stacy Spikes.

**Framing requirement:** No fraud, no misconduct, no named individual behaving badly — this is a
pure overhype/unit-economics failure, the definitive image of the entire dot-com bust. Never commit
to one precise Amazon ownership percentage or one precise IPO calendar date without the [VERIFY]
caveat noted in `sources-log.txt`. Never name a specific auto-lender for the sock puppet's post-
collapse ad career — not independently confirmed.
