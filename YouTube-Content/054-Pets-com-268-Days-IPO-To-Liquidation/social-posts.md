## LinkedIn Post

Everyone remembers the Pets.com sock puppet. Almost nobody remembers the actual math behind why the
company died.

I just published a full breakdown: $95.5M raised across three 1999 funding rounds (including
Amazon.com), $11.8M spent on marketing in a single year, a ~$1.2M Super Bowl ad — and a business model
that lost $3-4 on every order of dog food shipped for free, sold merchandise at roughly 27% below
cost, and spent about $2 for every $1 it made.

February 2000 IPO. November 2000 complete liquidation. Commonly reported as 268 days.

The part most retellings skip: CEO Julie Wainwright didn't disappear after this. She founded The
RealReal in 2011 and took it public in 2019.

The lesson isn't "the idea was bad" — Chewy.com later proved the exact same thesis could work at
scale. The lesson is that a beloved brand and a broken unit economy can exist in the same company at
the same time, and the brand will not save you.
