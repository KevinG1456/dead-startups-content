# Video Description

## Main Description (YouTube, long-form)

This company had a Super Bowl ad, a 36-foot balloon in the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, and
roughly $95 million in funding from investors including Amazon.com. Its mascot was one of the most
recognizable characters of the entire dot-com era.

It still lost money every single time someone bought something.

Shipping a 50-pound bag of dog food for free cost the company $3 to $4 per order. Merchandise sold
for roughly 27% below what it cost to buy. For every dollar in sales, the company spent about two
dollars on marketing and fulfillment combined.

February 2000: IPO at $11 a share, raising $82.5 million. November 2000: complete liquidation —
commonly reported as 268 days later, having burned an estimated $300 million.

This is Pets.com. You know the puppet. This video is about the actual math nobody remembers — plus
the genuinely funny lawsuit against Saturday Night Live nobody talks about, and what happened to the
CEO after the company that made her famous completely collapsed.

Correction up front: this video corrects a common retelling error — the company was founded by Greg
McLemore and Eva Woodsmall in 1998; CEO Julie Wainwright bought in and took over in 1999. Full source
list in the pinned comment and in this channel's production notes.
