# Shorts Scripts

Rule for this package: stay strictly to sourced, dated facts. Never commit to one precise Amazon
ownership percentage or an independently-recomputed IPO date — attribute the "268 days" figure as a
press retrospective count. Never name a specific auto-lender for the puppet's post-collapse ad career.
Each script targets 30-45 seconds.

## Short 1 — "They Spent $2 to Make $1"
- Hook (0-3s): "This company spent two dollars to make one dollar. On purpose. For two years."
- Body: Pets.com sold pet supplies below cost and shipped heavy, bulky products for free. Shipping a
  50-pound bag of dog food cost the company $3 to $4 every single order.
- Payoff: "The puppet was never the problem. The math was."
- CTA: "Full story on the channel."

## Short 2 — "They Sued Saturday Night Live"
- Hook (0-3s): "This company sued Saturday Night Live. Over a sock puppet. And lost."
- Body: When Triumph the Insult Comic Dog's creator sent Pets.com a cease-and-desist, Pets.com sued
  him first for $20 million. The case was dismissed about a month later.
- Payoff: "A company that couldn't make money on dog food took on late-night TV — and lost that too."
- CTA: "Full story on the channel."

## Short 3 — "268 Days"
- Hook (0-3s): "268 days. That's how long it took to go from IPO to complete liquidation."
- Body: Pets.com went public in February 2000 at $11 a share, raising $82.5 million. By November, the
  stock was worth pennies and the company was gone.
- Payoff: "The Super Bowl ad worked perfectly. The business underneath it never did."
- CTA: "Watch the full breakdown — link in bio."

## Short 4 — "What Happened to the Puppet"
- Hook (0-3s): "Everyone remembers the sock puppet. Almost nobody knows what happened to it after."
- Body: When Pets.com collapsed, the puppet's rights sold for $125,000 in the bankruptcy proceedings
  and reportedly went on to appear in car-loan ads.
- Payoff: "The mascot outlasted the company by decades."
- CTA: "Full story on the channel."

## Short 5 — "This CEO's Comeback"
- Hook (0-3s): "Pets.com collapsed in 2000. Its CEO didn't."
- Body: Julie Wainwright went on to found The RealReal in 2011 — and took it public in 2019,
  reportedly becoming the 23rd woman in history to found and lead a company to its own IPO.
- Payoff: "This isn't a story that ends at the liquidation."
- CTA: "Full story — link in bio."
