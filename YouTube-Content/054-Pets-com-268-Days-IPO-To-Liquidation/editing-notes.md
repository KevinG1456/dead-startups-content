# Editing Notes

## Pacing
- Cold open through Segment 6 (the SNL lawsuit) should move briskly and stay light/nostalgic in tone
  — this is the "fun" half of the story.
- Segments 7-9 (IPO, the real math, the collapse) are the serious/analytical core — slow down here,
  let the shipping-cost math land visually before moving on.
- Segment 11 (Wainwright's redemption) should breathe — this is the emotional payoff, don't rush past
  it into the closing lesson.

## Graphics Priorities (build these first)
1. Shipping-math graphic ($1 in, half a dollar out of a box) — the single most important visual,
   needs to land clearly
2. Falling stock-line graphic ($11 to $0.19)
3. Full-arc timeline (closing lesson)
4. Three ascending funding-round bars
5. Balloon-silhouette rising over a skyline (nostalgia beat)

## Text-Card Discipline
- Never render one single precise Amazon ownership percentage — use "reportedly as much as half the
  company or more."
- Never render a specific auto-lender company name for the puppet's post-collapse ad career.
- Attribute "268 DAYS" as a reported/press figure on screen (e.g. small "(reported)" tag) rather than
  presenting it as an independently-verified exact count.
- Do not depict the real trademarked sock puppet design in any generated graphic — use a generic,
  unbranded sock-puppet silhouette throughout.

## Audio/Music Direction
- Upbeat, slightly nostalgic bed through the founding/marketing/Super Bowl segments (1:40-5:30).
- Shift to a wry, slightly comedic bed for the SNL lawsuit segment (5:30-7:00) — this beat should feel
  fun, not tense.
- Drop to a more serious, analytical bed for the shipping-math and collapse segments (7:45-10:00).
- Warm, uplifting bed for the Wainwright redemption segment (10:45-11:30) — this is a genuine
  good-news beat, treat it as one.

## Caption/Subtitle Note
- Spell out "unit economics" in plain language on first use if it appears in captions — assume a
  portion of the audience is non-technical.
- Caption the direct SNL/Triumph reference accurately but keep it light in tone, matching the
  script's comedic framing of that segment.
