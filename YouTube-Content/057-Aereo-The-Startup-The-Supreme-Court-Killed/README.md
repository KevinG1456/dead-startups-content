# 057 — Aereo Didn't Run Out of Money. The Supreme Court Ruled It Out of Existence.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Aereo's fully documented public history
(Variety, GlobeNewswire, Crain's New York, SCOTUSblog, Justia, CNBC, TechCrunch, Wikipedia — full
citations in `sources-log.txt`). New title, user-picked directly (not from a batch file), part of a
round following Theranos/Juicero/Pets.com/Jawbone/Yik Yak (052-056).

**The real case:** Aereo, founded February 2012 by engineer Chaitanya "Chet" Kanojia and backed by
IAC chairman Barry Diller, let subscribers stream local over-the-air broadcast TV over the internet
for $8-12/month — no cable subscription required. The technical trick: Aereo assigned each individual
subscriber their own dime-sized remote antenna, capturing broadcast signals and storing a personal
copy for that one subscriber. This design wasn't an engineering afterthought — it was a direct
application of the Second Circuit's 2008 *Cartoon Network v. CSC Holdings* ("Cablevision") ruling,
which held that a unique, individually-stored copy per subscriber counted as a private performance,
not a public one, under copyright law. Broadcasters (ABC, CBS, NBC, Fox, and others) sued anyway.
Aereo won at the district court and Second Circuit level, citing the Cablevision precedent directly.
On June 25, 2014, the Supreme Court reversed, 6-3, in *American Broadcasting Cos., Inc. v. Aereo,
Inc.* — ruling that Aereo's near-live retransmissions counted as public performances because Aereo
functioned like a cable system, the exact kind of entity Congress targeted when it rewrote the
Copyright Act in 1976. Aereo suspended service three days later. It tried one last legal maneuver —
arguing that if the Court said it acted like a cable system, it should get a cable system's
compulsory license — and lost that too, in October 2014. It filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy in November
2014. Its assets, including $97 million worth of accumulated brand and technology, sold at bankruptcy
auction for less than $2 million combined.

**Framing requirement:** This is a genuinely different failure mechanism for this channel — no
fraud, no burn-rate mismanagement, no API cutoff, no down round. Aereo built a real product on a real
legal theory, litigated it in good faith all the way to the country's highest court, and lost on a
close 6-3 vote. Present it as a legal/regulatory-risk story, not a company-mismanagement story. No
individual is accused of wrongdoing anywhere in this package.
