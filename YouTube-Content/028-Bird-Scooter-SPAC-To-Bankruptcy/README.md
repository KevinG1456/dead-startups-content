# 028 — Bird Went Public at $2.3 Billion. Its Own Accounting Was Wrong. It Sold Itself to Its Own Lenders.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — built around Bird Global's full public history (SEC filings, TechCrunch, CNBC, Bloomberg, Fortune, Cassel Salpeter deal records — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This channel's first HARDWARE unit-economics autopsy — every scooter is a physical, depreciating, damage-prone asset, a fundamentally different cost structure than the software/leasing stories covered elsewhere on this channel.

**The real case:** Bird — founded September 2017 in Santa Monica by Travis VanderZanden (former Lyft COO, former Uber exec) — built a dockless electric-scooter rental business, raising roughly $700 million-plus in venture funding before going public via SPAC merger in November 2021 at a reported valuation as high as $2.3-2.5 billion. Within about a year, its market value had collapsed to roughly $70 million. In November 2022, Bird's own audit committee disclosed that the company had overstated revenue for two and a half years (Q1 2020 through Q2 2022) by recognizing revenue from rides customers never actually paid for — a self-reported accounting failure, not a fraud charge against any individual. The NYSE delisted Bird's stock effective September 25, 2023. Bird filed Chapter 11 on December 20, 2023. On April 5, 2024, its assets were sold for approximately $145 million — not to an outside buyer, but to Third Lane Mobility, a new holding company formed by Bird's OWN lenders (led by an Apollo Global Management affiliate) converting their debt into ownership.

**Framing requirement:** The spine is unit economics made physical — unlike a software subscription or a leased apartment, every Bird scooter is a real object that gets stolen, vandalized, or destroyed, and someone has to physically collect, charge, and redistribute it every night. Layer the accounting-restatement beat on top as the "even the numbers investors saw weren't real" twist — but keep it precisely framed as a disclosed, self-reported filing, not a fraud allegation, since no individual was criminally charged for it in the sourcing pass. Close on the lender-takeover structure, cross-referenced against 017 (Convoy)'s similar lender-forecloses ending.
