Sources Log — Package 028: Bird
Filled in: 2026-08-15

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
Bird is this channel's first HARDWARE unit-economics autopsy (every scooter is a
physical asset that gets damaged, stolen, or vandalized — a fundamentally different
cost structure than the software/leasing stories covered elsewhere on this channel).
It's also this channel's fourth SPAC-to-bankruptcy story (after 025/Sonder,
026/WeWork, 027/23andMe) but the only one where the company's OWN accounting was
later found to be wrong — a genuine "the numbers investors saw were not real"
beat, though this was an accounting restatement, not a fraud charge (no individual
was criminally charged in connection with the restatement in the material found in
this research pass — do not imply otherwise).

---

FOUNDING
- Founded September 2017 in Santa Monica, California by Travis VanderZanden, a
  former COO of Lyft and former Uber executive.
- Business: dockless electric scooter rental — riders find and unlock a scooter
  via app, ride, then leave it; Bird owned/managed a fleet of physical scooters
  requiring nightly charging/redistribution and constant repair/replacement due
  to damage, theft, and vandalism.
Source: Multiple corroborating company-history summaries (TechCrunch/Wikipedia-
style aggregation).

FUNDING
- Total funding figures are reported inconsistently across sources and periods:
  approximately $700 million in cumulative venture funding as of October 2019;
  a $700 million SERIES C round specifically closed April 28, 2021 (a separate,
  later, and much larger single round than the cumulative 2019 figure — do not
  conflate the two); aggregator totals across all funding rounds range from
  approximately $765 million to $1.1 billion depending on source and cutoff
  date. *** Use "raised roughly $700 million-plus in venture funding before going
  public" as the safe, hedged pre-SPAC figure — consistent with this channel's
  standard practice for messy aggregator totals (see 025/Sonder's identical
  hedging pattern). Do not state one single precise lifetime total. ***
- November 2021: Bird went public via SPAC merger (with Switchback II Corp),
  reaching a reported market value as high as $2.3-2.5 billion around the deal.
  The SPAC transaction itself brought in approximately $414 million in cash
  proceeds. *** Combining pre-SPAC VC funding (~$700M+) with the SPAC's own
  $414M cash proceeds gives a rough combined total north of $1 billion raised
  across Bird's full history — present as an approximate combined figure, not a
  single precise number. ***
Source: Forbes (Bird's Oct 2019 $275M Series D coverage, cites ~$700M cumulative
at that point); Fortune ("Bird...2.5 billion smash hit to roadkill in record
time", Jan 2024); Tracxn/PitchBook aggregator profiles (flagged as inconsistent
across each other).

THE ACCOUNTING RESTATEMENT — precisely detailed
- November 2022: Bird's own audit committee determined that its financial
  reports covering Q1 2020 through Q2 2022 (roughly 2.5 years) "should no longer
  be relied upon," per an 8-K filed with the SEC.
- The specific issue: Bird had been recognizing revenue from UNPAID customer
  rides — i.e., counting rides as revenue even when the rider was never actually
  charged/didn't pay — which overstated top-line revenue.
- The company initially planned to revise its first two quarters of 2022 alone
  by $12.5 million, part of a total revision of $31.6 million across the full
  2020-2022 restatement period.
- *** No evidence found in this research pass of any individual executive being
  criminally charged or personally sanctioned specifically in connection with
  this accounting restatement — present as a disclosed, self-reported accounting
  failure (an SEC filing Bird itself made), not as a fraud allegation. Do not use
  "fraud" language for this specific event; it is accurately described as
  overstated/misstated revenue recognition, self-disclosed via SEC filing. ***
Source: TechCrunch/Yahoo News ("Bird tells SEC it overstated revenue for two
years", Nov 2022); Tech Times; dot.LA.

THE COLLAPSE — precisely dated
- September 22, 2023: Bird received formal notice from the NYSE that its shares
  would be delisted, effective at market open September 25, 2023, after Bird
  failed to maintain the exchange's required minimum market capitalization
  ($15 million for 30 consecutive trading days). Bird moved to OTC trading.
- Bird's market value had fallen from its ~$2.3-2.5B SPAC-era peak to
  approximately $70 million within about 12 months of the SPAC deal closing —
  and continued falling from there before the eventual delisting roughly two
  years after the SPAC.
- December 20, 2023: Bird Global Inc. filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection
  in Florida federal bankruptcy court, seeking to sell the company.
Source: SEC Form 8-K (Sept 22 2023, NYSE delisting notice); Morningstar/
BusinessWire/Nasdaq (Sept 22 2023 press release republication); CNBC (Dec 20
2023, bankruptcy filing); Bloomberg (Dec 20 2023); Engineering and Technology
Magazine (Dec 21 2023).

THE SALE — precisely dated
- April 5, 2024: Bird Global's assets were sold, completing an approximately $145
  million transaction, to Third Lane Mobility — a new private holding company
  formed by a consortium of Bird's own first- and second-lien lenders, led by
  MidCap Financial (an Apollo Global Management affiliate) and Obelysk. ***
  Notable structural detail: this was Bird's OWN LENDERS buying the company out
  of its own bankruptcy — a debt-for-ownership conversion, not an outside
  strategic acquirer stepping in. Worth stating explicitly on screen, similar in
  spirit to how this channel handled Convoy's lender-foreclosure ending (package
  017). *** The $145M sale price is well above the ~$70M market value Bird held
  as a public company a year earlier — this reflects the value of the underlying
  operating business/fleet assets sold free of the public company's debt and
  legal liabilities, not a "the company was actually worth more than the stock
  market thought" claim — present carefully, don't imply the bankruptcy was
  unnecessary.
Source: Cassel Salpeter & Co. (deal advisor press release, Apr 2024); Dealroom.co
("Bird's journey: from rapid rise to bankruptcy and revival under Third Lane
Mobility"); MarketScreener.
