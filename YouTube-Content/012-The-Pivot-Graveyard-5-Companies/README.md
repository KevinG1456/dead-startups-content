# 012 — The Pivot Graveyard: 5 Companies That Pivoted Themselves to Death

**Package status:** Structural framework ready. This is a multi-segment video — 5 short case studies illustrating companies whose repeated pivots contributed to their failure, rather than saving them. Every segment's specific causal narrative must be confirmed against sourced reporting before recording; be careful to distinguish "pivoted and it didn't work" from "pivoting itself was the primary cause of failure," since these are related but not identical claims.

**Strong candidate segments (verify each before use):**
1. **Fab.com** — pivoted from a gay social network (Fabulis) to a flash-sale design-goods site (Fab.com), raised over $300M, then pivoted again into an inventory-heavy full-price retail model; collapsed and was sold for parts around 2015 `[VERIFY exact figures and timeline]`
2. **Jawbone** — pivoted from Bluetooth headsets to fitness trackers (UP band) to a broader health-data platform strategy, amid repeated product recalls and manufacturing issues; liquidated in 2017 `[VERIFY exact timeline and pivot sequence]`
3. **Color Labs** — raised a reported $41M for a photo-sharing app, pivoted the product multiple times without finding traction, eventually sold in what was widely characterized as an acqui-hire around 2012 `[VERIFY exact figures and outcome]`
4. **Quixey** — pivoted its business model from consumer app-search to enterprise deep-linking technology; shut down/sold for a small fraction of its raised capital around 2017 `[VERIFY exact figures and pivot sequence]`
5. **Beepi** — pivoted its used-car marketplace model multiple times (delivery/inspection logistics approach vs. inventory-holding approach); shut down in 2017 `[VERIFY exact timeline and pivot sequence]`

**Framing note:** for each case, be precise about what "pivoted to death" actually means — did repeated pivots burn cash and organizational focus without ever reaching product-market fit, or did the company have other independent problems (execution, market timing, funding environment) that pivoting merely failed to fix? The video should distinguish "pivoting was the cause" from "pivoting wasn't the cure" — the latter is the more defensible and interesting claim for most of these cases.
