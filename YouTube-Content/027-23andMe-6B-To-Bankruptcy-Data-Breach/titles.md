# Title Options — Ranked

Anchored on 23andMe: founded 2006 by Anne Wojcicki, Linda Avey, and Paul Cusenza, raised just
under $900M pre-IPO, went public via SPAC merger with Richard Branson's VG Acquisition Corp in
February 2021 (deal valued at ~$3.5B, ~$984M cash at close), briefly crossed a $6 billion market
value after listing (a separate, higher figure than the deal valuation). An October 2023 breach —
a five-month credential-stuffing attack, not a direct hack of 23andMe's own systems — compromised
~14,000 accounts directly and, via the "DNA Relative" family-matching feature, exposed data
belonging to ~6.9 million users. The attacker specifically marketed stolen data by ancestry,
posting a database labeled "Ashkenazi DNA Data of Celebrities" and separately claiming records for
350,000 users of Chinese heritage. A $30 million settlement followed in September 2024. Weeks
later, September 17, 2024, all seven independent board directors resigned the same day. The company
cut 200+ jobs and killed its cancer-research division in November 2024. Chapter 11 followed March
23-24, 2025; Wojcicki resigned as CEO the same day. In the bankruptcy auction, Wojcicki — bidding
through nonprofit TTAM Research Institute — beat Regeneron and won the company's core data assets
for $305 million. Cross-reference package 025 (Sonder) and 026 (WeWork) as this channel's other
SPAC-to-bankruptcy stories, and 026/029 (Ozy Media) for the "founder survives vs. founder convicted"
contrast. No fraud/scam/lied language — Wojcicki has not been charged with any crime.

## Recommended Title
**"23andMe Hit a $6 Billion Valuation. A Data Breach and a Board Revolt Ended It."**

Reasoning: leads with the single most recognizable, correctly-labeled figure in the whole package
(the post-listing $6B peak market value — kept explicitly distinct from the $3.5B SPAC-deal
valuation inside the video) and states the real causal chain — breach, then board revolt, then
ending — without overclaiming that the breach alone bankrupted the company. It's the least-covered
angle of this story anywhere else: most coverage treats the breach and the bankruptcy as separate
news events. Works fully truncated on mobile, names no unproven wrongdoing, and sets up both the
personal "what happens to your data" hook and the TTAM twist ending without spoiling it.
