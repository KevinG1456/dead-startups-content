## LinkedIn
"A real case study in how a security incident becomes a governance crisis, and how a governance
crisis becomes a bankruptcy:

23andMe went public via SPAC in 2021 — a deal valued at approximately $3.5 billion, with the stock
briefly reaching a $6 billion market value shortly after listing. In October 2023, the company
disclosed a five-month credential-stuffing attack (not a hack of its own systems) that exposed data
belonging to roughly 6.9 million users, including data specifically marketed by the attacker along
ancestry lines. A $30 million settlement followed in September 2024.

Six weeks later, on September 17, 2024, all seven of the company's independent directors resigned
on the same day, stating in their resignation letter that the founder had not delivered 'a fully
financed, fully diligenced, actionable proposal.' The company cut 200+ jobs and its cancer-research
division that November. It filed Chapter 11 in March 2025.

In the bankruptcy auction, founder Anne Wojcicki — bidding through a nonprofit she founded, TTAM
Research Institute — beat out Regeneron Pharmaceuticals and won back the company's core data assets
for $305 million. She has not been charged with any crime. I break down the full chain, sourced
