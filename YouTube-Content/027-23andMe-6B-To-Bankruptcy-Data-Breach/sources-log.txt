Sources Log — Package 027: 23andMe
Filled in: 2026-08-15

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
23andMe is a genuinely different failure shape from every other package on this
channel: not primarily a burn-rate, unit-economics, or platform-risk story, but a
company whose core asset — genetic data belonging to over 15 million real people —
became a liability after a catastrophic breach, which then collapsed shareholder
and board confidence, which then triggered the bankruptcy. It also went public via
SPAC (like 025/Sonder) and involves a still-living, non-charged founder attempting
a comeback (structurally close to 026/WeWork's Neumann arc, though the details are
very different) — worth cross-referencing both packages on screen.

---

FOUNDING
- Founded 2006 by Anne Wojcicki, Linda Avey, and Paul Cusenza.
- Business: direct-to-consumer genetic testing (saliva-sample DNA kits) providing
  ancestry and health-risk information; later built a research/pharma data-
  licensing business on top of its accumulated genetic database.
Source: Caproasia bankruptcy-filing summary; multiple corroborating company-history
aggregator pages (Wikipedia-style summaries).

FUNDING AND SPAC IPO
- Total funding raised prior to going public: reported as "just under $900
  million," including an $85 million Series F round announced shortly before the
  SPAC announcement (as of the Feb 2021 SPAC deal announcement). *** Treat as an
  approximate pre-IPO figure, not a single precise all-time total — consistent
  with this channel's standard hedge for messy aggregator figures (see 025's
  Sonder funding note for the same pattern). ***
- February 2021: 23andMe announced it would go public via a SPAC merger with VG
  Acquisition Corp., a SPAC backed by Sir Richard Branson's Virgin Group. The
  deal was expected to give 23andMe roughly $984 million in cash at close and
  valued the company at approximately $3.5 billion.
- After the merger closed (2021), 23andMe's market value briefly crossed $6
  billion at listing — the headline "peak" figure for this package (note:
  distinct from the $3.5B deal-announcement valuation; the higher figure reflects
  where the stock actually traded shortly after listing, not the deal terms
  themselves — state which figure is being used when citing either one).
- Anne Wojcicki became a self-made billionaire on paper as a result of the SPAC
  deal (June 2021 reporting).
Source: Forbes ("23andMe's Anne Wojcicki Becomes Newest Self-Made Billionaire",
Jun 17 2021); Built In SF (SPAC announcement coverage); Caproasia (bankruptcy
summary citing the post-listing $6B peak market value).

THE 2023 DATA BREACH — precisely detailed, handle with care (real people, real
sensitive data, an incident that specifically targeted people by ethnicity)
- Attack method: a credential-stuffing attack (reusing usernames/passwords leaked
  from OTHER, unrelated prior breaches to log into 23andMe accounts that reused
  the same credentials) — NOT a breach of 23andMe's own systems/database directly.
  Began approximately April 29, 2023, continued for about five months.
- Approximately 14,000 individual user accounts were directly compromised via
  credential stuffing. Because of 23andMe's "DNA Relative" family-matching
  feature, compromising those 14,000 accounts exposed data belonging to
  approximately 6.9 million total users (the feature shows genetic-relative
  connections across the platform, so compromising one account exposes
  information about people connected to it).
- 23andMe did not require two-factor authentication at the time, and its
  password policy required only a minimum of eight characters with minimal
  complexity requirements — contributing factors cited in subsequent security
  analysis (HIPAA Journal, Dark Reading, and a joint Canada/UK privacy-regulator
  investigation).
- The breach was publicly revealed October 6, 2023, after a hacker using the
  pseudonym "Golem" posted a database on BreachForums (a cybercriminal forum)
  labeled "Ashkenazi DNA Data of Celebrities," and separately claimed to possess
  data belonging to 350,000 users of Chinese heritage, offering both datasets for
  sale. *** This is a real, well-documented, and serious detail — the breach was
  specifically marketed by the attacker as targeting people of Ashkenazi Jewish
  and Chinese ancestry. Present factually and soberly, attributed to the
  reporting (JTA, Forward, Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post all covered this
  specific detail), without sensationalizing — this is a legitimate, necessary
  part of the story (it directly shaped the resulting lawsuits and regulatory
  response), not an optional shock beat. ***
- September 2024: 23andMe agreed to a $30 million settlement of the resulting
  class-action lawsuit covering the roughly 6.9 million affected users. (One
  source, HIPAA Journal, later references a revised $50 million settlement figure
  in bankruptcy-court proceedings — *** flag as [VERIFY]: the $30M figure is the
  more widely and consistently reported number as of the original Sept 2024
  settlement; a later, larger figure may reflect a bankruptcy-court revision —
  do not state one precise final settlement number without rechecking closer to
  recording. ***)
- A joint investigation by Canada's Privacy Commissioner and the UK's Information
  Commissioner's Office later concluded and publicly criticized 23andMe's
  security practices in connection with this breach (June 2025 report).
Source: Security.org breach summary; HIPAA Journal; Dark Reading; JTA, Forward,
Times of Israel, Jerusalem Post (Ashkenazi/Chinese targeting + $30M settlement,
all Sept 2024); Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (joint regulatory
findings, 2025).

THE BOARD COLLAPSE AND BANKRUPTCY — precisely dated
- Throughout 2024, Anne Wojcicki repeatedly proposed taking 23andMe private,
  submitting multiple proposals to the board's special committee.
- September 17, 2024: all seven independent directors resigned from the board on
  the same day — Roelof Botha, Patrick Chung, Sandra Hernández, Neal Mohan,
  Valerie Montgomery Rice, Richard Scheller, and Peter Taylor. Their resignation
  letter stated Wojcicki had not delivered "a fully financed, fully diligenced,
  actionable proposal that is in the best interests of the non-affiliated
  shareholders," despite months of engagement. *** This is a rare, on-record,
  board-level statement of no confidence — comparable in strength to the
  bankruptcy-filing causal statements this channel has used before (e.g., James
  Beriker's court-filed statement in 023/Munchery) — use it directly rather than
  a paraphrase. ***
- November 2024: 23andMe cut over 200 employees (about 40% of remaining
  workforce) and discontinued its therapeutics/cancer-research division as an
  emergency cost-cutting measure following the board exodus.
- March 23-24, 2025: 23andMe filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in Delaware. Anne
  Wojcicki resigned as CEO effective immediately (remaining only as a board
  member), citing declining stock value, the fallout from the 2023 breach,
  workforce cuts, and the board collapse as the converging causes.
- A two-stage bankruptcy auction followed, contested between Regeneron
  Pharmaceuticals and Anne Wojcicki herself (bidding through TTAM Research
  Institute, a nonprofit she founded specifically to try to reacquire the
  company's assets). The bankruptcy court approved a $305 million sale to TTAM
  Research Institute — meaning Wojcicki ultimately did regain control of the
  company's assets, including the genetic data of more than 15 million
  customers, but only after the public-company structure itself had been wiped
  out through bankruptcy. *** This TTAM outcome is a genuinely unusual ending —
  worth an explicit on-screen note that the founder ended up back in control of
  the core asset (the data), just via a nonprofit vehicle and a much smaller sum
  than the company's SPAC-era peak valuation. ***
Source: CNBC (Sept 17 2024 board-resignation coverage, includes CEO memo); Fortune
(Oct 17 2024 and Nov 12 2024, board fallout and layoffs); CNBC (Mar 24 2025,
bankruptcy filing + CEO resignation); Yale Daily News (Mar 27 2025); Al Jazeera
(Mar 24 2025); elevenflo.com ("23andMe: From $6B Unicorn to $305M Nonprofit Sale
in Chapter 11" — TTAM sale summary, corroborated by multiple bankruptcy-court news
outlets).

BEFORE RECORDING
1. The 2023 breach was a CREDENTIAL-STUFFING attack (reused passwords from OTHER
   breaches), not a direct breach of 23andMe's own systems — state this precisely,
   it changes where responsibility/negligence discussion should focus (weak
   password policy and no mandatory 2FA at the time, not a hacked database).
2. Present the Ashkenazi Jewish/Chinese-ancestry targeting factually and
   soberly, attributed to the specific reporting that covered it — this is a real
   and legitimate part of the story, not a detail to sensationalize or to omit.
3. Settlement figure: use $30 million as the primary, best-corroborated figure;
   flag the later-referenced $50 million bankruptcy-court figure as [VERIFY] if
   used, don't state one number as final without rechecking.
4. Distinguish the $3.5B SPAC-deal-announcement valuation from the ~$6B peak
   market value the stock actually reached shortly after listing — these are two
   different, both-real numbers.
5. Anne Wojcicki has not been charged with any crime — frame her story as
   governance/crisis-management, not wrongdoing. No "fraud/scam/lied" language
   applies to her personally.
6. The TTAM Research Institute outcome means Wojcicki ultimately reacquired the
   core assets — present this clearly as the actual ending, not a simple "company
   died" story; it's closer in spirit to 020's "where dead companies' data
   actually goes" theme, applied to the founder herself regaining control.
