## LinkedIn
"A real, precisely sourced case study in engineering a solution before checking whether anyone needed
it:

Doug Evans, who'd already built and sold one juice company, founded Juicero in 2013. The pitch: a $699
WiFi-connected press exerting four tons of force to squeeze proprietary produce packets, each verified
by QR code over the internet before dispensing. Investors — Kleiner Perkins, Google Ventures, Campbell
Soup Company, Thrive Capital — put in roughly $118.5 million, valuing the company at over $270 million.

On April 19, 2017, Bloomberg published a video showing the packets could be squeezed entirely by hand —
7.5 ounces of juice in about 90 seconds, against 8 ounces from the machine. One of Juicero's own
investors confirmed it on the record. Sixteen months after launch, the company shut down.

The genuinely good part: founder Doug Evans later built The Sprouting Company, reportedly reaching a
~$12 million annual run rate by 2026 — this time with the subscription model built in from day one.

Full breakdown: [video link]
