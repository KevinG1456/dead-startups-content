# 053 — Juicero: $120 Million for a Machine Two Bare Hands Could Beat

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced (2026-08-16) — built around Juicero's fully documented public
funding, product, and shutdown history (TechCrunch, Bloomberg, Fortune, CNN Business, Forbes, Crunchbase
News — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). One of five fresh titles researched cold this session
(Theranos, Juicero, Pets.com, Jawbone, Yik Yak) from a user-provided shortlist of famous, well-documented
dead startups — not drawn from the "dead-saas-30" batch file this channel worked through in earlier
sessions. No fraud, criminal, or non-convicted-founder sensitivity applies to anyone in this story.

**The real case:** Doug Evans, who had already built and sold one juice business (Organic Avenue, a NYC
cold-pressed juice bar chain, founded 2002, sold 2012), founded Juicero in San Francisco in 2013 to bring
"farm to glass" fresh juice into the home. The pitch: a WiFi-connected countertop press exerting four tons
of force on proprietary, single-serving "Produce Packs" of pre-chopped fruit and vegetables, each carrying
a QR code the machine had to verify online before dispensing. Investors — Kleiner Perkins, Google
Ventures, Campbell Soup Company, Thrive Capital, Artis Ventures, even Carmelo Anthony — poured in roughly
$118.5 million (commonly rounded to ~$120 million) across a Seed, Series A, and Series B round, valuing
the company at over $270 million by March 2016, the same month the $699 Press launched.

Eighteen months later, on April 19, 2017, Bloomberg published a video showing that the Produce Packs
could be squeezed by hand — no $699 machine required — yielding 7.5 ounces of juice in about 90 seconds,
against 8 ounces from the machine. Nearly identical results, no engineering required. Two of Juicero's own
investors told Bloomberg they were surprised; one, Doug Chertok, said plainly: "There is no doubt the
packs can be squeezed without the machine." CEO Jeff Dunn (a former Coca-Cola North America president who
had replaced founder Doug Evans five months earlier) publicly defended the machine's WiFi-based freshness
verification, then offered 30-day refunds to anyone who mailed their Press back. It wasn't enough: a
January 2017 price cut to $399 hadn't fixed the underlying problem, a July 2017 layoff of 25% of staff
didn't either, and on September 1, 2017 — sixteen months after launch — Juicero suspended sales entirely,
offered to buy back remaining machines, searched for a buyer for the company and its IP, found none, and
shut down for good.

**The coda, genuinely earned:** Doug Evans didn't disappear. He later founded The Sprouting Company, a
subscription home-sprouting business, and pitched it on Shark Tank in October 2025 — reportedly growing
from ~$200K/month in revenue before the episode to a ~$12 million annualized run rate by early 2026, this
time building the subscription model in from day one rather than bolting hardware-verification onto it.

**Framing requirement:** The comedic/absurd tone is well-earned here — unlike most packages on this
channel, Juicero is genuinely one of the most-mocked cases in Silicon Valley history, and that's fine to
lean into. But every number, date, and quote must stay exactly as sourced (see `sources-log.txt`
"Before Recording" section) — do not use the unverified "$32 million R&D for the packets alone" figure
some retellings repeat, and do not present the Crunchbase "Series C" data entry as a real fourth funding
round; it's very likely a duplicate of the Series B.
