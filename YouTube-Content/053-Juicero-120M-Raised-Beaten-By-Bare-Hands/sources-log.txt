FOUNDING AND FOUNDER BACKGROUND — precisely sourced
- Doug Evans co-founded Organic Avenue in 2002, a cold-pressed organic juice bar chain in NYC (grew to
  ~10 retail stores), motivated in part by losing his mother to cancer and his father to heart disease.
  Organic Avenue was sold in 2012; some investors reportedly received ~6x their investment.
- Evans founded Juicero in San Francisco in 2013, aiming to bring "farm to glass" fresh-pressed juice
  into the home via a machine plus proprietary produce packs.
Source: Paul Shapiro, Medium ("How Doug Evans Rose from the Ruins of Juicero"); Wikipedia ("Juicero");
Slidebean ("What happened to Juicero? The Doug Evans story").

THE FUNDING — precisely sourced, dated
- Oct 2013: $4M Seed round.
- Apr 2014: $15.8M Series A.
- Mar 2016: $70M Series B, led by Artis Ventures, with Kleiner Perkins, Google Ventures (GV), Campbell
  Soup Company, Thrive Capital, and Carmelo Anthony's investment vehicle (Melo7 Tech Partners) among
  participants. Reported post-round valuation over $270 million.
- Total lifetime funding: widely reported as ~$118.5 million (Crunchbase News, Dec 2017) — repeatedly
  rounded to "~$120 million" across sources (Axios, Fox News, TechCrunch, Crunchbase News). This
  package uses "~$118.5 million (commonly rounded to ~$120 million)" rather than forcing a single exact
  figure, since the rounding is consistent but the precise figure varies slightly by source.
  *** [VERIFY]: Crunchbase's own funding-round data also lists an unexplained "Series C" round dated
  around April 1, 2016 — one day after the Series B's March 31, 2016 date — with no separate amount
  found anywhere in press coverage. This is very likely a data-entry duplicate of the Series B round
  rather than a real separate raise. Do NOT present this as a distinct fourth funding round in any file
  — the real, well-documented rounds are Seed → Series A → Series B only. ***
Source: TechCrunch ("Investors pour $70 million into Juicero, a smart kitchen appliance maker"); Fox
News ("Silicon Valley invests $120M in hilariously inessential juicer"); Axios ("Juicero is shutting
down, draining VCs of $120 million"); Crunchbase News (Dec 2017, "$118.5 million" figure); Crunchbase
company/funding-round profiles.

THE PRODUCT — precisely sourced
- The Juicero Press: a WiFi-connected countertop machine that used complex mechanical systems capable
  of exerting four tons of force to squeeze proprietary, single-serving "Produce Packs" of pre-chopped
  fruits and vegetables.
- Each Produce Pack carried a QR code that had to be scanned and verified by the machine over an active
  internet connection before it would dispense — officially framed by the company as a food-safety
  measure to prevent packs being used past their expiration date.
- Produce Packs were sold on a subscription, reportedly $5-7 per single-serving pack.
- Launched March 2016 at $699.
Source: Wikipedia ("Juicero"); Bloomberg ("Silicon Valley's $400 Juicer May Be Feeling the Squeeze,"
Apr 19, 2017); TechCrunch ("Juicero cuts the price of its luxury juicer by $300," Jan 17, 2017).

THE CEO CHANGE — precisely sourced, dated
- Oct 31, 2016: Doug Evans steps down as CEO. Jeff Dunn, a former president of Coca-Cola North America,
  becomes CEO. Evans remained connected to the company (reported as staying on in some capacity/founder
  role) but Dunn ran day-to-day operations from this point through the shutdown.
Source: BevNET ("Juicero Cuts Staff, Evans Steps Back"); multiple secondary sources citing the same Oct
31, 2016 date.

THE PRICE CUT — precisely sourced, dated
- Jan 17, 2017: price cut from $699 to $399 — reportedly moved up 12-18 months ahead of an originally
  planned schedule, after a Black Friday 2016 test in which pricing the machine under $400 doubled new
  signups in a single day.
Source: TechCrunch (Jan 17, 2017, "Juicero cuts the price of its luxury juicer by $300"); Fortune (Jan
17, 2017).

THE BLOOMBERG VIDEO — Apr 19, 2017, precisely sourced, THE CENTRAL BEAT OF THIS PACKAGE
- Apr 19, 2017: Bloomberg publishes a story and video ("Silicon Valley's $400 Juicer May Be Feeling the
  Squeeze") showing that Juicero's proprietary Produce Packs could be squeezed by hand, without the
  machine at all, yielding nearly identical results: reporters got 7.5 ounces of juice in about 90
  seconds squeezing by hand, versus 8 ounces from the machine — comparable volume, comparable speed, no
  $399-$699 device required.
- Two Juicero investors told Bloomberg they were surprised to learn the packs could be squeezed by hand
  without the machine. Investor Doug Chertok is quoted directly: "There is no doubt the packs can be
  squeezed without the machine."
- CEO Jeff Dunn publicly responded, arguing the machine still served a purpose (freshness/expiration
  verification via the WiFi/QR-code check) and downplaying the comparison; within days, Juicero
  announced a 30-day refund offer, with Dunn stating: "if you send us your Press, we'll refund the money
  you paid for it."
Source: Bloomberg (Apr 19, 2017, "Silicon Valley's $400 Juicer May Be Feeling the Squeeze" — Bloomberg
Businessweek feature); Snopes (Apr 19, 2017, confirms the claim as True, not exaggerated folklore);
Slate (Apr 2017); Gizmodo ("Juicero CEO Begs You: Do NOT Squeeze Our Juice Bags"); Fortune (Apr 21,
2017, refund offer).

THE LAYOFFS — Jul 2017, precisely sourced, dated
- Jul 14, 2017: Juicero announces it will cut 25% of its staff (mostly sales and marketing roles) as
  part of a "strategic shift," alongside plans for a cheaper second-generation machine — a source close
  to the company told press the planned V2 price point was in the ~$200 range, though this was never
  officially confirmed before the shutdown.
Source: Bloomberg (Jul 14, 2017, "Startup Juicero Cuts 25% of Staff as Startup Struggles to Sell
Machines"); Engadget (Jul 14, 2017); The Register (Jul 14, 2017); Fortune (Jul 14, 2017).

THE SHUTDOWN — Sept 2017, precisely sourced, dated
- Sept 1, 2017: Juicero announces it is suspending sales of both the Press machine and the Produce
  Packs entirely, roughly 16 months after the March 2016 launch. The company offered to repurchase
  machines from existing customers and said it would search for a buyer for the company and its
  intellectual property.
- No buyer was found. The company wound down completely; secondary sources place final closure around
  Dec 1, 2017.
Source: CNN Business (Sept 1, 2017, "Startup behind ridiculed $400 juicer shuts down"); Fortune (Sept
1, 2017, "Juicero: The Maker of the Infamous $400 Juicer Is Shutting Down"); Forbes (Sept 1, 2017, "RIP
Juicero"); Wikipedia ("Juicero").

THE CODA — Doug Evans' later venture, precisely sourced, dated
- Evans later founded The Sprouting Company, a countertop home-sprouting subscription business (fresh,
  nutrient-dense sprouts grown without soil or sunlight).
- Oct 2025: Evans pitched The Sprouting Company on Shark Tank (Season 17). Per Entrepreneur.com and
  Shark Tank Blog reporting: the company was doing ~$200,000/month in revenue before the episode aired,
  grew to ~$540,000/month by December, and was reportedly tracking over $1 million/month by around
  March 2026 — a ~$12 million annualized run rate, built as a subscription business from day one (fresh
  seed refills to recurring subscribers, deliberately avoiding Juicero's own subscription-adjacent
  mistakes around forced hardware dependency).
Source: Entrepreneur.com ("This Founder Went on Shark Tank After His Last Startup Was 'Publicly
Eviscerated' — Now He's on a $12 Million Run Rate"); Shark Tank Blog ("Shark Tank Sharks Obsessed
Juicero Founder's $12M Sprout Revolution").
