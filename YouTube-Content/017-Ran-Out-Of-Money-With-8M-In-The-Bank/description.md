## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: Convoy, the Bezos-backed Seattle freight-marketplace startup that raised $260 million at a $3.8 billion valuation in April 2022 — including $100 million in secured venture debt from Hercules Capital and a separate $150 million line of credit from J.P. Morgan — and shut down eighteen months later, in October 2023.

We walk through how venture debt actually works, what a secured lien and a loan covenant are and why lenders attach them, and what specifically happened when Convoy's freight-market business collapsed: Hercules Capital foreclosed on Convoy's assets and took effective control of the wind-down, and Convoy's technology was ultimately sold to Flexport for a reported fraction of the company's peak valuation. We close with practical guidance: the specific covenant terms and lender rights founders should scrutinize closely before signing a venture debt agreement.
