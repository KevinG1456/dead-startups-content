## LinkedIn
"One of the least-understood risks in startup finance: venture debt is often secured by a blanket lien on company assets — not just equipment, but cash, receivables, and even technology.

That means when a company runs into distress, the venture debt lender's claim can outrank everyone else's, including the founders and the board.

Convoy is a real, well-documented example: it raised $260M (including $100M in secured venture debt from Hercules Capital) at a $3.8B valuation in April 2022. Eighteen months later, when the freight market collapsed, its lender foreclosed on its assets and took effective control of the wind-down — Convoy's technology was ultimately sold to Flexport for a reported fraction of that peak valuation.

I broke down exactly how this mechanism works, and what specific terms founders should scrutinize before signing a venture debt agreement.
