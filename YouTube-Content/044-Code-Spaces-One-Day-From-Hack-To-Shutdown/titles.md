## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **Hacked on a Tuesday. Out of Business by Wednesday.** — the sharpest, most self-contained
   speed-shock hook; needs no company name or AWS jargon to land, and is accurate to the real dated
   timeline (attack Jun 17, shutdown announced Jun 18) without claiming a precise hour count
2. This Company Had Backups. The Hacker Deleted Those Too. — the single most quotable irony in the
   whole story, sets up the entire technical lesson in one sentence
3. No Two-Factor Authentication. One Bad Password. One Dead Company. — precise, concrete, and
   immediately actionable for the DevOps/founder segment of the audience
4. One Hacker Deleted a Company's Backups. The Backups Were the Problem. — strong paradox framing
5. They Tried to Lock the Hacker Out. He Was Already Back In. — strong mid-story tension beat
6. This Company Refused to Pay a Hacker. It Cost Them Everything Anyway. — strong moral-complexity
   hook (doing the "right" thing still wasn't enough)
7. The Ransom Note Came With a Hotmail Address. The Company Still Said No. — a strong, specific,
   slightly absurd detail that humanizes the attack
8. One Missing Checkbox in AWS. One Dead Company, the Next Day. — very strong for a technical
   audience, slightly less accessible for a general one
9. This Is the Most Cited Cloud Security Horror Story in Tech — Here's What Actually Happened. —
   strong authority/curiosity hybrid, good for viewers already loosely familiar with the incident
10. What Happened to Code Spaces? The Full Story — best pure-SEO, lower CTR

## Recommended Title
**"Hacked on a Tuesday. Out of Business by Wednesday."**

Reasoning: leads with pure speed and stakes — no company name, AWS jargon, or prior familiarity
required to feel the hook's full weight. Stays strictly accurate to the real, dated timeline (attack
began June 17 2014, a Tuesday; shutdown announced June 18 2014, a Wednesday) without claiming an
unverified precise hour count like "12 hours." Sets up the full downstream story (the extortion
demand, the failed password reset, the co-located backups, the final statement) as the video's proof
of exactly how that happened. Matches the title already used in this package's README.
