# 050 — I Tracked Down Every Company From Techstars' First Class Ever. Half of Them, Nobody Can Even Confirm.

**Package status:** Filled in and sourced — a full, individually-verified company-by-company audit of Techstars Boulder's inaugural Summer 2007 class (TechCrunch's contemporaneous 2007-2008 coverage, Brad Feld's own 2010 aggregate data post, CBInsights, Crunchbase — full citations in `sources-log.txt`). This package fills dead-saas-30 batch title #29 ("The cohort study: tracking every startup from one accelerator demo day, 10 years on"), one of 6 titles remaining from that batch's untouched second half (#21-30) after 4 others (#21, #24, #27, #28) were already used for packages 044-047. Confirmed zero overlap with package 007 (YC Summer 2006) — different accelerator, different companies, different year.

**The real batch:** Techstars Boulder, Summer 2007 — the very first Techstars class ever run, founded by David Cohen (with David Brown and Brad Feld). 10 companies: EventVue, Intense Debate, Socialthing!, J-Squared Media, search-to-phone, Villij, Filtrbox, KBLabs, Brightkite, and madKast. Each gave up roughly 5-6% equity for a small amount of seed capital (commonly cited around $15-18K per team) plus mentorship and office space, culminating in an August 17, 2007 demo day.

**The real finding — and it's a genuinely different one than package 007's:** where 007's YC Summer 2006 audit could confirm all 11 companies' outcomes with real confidence, this earlier, smaller, less-covered batch cannot. Only 4 of the 10 companies have clean, well-documented outcomes: EventVue (shut down 2010, founder's own on-record post-mortem), Intense Debate (acquired by Automattic in 2008, **still actively maintained today** — this batch's one confirmed long-term survivor), Socialthing! (acquired by AOL in 2008, discontinued in 2017 — an acquire-then-kill story, but on an 8.5-year timeline, not the ~1-year pattern seen in 007), and Brightkite (merged with Limbo in 2009, wound down by 2011-2012). One more, Villij, is complicated but reasonably clear: the original company effectively dissolved, and its name was later reused by an unrelated acquirer in 2012 — present as gone, not survived. The remaining 5 — J-Squared Media, search-to-phone, Filtrbox, KBLabs, and madKast — have genuinely UNCONFIRMED final outcomes: two were acquired but their post-acquisition fate as standalone products was never confirmed either way, and three have no shutdown, acquisition, or continued-operation evidence at all. KBLabs in particular could barely be corroborated as a company at all beyond appearing in secondary rosters.

**Framing requirement:** the video's actual thesis is NOT "9 out of 10 startups fail" (that's 007's territory) — it's "even for a real, once-press-covered startup batch, 'just go check what happened to them' gets meaningfully harder to do honestly the smaller and older the batch gets, and pretending otherwise would mean inventing outcomes." Present the three-way split (confirmed clean / complicated-but-clear / genuinely unconfirmed) explicitly on screen as the finding itself, not as a shortcoming to gloss over.
