# Script Package

Anchored on Techstars Boulder's Summer 2007 class — full citations in `sources-log.txt`. THE SINGLE
MOST IMPORTANT RULE FOR THIS SCRIPT: do not force a flat "X of 10 gone" ratio. Present the honest
three-way split — 4 confirmed clean outcomes, 1 complicated-but-clear (Villij), 5 genuinely
unconfirmed (2 acquired-with-unknown-fate, 3 no-evidence-found) — as the video's actual finding.
Intense Debate is a confirmed long-term survivor, still maintained under Automattic today — never
lump it in with "gone."

## Complete Viral Script (Main Cut)

**Target length:** 13–15 minutes

### Cold Open (0:00–0:15)
- VO: "I tracked down every company from Techstars' very first class ever. Half of them, nobody can even confirm what happened."
- Visual: 10-square grid animates in; instead of a clean X-out pattern, several squares fade to a flickering "?" mark
- Pattern Interrupt: hard cut to silence, white text on black: "This isn't the video where I give you one clean number. Here's why."

### Hook (0:15–1:00)
- VO: "Before Techstars became one of the biggest startup accelerators in the world, it was 10 unknown companies in Boulder, Colorado, in the summer of 2007 — the very first class the program ever ran. I went and checked what actually happened to every single one."
- Open loop: "By the end of this video, you'll know exactly what happened to all 10 — and which of them, honestly, I could not fully confirm, even now."

### Introduction (1:00–1:45)
- Method statement: reconstructed from TechCrunch's contemporaneous 2007-2008 coverage, Techstars co-founder Brad Feld's own 2010 aggregate data post, and cross-checked against CBInsights/Crunchbase — sourced on screen throughout
- Explicit framing, stated early: "One honesty note up front — I already did a full audit like this for a Y Combinator batch on this channel, and every single one of those 11 outcomes was confirmable. This batch is different. It's smaller, it's older, and it was covered a lot less at the time. So instead of forcing a clean ratio, I'm going to show you exactly what I could confirm, what's genuinely complicated, and what I honestly could not track down."

### The Audit — 4 Confirmed Clean Outcomes (1:45–6:30)
- **Segment 1 — EventVue (1:45–3:00):** built private social networks for conference attendees. Raised ~$500K post-Techstars. Shut down in 2010 — and co-founder Rob Johnson gave a real, on-record explanation: "We were not seeing enough customers or growth to justify our market or product." Visual: "SHUT DOWN — 2010" title card with the founder's quote.
- **Segment 2 — Intense Debate (3:00–4:15):** a blog-commenting widget with threading, reputation, and moderation tools. Acquired by Automattic — WordPress's parent company — in 2008. Still actively maintained today, distributed as a WordPress plugin, "in maintenance mode" but genuinely alive. This is this batch's one confirmed long-term survivor — it has now outlived its own founding company's involvement by roughly 18 years. Visual: "ACQUIRED 2008 — STILL MAINTAINED TODAY" title card.
- **Segment 3 — Socialthing! (4:15–5:15):** a dashboard for managing a user's blogs, photos, and social activity in one place. Acquired by AOL in August 2008. Rebranded "AOL Lifestream" in 2010. Actually shut down February 24, 2017 — an acquire-then-discontinue story, but nearly 8.5 years later, not the roughly one-year pattern this channel has documented in other cases. Visual: acquisition-to-shutdown timeline stretched across nearly a decade.
- **Segment 4 — Brightkite (5:15–6:30):** an early location-based social network — check in by text, see who's nearby. Merged with mobile social network Limbo in April 2009. By December 2011, its apps were pulled from every app store and its site replaced with "This is not goodbye... we're just moving on to something better." By April 2012, it was gone. Visual: "MERGED 2009 → WOUND DOWN 2011-2012" title card.

### The Audit — Complicated (6:30–7:30)
- **Segment 5 — Villij (6:30–7:30):** an early social-graph platform. The original company was, in its own team's later account, "massively underfunded" — staff shrank to almost nothing, and the founders moved on to separately build other startups. No clean shutdown announcement exists. Then, in 2012, a completely different, unrelated company bought the Villij name and rebranded itself as Villij. On-screen framing card: "The name still exists. The company that built it doesn't. Those are two different things." Visual: name/company split-screen graphic.
- Mid-video CTA (~7:15): "This channel usually gives you a clean answer by now. Keep watching — the next part is honestly the more interesting half. Subscribe for a new autopsy every week."

### The Audit — Genuinely Unconfirmed (7:30–10:30)
- **Segment 6 — Filtrbox & madKast (7:30–9:00):** both real, both acquired — Filtrbox by Jive Software in 2010, madKast by ShareThis in 2008 — and in both cases, the acquirer said the technology would be absorbed into their own platform. What happened after that, whether either product kept existing under a different name or was quietly folded away entirely, is genuinely not confirmed anywhere I could find. On-screen framing card: "Acquired. Absorbed. And then — as far as the public record goes — nothing." Visual: both acquisition headlines, then a fade to a blank "?" card.
- **Segment 7 — J-Squared Media & search-to-phone (9:00–10:00):** one built a viral early Facebook app with over a million users in weeks; the other was a voice-search service for finding businesses on the go. Neither has a shutdown announcement, an acquisition, or any evidence of continued operation anywhere I could find. On-screen framing card: "I'm not going to guess a death date for a company just because I can't find one. This is what 'presumed gone, not confirmed' actually looks like."
- **Segment 8 — KBLabs (10:00–10:30):** the tenth company. Appears in secondary rosters of this exact batch. I could not independently confirm even the basics of what it built through a primary source. On-screen framing card: "Included because it's genuinely part of this cohort's record — flagged because it's the least-confirmed entry in the entire audit."

### The Actual Finding (10:30–12:30)
- Plain-language reveal: 4 of 10 have a clean, confirmed outcome. 1 is complicated but reasonably clear. 5 are genuinely unconfirmed — not "probably dead," just actually unconfirmable from any public record this research could locate.
- The practical lesson, stated directly: "'Just go check what happened to a startup' sounds simple. For a big, famous batch like a top YC class, it mostly is. For a real, smaller, once-covered batch that's just old enough — half the trail can genuinely run out. That's not a research failure. That's what actually happens to most startup history that isn't attached to a famous name."
- Visual: the full 10-company table, color-coded by confirmed / complicated / unconfirmed, shown all at once as the final proof

### Final CTA + Outro (12:30–14:00)
- VO: recap the one-sentence lesson — most startup outcomes aren't dramatic collapses or clean success stories, they're just quiet enough that even a real researcher, checking honestly, runs out of trail
- "Subscribe — I already did one of these for a Y Combinator batch, and this is the second in what I want to make a recurring format: audit a real batch, show you exactly what holds up"
- Engagement prompt: "Did you work at, use, or remember any of these 10 companies — especially the five I couldn't confirm? Tell me in the comments. I'll actually update this if something checks out."
- End screen: most recent autopsy + subscribe

## Alternative Scripts

### Script Version A — Data-First / Analyst Style
Opens directly on the full 10-row color-coded audit table, then works through each row in order of confidence (confirmed first, unconfirmed last), saving the "why this matters" reflection for the final third. Measured, table-forward, treats the video as a genuine reference resource — pairs naturally with 007's Version A.

### Script Version B — Narrative / Documentary Style
Opens on Intense Debate's story specifically — a small 2007 blog-comment widget that's quietly still alive today inside a company most people have never thought about — before pulling back to the full batch. Slower pacing, holds the "half unconfirmed" reveal until the second half.

### Script Version C — Fast-Cut / Listicle Style
Structured as "10 companies, one summer, here's what's actually left of each one," rapid-fire through all 10 in under 90 seconds before the three-way-split reveal. Strong Shorts-extraction potential — the "acquired, absorbed, and then... nothing" beat (Filtrbox/madKast) works especially well as a standalone Short.

---

## Audience Retention Optimization
- **Drop-off risk:** the unconfirmed-outcomes segment (7:30–10:30) if it reads as anticlimactic after the cleaner first half
- **Fix:** frame it explicitly as the more interesting, more honest half rather than a letdown — the mid-video CTA already sets this expectation, reinforce it going into Segment 6
- **Faster pacing opportunity:** trim Segment 8 (KBLabs) to the single sharpest line if retention data shows drop-off this late in the video
- **Curiosity increase opportunity:** tease "half of them, nobody can even confirm" even more explicitly in the cold open if early-retention data shows drop-off before Segment 6
- **Recommended visual change:** keep the 10-square grid visible in a corner throughout, updating from blank to confirmed/complicated/unconfirmed color-coding as each segment resolves it
