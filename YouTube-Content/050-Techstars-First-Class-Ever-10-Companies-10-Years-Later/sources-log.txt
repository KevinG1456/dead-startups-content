Sources Log — Package 050: Techstars Boulder 2007 (The First Class Ever)
Filled in: 2026-08-16

WHY THIS FILE EXISTS
This title fills dead-saas-30 batch title #29 ("The cohort study: tracking every
startup from one accelerator demo day, 10 years on"). It requires an actual
company-by-company audit of one real, named accelerator batch — same method as
this channel's package 007 (YC Summer 2006). This log is the full roster, with
each company's status individually sourced, and an honest accounting of which
outcomes could and could not be confirmed nearly 20 years later.

BATCH SELECTION — CONFIRMED DISTINCT FROM PACKAGE 007
Package 007 audited Y Combinator's Summer 2006 batch (11 companies). This package
uses a DIFFERENT accelerator entirely — Techstars, not YC — and specifically its
very first-ever class: Techstars Boulder, Summer 2007, founded by David Cohen
(with David Brown and Brad Feld). Zero company overlap with 007's roster. Chosen
because it's small (10 companies, fully auditable rather than sampled) and, as
Techstars' inaugural class, has genuine "first ever" framing distinct from 007's
"just a normal early batch" angle.

Structure: each of the 10 startups gave 5% equity (some sources say ~6%) in
exchange for a small amount of seed capital (commonly cited as $15,000-$18,000
per team, sources vary slightly) plus mentorship and office space, culminating in
a public demo day. Per Brad Feld's own 2010 blog post aggregating 2007-2009
Techstars data, 8 of the 10 companies in this class went on to raise a combined
total of just over $5 million in follow-on funding, and 6 of the 10 were
eventually acquired in some form.
Source: feld.com/archives/2010/03/techstars-data-from-2007-to-2009/

FULL ROSTER — Techstars Boulder, Class of 2007 (10 companies)
Primary roster source: TechCrunch, "TechStars Demo Day – Class of 2007" (Aug 17,
2007), techcrunch.com/2007/08/17/techstars-demo-day-class-of-2007/, cross-checked
against CBInsights company/investor pages and startupjedi.vc's Techstars network-
effect retrospective.

1. EVENTVUE — built private social networks around conferences, letting attendees
   connect before/during/after events online.
   STATUS: (a) SHUT DOWN, 2010. CONFIRMED, well-documented. Raised ~$500K from
   angel investors post-Techstars. Co-founder Rob Johnson gave an on-record
   explanation: "We were not seeing enough customers or growth to justify our
   market or product" — the team published a detailed public post-mortem. Widely
   cited afterward (including in Techstars' own "Do More Faster" book) as an
   example of building a "vitamin" (nice-to-have) instead of a "painkiller"
   (something organizers needed enough to pay for).
   Source: techcrunch.com/2010/02/05/eventvue-deadpool/; westword.com/news/
   techstars-post-mortem-could-boulder-startup-next-big-sound-suffer-same-fate-
   as-eventvue-5828281/; failory.com/cemetery/eventvue; mixergy.com/interviews/
   eventvue-josh-fraser/ (founder's own on-record post-mortem interview).

2. INTENSE DEBATE — a "souped-up" blog-commenting widget: threading, comment
   analytics, bulk moderation, user reputation, comment aggregation.
   STATUS: (d) ACQUIRED, STILL OPERATING TODAY as part of its acquirer. Acquired
   by Automattic (WordPress's parent company) in 2008. CONFIRMED still actively
   maintained as of 2025-2026 — described in its own current support materials
   as "in maintenance mode" (core functionality preserved, no major new feature
   development) but genuinely still live, still owned by Automattic, still
   distributed as a WordPress plugin. This is this batch's clearest survivor —
   distinct from OMGPOP/Xobni's pattern in package 007 (acquired then killed
   within about a year) — IntenseDebate has now outlived its founding company by
   roughly 18 years under its acquirer's ownership.
   Source: techcrunch.com/2007/08/07/intense-debate-soups-up-your-blog-comments
   (original 2007 launch coverage); intensedebate.com/wordpress and
   support.intensedebate.com/wordpress-plugin/ (current status, "© 2025/2026
   Automattic Inc." branding confirms ongoing ownership).

3. SOCIALTHING! — a dashboard for managing/aggregating a user's blogs, photos,
   music, friends, and social-network activity in one place.
   STATUS: (b) ACQUIRED THEN EVENTUALLY DISCONTINUED — but on a MUCH LONGER
   timeline than package 007's OMGPOP/Xobni pattern. Acquired by AOL, confirmed
   August 2008. Rebranded as "AOL Lifestream" in 2010. Finally shut down
   February 24, 2017 — roughly 8.5 years after acquisition, not the ~1-year
   pattern seen in 007. [VERIFY]: acquisition price was not disclosed/found in
   this research pass.
   Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialthing; huffpost.com/entry/socialthing-
   starts-spread_n_192328.

4. J-SQUARED MEDIA — built "Sticky Notes," an early viral Facebook application
   (1.7 million users within six weeks of launch, reportedly $30K/month revenue
   at demo day).
   STATUS: (e) UNCONFIRMED — presumed defunct, NOT independently corroborated.
   No shutdown announcement, acquisition, or continued-operation evidence was
   found in this research pass. Facebook's platform-API changes circa 2009-2010
   killed off most of that era's viral Facebook-app businesses, which is
   circumstantial context, NOT direct evidence of this specific company's fate.
   [VERIFY] — flag explicitly as "unable to confirm status," same honesty
   standard package 007 applied to Zanbazaar. Do not state this company shut
   down as a confirmed fact — state only that no evidence of continued
   operation was found.
   Source: jsquared.wordpress.com (company's own blog, last meaningful activity
   traced to the Facebook-app era; no closure announcement found on it).

5. SEARCH-TO-PHONE — a voice-enabled, automated Yellow-Pages-style search
   service, reportedly targeted at conferences/bars/public places; reportedly
   raised around $120K total (seed stage).
   STATUS: (e) UNCONFIRMED — presumed defunct, NOT independently corroborated.
   No shutdown, acquisition, or continued-operation evidence was found. One
   aggregator source lists status as "Alive," but this could not be
   independently corroborated against a primary source and should NOT be
   treated as confirmed — flag explicitly, same as J-Squared Media above.

6. VILLIJ — an early social-graph/interest-mapping platform ("connects users
   based on similar interests and online activity").
   STATUS: (c) COMPLICATED — the ORIGINAL 2007 company (operating as "Localcents,
   Inc.") did not shut down with a clean announcement, but was, per available
   reporting, "massively underfunded" relative to its technical ambitions; staff
   was reduced to a minimum, and the founders moved on to separately co-found
   other startups (DandyID.org, Claim.io) — in substance, the original company
   faded out rather than continuing to operate. In October 2012, a DIFFERENT,
   unrelated company called Tout'd (a social-recommendation platform, co-founded
   by someone who had been involved with the original Villij) acquired the
   Villij name/some assets and rebranded ITSELF as "Villij." This means the
   brand name technically still exists today, but attached to a different
   company/product than the one that went through Techstars in 2007 — do not
   present this as the original company "surviving." Present the original
   2007 Villij as effectively dissolved, with its name later reused elsewhere.
   Source: techcrunch.com/2012/10/10/toutd-is-now-called-villij-after-the-
   social-recommendation-platform-buys-a-techstars-alum-and-integrates-its-
   platform/.

7. FILTRBOX — a social-media monitoring platform for marketers/PR/brand
   management (real-time web monitoring).
   STATUS: (b) ACQUIRED, POST-ACQUISITION FATE UNCONFIRMED. Acquired by Jive
   Software, announced January 7, 2010, for an undisclosed amount; Jive stated
   Filtrbox's monitoring technology would be "absorbed into Jive's platform."
   [VERIFY] — no source found confirming whether Filtrbox continued to exist as
   a distinct, branded product after absorption, or was fully discontinued as a
   standalone name. Present as "acquired, technology absorbed into Jive's
   platform" — do NOT state definitively that the product was "killed" or that
   it "survived," since neither is confirmed.
   Source: techcrunch.com/2010/01/07/jive-software-acquires-social-media-
   monitoring-startup-filtrbox; huffpost.com/entry/jive-software-acquires-
   bo_n_415589; crunchbase.com/acquisition/jive-software-acquires-filtrbox.

8. KBLABS — appears in secondary/aggregated rosters of this cohort (e.g.
   startupjedi.vc's Techstars retrospective) alongside J-Squared Media,
   search-to-phone, and Villij as part of "the first Techstars program in 2007."
   STATUS: (e) LARGELY UNCONFIRMED — the WEAKEST-SOURCED entry in this entire
   roster, comparable to (or a step below) package 007's single-sourced
   Zanbazaar entry. This research pass could not independently corroborate even
   basic details about what KBLabs' product was, via a primary contemporaneous
   source (e.g. the original Aug 2007 TechCrunch demo-day coverage does not
   appear to name it directly in the excerpts retrieved). Flag prominently on
   screen with an explicit "unable to fully corroborate — included because it
   appears in secondary rosters of this cohort, not independently confirmed"
   caveat, presented with visibly less confidence than the other 9 entries.

9. BRIGHTKITE — an early location-based social network (SMS/app "check-ins,"
   see who's nearby).
   STATUS: (b) ACQUIRED-THEN-DISCONTINUED, CONFIRMED with real dates. Founded
   2007 by Brady Becker, Martin May, and Alan Seideman. Merged with mobile
   social network Limbo in April 2009 (the combined company kept the Brightkite
   name; Limbo also announced a $9M funding round at the same time). By
   December 2011, Brightkite's apps had been pulled from app stores and its
   website replaced with "This is not goodbye... we're just moving on to
   something better." By April 2012, the site was no longer operating. Total
   arc: merger to full shutdown in ~2.5-3 years.
   Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightkite; techcrunch.com/2009/04/07/mobile-
   socializing-limbo-merges-with-brightkite-and-announces-9-million-funding-
   round/; techcrunch.com/2011/12/20/brightkite-winds-down-says-it-will-come-
   back-with-something-better-again.

10. MADKAST — a widget letting publishers easily syndicate blog content in one
    line of embeddable code (notably, TechCrunch coverage frames it as the
    FIRST company from this class to publicly launch, Aug 3 2007, ahead of the
    group demo day two weeks later).
    STATUS: (b) ACQUIRED, POST-ACQUISITION FATE UNCONFIRMED. Acquired by
    ShareThis — described by TechCrunch at the time as "a fellow widget maker"
    — announced September 23, 2008. [VERIFY] — no source found confirming
    whether madKast continued as a distinct branded product after acquisition
    or was fully folded into/discontinued within ShareThis's own product line.
    Present as "acquired by ShareThis, terms undisclosed" — do not state a
    definitive kill date or claim of continued survival, since neither is
    confirmed.
    Source: techcrunch.com/2007/08/03/madkast-easily-sydicate-your-blog-in-
    one-line-of-code/ (original launch); techcrunch.com/2008/09/23/sharethis-
    to-acquire-fellow-widget-maker-madkast/ (acquisition); crunchbase.com/
    acquisition/sharethis-acquires-madkast--cd3ee973.

THE REAL FINDING — this batch is genuinely harder to trace than 007's, and
THAT is itself part of the honest story, not a flaw to hide

Unlike package 007 (YC Summer 2006), where all 11 companies' outcomes were
confirmable with real confidence, this smaller, earlier, less-covered
accelerator's inaugural batch has a meaningfully higher proportion of
UNCONFIRMED outcomes almost 20 years later:

CONFIRMED, CLEAN OUTCOMES (4 of 10):
- EventVue: shut down (2010), well-documented, founder's own post-mortem.
- Intense Debate: acquired (2008), STILL ACTIVELY MAINTAINED under Automattic
  today — this batch's one clear, confirmed long-term survivor.
- Socialthing!: acquired (2008), discontinued (2017) — acquired-then-killed,
  but on an 8.5-year timeline, not 007's ~1-year pattern.
- Brightkite: merged (2009), wound down (2011-2012) — a clean, dated arc.

COMPLICATED BUT REASONABLY CLEAR (1 of 10):
- Villij: original company effectively dissolved (underfunded, team dispersed
  to other startups); its NAME was later reused by an unrelated company in
  2012. Present as "original company gone," not "survived."

ACQUIRED, BUT POST-ACQUISITION FATE GENUINELY UNCONFIRMED (2 of 10):
- Filtrbox (acquired by Jive Software, 2010): "absorbed" — survival vs.
  discontinuation as a standalone product not confirmed either way.
- madKast (acquired by ShareThis, 2008): same — absorption confirmed,
  standalone survival/discontinuation not confirmed either way.

WHOLLY UNCONFIRMED STATUS, NO SHUTDOWN OR ACQUISITION EVIDENCE FOUND (3 of 10):
- J-Squared Media, search-to-phone: presumed defunct given no evidence of
  continued operation, but this is NOT the same as a confirmed shutdown — flag
  clearly as unconfirmed, not asserted as fact.
- KBLabs: the weakest-sourced entry in the whole roster — flag it with visibly
  LESS confidence than every other entry, similar to (or below) 007's
  Zanbazaar precedent.

RECOMMENDED FRAMING FOR THE VIDEO: do not force a single clean ratio the way
007 could. The honest finding here is TWO-LAYERED: (1) among the outcomes that
CAN be confirmed, the pattern of "founded 2007, several small quiet failures,
a couple of acquire-then-eventually-discontinue stories, one company that
genuinely outlived its own founding company by ~18 years under an acquirer" is
real and interesting; AND (2) the fact that 5-6 of 10 outcomes from a real,
named, once-covered accelerator batch are now UNCONFIRMABLE after not even 20
years is itself a genuine, sourced finding worth stating directly on screen —
a rebuttal to the idea that "just check what happened to them" is always easy,
even for real, once-press-covered companies. This is a DIFFERENT, complementary
lesson to 007's dual-ratio finding, not a weaker version of the same video.
