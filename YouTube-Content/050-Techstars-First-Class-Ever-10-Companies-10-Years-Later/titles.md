## Ranking (Strongest → Weakest)
1. **I Tracked Down Every Company From Techstars' First Class Ever. Half of Them, Nobody Can Even Confirm.** — leads with the genuinely novel finding (untraceability) rather than a flat failure stat, differentiates hard from 007
2. 10 Companies. 1 Confirmed Survivor. 5 Nobody Can Even Confirm Are Dead. — sharp, numeral-forward, sets the honest three-way split up front
3. This Company Got Acquired in 2008. It's Still Being Maintained Today. Nobody Talks About It. — strong specific-detail hook (Intense Debate), highly clickable on its own
4. Half of Techstars' First-Ever Class Doesn't Show Up in Any Record Anymore. — clean restatement of the core finding
5. One of These 10 Companies Quietly Outlived Its Own Founding Company By 18 Years. — strong specific-irony hook
6. This Startup's Name Still Exists Today. The Company That Built It Doesn't. — good mystery framing (Villij)
7. I Wanted a Clean "X of 10 Failed" Number. The Real Answer Is Messier Than That. — strong authority/honesty framing, good for the analyst-style audience
8. Before Techstars Was Huge, It Was 10 Companies in Boulder. Most of Them Vanished Without a Trace. — good historical-curiosity framing
9. What Happened to Techstars Boulder 2007 Companies — best pure-SEO, lower CTR
10. Techstars First Class 2007 Explained — strong secondary search title

## Recommended Title
**"I Tracked Down Every Company From Techstars' First Class Ever. Half of Them, Nobody Can Even Confirm."**

Reasoning: leads with the video's actual, more novel finding — not "most startups fail" (already 007's territory) but "even a real, once-covered startup batch becomes genuinely hard to trace after 20 years, and honestly admitting that is more interesting than forcing a clean number." Sets up the full three-way audit (confirmed / complicated / unconfirmed) as the payoff, and differentiates unmistakably from package 007 in both method and finding.
