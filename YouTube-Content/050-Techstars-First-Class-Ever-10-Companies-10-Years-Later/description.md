## Long Description
Every week on this channel, we reconstruct startup outcomes using primary sources. This week: a full
audit of Techstars Boulder's Summer 2007 class — the very first class the now-massive accelerator
ever ran, founded by David Cohen (with David Brown and Brad Feld).

10 companies went through the program: EventVue, Intense Debate, Socialthing!, J-Squared Media,
search-to-phone, Villij, Filtrbox, KBLabs, Brightkite, and madKast. Each gave up roughly 5-6% equity
for a small amount of seed capital plus mentorship, culminating in an August 2007 demo day.

Four have clean, well-documented outcomes. EventVue raised roughly $500K post-Techstars and shut down
in 2010, with co-founder Rob Johnson giving a real, on-record explanation for why. Intense Debate was
acquired by Automattic — WordPress's parent company — in 2008, and is still actively maintained
today, nearly 18 years later. Socialthing! was acquired by AOL in 2008, rebranded "AOL Lifestream" in
2010, and finally discontinued in February 2017. Brightkite merged with Limbo in April 2009 and wound
down between December 2011 and April 2012.

One, Villij, is complicated: the original 2007 company was, by its own team's later account,
massively underfunded, and effectively dissolved as the founders moved on to other startups. Its name
was later bought by an unrelated company in 2012, which rebranded itself as Villij — the name lives
on, the original company doesn't.

The remaining five have genuinely unconfirmed final outcomes, despite real research effort. Filtrbox
and madKast were both acquired (by Jive Software in 2010 and ShareThis in 2008, respectively) and
their technology "absorbed" into their acquirers' platforms — but neither's fate as a standalone
product afterward could be confirmed either way. J-Squared Media and search-to-phone have no
shutdown, acquisition, or continued-operation evidence at all. KBLabs is the weakest-sourced entry in
the whole roster.

The real finding of this video isn't a clean failure-rate number — it's that even a real, once
press-covered startup batch gets meaningfully harder to trace, honestly, the smaller and older it
gets. That's presented directly on screen, not glossed over.
